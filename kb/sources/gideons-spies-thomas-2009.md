Gordon Thomas's access-driven history of Mossad, alternating genuinely documented operational history (the Vanunu kidnapping, the Meshal fiasco, the Pollard/LAKAM case, the Liebefeld bugging bust, the CIA rendition architecture) with novelistic set pieces sourced to a small stable of repeat intelligence sources. Thomas presents himself as the only non-Israeli writer Mossad has let this close; the scaffolding is often real, the interiors often not, and the book almost never tells the reader which is which.

Phase-1 reliability finding: unreliable as a sole source for any extraordinary claim. Nearly every claim adjudicated 'doesn't hold' or 'unresolved' in phase 1 traces to one of five repeat sources, named here explicitly as the book's repeat-source pool: Ari Ben-Menashe, Victor Ostrovsky, Joel Bainerman, Barry Chamish and Michael Shrimpton. Thomas has effectively no footnote apparatus, renders verbatim dialogue from rooms he never entered, inflates scale numbers as atmosphere, and states in Ch. 30 that he cannot recall a source ever lying to him. Where a public skeleton exists (trials, resignations, official transcripts, flight logs) his account generally matches it; anywhere a single colourful source offers a total theory (PROMIS as skeleton key, Diana, Kelly, Rabin, Los Alamos, the Niger forgeries) the claim should be treated as that source's assertion, not a finding.
