## The shape

Two curves move in opposite directions across eleven years. Frequency rises ~3x
(1–2.5 → 4–6 apologies per 100 messages). Substance falls: bare "sorry"s climb from a
steady ~50% share to 67% in the final quarter; commitment language runs 2:1 behind
self-flagellation; the one behavioral commitment on record dies mid-message.

## The mechanism

The causal arrow runs behavior → apology-inflation, not apology → behavior-change.
Same behaviors get apologized for across 7+ years (trust: "destroy your trust in me"
2019-02-16 → "You haven't made any sort of effort to gain your trust" 2026-01-27, her
echo of his complaint). Each recurrence makes the next apology bigger in language
("I am a horrible person for all the hell I have put you through") and thinner in
behavioral content. By the end "sorry" is conversational punctuation
("Sorry just got out of the shower"), which trains the receiver to discount the rare
substantive ones. She debased her own currency.

Note on the counterexample (`dat:1505`): the 2019 alternate-number thread ran at
4.16/100, *higher* than the 2023–2025 NYC thread at 2.93/100. Pure temporal inflation
would predict the later window exceeding the earlier one. The inversion shows the
rate tracks per-channel conflict intensity, not calendar time alone — the
decade-level tripling holds, but the driver is conflict, not the clock.

## What it is not

It is not insincerity — see `int:annie-apology-sincerity-verdict`. The inflation is
compatible with (and arguably evidence for) genuine remorse that cannot convert into
behavior: the worse the conduct gets, the more there is to be sorry for, the more
"sorry" gets said, the less each instance can carry.
