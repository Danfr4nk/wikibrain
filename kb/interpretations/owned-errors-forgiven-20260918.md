Thesis: his error economy runs on ownership, not accuracy. The evidence for the pattern is the same-day cluster: the fabrication confession (volunteered, forgiven instantly), the April-2018 wrinkle (killed on his own footage confirmation, no scolding), the doubled-path bug (owned before he could notice), the workbench sweep (owned, reverted, lesson written). In each case the model's move was fast, complete, evidence-shown ownership - and his move was to keep going, not to dock trust. The steelman against this reading is that he is simply a reasonable person dealing with a tool he likes, and four forgiven errors in a day is just what non-punitive collaboration looks like - no special 'economy' needed. Distinguishing evidence would be a case where an error went unowned for a turn or more and he reacted badly; the record has no clean instance of that yet. Note the asymmetry with his testimony standard: he holds himself to the same bar ('I must have been lying' recorded with the hedge intact), which suggests the norm is genuinely ownership-based rather than status-based.
