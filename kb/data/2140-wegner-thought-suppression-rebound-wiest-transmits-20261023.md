## Personal bearing

Suppression is already a named mechanism on this wiki at higher strength: the shadow page's Vol 11 material makes integration versus suppression the therapeutic crux in neurosis — suppression is the mechanism that keeps the neurosis running — and Dan's documented memory practice is built on the same side of that line (retain verbatim, never consolidate away, let the record hold what consciousness would rather drop). Wegner's rebound is the experimental literature's version of the same point, and it is the one piece of laboratory psychology in Wiest's book that lands directly on ground the wiki already works. It is entered as the finding it is, with Wiest as the transmitting source, not the authority — Wegner 1987 is citable; Essay 56's symptom inventory is not.

## Evidence

Phase 1 rated the underlying finding high confidence: Wegner's white-bear experiments (1987) are real, and the rebound of suppressed thought is replicated in spirit across the ironic-process literature. The essay's use of it is the standard Wiest pattern the phase-1 report documents throughout — a real finding carrying an invented superstructure in the same register, the join unmarked.

## Limits

The finding is about thought suppression under instruction in experimental settings; it does not by itself establish the book's broader emotional doctrine (that every suppressed feeling produces life symptoms), and the sixteen-item inventory has no evidence behind it at all. The wiki's stronger home for the suppression/integration claim remains the Jung-sourced shadow page; this node records the experimental finding and the boundary of the essay built on it, nothing more.
