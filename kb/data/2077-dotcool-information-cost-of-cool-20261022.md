## Personal bearing

This is Dan's scene's actual economy. The tracks are cheap or free; the scarce currency is knowledge - the orbit, the right remix, the right era, the right version. His crate filter (only about a third of liked tracks passing into the VIP CRATE in the documented pipeline) and the commissioned crate covers are the visible receipt for invisible expertise: the artifact anyone can copy, the judgment nobody can copy without the knowledge. Curation, on this account, is not a by-product of his taste system - it is the costly signal the system produces.

## Evidence

Berger & Ward's signal-cost analysis is the book's cited basis, and the phase-1 report treats the information-cost theme as naming what Dan's documented curation already does (crate pass rates and cover commissions are held record, dat:0077 / dat:0078 lineage). The DotCool chapter's broader civilizational claims are not relied on here.

## Limits

Limits: Confidence is med: the information-cost mechanism is well-grounded as a description of insider-signal economies, but its application to any single crate decision is interpretive. Information-cost signals only work on audiences equipped to read them; to everyone else they are invisible, which is the point and the limit at once.
