## Personal bearing

Dan maintains several group identities at once and runs his circles through shared artifacts and rituals - crates, enshrinements, public credit. On this distinction, those shared forms are an expandable currency, not dilutions of his standing: every person brought inside a shared ritual increases the esteem the membership itself carries. The practical boundary is equally clear: one-over-one rank contests inside the same groups remain exactly as zero-sum as they feel, because rank is the other currency. Knowing which game a given friction is in - esteem or rank - tells him whether sharing fixes it or cannot.

## Evidence

The status-versus-respect distinction is the phase-1 report's pick for the book's cleanest usable idea, and it is conceptual - carried by the local-status and ethnographic material, not by any scan. It coheres with dat:2078 (status computed locally): the local ladder is where both currencies circulate.

## Limits

Limits: Non-rival at the margin is not infinite: esteem attached to membership can still be debased if membership stops meaning anything, and the book does not measure how far sharing can run before that happens. The claim distinguishes two currencies; it does not promise the esteem currency never inflates.
