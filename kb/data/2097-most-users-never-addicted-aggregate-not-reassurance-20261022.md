## Personal bearing

This is the statistic that frames Dan's documented self-understanding as a regular cocaine user who is not addicted — and this node is written as the honest version of it, not the reassurance version. The aggregate rates are real, and they do support that compulsion is not the default outcome of cocaine use. They do not measure Dan's personal risk: the denominator is everyone who ever used, even once, which dilutes the rate with experimenters, while conditional risk concentrates with exactly the variables Dan's own logging tracks — frequency, dose and comorbidity. Read beside the wiki's finance-driven dosage arc (dat:0062), the usable conclusion is the narrower one: non-addiction is statistically ordinary, and a frequent pattern still has to be priced on its own frequency and dose, not on the lifetime-ever-user base rate.

## Evidence

Hart's permission-granting figures — 70 percent or more of users never meeting addiction criteria, and nearly 80 percent of illegal-drug users using without problems (Ch. 9) — trace to the Anthony / Warner / Kessler conditional dependence rates above, which the phase-1 report checked against the public record and found real, including the point Hart under-uses: tobacco, the legal drug, tops the table at roughly 32%.

## Limits

Limits: the reassurance reading is the fail this node exists to name. Hart never states the denominator caveat, and the inference his prose sometimes leans on — that a regular, frequent user's risk is likewise small — does not follow from a lifetime-ever-user rate. The theme survives the correction (addiction is not the usual outcome of use); the personal reassurance is weaker than the prose. No claim here is presented as Dan's belief, and nothing here adjudicates Dan's own status — that is a question for his record and his clinicians, not for a population base rate.
