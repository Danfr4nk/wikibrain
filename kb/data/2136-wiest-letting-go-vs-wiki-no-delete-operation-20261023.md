## Personal bearing

This is the sharpest point of contact between the book and Dan's documented life, and it is a collision, not a fit. The no-delete page is one of the wiki's load-bearing models: the record shows commitments suspended, contact withheld, material retained verbatim and unconsolidated — and, on the page's own test, every deletion Dan performs or reports across 102,035 sent rows has a digital object; deletion works on machines, never on people. Wiest's prescription asks for exactly the operation the record does not contain: an internal acceptance that closes the file on what is gone. Filed as a tension because a future reader of either page should meet the other page's position at full strength — the book's most-repeated remedy is, on this wiki's evidence, a description of an operation its subject does not run.

## Evidence

Phase 1 located the prescription at Essays 66 and 74 and stated it as the book's core release mechanism; the no-delete page's claim, five instances, and deletion-vocabulary test are held on the wiki itself. Neither side is caricatured here: Wiest's "accepting what's already gone" is an interior act, not an instruction to delete records, and the no-delete model is an observed operation, not a refusal of acceptance in the abstract.

## Limits

A tension is not a refutation in either direction. The book cannot show that Dan's retention is resistance — its own vocabulary would reclassify any failure of its advice as exactly that, which is the list form's unfalsifiability, not evidence. And the wiki model describes what the record shows; it does not by itself settle whether an acceptance operation is available to Dan or would be good for him. The two positions are recorded side by side, unresolved, because that is what the evidence supports.
