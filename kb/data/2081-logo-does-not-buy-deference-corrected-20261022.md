## Personal bearing

Dan should not budget for logo effects on strangers in his documented styling direction (urban skate culture / cool counterculture, his words, 2026-10-16). Whatever his styling purchases, on the best replication evidence it is in-group legibility - being read correctly by people inside the code (see dat:2077) - not deference, generosity or compliance from people outside it. A luxury mark spent expecting the alligator effect is money spent on an effect that failed replication.

## Evidence

The original Lacoste field experiments are the book's Ch. 4 set-piece for prosocial signaling. Berger's 2017 PLOS ONE replication is the corrective evidence the phase-1 audit relies on: double-blind, extended, five experiments, no positive effect, with the perceived-wealth / lowered-prosociality split measured directly.

## Limits

Limits: The book's version named as the fail: the alligator effect as advertised in Ch. 4. The corrected claim covers stranger-directed deference effects; it does not say logos carry no information - perceived wealth did rise - and it does not touch in-group legibility, which runs on different machinery.
