## Personal bearing

The wiki's Jung concept pages are part of the vocabulary Dan's record is interpreted in — [[wiki/mind/concepts/shadow]] is built directly from Jung's *Collected Works* (*Aion*, Vol 9-I, Vol 7, Vol 11), and it insists on what surrounds the projection claim: the shadow as the first stage of individuation, integration rather than suppression as the clinical crux, the moral effort admission requires. Wiest's formula, repeated almost verbatim across five essays, is that page's central mechanism with everything around it removed — no stages, no integration, no source. The bearing is a citation hazard, not a discovery: this is the version of the idea that circulates (as quotes, captions, shares — the economy the book was built for), so it is the version most likely to arrive in Dan's orbit and be quoted back at the strength of the wiki's Jung pages. This node exists to fix the two at their different strengths in one place.

## Evidence

The phase-1 report verified the formula's recurrence (Essays 1, 5, 28, 41, 42) and its near-verbatim repetition, and checked it against the wiki page's Jung-sourced position: the unrecognised shadow is projected; what is hated in others is the disowned trait. The sentence matches. The apparatus does not: Wiest cites no Jung text, names no mechanism beyond the aphorism, and offers recognition ("does this feel true of you?") as the only test.

## Limits

The mapping is vocabulary-level, and this node does not upgrade Wiest into a Jung source: she is a transmitting artefact of the genre, and any claim resting on her version should be re-derived from the *Collected Works* material the shadow page already holds. Nor does the folk form's popularity count as evidence for the mechanism — the list-book's recognition test is one the reader grades themselves, and a hit is not confirmation.
