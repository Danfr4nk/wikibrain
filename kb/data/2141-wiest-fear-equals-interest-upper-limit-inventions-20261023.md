## Personal bearing

This node is the calibration guard for everything else mined from this book. Dan's documented practice grades claims — a finding, a folk restatement, and an invention get different numbers and different weight (see dat:2139 for the graded/ungraded contrast). Wiest's book defeats that grading from inside the reading experience: "fear = interest" is load-bearing for its most distinctive theme (discomfort as signal), and the "upper limit" does the same work for its self-sabotage theme, yet neither has anything behind it — and both are written in the identical register as the book's genuine findings (affective forecasting, hedonic adaptation, Wegner rebound). Naming the two inventions explicitly is what licenses the rest of the Wiest material to sit on the wiki at all: without this node, a later reader could meet the aphorisms at finding strength.

## Evidence

Phase 1 checked both claims against the record: no established result in the form "fear = interest" is cited in the book or known to the report; the "upper limit" traces to Hendricks, a self-help author, with no empirical programme. Both verdicts were rated high confidence. The surrounding baseline literature Essay 10 borrows credibility from (hedonic adaptation) is real — that is precisely the mechanism of the borrow.

## Limits

"Invention" here means no empirical programme and no citable finding, not that the metaphors can never describe anyone's experience — a reader may genuinely sabotage good stretches, and fear can accompany interest. What the inventions cannot do is what the book has them do: serve as premises. Any future wiki use of either phrase should cite this node for its standing, not the essays for its truth.
