## Personal bearing

Direct bearing on Dan's documented supply environment. He bought cocaine through the Menore delivery operation (November 2018 – February 20, 2025, roughly 280 deliveries, $60 / 0.5 g and $100 / 1 g) at a premium he himself characterized as paying in part for tested product; a fentanyl warning already circulated inside that thread. Hart's argument is the public-health version of a purchasing decision Dan had already made — and it prices part of what was lost when that service ended in February 2025: a benchmark supplier whose product identity was part of the price. This node records Hart's claim and that documented purchasing rationale; it does not assert that any particular delivery was or was not contaminated.

## Evidence

Phase-1 checks: opioids were involved in roughly 73% of U.S. cocaine-involved overdose deaths in 2017, the documented modern pattern for cocaine users specifically; Hart's worked examples include Energy Control in Barcelona testing thousands of samples for free and the Belfast street team handing clean needles to alley injectors (Chs. 3, 10). His criticism of Baltimore health commissioner Leana Wen (Ch. 3) is precisely for reciting fentanyl death statistics while refusing to endorse drug-checking.

## Limits

Limits: confidence is high that adulteration is a major and rising driver of deaths; it is only medium for Hart's stronger version, that ignorance explains most opioid deaths. His presentation that single-opioid deaths are only about a quarter of the total leans on overlapping multiple-cause counts, and CDC category counts are not a clean partition — polysubstance involvement is real and large, but the categories do not sum the way his prose implies.
