## Personal bearing

Dan builds and evaluates AI and agent pipelines and is routinely sold measurement mystique; this book is a clean specimen of the sales pattern - an unpublished, TV-origin, roughly-24-subject scan narrated for a decade as a research programme's foundation, by authors one of whom sells neuromarketing. The transferable rule for his own tooling decisions: demand the paper, the n, and the replication before the dashboard. It is the same discipline his lexical-measurement and instrument-audit practices already enforce on his own wiki tooling; this node records the book as a case where skipping it would have bought a decade-old television segment as settled neuroscience.

## Evidence

Phase-1 audit, background evidence section: flagship experiment's provenance (2004, for a television programme; Lisa Ling's inquiry as its origin), absence of any peer-reviewed publication or reported statistics, and the reverse-inference problem stated in the report. Quartz's dopamine / prediction-error material (Ch. 2) is separately assessed as the book's most reliable neuroscience - the fail is specific to the cool scan, not to the author's whole field.

## Limits

Limits: The book's version named as the fail: the scan reveals the brain's cool circuit. What actually holds is narrower and more useful: an object lesson in provenance discipline. The failure does not touch the book's behavioral claims, which stand or fall on their own literatures (see dat:2075, dat:2076, dat:2078).
