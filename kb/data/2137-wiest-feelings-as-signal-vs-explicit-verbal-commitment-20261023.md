## Personal bearing

Dan's documented inbound format rule is the spine of several wiki models at once: what binds is what is stated, explicitly, in words — the severance sequence, the witness channel, the write-only rule system on the explicit-verbal-commitment page all turn on it. Wiest's book instructs the opposite epistemology at its most confident: the real signal is the one that cannot be justified, reasons offered for a choice are evidence it is forced, and the trustworthy voice is the one that doesn't use words. On Dan's record, that channel is precisely the one that does not carry load — an unstated feeling, his own or anyone else's, changes no rule. The tension matters because the book's advice is generic-reader advice: applied to this reader it does not merely fail to help, it points at the channel his architecture is documented not to run on.

## Evidence

Phase 1 located the feelings-as-signal doctrine at Essays 6, 7, 44 and 99, including Essay 44's fullest statement — the inner voice "will speak to you without you ever knowing that it is," its deliverances "unjustifiable," choices backed by a list of "because" marked as not genuinely wanted. The wiki side is the explicit-verbal-commitment page's measurements and founding rule, held from the corpus. The book also contradicts itself here on its own terms (Essay 97's trustworthy gut against Essay 99's "don't trust all of your feelings"), which the phase-1 report records as the list form's contradiction tolerance, not a nuance to resolve.

## Limits

The conflict is between a prescription and a documented architecture, not between two measurements: Wiest offers no evidence that wordless feeling is a reliable signal (her one cited prop, the "Emotional Oracle Effect," is a narrow, contested lab effect upgraded in the telling), and the wiki page describes how Dan's system registers commitment, not how all minds should. Nothing here adjudicates intuition as a human faculty; it records that this book's central channel and this subject's documented channel are different channels.
