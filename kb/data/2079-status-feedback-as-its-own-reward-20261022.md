## Personal bearing

Dan should read the Wiki Brain's daily line counts and growth as a recognition feed, not merely as output metrics. That reading predicts the documented pattern better than any productivity rationale: the 10,000-line floor is felt as a structural obligation, and production running under it registers as frustration rather than as a scheduling fact. What he should not assume is the book's neuroscience version - that a scan has located the machinery. The behavioral claim is enough to explain the pull, and it is the version that survived audit.

## Evidence

The behavioral stratum: status-as-its-own-reward findings presented in Ch. 5, consistent with the wider sociometer literature (Leary) the book draws on. The phase-1 audit separates this stratum explicitly from the Ch. 3 scan: roughly 24 design students, run in 2004 for a television programme, never published in a peer-reviewed journal, no statistics anywhere in the book, and medial prefrontal activation for uncool items too.

## Limits

Limits: The book's version named as the fail: a dedicated cool circuit revealed by the scan. The corrected claim is behavioral only. Applying it to the wiki's line-count floor is an interpretation of Dan's documented pattern (floor mandate, sub-floor frustration), offered because it predicts that pattern - it is not a measurement of his motives, and no book claim here is Dan's stated belief.
