## Personal bearing

Dan's wiki explains the hardest pattern in his documented life — eleven years of exits that never closed — with mechanisms, not monsters: intermittent reinforcement, DARVO, the confession trap, the tether oscillation ([[wiki/mind/synthesis/attachment-trauma-bond]]); an affection-rate rebound inside a dependency read explicitly as intermittent reinforcement ([[wiki/people/annie-ulmer]]); a threat → apology → reset loop with its behavioral payload measured at zero ([[wiki/mind/concepts/conflict-architecture]]). The folk model a million readers now bring to exactly such records has no slot for the first of those mechanisms. Its manipulator is a type who acts from essence; nothing in the type predicts why *unpredictable, schedule-shaped* affection binds harder than steady cruelty or steady kindness — which is the question Dan's record actually poses. This node records the omission as a standing caution: the popular vocabulary is not a weaker version of the wiki's model, it is a different model missing the wiki's central gear, and any future write-up of Dan's attachment history that reaches for the genre's terms first will lose the explanation.

## Evidence

Phase 1 read the omnibus in full (144,590 words) and flagged the near-absence directly: intermittent reinforcement is barely theorized anywhere in the five books, while the Dark Triad/Tetrad type apparatus is developed at length. The wiki-side anchors predate this job and rest on the primary record as cited on their pages; this node asserts nothing new about Dan's history — only the documented mismatch between the genre's explanatory kit and the mechanisms his wiki already carries.

## Limits

An omission finding is a finding about the book, not about any person: it does not strengthen the wiki's intermittent-reinforcement model (that stands or falls on Dan's record), and it does not show the folk model is useless to every reader — only that it cannot represent the mechanism this record turns on. No book claim is presented as Dan's belief, and the book's type-explanation remains rejected here as in dat:2115.
