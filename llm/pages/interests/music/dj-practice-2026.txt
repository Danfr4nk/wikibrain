# DJ practice — 2026-09-15 assessment

On 2026-09-15 Dan uploaded six performance videos to the main chat and
asked for two things: audio analysis of the transitions, and a blunt
answer — "Are these evident of someone who knows djing? Don't give me
bullshit" (16:43:05Z). What follows is the session's verdict, in his
words and Sammy's, with the one correction the session produced.

## The first verdict: the programmer brain

The first assessment (16:43:10Z) came back yes, with one precise
boundary:

> "What these prove: you think like a programmer. The macro-arc across
> four transitions (morph → exhale → wake-up → swell) is energy
> management, which is the actual job. ... A non-DJ makes four
> identical crossfades. You made four different moves."

The boundary: the beatmatching was handled by software — "Spotify is
beatmatching for you. Sync, time-stretch, phrase grids — the app
handles it. So this proves the selector brain (programming, tension,
release), not live execution (riding pitch, reading a room,
recovering from a trainwreck)."

## The correction: it was an iPhone DJ app

Mid-session Dan corrected the interface identification. Sammy had read
the footage as Spotify's own DJ mode; Dan's correction (17:32:40Z):

> "No it's me dj'ing in an iPhone app"

A third-party iPhone DJ app pulling from his Spotify library — manual
crossfader moves, loading the next record live, cueing while the
current track plays out. The correction was accepted in-chat
("You're right, my mistake — that's a third-party DJ app on your
iPhone pulling from your Spotify library, not a Spotify feature. I
saw the Spotify tab and branding and jumped."). The app's name was not
established. This matters because it upgrades the first verdict: the
blends were automated in the transition-editor pass, but the
performance footage is hands on decks.

## The second verdict: the hands

After Dan shared a Google Photos performance link
(https://photos.app.goo.gl/6S9QPTCVLGceR2xZ9, 17:30:45Z), the full
performance assessment landed (17:39:07Z) — three transitions in 4:15,
everything locked at 130 (High → BLAH BLAH BLAH → car crash → Eat Me
Better):

**What worked:** deck preparation was genuinely good — the next record
always loaded, cued, and pitch-locked before needed. The first
transition was proper technique: high-pass opening on the incoming
track, low-pass filter sweeping the outgoing out to −77%, crossfader
riding underneath. Tempo held at 130 while pulling one track down
3.7% and another 1.5% — "SYNC is on, but you're still choosing the
tempo, not the app."

**What was off:** about 35 seconds of nearly kickless audio after the
first transition (the filter possibly left engaged — "a dead zone");
levels running hot, the master hitting digital full-scale during
stacked blends; and all three transitions using the same move — the
long crossfader blend, no cuts, no drop-swaps, no loop exits.

When Dan pressed — "So you think my transitions were good?"
(17:44:40Z) — the answer held: "Yeah. The blends themselves were
good — clean handoffs, no clashes, the filter work on the first one
was genuinely proper technique. My critiques were about what happened
*around* the transitions (filter left on, hot gains, same move three
times), not the transitions failing. Nothing trainwrecked. That's the
bar, and you cleared it."
