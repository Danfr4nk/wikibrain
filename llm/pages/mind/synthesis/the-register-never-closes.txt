# The Register Never Closes

Across every chemical register in the record, he has never closed one. Quits
are substitutions. The single true zero — alcohol — is a categorical exclusion
coded to betrayal, not a health decision.

The rule, stated plainly: **the architecture has exactly two moves —
substitute the delivery system, or exclude by verdict. Never taper-and-close.**

This page carries the complete dated log for every register the wiki holds,
the one measured night that exists, and the state of the instrument that
measured it — which is worse than the pages downstream of it imply.

## The two moves, and why the distinction is not semantic

A **substitution** keeps the register open and changes the hardware. The
announcement uses completion language, the substance is not the thing being
removed, and the new delivery system is described as the solution rather than
as the same problem in a different housing. The 2008 nicotine quit and the
2010 e-cigarette are the template.

An **exclusion by verdict** closes the register in one move, with no taper, no
announced attempt, no counter, and no relapse arithmetic — because there was
never a process to relapse from. Alcohol is the only instance, and its coding
is not medical.

The move the record never contains is the ordinary one: a declining dose over
a stated period, ending at zero, with the register closed and stated as
closed. Sixteen years of Suboxone, twenty years of cocaine, eighteen years of
nicotine, and daily cannabis, and **no register has ever been tapered to
zero.** One within-register descent is implied — the 2013 *"down to 2mg/day"*
— and it terminates at a held floor rather than at a closure; it is worked in
full under [The null case](#the-null-case-what-the-rule-cannot-classify-and-what-that-costs).

A third caveat, conceded at the top rather than buried: the *positive* half of
the rule — that there are exactly two moves — is unfinished. The Suboxone run
is a null case neither move classifies, and
[[wiki/health/maintenance-vs-verdict]] keeps three readings of it live. The
*negative* half survives all three.

| 2022-03-31 | disposable vape, complained about by flavour | disposable |

**Two rows carry the whole argument.**

The **2019-10-09** row states the substitution logic in the subject's own
words, and neither stated reason is nicotine: the animals, and the flavour.
What is being removed is combustion and taste. The register is not in the
sentence.

The **2011-01-10** row is the load-bearing arithmetic. Day one of the
e-cigarette quit was 13 December 2010. One week from day one is 20 December.
On 10 January 2011 he posts *"1 week smoke free!"*, which dates a fresh day
one to roughly **3 January**. The December attempt broke somewhere in between
and **the relapse appears nowhere in the archive** — it is visible only
because a counter restarted. He is not concealing it; he is simply not posting
failures, and an eighteen-year public feed preserves it anyway, in the
arithmetic.

That is also the methodological point this whole page runs on. Self-report
about consumption is the least reliable testimony a person gives, and the
places it fails are not lies — they are omissions that leave a shape. The
counter restart is a shape. The measured night below is an attempt to build an
instrument that does not need one.

**Onset dating, stated at its real strength.** Nicotine onset is 2007, the
same year the History section of [[wiki/health/chemical-architecture]] dates
the first opiate. Two rows of the stack begin within twelve months of each
other, in one set of life conditions — which is a claim about when the
architecture starts, not a causal claim. The 2007 show is probably datable
further: the concert table holds two 2007 Fall Out Boy dates and only one is
*promotional* — 6 February 2007, Times Square, the Infinity On High release-day
free show, at eighteen. Stated as likeliest, not settled; he never names the
venue.

**Still unmeasured.** No dose, no frequency, no rate. The ledger holds no
nicotine unit and cannot be back-filled from a tweet. The row is dated at both
ends, continuous in between, and unmeasured throughout. The longest untested
gap is **2011–2015**, where the archive is a fetch artefact and absence proves
nothing.

**Sixteen unbroken years, zero stated relapses to full opiate use, no
recovery-program participation anywhere in the record.** The chassis was never
tapered, and the 2013 line says why in the subject's own voice: *"still too
terrified to get off."* That is not a description of a quit in progress. It is
a description of a register held open deliberately.

**The day-zero date is the wiki's best worked example of a claim outliving its
own reasoning** ([[wiki/health/chemical-architecture]]). 17 February 2010 was
*computed*, not recalled — a model reading email logs told Dan a train left on
18 February, and he replied *"if the train left on feb 18 that means that the
first day i began my successful suboxone streak was feb 17, 2010."* The train
anchor is wrong: the archive has him already in New York on the 17th and *"On
the train back to PA"* on the 18th. The date survives on the contemporaneous
15:07 tweet instead — first-party, same-day, and with the obvious alternative
referent (the Brooklyn move, decided and celebrated on 26 January) excluded.
It is a dated alignment, not a proof: he never names the decision.

**The prescriber is a logistics relationship, not a clinical one.** Every
recorded exchange about the doctor concerns moving a script between
pharmacies. None concerns a dose, a taper, or a review. That is the specific
reason a sixteen-year prescribed regimen has produced exactly one dosage
figure in the corpus, and it is also the register rule showing up in the
medical record: there is a mechanism for *continuing* and none at all for
*ending* it.

**The dose arc, complete:**

| Period | Daily quantity | Driver |
|---|---|---|
| ~2006–2016 (baseline) | ~1 g/day | pre-inheritance constraint |
| 2017–2020 (peak) | **3.5–7 g/day** | Fran's inheritance removed the capital constraint |
| 2020–present (contraction) | **~0.5–1 g/day** | inheritance exhausted |

The arc is the rule's cleanest instance and the reason the page is phrased
around *registers* rather than *quantities* — **the quantity moved by a factor
of seven and back, driven entirely by money, and the register never once
touched zero.** The source retrospective's own reading is blunt: the
escalation shows the desired state is maximum intensity limited only by supply
and money, not by any internal ceiling — and the later reduction is a resource
adjustment, not a step toward moderation ([[wiki/health/cocaine]]).

The 2007–08 stop is the only thing in the register's history that looks like a
closure, and it is not one: it is a **suspension** produced by a competing
register taking priority, and it ends with a resumption five years later that
is announced as an upgrade. The move is the same one the relational record
makes — see [[wiki/mind/synthesis/dormancy-not-exit]].

One countervailing figure belongs here because the page would otherwise read
as a story about capability: the 2017–2020 window of maximum unconstrained
supply is also the window of **least** reported wellbeing. First-person
happiness-claim rates run 0.87 / 0.34 / 0.41 per 1,000 messages across those
years against 7.86 in late 2015 ([[wiki/health/cocaine]], citing
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/estate-money-spine]]). Maximum intensity and minimum
reported happiness are the same period.

## Register 4 — Cannabis: the untested register

Daily, per the stack table ([[wiki/health/chemical-architecture]]). **No
quit-attempt history exists anywhere in the corpus**, so the rule makes no
claim here. That is not a weakness; it is the clean test, and it is the one
register where either outcome would be informative.

What the record does hold is a production history rather than a cessation
history. Fifteen EXIF-dated photographs, **2015-08-18 through 2015-11-01**,
document an indoor grow at [[wiki/places/155-virginia-ave|155 Virginia Ave]]:
vegetative growth in terracotta pots under CFL lighting, a clamp lamp and a
desk-lamp rig, early flower with white pistils on 9 October, developed colas
with ambering pistils on 28 October, and a trichome macro on 1 November.
Thirteen of fifteen frames carry GPS clustering at the Virginia Avenue fix.
Dan's testimony: zero growing experience, grown in a closet with clamp lights
and incandescent bulbs; the strain was his favourite and he named it
**"Dream Queen"** — his label, not a verified strain ID. The series ends four
weeks before the switch, and the harvest was never photographed because per
his 2026-09-12 testimony [[wiki/people/casey-bondarenka|Casey]] took it
uncured and smoked it in one night during the Alexis-to-Annie handoff
([[wiki/places/155-virginia-ave]]).

The register-relevant reading is narrow and worth stating narrowly: the one
chemical register with no quit in its history is also the only one with a
documented attempt to **internalise supply**. That is the opposite of closing
a register. It is not evidence for the rule — the rule makes no prediction
here — but it is the kind of move a person makes about a register they do not
expect to end.

## Register 5 — Alcohol: the single closure, and the control that bounds the rule

Zero. Thirteen to fifteen years. Active aversion. *"Categorical exclusion, no
exceptions in the record"* ([[wiki/health/chemical-architecture]]).

There is no announced quit, no counter, no delivery-system swap, no relapse
arithmetic and no taper — because there was never a process. The closure is a
verdict.

And the verdict's coding is documented: **four functional alcoholics across
three generations** — mother, grandmother, great-grandmother, father — and
alcohol is specifically the substance present at the household's defining
betrayals, the father's rehab admission and the mother's affair with their
dealer, in November 2005. Opiates and cocaine carry no equivalent charge and
were adopted freely. The reading the source pages converge on: **abstinence as
anti-identification with the family's mechanism of betrayal, rather than with
substance use itself.** Filed as his framing, medium confidence, stated as
such.

**This is the control, and it bounds the rule in the rule's favour.** The one
closed register did not close the way a health decision closes a register. It
closed the way a verdict closes a question. Closure exists in the repertoire
only as a verdict, never as a taper — which is the rule's actual shape, and
why "he can quit things" is not a counterexample to it.

One dated artefact makes the vocabulary visible with the substance detached:
on **2018-06-20**, on a dealer thread, *"I will never again date a girl that
drinks"* ([[wiki/mind/synthesis/severance-language-atlas]]). No counterparty,
no channel, no severance — the categorical-exclusion grammar with the dyad
removed. The words survive without the performance, which is what proves they
are vocabulary.

## The one documented attempt at a mutual taper, and what happened to it

There is exactly one exception to a strict "no detox attempt" reading, and it
belongs on this page because it is the only instance of the third move.

On **2015-12-08** — ten days after the corpus's first *"YOU ARE MY
EVERYTHING"* — Dan and [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] discuss quitting an
unspecified substance together, cold turkey: *"if you are okay going cold
turkey, I will do it with you."* The framing is financial and cautionary —
*"I don't want to spend every penny I have on it"*, and a wish to avoid *"the
road you and lex did"* ([[wiki/health/chemical-architecture]]).

**Nothing in the corpus indicates the attempt held.** The relationship's later
record shows the same substance patterns continuing for a further decade, and
the exchange reads as an early pact rather than a sustained effort.

Three things about it are worth keeping. It is **mutual** rather than
individual — the only cessation move in the record that is negotiated with
another person. It is **cold turkey** rather than a taper, which means even
the one attempt did not use the missing move. And its stated reason is
**money**, not health — the same driver that moves the cocaine dose arc eleven
years later. The register rule is not challenged by this datum; it is dated by
it. The one time a closure was attempted, it was attempted in the two-move
vocabulary the rule describes.

## The test layer — the one measured night, complete

Every quantity above this line is recollection. On the night of 2026-08-30 the
intake ledger recorded a cocaine unit end to end. It is the only measured
datum this page has, and it is printed complete.

**Unit `intake_unit_01M1AJ47K2HKZ8TZZ75CPNGFJ7`** — 0.75 g, received 17:04 on
30 August, closed 02:35 on 31 August, disposition *consumed*, reconciled
balanced with nothing unaccounted for ([[wiki/health/cocaine]]).

| | |
|---|---|
| Duration, received to close | 9h 31m |
| Intake events | 6 — 4 flagged `measured`, 2 `estimated`, 0 unquantified |
| Quantified intake | 0.75 g (0.4 g measured, 0.35 g estimated) |
| Median dose | 0.1 g |
| Mean dose | 0.125 g |
| Largest dose | 0.25 g |
| Dose variability | 0.49 CV |
| Median interval | 1h 07m |
| Mean interval | 1h 18m |
| Peak window | 30 Aug, 8pm–midnight — 5 of 6 events inside five hours |
| Coverage | 6 of 6 events carry a quantity |

**The shape of the night, as the source page records it.** Five doses of 0.1 g
between **20:05** and **00:01**; a two-and-a-half-hour gap; then a single
**0.25 g at 02:35** that finished the unit and closed it thirteen seconds
later. The first dose is smaller than the later mean; the largest dose is the
last one.

**The same night, cannabis — three separate single-serving units**, each
opened and closed in one action:

| Time | Quantity | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 22:06 | 0.05 g | one-hitter preset |
| 00:37 | 0.05 g | one-hitter preset |
| 02:36 | 0.05 g | opened **24 seconds after the cocaine unit closed** |

**Corrections on the log, both visible in it:** unit 2's substance was typed
`cannibis`; unit 4 was opened and logged in milligrams where cannabis defaults
to grams. Neither touched the cocaine unit. The per-unit archives under
`raw/health/intake/` were backfilled rather than written at close and say so on
their face — the portal does not call `close`, so no capture was filed that
night ([[wiki/health/cocaine]]).

**What the night settles, which is less than it looks.** The dose arc puts
2020–present at ~0.5–1 g/day on self-report. This night lands inside that band.
The first measurement does not contradict the recollection it was set against.
That is worth something and it is the whole of it.

**What it does not settle, stated plainly because the figures invite the error:**

- **n = 1.** One unit, one night. It establishes nothing about a daily or
  weekly rate, or whether the night was typical.
- **The "1.89 g / day" figure must not be cited.** `bin/intake report` prints
  it per unit; it is 0.75 g extrapolated across 24 hours from a unit that
  existed for 9h 31m — an arithmetic restatement of the unit's lifespan, not
  an observation about any day. The ledger page withholds it on purpose.
- **The `measured` flags probably overstate precision.** Four of six events
  carry exactly 0.1 g, the value of the portal's `ONE LINE` preset, which
  `intake/substances.json` defines as *estimated, low confidence* — "by eye,
  the widest-spread estimate here". They reached the log flagged `measured`
  anyway. Coverage counts how many events carry a number; it does not certify
  where the number came from.
- **Silence is not abstinence.** An unlogged night is indistinguishable from a
  night with nothing in it. The ledger's silence before 2026-08-30 is the
  absence of an instrument.

**Two rows of the stack are corroborated by this night; three are not.** The
cocaine and cannabis "Daily" entries now have one dated first-party
observation behind them. **Suboxone and nicotine have no ledger entries at all
and remain description** ([[wiki/health/chemical-architecture]]) — which means
the two registers with the longest continuity claims are the two with no
measurement whatsoever.

## The instrument's own status in this repository

This is the page's sharpest current limit and it is not in any source page,
because each source page describes the ledger as it was rather than as it is
held here.

[[wiki/health/intake-ledger]] declares itself generated by `bin/intake page`
from `intake/events.jsonl`, and states on its face that "the append-only log
is the record; this page, `intake/units.json` and `intake/SUMMARY.md` are all
derived from it."

**In this repository, `intake/` does not exist. Neither does `raw/health/`.**
The ledger page as reconstructed reports **Units opened 0 (0 closed), Intake
events 0, Carrying a quantity 0, Corrections on the log 0** — a rendered
instrument with no data behind it, whose own "First unit received" row reads
2026-08-31 against zero units.

So the measured night above survives here **only as transcription on
[[wiki/health/cocaine]]**. The unit id, the six events, the timings and the
reconciliation are the cocaine page's report of a record this repository does
not hold. That is a weaker evidentiary state than either page implies, and it
is the same failure mode this wiki documents elsewhere: a derived page
outliving its source and continuing to read as measurement.

**It does not change the night's findings** — the cocaine page's transcription
is detailed, internally consistent, self-critical about its own precision, and
dated. It does change what can be said about the instrument going forward:
**the test layer is currently a description of a test layer.** The next
announced quit cannot be checked against a ledger that is not in the tree.

## The null case: what the rule cannot classify, and what that costs

The rule as stated at the top of this page has a positive half — *exactly two
moves* — and a negative half — *never taper-and-close.* They have different
fates, and [[wiki/health/maintenance-vs-verdict]] is the page that worked out
which.

**The object neither move covers is the Suboxone run.** Nicotine resolves by
substitution. Alcohol resolves by verdict. Cocaine resolves by suspension,
which is already a strain on the positive half. Cannabis has no quit history.
And Suboxone does *nothing* — sixteen years of continuation with no announced
quit, no swap presented as a quit, no verdict, and no taper. It is a null
case, and a rule with only two moves cannot say what a null case is.

Three readings are live and the corpus cannot adjudicate between them
([[wiki/health/maintenance-vs-verdict]]):

1. **Maintenance is a third move.** Continuation is its own act rather than
   the absence of one — a register held open by daily decision.
2. **The null case is a frozen compound move.** Maintenance *is* a
   substitution that stopped rotating: the substance was already the
   substitute, and the hardware rotation continued underneath it.
3. **The rule's domain is registers-in-motion**, and a register under
   maintenance is simply outside it.

Reading 2 has documentary support this page should carry, because it is a
substitution chain hiding inside the register the rule calls inert. In the
2013-07-10 exchange: *"i was on the strips when i had a doctor in nyc"* — past
tense, strips already behind him — and by 2020–21 he is offering *"six
suboxone pills"* and asking *"are the suboxone or subutex?"*. **The Suboxone
row has its own five-delivery-system rotation, of the same shape as nicotine's
— except the thing being substituted was already the substitute.**

**What this page concedes, and what it does not.** The positive half is
conceded as unfinished: "exactly two moves" is a claim about the moves the
record *contains*, not a demonstration that no third exists, and the null case
is the object that will settle it. The negative half stands over every reading
and every register: **no taper, anywhere, ever.** Sixteen years of Suboxone
contain no taper. Eighteen years of nicotine contain no taper. The alcohol
zero contains no taper. The cocaine arc moves on money, never on a staircase.
Even the one imagined closure — the December 2015 pact — proposed *cold
turkey* and skipped the middle.

That is also why the falsifier is well chosen. A genuine taper-to-zero on
Suboxone aims at the load-bearing wall rather than the paint, and the register
under maintenance is structurally the likeliest place for a taper to ever
appear, because maintenance is the only move whose continuation is a
daily decision-shaped object.

### The one thing in the corpus that looks like a taper

It has to be named, because it is the nearest counterexample the record holds
and this page would be dishonest to leave it in a downstream footnote.

[[wiki/health/suboxone-dose-curve]] is the evidence ledger for the Suboxone
row, and it reads the 2013 sentence grammatically: **"down to 2mg/day"
presupposes a prior higher dose.** It is a taper-completed or
taper-in-progress claim, not a steady-state-from-induction claim. What the
prior dose was, how many steps, when it started, whether a clinician directed
it — none of that is in the record. The sentence implies a history the corpus
does not supply.

Two further bearings from the same ledger sharpen the picture and neither is
a dose:

- **2011-08-04**, outbound: *"I was supposed to go to my Suboxone appointment
  today… I got one of those At-Home test kits just to make sure I'd pass the
  test that they give me there, and apparently I did just a liiittttle bit too
  much"* — followed by a request for clean urine. This establishes **monitored
  treatment in 2011**: scheduled appointments at a place, with a drug screen.
  A dose was being *set* by somebody. It did not survive into the message.
- **The induction dose is absent entirely.** The corpus names the start date
  of the run and never the start value. One dosage figure across roughly
  **6,050 days** of dosing is one observation per ~6,050 executions — a
  sampling density that supports no interpolation and no slope.

**How this page resolves it.** The implied taper is real as an implication and
is **not a closure**: whatever descent produced "down to 2mg," it terminated
at a maintenance floor the subject describes in the same breath as one he is
*"still too terrified to get off."* A taper that ends at a held floor is the
register staying open with a smaller number in it — which is the cocaine dose
arc's move in a different register, and the cocaine arc moved by a factor of
seven without ever touching zero.

So the rule's negative half is more precisely stated as: **no register in this
corpus has ever been tapered to zero.** Within-register descent exists, is
implied once, and is undocumented in every particular. That is a narrower
claim than "not one taper in the file" and it is the one the evidence
supports. The earlier phrasing is superseded here rather than left standing.

## The profile layer

[[wiki/mind/profile/intp]] documents **Si-tertiary as a non-pruning archive** —
"suspend, not delete" — and names it the function-level substrate of the
wiki's system-wide no-delete rule. That is this page's rule in cognitive
vocabulary: a stack with no pruning faculty has no mechanism for *closing* a
category, only for adding to it or ruling it out at the boundary. Substitution
adds; verdict rules out at the boundary; taper-and-close would require
pruning, and the pruning function is the one that is not there.

The same profile supplies the reason the closure that *does* exist is a
verdict. [[wiki/mind/profile/intp]] records the Ti-dominant / Fe-inferior
split at **96% latent against 10% valuing**, and the corpus-wide consequence:
verdict questions collapse to two states, because the dominant function tests
only for binary closure while the function that would grade a verdict
relationally is nearly absent. Alcohol is that mechanism applied to a
substance. It is not a graded reduction in drinking; it is a category ruled
out, once, permanently, and the coding is relational — the family's mechanism
of betrayal — because binary is the only resolution available.

[[wiki/mind/profile/deviance-mapping]] scores this domain **99/100 against the
population baseline** — the single most statistically extreme fact in the
profile — and scores it that way *because of the deliberateness*: "not casual
use; self-administered neurochemical engineering." That is the audit agreeing
with the subject's own framing, which is worth flagging rather than citing
approvingly: an audit that scores a self-model highly on the self-model's own
terms has not independently tested it.

## The relational twin

The rule's structural parallel is [[wiki/mind/synthesis/dormancy-not-exit]]
run on chemistry: ties suspend and never disappear; chemicals substitute and
never close. The same refusal of irreversibility, in a different register.

That parallel is unusually well measured on the relational side, which is why
it is worth stating precisely rather than gesturing at.

Searching Dan-sent severance language across the **95,067-row merged Annie
corpus (2015-11-28 → 2026-05-28)** returns **258 messages, collapsing to 129
distinct episodes** under a 24-hour rule ([[wiki/mind/synthesis/block-unblock-loop]],
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/severance-declarations]]):

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Declaration episodes | 129 |
| Episodes followed by a further message | 128 (the 129th is the last row in the file) |
| Of those, resumed | **128 of 128 — 100%** |
| Median gap, declaration to next message | **0.01 hours — thirty-six seconds** |
| Resumed inside one hour | 89.1% |
| Resumed inside one day | 99.2% |
| Longest gap in eleven years | **46 hours** |

The earlier 87% relapse figure is withdrawn; it counted as non-relapses
declarations the primary record shows resuming within minutes. A second,
stricter instrument — [[wiki/mind/synthesis/severance-language-atlas]], built
2026-09-13 on a different vocabulary — collapses the Annie handles to **104
outbound episodes** and finds the same invariant: every outbound episode on
every handle resumes. *"The instrument finds no outbound declaration by Dan,
on any handle, that ends contact."* Two builds, two counts, one resumption
rate.

**Against that base rate, the exceptions are measurable rather than
rhetorical.** The 1 June 2026 closure produced a **fifty-two-day silence —
twenty-seven times the eleven-year maximum** — and it still broke. The 19
August 2026 severance passed the 46-hour ceiling on 2026-08-21 and is the
first in the record to name and pre-close its own most likely reopening
channel. The dormancy page states the test and the date: if contact resumes,
it is the strongest dormancy datum in the wiki; if it has not by roughly
**2027-02-19**, it is the first attested exit.

**Confidence: medium, and the ceiling is structural.** The chemical registers
and the relational ones are measured on entirely different instruments — a
Twitter archive and a self-report arc on one side, a 95,067-row message corpus
on the other. The parallel is asserted as a shape, and a shape shared between
a well-measured domain and a poorly-measured one is exactly where a borrowed
confidence would come from. It is stated here as a structural precedent, not
as a corpus claim.

## Prediction, and what would break the rule

**The prediction.** The next announced "quit" will resolve to a substitution —
new delivery system, same register — within six months of the announcement,
and the announcement will use **completion language first**. *"Another problem
solved"* (2008-10-16) is the template; the behaviour follows or it does not.

**Falsifiers of the strong form**, in ascending order of how badly each would
damage the rule:

1. **A nicotine-free interval exceeding six months with no substitute**,
   visible in the archive or the message corpus as absence rather than
   re-housing. The longest untested window is 2011–2015, where absence proves
   nothing because the archive is a fetch artefact.
2. **A cannabis quit in either direction.** The register has no quit-attempt
   history, so any dated attempt is a clean test with no prior.
3. **A genuine taper-to-zero on Suboxone**, with a dated declining-dose
   sequence ending at zero. This would falsify the rule outright: it is
   precisely the missing third move, on the register with the strongest
   continuity claim, and the 2013 line — *"still too terrified to get off"* —
   is what it would have to overturn.

**What would not falsify it:** another announced quit that resolves to a
substitution; a dose reduction driven by money, which the cocaine arc already
shows twice; or a verdict-style exclusion of a new substance, which is the
rule's second move rather than an exception to it.

- **The alcohol coding is his framing.** The betrayal-coding reading of the
