# The Creative License Whistleblower Exit (February–June 2012)

Dan's first real NYC job — Special Projects Coordinator at a small West
Village celebrity-talent and licensed-music brokering company — ended in a
six-week payroll fight, a filed IRS and NYS Department of Labor complaint,
an unprompted May 2012 whistleblower disclosure about altered intern
contracts, and a retaliatory June 2012 attempt to erase his employment from
the record via notarized false statements to LinkedIn. He was 23. The
whole sequence is the earliest fully documented instance of a shape that
recurs across fourteen years of his working life: capability entering an
institution through the vertical axis, exiting through a fight about the
record — and choosing documentation over capitulation every time. The
job's full context lives at [[wiki/work/creative-license|Creative License
(NYC, 2011–2012)]]; this page is about the ending, and what the ending
reveals.

## The setting: one audio-engineering stretch in the first NYC era

Dan moved to New York in April 2010, at the front edge of his post–Full
Sail push into the recording-arts career the degree pointed at, and the
Creative License job sits inside the only stretch of his life where
audio-engineering training was the actual job. It was the second of two
consecutive studio-adjacent entries on his own résumé: first an
internship/engineer role at Ishlab Studios in DUMBO under Jamin Gilbert,
then, from about April 2011, the West Village job under company president
Kevin McKiernan at 71 8th Avenue. He worked there while living at 424
Bedford Avenue in Williamsburg — the job ran the entire Bedford
occupancy — and into the next apartment year, covering the whole span of
the [[wiki/timeline/periods/nyc-first-era-2010-2013|first NYC era]]'s
career-track phase. That context matters because the era never repeats: no
later job in his life is a music-industry job. Ishlab and Creative License
are the only two entries on that particular ledger, and the second one
ended with him filing federal and state complaints against his own
employer.

What the job actually was, per his own résumé: assisting the company
president with correspondence, travel logistics, calendar management and
personal projects; graphic design elements for the website and business
development; and "oversight of office and employee expenses" — the last
item being the one that matters, because it is the job description that
put him in a position to notice the thing he later quit over. His
contemporaneous title was **Special Projects Coordinator** — the résumé's
"Executive Assistant / Office Manager" is a later, self-flattering
rewrite, and the record settles it two ways: his own email signature during
the job used the Coordinator title, and LinkedIn's own 2012 notice to him
used it too. Client accounts on the record are Walmart (contact Simona
Rabsatt) and Molson Coors (Lori Estabrooks). Day-to-day the recovered
emails show a normal office job — client coordination, covering for
migraines and doctor's appointments, helping prep a colleague for speaking
appearances — with a May 2011 non-disclosure/non-compete agreement sent by
the company's Director of Business Affairs, Katherine Palakovich, which Dan
forwarded to his father the same day with "Thought you might want to take
a look at this." Rick was consulted on the employment paperwork from the
first month of the job, not only at the acrimonious end.

## How he tells it: the airfare citation

The stated reason for the exit — the reason he cited at the time, in his
own later words — was "some kind of small fraud with airplane tickets."
That sentence comes from Dan's own testimony inside the Gemini-58 session
(lines 580–625), which makes it primary testimony under this wiki's
source-tier discipline, and it deserves to be quoted at honest length
because the self-doubt in it is as load-bearing as the accusation:

> "the thing i cited when i quit had to do with some kind of small fraud
> with airplane tickets that i blew it up into a much much bigger thing
> but maybe i was just desensitized."

The same passage explains the mechanism of his own decision: a personality
complaint would have been dismissed as him being "sensitive," so he used
the financial anomaly as the "'legal ground' to detach" — a way to make
the departure "an objective failure" on the company's part rather than his
own. Nothing about the specific mechanism of the alleged fraud is
independently verified. What is confirmed is that he cited an airfare
issue, in his own words, as his stated reason for quitting. The elaborated
version — mismarked travel-expense billing to Walmart/Molson Coors — is
the Gemini model's speculative fleshing-out of one prompt sentence and
should not be treated as verified.

This is the wiki's own documented three-pass correction history on the
point, and it's worth holding: pass one flagged the AI dossiers'
"airfare-billing discrepancies and altered intern contracts" claim as
having zero primary corroboration. Pass two, after a live Gmail search
recovered ~35 real threads documenting a genuine conflict, briefly and
wrongly concluded the airfare claim was outright invented. Pass three found
Dan's own primary testimony in a source that had been sitting in `raw/`
the whole time. The final settlement: the airfare citation is real; the
mechanism is not verified; the altered-intern-contracts substance turned
out to be independently documented after all, through a different channel
(the May 2012 disclosure, below), which means the dossiers' garbled line
was wrong in its packaging and partly right in its substance.

## The exit itself: four acts

**Act zero — the stated reason (February 2012).** The airfare citation, as
above: a billing irregularity in travel expenses he was positioned to
notice through his oversight-of-expenses role, deployed as legal ground
for detachment rather than as a pure personality break. Two and a half
years of documented self-doubt trail behind it — whether he blew a small
thing up or was simply desensitized to normal industry practice. Hold both
readings; he does.

**Act one — the payroll dispute (February 15 – April 9, 2012).** Dan's
last day was on or before February 15, 2012 — the date of his first email
flagging that his final paycheck carried a **$104 charge for a MetroCard
he says he never requested or received**, and arrived unsigned. Over the
next six weeks he and Marty Jackson in Accounting went back and forth over
the missing signature, the disputed charge, and a W-2 that didn't arrive
on schedule; Marty's position was that Dan needed to have opted out of the
automatic MetroCard program "in writing before you quit," which Dan
disputed ever being told. On February 27, after Dan pushed back on Marty's
math — Marty claimed the returned check "took 10 weeks to come back,"
which Dan corrected, since he'd only been gone from the company a month —
Marty wrote the line that anchors the whole record:

> "It's a trust issue. We don't trust you. Please do not forget the keys
> or any other CL property you have."

Dan escalated by filing complaints with the **IRS and the NYS Department
of Labor**, and CC'd his father throughout. Rick, in this exchange,
switched registers entirely from brief/logistical to active tactical
coach: "You sound so desperate. That is what kevin wants. We'll talk about
a strategy for this." It is the corpus's clearest instance of Rick
operating as strategist rather than distant authority figure, and the exit
is where it happens. By April 9 Dan closed the dispute directly with
Marty — conceding the $104 to end it, and explicitly separating the
accountant from the president: "I didn't mean to give the impression that
my hostility was directed at you personally... I don't believe that your
intention was to create a volatile or unpleasant situation." The
distinction between the functionary and the principal is Dan's own, made in
real time, not an interpretation added later.

**Act two — the catharsis (April 26, 2012).** Two months after leaving,
Dan sent Rachel Rauch, per the Gemini-58 session, a one-line email —
"Enjoy!!!" — with an attachment titled `BEST OF KTM VOICEMAILS.mp3`, a
compiled recording of McKiernan's "erratic, high-volatility communication
style." Not independently verified against the Gmail record — the message
wasn't among the threads recovered by keyword search — but consistent with
everything else documented: the relationship's end produced an archive,
not just a grievance.

**Act three — the whistleblower disclosure (May 2012).** Unprompted,
months after the payroll dispute closed, Dan wrote to a former Creative
License contact, Renae Holland, to explain something that had been
bothering him: in December 2011, while he was still employed, Kevin had
used Dan's own email/LinkedIn account to send recommendation-removal
requests to former employees — Renae among them. In Dan's own words:

> "I (my account, rather) was acting as a channel for Kevin to act out
> his most immature fantasies and I'm glad I can say that I didn't even
> help write those emails... The more alarming aspect of this was the
> employment contracts that Kevin had me send to the two former interns.
> These documents had been altered before they were sent and a clause was
> added that prohibited recommendations without CEO approval... I think
> that the two interns should be aware of this for their own legal
> protection and because it's just disgustingly wrong."

Renae's reply corroborates independently, from her own tenure at the
company: "in my time there I was Kevin falsify more documents that I can
count — I know what he is capable of." This is the real substance behind
the AI dossiers' garbled "altered intern contracts" line — a specific,
named mechanism (a CEO-approval gag clause quietly inserted into departing
interns' paperwork), not a vague allegation. Note the sequencing: the
disclosure was unprompted, arrived months after his own fight was
over, and was aimed at protecting two interns (Chris Marraffino and
Michael DiTullio, named in the Gemini-58 session) who weren't him. That
is the part of the record that reads least like grievance and most like
the thing the label claims.

**Act four — retaliation (June 2012).** A month after the disclosure,
Kevin filed a formal LinkedIn intellectual-property complaint against
Dan's profile, backed by **two notarized documents asserting Dan had never
been a Creative License employee and had fabricated his job title** —
notarized by Katherine Palakovich, using her own notary commission on
behalf of her employer against a former coworker. Dan named it precisely
in his reply to her: "the notarization of documents which the notary
public believes to be false, deceptive, or fraudulent is forbidden by the
Notary Public Code of Professional Responsibility." LinkedIn threatened to
terminate his account within three business days. Dan filed a
counter-notice, offering "paystubs, tax documentation, or any other
official documentation," and named the retaliation motive directly: "Mr.
McKiernan has and continues to attempt to smear my name because of my
actions as a whistleblower while under his employ." No resolution email
was located; whether LinkedIn sided with Dan is unconfirmed, though his
profile clearly survived.

## The exit as moral act: his framing, and what it reveals

Dan's own telling of this story, years later, is not a hero narrative.
The headline fact he volunteers about his own conduct is the self-doubt:
that he "blew it up into a much much bigger thing," that he may have been
"desensitized," that he chose the financial anomaly as legal ground
because a personality complaint would have read as sensitivity. That is a
remarkably forensic self-reading — the analyst turned inward, auditing his
own motives the way he audits everything else — and it is exactly the
reading the wiki's human-story discipline requires here: what happened is
documented; why he did it is his own testimony, hedged by him at the
source, and should stay hedged in the retelling.

What the episode reveals, read against the rest of his life, is the
conjunction rule at work in his earliest professional setting. His stated
requirement for respect runs two ways — build weird things AND hold large
cultural literacy; database plus proof of life, miss either and you're
out — and the Creative License exit is the rule applied as a moral
act rather than as an aesthetic judgment. The company failed the
building test not on taste but on conduct: a payroll dispute answered with
"we don't trust you," intern contracts altered before sending, a president
routing recommendation-removal requests through a junior employee's own
account. Dan's response was not to argue the point but to document it —
IRS, Department of Labor, a written disclosure to a former contact, a
counter-notice naming the retaliation motive — and then, having made the
record, to concede the $104 and walk away. The forensics appendix belongs
in the kb nodes; the human shape is this: he was 23, it was the only
music-industry job he would ever hold, and he blew it up on principle
while explicitly doubting his own proportionality for years afterward.

There is also, in the record, a quieter register that complicates the
clean "principled whistleblower" reading and should not be sanded off. By
February 2012 he was already job-hunting in the ordinary sense — the
Gemini-58 session describes Craigslist applications to EA/Office Manager
roles across Manhattan and outreach to firms like the Bond Street Group,
unconfirmed against email in that pass. The departure was probably also an
ordinary job change in progress, not purely a dramatic break. And the
postscript refuses to resolve into enmity: Dan kept McKiernan's contact
information for close to a decade (Facebook address-book snapshots from
2016 through 2021 still carry `kevin@creativelicense.com`), and in
December 2014 Katherine Palakovich — by then leaving the company herself
— bcc'd him on a mass "Ex-Creative License Happy Hour" invitation to "all
my favorite ex (and current) CL employees," two and a half years after a
conflict serious enough to involve the IRS, the Department of Labor, and a
notarized false statement. Neither fact cancels the other. The wiki holds
them together rather than letting the dramatic ending overwrite the more
ordinary, ongoing relationship around it.

## The arc: the job he blew up and the job he held

The employment-arc reading is the one the corpus itself invites. Creative
License was the job Dan blew up — ten months, ending in a payroll fight
and a whistleblower disclosure. [[wiki/work/au-zaatar-41-months|Au
Za'atar]] was the job he held — 41 months, March 2021 to August 2024, the
longest employment of his life, ending without a blowup and with its own
documented texture. The contrast is structural: the 2011–12 exit was a
collision with vertical authority (a president who used a junior's accounts
as channels, an accountant who put distrust in writing), met with
escalation and documentation; the 2021–24 tenure was lateral endurance
inside a crew he stayed loyal to. One job ended because the institution
failed his test; the other lasted because it didn't need to pass one.

And fourteen years later the corpus names the recurrence explicitly: the
2026 BFS Foods dispute — a trusted vertical figure's undocumented
financial claim, met with exit and documentation rather than
confrontation — is the same shape first seen at Creative License. The
corpus's own dossiers call it out as the first and most recent instances
of one pattern. The difference is only scale: $104 and a MetroCard in
2012, a disputed $50 drawer shortage and a firing in 2026, both answered
the same way — make the record, concede the point if needed, leave.

## Evidentiary appendix: limits, per claim

This page draws on exactly four source classes, and the limits are the
price of honesty about which class each claim belongs to.

**Class A — dated contemporaneous email (Gmail capture, ~35 threads,
2011–2012).** The payroll dispute timeline (Feb 15 first email; Feb 27
"trust issue" line; April 9 closure), the non-compete forwarding to Rick
(May 2011), the December 2014 happy-hour invitation, and the December
2011/January 2015 date anchors for the Katherine/Renae material. Strongest
tier. Limits: the capture is a keyword search ("mckiernan" /
"creative license"), not a full mailbox — threads that never named the
company or the president would not have surfaced, and the `BEST OF KTM
VOICEMAILS.mp3` email was not among them.

**Class B — Dan's own retrospective testimony (Gemini-58 session,
raw/self/dox-scan/Gemini-_58.txt, lines 580–625).** The airfare citation
("small fraud with airplane tickets"), the "blew it up" self-doubt, the
legal-ground framing, the survivors' network names (Rachel Rauch, Sarah
Bromberg, Charley Siegel), the interns' names (Chris Marraffino, Michael
DiTullio), the voicemail compilation, the February 2012 Craigslist
job-hunting. Primary testimony per this wiki's tier discipline — Dan's own
words — but testimony, not record: hedged by him at the source on the
airfare proportionality question, and unconfirmed against email for the
survivors'-network and job-hunting material. The session also contains the
Gemini model's own speculative elaboration (mismarked Walmart/Molson Coors
billing), which is NOT Dan's testimony and must never be cited as such.

**Class C — résumé and address-book records (Resume.txt; Facebook
address-book snapshots 2016–2021).** The duties list, the employment
dates (April 2011 – February 2012), the continued presence of
`kevin@creativelicense.com` in the address book for a decade. Limits: the
résumé's title ("Executive Assistant / Office Manager") is
self-authored and inconsistent with the contemporaneous "Special Projects
Coordinator" — treat résumé fields as self-presentation, not record.

**Class D — AI dossiers and reconstructions.** The bootloader/manual/
cognitive-profile documents and the Menore-corpus line naming "Creative
License style fraud" as a byword for institutional unreliability. Useful
for showing how the episode survived in Dan's vocabulary years later;
carries no independent evidentiary weight on the events themselves.

**Per-claim status summary.** Confirmed by contemporaneous email: the
payroll dispute dates and the $104 MetroCard charge; Marty's "trust issue"
line; the IRS and NYS Department of Labor filings (filed, per Dan's own
emails — agency receipt is not independently confirmed); the LinkedIn
retaliation's existence and Katherine's notarization (per Dan's
counter-notice and his reply to her — her side is unrecorded); the May 2012
whistleblower disclosure to Renae Holland and the altered-intern-contract
mechanism (per Dan's email and Renae's corroborating reply); the December

own testimony: the airfare citation as the stated reason for quitting.
Unverified: the specific mechanism of the alleged travel-expense fraud;
whether LinkedIn ruled for or against Dan; the voicemail compilation email;
what specifically happened to Renae Holland ("his attack against me" —
her phrasing implies she was herself a target of some campaign, substance
unrecovered); whether Katherine's notarization was made knowingly or under
