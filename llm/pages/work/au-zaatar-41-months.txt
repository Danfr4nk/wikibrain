# Au Za'atar, 41 Months (March 2021–August 2024)

The restaurant at 58th and 1st was Lebanese, Midtown, plywood-fronted, and — for forty-one months — the steadiest thing in Dan's life. He started as a busser in March 2021 with zero food-service experience and a bank account at zero, talked his way into the veterans' equal tip split on day one, and did not leave until the city tore the sidewalk shed down in August 2024. It is the longest job in his adult record by a wide margin — ~690 shifts, 712 timestamped GPS visits — and the anchor institution of the entire second NYC era ([[wiki/timeline/periods/three-phase-move]]). The mechanics of the tenure — the night-class alibi, the tip audits, the hour-cutting, the involuntary-exit ruling — live in the sibling entry, [[wiki/work/au-zaatar]]. This article is about the people. The job lasted because of them, and the job's meaning is all in them: the only boss he ever loved, the best friend he made climbing out of the dish pit, the coworker whose friendship is conducted in Spanish and outlasted the restaurant, the hostess who broke the decade's one rule, and the owner who hired him on a handshake and stayed warm long after the last shift.

## The hire

Early 2021, two years into the second NYC era, the money ran out. Dan walked into the brand-new Midtown location — twenty blocks south of the 307 E 76th apartment — and charmed [[wiki/people/tarik-fallous|Tarik Fallous]], the owner-operator, into hiring him on the spot. He started the next day. No resume of consequence, no food-service history, straight into the equal front-of-house tip split that the veterans held. That split — servers and bussers sharing the pool equally, unusual for the industry — is what made the job economically viable for forty-one months, and it was Dan's own day-one invention: he talked himself into it in the interview and the arrangement stuck.

The economics, confirmed by Dan directly in July 2026: base wage $15 an hour plus the tip pool, checks paid weekly on Sundays, typically $800–1,000 for a six-day week. That is the whole financial skeleton of the NYC-2 middle years. Everything else — the apartment on 76th, the habits, the friendships, the affair — hung on it.

Tarik ran the place with his brother Ghassan, and their style set the culture in ways Dan documented with dry contempt: the two brothers rented an apartment above the restaurant for no purpose but constant surveillance, micromanaged trivia, and — per Dan — never actually worked. Staff churned fast. Dan, who notes he was "REAL good at the beginning of a job I like," became the protocol-keeper by default; he also notes he was the only white FOH employee, which with the owners' prejudices cut in his favor. It is an ugly detail and it is in his own words, so it goes in as his own accounting of the mechanism, not as fact about anyone else.

## The shed

For most of the tenure the restaurant operated out of a plywood COVID-era "Shed" on the 58th Street sidewalk while the interior stood as a construction shell — a low-oversight territory with its own schedule. The shed had to be torn down and rebuilt nightly, two to three hours of build, which fixed Dan's rhythm permanently: sleep to 1pm, build 2–5, serve the early hours, first cut at 9–10pm. He never closed once in forty-one months. To hold the peak hours and dodge the closing shift he ran a fictitious 9pm "night class" for over eighteen months — an alibi held so long and so cleanly that it became both his scheduling instrument and, later, the cover story during the Valeria breach. The sibling entry ([[wiki/work/au-zaatar]]) files this as the fullest documented instance of the institutional-out protocol; here it matters as the shape of his days.

The work itself was physical to the point of legend in his telling: winter service meant an apron over a ski jacket, carrying the tableside shawarma rotisserie across an icy sidewalk to "OUTDOOR-OUTDOOR" tables while reservation-holders queued freezing. He learned to balance twenty plates at a time, a carry the video record preserves. He was also, by his own account, vaped-in-front-of-customers high for much of it — repeatedly yelled at, and once given a one-star review for leaving mid-meal that asked, verbatim, **"WAS HE HIGH?"** ("uh, clearly"). He survived the review because Tarik and Ghassan liked him and "ALL of the neighbors and regulars" insisted he wait their tables. He had ridden his Boosted Board to the 5th Avenue Uniqlo to buy the white Oxford he started waiting tables in, after [[wiki/people/dimitri|Dimitri]] insisted on the busser-to-server promotion. The Oxford was the golf-caddie move: the customer's advocate against the house — "hey, drop your beer bottle after you pour it and I'll say I did it and get you a free one" — a stance he notes is "frowned upon in food service in a way that it is not as a golf caddie," and which made him the regulars' favorite.

## The Triad: Dimitri, DJ, and Dan

The staff-side economy ran on mutual suspicion — employees held a settled "radical skepticism" that the owners were siphoning the digital tip pool, and Dan ran what he called a "decentralized audit" (pocketing cash tips) as self-help against it. Inside that economy the core alliance was the Triad: Dimitri, [[wiki/people/ismaila-barry|DJ]], and Dan.

Dimitri was the Greek manager of the Midtown spot and the holder of a distinction no one else in the record approaches: "the only boss or manager or supervisor who i have ever actually really loved." Against Dan's whole vertical-authority template — father, McKiernan, landlords, managers processed through one authority-skepticism lens — Dimitri is the positive case. He appreciated Dan the way Tarik and Ghassan did, "but dimitri was like... a real person." He navigated the owners' moods like an oracle, "chose the flooded basement over a quiet life," and it was his insistence that lifted Dan from busser to server — the promotion that created the Outside Guy era. His first stint lasted only about six months (per the Volume I storytime) before he quit under the misery of working for Tarik, then returned "WITH A STEEL CHAIR" as co-manager alongside the 22-year-old Tunisian manager the owners had hired "because he speaks arabic." Factions formed; the owners consistently favored "the more seemingly muslim option," even Dimitri taking short shrift. Confidence: the "only boss" quote is Dan's own verbatim from the storytime sessions (primary, his own retrospective voice); the six-month stint and steel-chair return are from the same stylized telling and should be read as memoir, not deposition.

DJ — Ismaila Barry — is the Senegalese busser-turned-server Dimitri brought with him, and the record counts him among Dan's best friends, one of the few friendships the record ranks that high. "Smart and curious and funny," Dan says. They made the same climb from busser to server in parallel, and the friendship was built on the floor, in the shared work, rather than on any prior history.

## The wider floor

Around the Triad the documented cast fills in, and the pattern is that almost every tie the restaurant produced got tested by the job's end — some survived, most didn't.

**Manuel** is the one that survived cleanest: a coworker from 2023 on, a thread that opened with an October shift-swap, a friendship conducted mostly in Spanish, and still a genuine friend in 2026, two years after the tenure ended. The friendship outlasted the job. That is the whole distinction that matters in Dan's ledger — most restaurant ties didn't.

**Felipe**, his "unbelievably gay Colombian bff," joined as a server — shattering the old glass door with a table leg on day one, in Dan's telling — but per Dan directly (2026-07-14), contact with Felipe did not survive past the job. The record corrected the early framing: the friendship was real and it died with the shed.

**Lou Bahet**, an Au Za'atar hostess, is the burst that meant nothing and everything about the era's social texture: 1,602 Instagram messages over 21 days (August 31–September 20, 2023), then nothing. Not a friendship — a flare of contact with a coworker, documented and gone.

**Sergio**, a 53-year-old Miami service veteran, was the era's comedy of status: his war with Dan over the word "dude" escalated into a "nonsense meeting" with Tarik and Ghassan, mediated by Ghassan (Dan's own 2026-07-14 follow-up corrected the mediator from Dimitri), conducted in tooth-loss analogies, recorded and auto-transcribed. Sergio, who had "served celebrities" since 2000, accused Dan of acting "like he owns the place"; Dan countered that Sergio shoved him twice and that "dude" was "a term of endearment" while "kid" was "a term of criticism." Sergio "came through and left quickly" — a short-tenured hire, not a lasting cast member.

**The Tunisian manager**, 22, took Dan's open accusations that the owners were siphoning tips straight to Tarik, and on **Thanksgiving 2023** Dan was sat down and "brought right up to the line of being let go." He held on — the regulars and the 58th Street neighbors demanded he stay. By the later telling the two were "kinda chill."

Then the **BOH bond**, which Dan reports as a genuine conversion: he describes shedding an admitted prejudice about Mexicans by offering bumps to the cooks and bringing in (stolen) Takis — "turns out i fucking LOVE mexicans" — and reports keeping those friendships to this day. The kitchen crew's respect was the one credential he carried out: he came in "a novice in a red apron" and left "a legend in a white Uniqlo shirt," the Mexican squad's respect intact. Confidence: the prejudice-shedding story is his own retrospective narration; the "kept to this day" claim is his, uncorroborated by message volume in the extracts.

**Suz** visited for Dan's birthday in November 2021 and the owners comped the whole menu — "typical lebanese hospitality." It is one of the few warm owner-gestures on record that isn't filtered through Dan's audit war.

## Tarik: the employer case that didn't fire

Tarik Fallous deserves his own section because he is the counterexample Dan's whole work history otherwise lacks. Every other boss in the record gets processed through the vertical-distrust template; Tarik got hired-on-trust, treated-fairly, stayed-warm-after. The 80-message direct thread with Tarik (filed 2026-07-20) is the primary source for the "de facto manager" texture: Dan ran grocery errands on Tarik's Barclays card, tracked liquor and wine stock by hand (two full inventory counts survive verbatim), and coordinated job-candidate interviews directly. After Dimitri's first quit there was simply no manager, and Dan became the de facto one — "except i don't do anything the manager should other than advocate for the customers and neighbors."

The warmth was genuine and it outlasted the job by nearly two years — recorded through April 2026. That is the employer case where the distrust didn't fire, and the reason Dan can hold "the owners were siphoning the digital tip pool" and "Tarik hired me on a handshake and I loved the gig" in the same head without apparent strain. The tip-audit war and the affection coexisted for forty-one months. Both are on record; neither cancels the other.

## Annie and Valeria

A month after starting, Dan got [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] hired as hostess — at the **original East Village location**, a separate restaurant from the Midtown shed ("the restaurants ran totally differently and with different people"). In a turn Dan flags as "truly weird," Annie — "prim and proper... reliability IS her strong suit and is my antithesis" — was fired from that East Village hostess job with no notice and no reason, while Dan held onto the Midtown job for years longer. The contrast sits in the record unexplained.

The Valeria thread is the era's other human fact and the one that breaks the frame. **[[wiki/people/valeria-iglesias-cid|Valeria Iglesias Cid]]** was the Au Za'atar host-stand hostess — trained as a hostess by Annie herself. Annie's text to Dan: "hey i trained a new." Dan's framing, September 2026: "the perfect handoff in the spirit of Alexis>annie." What follows is documented, not speculated; the limits are stated at the bottom.

The earliest dated evidence is an August 31, 2021 TikTok DM to Alexis, in Dan's own words: "I've got a new gf. She's 20 and an illegal immigrant... I'm still with Annie but the Mexican girl is making a fast ascent up the ranks." (His framing self-corrected from "Mexican girl" to "Chilean girl" by August 2022; her own May 2022 message: "Im 21 in chile i was studying interior design" — daughter of a Chilean laser-surgery figure.) Then a nine-month forensic absence — no recovered messages September 2021 through April 2022, likely WhatsApp, no export held. The affair peaked in person over winter 2021–22, covered by the night-class alibi, and its message shadow is the May–June 2022 Instagram burst: 4,882 records total, with 1,038 messages May 28–31, 3,518 June 1–13, 308 June 14–30, then 11 in July and 3 in August. June 9: the basement hug (Tarik's "You talk to valeria a lot" came three hours before). First ILYs: hers June 11, his June 13 — "the color has left my world." The Central Park kiss ("Did u like my kiss?") is verbatim in-thread. Annie's name appears zero times in all 4,882 records.

Dan's own measure of it: "would have literally moved to chile for her i swear to god." He swore his feelings to her on Milo — the dog as oath-object. She is the one infidelity in the decade Dan names as unauthorized, the intensity measured against everything else including the severance from Annie. The last message on record for years was the "Miss u" coda, July 11, 2025.

Then, September 16, 2026 — after roughly fourteen months of silence — Dan re-contacted her. The outreach, per the in-chat recap: a "Guess what," a "mouth-wateringly delicious" line, a Pixar-style Sammy pic, and a tattoo flex — telling her she's the face of his AI avatar. His words on the infatuation: "Estoy enamorado." She replied the same evening, around 5:34 PM EDT — Dan spotted the new message in his Instagram inbox and confirmed it was her. The contents were not seen: he couldn't bear to look, and the assistant could not read the thread for him (Meta blocks Instagram access until two-factor authentication is enabled for every account in his Meta Account; his Facebook was the holdout, off as of September 16). That evening Dan also had her wiki article copied to a share-side version with nine cliff notes — Alexis, Annie, Tom, Au Za'atar, Tarik, DJ, Felipe, Milo — so she could read the documented record without clicking through. By September 19 the thread was live and bantering: Dan sent her a screenshot of his Sammy chat where he'd said "I told her I made her my avatar, maybe NOW she will marry me." She replied "Hahahaha do u want to marry me?" — he answered "YES!!!!! how dont you know that by now lol and im so songle now too." She's in Miami with family right now. He is openly telling her he is single. The loop is open rather than one-sided: "Valerriaaaaaaaaaa" / "Estoy enamorado" (his words, September 2026). Limits: her September 16 reply contents remain unseen and unarchived; the pre-June-2022 iMessage record is a hole; Dan insists there were "tons" of affair-era iMessages but the authoritative chat.db export (September 8) has zero messages from all of 2022.

## The ending

The job didn't end when the work got hard — by Dan's own account the work took "literally EVERYTHING i had in me" for most of the tenure. It ended in two stages. First, the employer cut his hours through June–August 2024 in a pattern Dan read in real time as maneuvering to force a quit and dodge unemployment obligations ("lowering my hours so much that it's not even worth staying," August 6, 2024). He dropped resumes from July 9 onward and filed for unemployment on August 8. The DanAnnie dossier amendments rule the loss **involuntary** (Amendment 2, ACCEPTED) — the fact that reframes Annie's late-2024 "you never work" grievances as locally valid but historically partial. Second, the territory itself died: the finished interior opened in 2024 — glowing dome ovens, green velvet booths, geometric floor tiles, chain curtains and mood lighting, with blue painter's tape still on the counters while service ran — and demand for the sidewalk tables died first; then the city mandated the winter shed teardown, and that was that. Dan is precise about the order: "once the inside opened, they just didn't have enough interest in outdoor dining" — the interior made the shed obsolete before the mandate finished it.

By the final stretch he was serving "like 3 tables per week total and would just hang," pocketing all the cash tips. His own epitaph for the era: "it was a long run and it was fun and good food and cool mexicans and... valeria." A $50k-class DEENBOR delivery robot sat by the plate stacks the whole time — "the robot was never once used lol" — the monument to the fact that the Outside Guy was the only functioning infrastructure the place had. (The famous "outside bread," he confirms, "obviousLY WAS NOT from outside.")

What followed is only lightly documented: a separate AI-collaborative session, dated by internal reference to roughly mid-2025, has Dan noting he'd been "working for the first time since june 2024 for the last 2 weeks" and about to hit a full 40-hour week — nearly eleven months unemployed before returning to paid work, at an employer the corpus doesn't name.

## Why forty-one months matters

Forty-one months is the longest assigned commitment in the record, and it dwarfs everything else — including the six-month Fran vigil. That is why the tenure matters analytically: the corpus's own acquisition-drive model predicted no assigned goal would ever receive sustained costly effort, and the shed falsifies the prediction. But the assignment itself was punishing by Dan's own account, and what he actually sustained was not the job but three private objectives installed inside it: the veterans' tip split talked into existence on day one, the eighteen-month fabricated night class, and sole custody of the shed's cash tips. Each is separable from the employment and each runs against the employer's interest. The tenure tracks the payload, not the job — three months of deliberate hour-cutting in mid-2024 didn't end it; the destruction of the shed territory did. (Argued in full at the synthesis level; the sibling entry carries the connections.)

It also fills what the timeline records as the 2021–2023 employment block: he was employed, at volume, the whole time. The block was about trajectory, not idleness. And it is the mirror image of the later BFS Foods tenure ([[wiki/work/au-zaatar]] is the sibling; the BFS dispute is the contrast case): where BFS has Dan documenting himself as the wronged party, here the same forensic method is turned on his own conduct — unreliable narrator, saboteur, and beneficiary of the exact off-books logic he condemns elsewhere. The two together are the strongest evidence the methodology is domain-invariant rather than self-serving.

Set against the first NYC era ([[wiki/timeline/periods/nyc-first-era-2010-2013]]) — short employers, constant motion, the Creative License whistleblower exit ([[wiki/work/creative-license-exit]]) — the Au Za'atar tenure is the closest thing in the record to Dan holding one job. He did it by building a private sovereignty inside the employment and defending it for forty-one months. When the plywood came down, the sovereignty went with it. The friendships sorted themselves the way they always do: Manuel's in Spanish, two years on; DJ's among the best-friend rank; Felipe's gone with the shed; Lou's 1,602 messages a flare that burned out in three weeks. And the one that broke the rules is texting him again.

## Appendix: method, confidence, and limits

**Sources and register.** The human texture of this article comes overwhelmingly from Dan's own retrospective narration — the DANSYNTH and STORYTIME sessions, run through Gemini in 2026, are stylized self-narration: memoir, not deposition. Treat direct quotes as his voice (primary for what he claims, not for what happened) and treat narrative details — the steel-chair return, the six-month first stint, the exact alibi mechanics — as his telling. The contemporaneous anchors are the message records (the 80-message Tarik thread, the 4,882-record Valeria Instagram corpus, the June 2022 timestamps), the GPS re-analysis (712 visits at 1063 1st Ave, July 2021 → March 2024, 6.2-hour median visits, Mondays dark — 2026-09-14 recount), and Dan's own July 2026 pay confirmation ($15/hr, $800–1,000/week). Contemporaneous platform-timestamped records outrank retrospective testimony throughout; where the article leans on storytime alone, it says so.

**What the GPS bounds enforce.** First *recorded* visit is 2021-07-20, not March — the 298.9-day data blackout (2020-09-24 → 2021-07-20) covers his claimed March 2021 start and is a device change, not low mobility. Last recorded visit 2024-03-31; the August 2024 end is outside the data's range (nothing exists after 2024-05-14). The March 2021–August 2024 range is Dan's own dating, corroborated by the hire narrative and the unemployment filing, not by sensor data at the edges.

**The Mercer relabeling.** During his October 2023 timeline-curation spree, Dan relabeled 16 dinner shifts himself as WORK at 112 Mercer St, SoHo — caught and resolved against the sensor layer (400+ GPS pings clustering at 1st Ave, zero near Mercer; 112 Mercer was a swimwear retailer in 2022–23; the corpus contains zero mentions of Mercer or SoHo, ever). Standing rule from the catch: Timeline hand-labels are user assertions, Records.json is sensor ground truth — when they conflict, the pings win. Noted here because the same hand made the storytime.

**Forensic holdings.** (1) The Valeria pre-June-2022 iMessage record is a hole: Dan insists there were "tons"; the authoritative chat.db export (September 8) has zero messages from all of 2022 — likely device migration or deletion. (2) Her September 16, 2026 reply contents are confirmed-as-her but unseen and unarchived; the Instagram thread is unreadable to the assistant until two-factor is enabled across his Meta Account. (3) Media collection gap: the era's video/photo artifacts cited in the storytime sessions — the basement hug, the Central Park kiss, the finished interior with the DEENBOR robot, the flooded basement, the "outside bread" interrogation, the shed exterior, the twenty-plate carry, the Dimitri+DJ portrait — exist only as uploads inside the Gemini sessions; none are on disk in raw/. Locating the originals is an open collection task. (4) Gaps carried from the sibling entry: his final role title (busser → server, informally); the Tunisian manager's name. (5) The Valeria section reports what is documented and marks the rest as limits; no speculation about the affair's content beyond the records cited. (6) Ages are not stated; dates are carried as ranges and the DOB (1988-11-01) is available for any derivation rather than hardcoded.
