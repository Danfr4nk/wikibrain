# 90th St, Manhattan (NYC-1's third year)

A sublet on 90th Street between 3rd and Lexington, on the Upper East Side,
was Dan and [[wiki/people/alexis-armel|Alexis]]'s final New York address —
the third and last year of the **NYC-1** period, following two years at
[[wiki/places/424-bedford-ave|424 Bedford Ave]] in Williamsburg. The address
itself is thinly documented in the way the whole tenancy history is thinly
documented: no rent figure, no lease, no signatory, no photograph
([[wiki/places/the-unpapered-address]]). What it has instead is a **public**
**record** — 205 recovered @danfrank originals fall inside 2012 and 69 in
January–May 2013 ([[wiki/timeline/periods/2010s]]) — and that record turns
out to carry the year's physical, economic and professional facts in more
detail than any private source in the corpus does.

This page was rewritten on 2026-09-22 against that record and against the
authoritative message corpus. Three things changed: the move-in date is now
fixed to the day and is **not** the date this page carried; the Creative
License conflict is re-located relative to the tenancy; and the apartment
acquires its first surviving physical detail, which is a view of Lexington
Avenue during a hurricane.

## The dates, and which of them survive

The page has carried **1 March 2012 – 1 April 2013, "exactly thirteen**
**months."** The tidiness was the warning. The tweet archive dates the move
directly, in two consecutive posts that need no interpretation:

| When (UTC) | When (EDT) | Post |
|---|---|---|
| 2012-03-29 19:40:59 | 15:40 | *"Goodbye, Brooklyn!"* |
| 2012-03-30 19:55:24 | 15:55 | *"Hello, Manhattan."* |

So the tenancy begins on **29–30 March 2012**, four weeks after the date
this page asserted, and the "exactly thirteen months" arithmetic was an
artefact of two round numbers rather than a measurement. The
[[wiki/timeline/periods/2010s|NYC-1 period page]] and
[[wiki/people/alexis-armel]] both already carry the corrected start; this
page was the last one holding the old figure.

**The end date is not fixed and this page should stop implying that it**
**is.** Three values are in play across the wiki:

| Value | Where it comes from | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 April 2013 | this page's own frontmatter and lead | unsourced; derived from the "thirteen months" arithmetic that the tweets have now broken |
| April 2013 | [[wiki/places/424-bedford-ave]]'s typed edge | inherited from this page |
| May 2013 | context-core's residence timeline, [[wiki/timeline/periods/2010s]], [[wiki/timeline/periods/uniontown-return-2013-2015]] | the canonical value everywhere else in the wiki |

The archive corroborates a spring departure without pinning it. New York
references run 8.8%, 8.9% and 10.2% of originals across 2010, 2011 and 2012
and then fall to **0.3% in 2013**; the last post placing Dan in the city is
the **18 November 2012** internship pitch (*"a recent music engineering*
*grad with pro tools certification living in NYC"*). Two thin markers sit in
the gap. On **2 March 2013** he asks *"is there a Texas Roadhouse in*
*manhattan?"* — which reads like somebody in Manhattan wanting one, though
it is equally readable as somebody elsewhere asking about Manhattan, and the
chain has no Manhattan location either way. On **26–27 May 2013** he is
watching the fourth season of *Arrested Development* with *"@alexisarmel*
*@YourLadyV"*; the second handle appears four times in the 2013–2014 archive
and **is not identified anywhere in the wiki**, so it places nobody. Neither
post is a statement of address. The nearest first-person statement is three
months later still: *"i just moved back from NYC and i'm super output*
*recently"*, said to [[wiki/people/ej-rags|Ej Rags]] on **12 July 2013**.

The honest reading is that the tenancy ran from **late March 2012 to some**
**point in spring 2013**, most likely May on the weight of context-core, and
that this page's April date has no independent support. The frontmatter
`date_range_end` is left at 2013-04-01 rather than silently changed, because
moving it would propagate a second unsourced number over the first; the
correction belongs in whichever pass can date the departure, not in this
one.

## The one physical detail that survives

Every prior version of this page recorded, correctly, that nothing about the
unit itself survives — no layout, no rent, no photograph. One thing does,
and it is in the Sandy sequence below. At 08:41 on 30 October 2012, awake
for a second night, Dan writes to a radio producer:

> *"this seems like a fucked up dream. I'm listening to Gangnam Style cue*
> *on O&A and looking at an empty destroyed Lexington Ave"*

He is *looking at* Lexington Avenue. The address the corpus carries is "90th
between 3rd and Lexington," and the line establishes that the apartment had
a sightline onto the Lexington end of the block — the only statement
anywhere in the record about what could be seen from inside it. Beside it,
from 06:27 the same morning: *"Thank god for Korean deli though. Everything*
*else on Upper East Side has been closed since 4pm Sunday"* — a working deli
within reach on foot, which is the second and last physical fact about the
apartment's surroundings.

This does not close the gap. It narrows it from *nothing* to *a view and a*
*deli*, which is more than either Bedford Ave or this address had before.

## Complete venue log, March–April 2012

[[wiki/self/location-history]] records fifteen Foursquare check-ins between
March 2010 and January 2011 as the only address-level location data the
corpus holds before the Google Timeline export begins in April 2014. It then
notes "a second, thinner run of 'I'm at' posts through Twitter's own venue
tagging … from 31 March to 15 April 2012, **all Manhattan**, immediately
after the move off Bedford." Six of the seven are Manhattan. The seventh is
not, and it is the interesting one.

This is the complete run — every venue-tagged post in the tenancy, in order,
nothing omitted:

| Date (EDT) | Venue | As posted | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2012-03-31 19:01 | Osso Buco – UES | *"I'm at Osso Buco - UES (New York, NY)"* | first check-in as a Manhattan resident, ~30 hours after "Hello, Manhattan." |
| 2012-04-06 15:23 | New York Sports Club | *"I'm at New York Sports Club (New York, NY) w/ 3 others"* | with company |
| 2012-04-06 18:28 | National September 11 Memorial | *"I'm at National September 11 Memorial at World Trade Center (New York, NY) w/ 11 others"* | a twelve-person group, the largest in the run |
| 2012-04-07 18:15 | Josie's West | *"I'm at Josie's West (New York, NY)"* | Upper West Side |
| 2012-04-08 19:26 | Little Brown Chocolate Bakery and Coffee | *"I'm at Little Brown Chocolate Bakery and Coffee (New York)"* | |
| 2012-04-09 19:11 | Le Pain Quotidien | *"I'm at Le Pain Quotidien (New York, NY)"* | |
| 2012-04-15 01:00 | Zablozki's | *"I'm at Zablozki's (Brooklyn, NY) w/ 4 others"* | **Brooklyn** — a Williamsburg bar, two weeks after leaving Williamsburg |

**Two findings from the complete list rather than a sample.** The run is
**sixteen days long** and then stops permanently: no venue-tagged post
appears anywhere in the remaining eleven months of the tenancy, or ever
again. It is the same shape as the 2010–11 Foursquare habit — four seasons,
then abandoned — compressed into a fortnight, and it makes the 2012 run a
**moving-in behaviour** rather than a location record. Six venues in ten
days is somebody announcing a new neighbourhood; the silence after 15 April
is the announcement ending, not the movement.

And the last entry is in Brooklyn, with four other people, at one in the
morning. The [[wiki/self/location-history]] line "all Manhattan" is wrong by
one row, and the row it is wrong about is the one that says something: the
Williamsburg social circle survived the move across the river by at least
two weeks. Corrected here; the location-history page should be amended to
match.

## Creative License: the job ended before the address, the fight happened in it

This page has always carried the [[wiki/work/creative-license|Creative
License]] exit as its central event. The sequencing needs adjusting. Dan's
last day at the West Village talent and licensed-music brokerage was **on**
**or before 15 February 2012** — the date of the first email flagging that
his final paycheck carried a **$104 charge for a MetroCard he says he**
**never requested**, and that the check had arrived unsigned. That is six
weeks before "Goodbye, Brooklyn!". **The resignation belongs to Bedford**
**Ave. Everything that came out of it belongs here.**

The acts, dated, all of them falling inside this tenancy except the first:

- **The stated reason (Feb 2012, Bedford Ave).** Per Dan's own later
  testimony, what he cited at the time was a *"small fraud with airplane*
  *tickets"* in [[wiki/people/kevin-mckiernan|Kevin McKiernan]]'s travel
  billing — something he was positioned to notice in a role carrying
  "oversight of office and employee expenses." His own retrospective framing
  is self-doubting: he used it as *"legal ground"* because a personality
  complaint would have been dismissed, and wonders whether he *"blew it up*
  *into a much much bigger thing."* The citation is confirmed; the mechanism
  behind it is not.
- **The payroll dispute (15 February – 9 April 2012).** Six weeks with
  accountant [[wiki/people/marty-jackson|Marty Jackson]] over the unsigned
  check, the disputed charge and a late W-2, producing the line the whole
  employer page turns on — *"It's a trust issue. We don't trust you. Please*
  *do not forget the keys or any other CL property you have"* (27 February).
  Dan filed complaints with the **IRS and the NYS Department of Labor**,
  CC'd his father [[wiki/people/rick-frank|Rick]] throughout, and received
  tactical coaching rather than authority in return (*"You sound so*
  *desperate. That is what kevin wants. We'll talk about a strategy for*
  *this."*). He conceded the $104 on 9 April to end it, and separated the
  functionary from the principal in writing while doing so.
- **The catharsis (26 April 2012).** A one-line email — *"Enjoy!!!"* —
  with `BEST OF KTM VOICEMAILS.mp3` attached, sent to a former colleague.
  Sourced to the Gemini-58 session, not located in the Gmail pull.
- **The whistleblower disclosure (3 May 2012).** Unprompted, months after
  the money was settled, Dan told a former contact,
  [[wiki/people/renae-holland|Renae Holland]], that McKiernan had used Dan's
  own account to send recommendation-removal demands to departed staff, and
  — the serious part — that Dan had been made to send two departing interns
  **altered employment contracts** with a clause inserted "that prohibited
  recommendations without CEO approval." Renae corroborated from her own
  tenure.
- **The retaliation (June 2012).** A formal LinkedIn intellectual-property
  complaint against Dan's profile, backed by **two notarized documents**
  **asserting he had never been a Creative License employee** — notarized by
  a serving CL employee. LinkedIn threatened termination within three
  business days; Dan filed a counter-notice naming the motive ("*because of*
  *my actions as a whistleblower while under his employ*"). The outcome is
  unrecovered; the profile survived.

Three months of that ran out of an Upper East Side sublet by a
twenty-three-year-old with no lawyer. It is the founding case for
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/vertical-authority-skepticism]] and the direct
structural rhyme with [[wiki/work/bfs-foods|BFS Foods]] fourteen years
later, and the two prior correction notices on this page — which fought over
whether the airfare claim existed at all — are preserved below because the
sequence of getting it wrong is itself part of the record.

> **CORRECTED [2026-08-10]:** This section previously stated the exit as
> "February 2012" without qualification. A dedicated pass on
> [[wiki/work/creative-license]] and [[wiki/people/kevin-mckiernan]] found
> the date is contradicted between sources: Dan's own résumé gives "Jan 2011
> – Feb 2013," a full year later than the AI-dossier chronology this page
> had adopted as settled. It also found the specific fraud allegation itself
> has **no primary-source corroboration anywhere in** **the corpus** — it
> exists only in two AI dossiers restating each other's language, not two
> independent accounts.

> **RE-CORRECTED [2026-08-10, same day]:** The paragraph above is superseded
> by a live Gmail search that recovered the operator's actual 2011–2012
> correspondence with Creative License. The February 2012 date is
> **confirmed directly** (a dated February 15, 2012 email flags the
> final-paycheck error). A third pass the same day found Dan's own primary
> testimony that he did cite an airfare issue when he quit, so the airfare
> claim is real and only its elaborated mechanism is unverified. See
> [[wiki/work/creative-license]] for the full account and the three-pass
> correction history.

## Hurricane Sandy, 28–30 October 2012: the complete log

Sandy is carried elsewhere in the wiki as a shock to the household economy —
Alexis worked at the **Bridge Cafe**, 279 Water Street, the city's oldest
continuously operating tavern, and the storm's flooding of the Seaport
closed it and ended her job. That is true and it is half of it. The other
half is what Dan was doing for the forty-eight hours the storm was overhead,
which the tweet archive holds in full.

This is every post in the window, nothing omitted, local time:

| 10-28 20:26 | *"Grand Central Station is shut down. #sandy #nyc"* |
| 10-28 20:43 | *"The worst of #sandy novelty twitter accounts:"* |
| 10-28 21:37 | *"NYSE will be closed tomorrow due to #sandy. First weather related closure in 27 years."* |

| 10-29 05:37 | *"Alright, so where's this storm?"* |
| 10-29 16:11 | *"#Sandy pics from NYC on the liveblog gogo."* |
| 10-29 16:55 | *"#sandy liveblog updated. pics/gifs/nyc news."* |
| 10-29 19:04 | *"Manhattan is straight empty. No cabs. No stores. No people."* |

| 10-30 06:23 | to @OpieRadio — *"here's the video of 50+ house, 6-alarm fire in Breezy Point, Queens"* |

Twenty posts across forty-six hours. Read as a sequence rather than as a
quotable line, it is an **operation**: infrastructure notices (Grand
Central, the NYSE, the MTA), a running liveblog updated at least twice,
source triage (*"the worst of #sandy novelty twitter accounts"* is media
criticism published mid-storm), original footage and hard numbers fed to a
national radio show, and a first-hand report from the street. The famous
sentence — *"first true 'digital disaster'"* — is posted at 08:25 on the
30th, **after** all of that. It is a practitioner's observation about a
practice he had just executed at scale, not an observer's aside, which is
the strengthening [[wiki/mind/synthesis/millennial-digital-witness]] should
carry.

And the whole of it was run from this apartment, on no sleep, in the same
week his partner's employer was destroyed.

## The economic floor of the year

The tenancy's other throughline is that the audio-engineering career the
Full Sail degree pointed at was already over and nobody in the record says
so. Two posts from the same week in September 2012:

> *"If I had a peso for every South American I meet at The Gap who speaks*
> *zero English.........................I'd have a lot of fucking pesos"*
> (29 September)
>
> *"Sometimes I write my own stories about the cartoons on the fitting*
> *room walls at work."* (30 September)

Retail, with fitting rooms, and boredom deep enough to have generated a
private fiction about the wall art. In the same week: *"I'd sooner buy a*
*GSL premium sub than a new (probably factory scratched) iPhone.*
*Unfortunately, neither is in my current budget."* Seven months after
leaving a salaried office job over $104, a Pro Tools-certified engineer is
on a shop floor, and mentions it once, as an aside about customers.
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/failure-to-launch]] treats this as the period's lowest
documented point; the tone is the finding.

The single documented job application of the entire 2010–2012 record lands
seven weeks later, on **18 November 2012**, and it is for an *unpaid*
internship at the Opie & Anthony show — pitched as *"a recent music*
*engineering grad with pro tools certification living in NYC"* when the
certification was from January 2010 and the degree older still. Nothing in
the corpus says what came back.

The household's other income was Alexis's. The chat archive documented on
[[wiki/people/alexis-armel]] — the only daily record either of them left —
is, in its own words, about money: rent, shifts, short checks, a $100
monthly student-loan payment she resents, *"a whopping 225"* for three days'
work. Sandy removed that half of it in October. The return to Uniontown
follows within roughly six months.

That last row deserves its own sentence. The authoritative Messages export
holds 192,140 rows spanning 2011-03-19 → 2026-09-07
(`corpus/manifest.json`), and a direct count over it returns **one** row
dated before 12 November 2015 — a single attachment-only message from
2011-03-18. There are **zero** rows in 2012, 2013 or 2014. Whatever was said
privately during this tenancy is not in the instrument, and no future pass
should read that silence as quiet; `CORPUS_POLICY.md` names this exact
failure mode, and here the absence is a property of the export window,
flatly.

This is the second consecutive address to leave no rent figure, no lease and
no signatory. [[wiki/places/the-unpapered-address]] makes the structural
argument: seven addresses, the same void at each, which means the pattern
predates both the poverty floor and the Annie decade and is not a product of
either. The contribution of this address specifically is that it is the
**second** instance — early enough to rule out the later explanations.

## Coverage limits

- **The departure date is unresolved**, and this page is one of the
  sources of the confusion. April 2013 has no support outside this page's
  own arithmetic; May 2013 is canonical elsewhere; nothing dated settles it.
  A lease end, a forwarding address, or a dated message would.
- **The tweet archive is a floor, not a total.** The operator spreadsheet
  begins 2013-08-17; 2012 and early 2013 are live-scrape reconstructions in
  batches, and pure reposts are excluded by the archive's own rule. A quiet
  month here means quiet in recovered originals, not silence.
- **The monthly volumes cannot be read as activity.** Recovered 2012
  originals run 27/20/19/10/20/10/10/19/20/20/26/4 by month and 2013 runs
  4/29/6/20/10/10/11 through July. The recurring value of 10 is the scrape
  cap, not behaviour. No trend should be drawn from this series.
- **No private record exists for the tenancy.** Zero corpus rows, no
  Google Timeline (it starts April 2014), no photographs.
- **The Creative License airfare mechanism is unverified** — only Dan's
  citation of it is confirmed. The LinkedIn outcome, the text of the
  notarized filings, and the `BEST OF KTM VOICEMAILS.mp3` email are all
  unrecovered.
- **Inferred vs documented.** Documented: the move dates, the seven
  venues, the twenty Sandy posts, the retail posts, the internship
  application, the Creative License sequence. Inferred: that the Lexington
  Ave line describes a view from the apartment rather than from the street;
  that the Zablozki's check-in indicates a surviving Brooklyn social tie;
  that the venue-run's sixteen-day span is a moving-in behaviour. Each is a
  reading of a dated primary post, and each is labelled here rather than
  asserted.
- **The choice of the Upper East Side** over staying in Brooklyn, and the
  circumstances of the decision to leave New York entirely rather than
  relocate again within the city, remain undocumented — the two questions
  the original version of this page named, and neither is closed.
