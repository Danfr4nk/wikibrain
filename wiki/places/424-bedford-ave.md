# 424 Bedford Ave (NYC-1)

424 Bedford Ave, Apartment 6C, in Williamsburg, Brooklyn was Dan's first New York apartment — the home base of the NYC-1 period (April 2010 – May 2013) for its first two years, before a final year at a Manhattan sublet on 90th Street. At two years it is the longer of the period's two addresses, and the setting of Dan's first sustained adult household: [[wiki/people/alexis-armel|Alexis Armel]] lived with him for the apartment's full run, in the relationship's first sustained cohabitation, five years before the [[wiki/places/155-virginia-ave|155 Virginia Ave]] household of the Annie era.

It is also the corpus's least physically documented major residence. No photographs of the unit survive, no rent figure, no lease, no deposit, no signatory. A 2011 Google Talk instruction to [[wiki/people/jamie-mohler|Jamie Mohler]] — "dial to 63 when you get here," the buzzer code for getting into the building — is the only concrete physical detail attached to the apartment itself in the entire record (`dat:1392`). Almost everything else known about the address is reconstructed from what happened around it rather than in it: the jobs run from it, the people who visited it, the two dated group events organised out of it, and the one supply relationship that began there and kept producing for thirteen years after the lease ended. This page treats that thinness as its subject as much as its limit. 424 Bedford is the earliest instance of a documentary pattern the wiki has since found at every address Dan has occupied, and the clearest case of a place that stopped mattering as a residence and never stopped mattering as an origin.

## Arrival and the studio-work era

Dan moved in April 2010, at the front edge of his post-Full-Sail push into the recording-arts career the degree pointed at — the one stretch of his life where audio engineering was the actual job. The work ran through ishlab (2010–11) and [[wiki/work/creative-license|Creative License]] (2010–2012), and the Creative License job ran for the address's entire occupancy and into the following year at 90th Street. The apartment was, in that sense, a work address before it was anything else: the base from which the studio-work era was conducted, and the household that era's wages and hours had to sustain.

The move was underwritten from Uniontown. His father [[wiki/people/rick-frank|Rick Frank]] funded the exit itself: an apartment-hunting trip dated to February 18, 2010, and a standing offer of $2,000 a month "while you intern," floated as the alternative to an Entourage-style West Hollywood move (`dat:1392`, carried as page testimony). The paternal relationship is therefore a direct enabling condition of NYC-1 rather than a backdrop to it — the first New York address exists because Rick paid for the search and offered to pay for the internship months. The May 1, 2010 Brooklyn Bridge birthday photograph — Dan with his mother [[wiki/people/suzanne-frank|Suzanne Frank]] and [[wiki/people/danielle-onesi|Danielle Onesi]] — is framed elsewhere in the corpus as his own "I arrived" shot, timed to the move. No photograph of the apartment itself accompanies it. From the first month, the record documents the life around the address and not the address.

The arrival window carries one further piece of logistics. A January 24, 2010 exchange with [[wiki/people/charles-davenport|Charles Davenport]] — "i'm in need of another run down there" — lands inside the preparation period for the move and reads as early supply or transport support around it. The corpus files it as co-occurrence rather than cause; it is noted here because it dates the move's planning to the first weeks of 2010, consistent with the February hunting trip, and because it is typical of how this address enters the record at all: sideways, in other people's threads, as the unstated destination of arrangements made elsewhere.

## The household

Alexis lived with Dan for the address's full two-year run — "with me the whole time we were in nyc," in Dan's summary — and the Bedford apartment is where the six-year relationship (2009–2015) first became a household. During this period she started work at the historic Bridge Cafe at 279 Water Street, the city's oldest tavern, under the Brooklyn Bridge at the South Street Seaport — a job she introduced to him in the chat archive with genuine delight: "its the oldest restaurant in nyc lol it's neat inside… yes like 1744 or something crazy like that," interviewed for on the strength of wanting day shifts. The job outlasted the address: it ended when Hurricane Sandy flooded the Seaport and closed the restaurant in October 2012, six months after the household had already relocated to [[wiki/places/90th-st-manhattan|90th Street]] — the final economic shock of NYC-1, arriving after the Bedford tenancy it belonged to had already closed, with the archive showing the two of them following the storm coverage together the day it hit. Alexis is present for the whole of NYC-1, Brooklyn through the Upper East Side, and is the only continuous personal thread the decade's first half has; her page carries the relationship's full account, including the 495-block chat archive from these years that is the only daily record either of them left.

The archive deserves a paragraph of its own, because it is the fullest daily-life record this address ever produced and it survives in her thread rather than in any document about the apartment. Dan was making her a "welcome to BK mix" on February 25, 2010, four days before leaving Florida, having told her in January that she would have to develop a taste for Grizzly Bear "otherwise you would fail your BK entry exam." What the archive is actually about, across hundreds of exchanges, is money: rent, hours, shifts, checks that come short, an electric bill credited back, a $100 monthly student-loan payment she resents, a paycheck of "a whopping 225" for three days' work, tips paid separately from wages, and a running joke-economy of Chipotle orders and birthday presents bought with money neither of them had. It is a two-income household in Williamsburg surviving on restaurant shifts and whatever Dan's audio and office work paid, and both of them are visibly tired. When she thinks she is about to be fired she has "a fucking meltdown"; when he is passed over at work he sends her the room-temperature version and she sends back "we're going to do something reaaaaaally special when u get hired. because i know you will." The register is equally striking and equally unrepresented elsewhere in the corpus: both of them write in sustained, unembarrassed baby-talk across years, with no irony and no audience, while the same years of Dan's public output run on weaponised irony. Nothing else in the wiki shows that contrast at this resolution. For this page the point is documentary: the household at 424 Bedford is recoverable in daily texture — tired, broke, affectionate, shift-driven — but only because a chat archive preserved the life, not because any record of the residence itself did. The apartment has no paperwork. The tenancy has a transcript.

The apartment was a genuine social hub for the wider Full Sail and Uniontown-diaspora circle. Jamie Mohler was a routine visitor, closely tracked enough that Alexis asked after a missed visit attributed to a knee injury in August 2011, and her 2011 Google Talk log is the corpus's only surviving description of getting into the apartment at all — the buzzer code above, preserved because someone needed to be let in. The saved log preserves the fullest single scene the address ever got: on an autumn night in 2011, during the Occupy Wall Street protests, Dan invites her over — "Jimbo! it's Dan… do you want to come with us to march on times sq tomorrow with the 'occupy' crowd" — and she agrees immediately ("Yes! I'll stop by if you folks care for a bit of crown royal! Omnomnomnomnom"), takes the J train from her own corner of Brooklyn, and gets buzzed up with the door code, with Dan needling her about the sukkahs going up around Williamsburg for the fall holidays and her replying, "I'm SO pumped. 4 stops, I'll give you a full report." A rediscovered 422-message thread attributed to the same friendship (internal-evidence identification, flagged as inference on her own page) corroborates the pattern from a second archive, including an August 25, 2011 evacuation-planning conversation ("if things start to look worse, my mom is going to come get us") landing the day before the independently dated hurricane message. The address anchors two dated group events, both organised directly out of the apartment. On August 25–26, 2011, Dan and Jamie left together the night before Hurricane Irene's landfall — an evacuation pinned to a single day by a third party's message ("mohler and i are heading out of town tonight because of the hurricane," August 26, 2011), the most precisely dated event in that friendship's record. In October 2011, during the Occupy Wall Street protests, Dan invited Jamie to come over and join the "occupy" crowd marching in Times Square the following day; she agreed immediately and was buzzed up with the door code. A residence with no surviving floor plan, no rent, and no photograph nonetheless has a dated evacuation and a dated protest organised from its kitchen-table equivalent. That is the shape of the whole file: the apartment is documented exactly to the extent that other people's plans passed through it.

It is also where Dan first met [[wiki/people/menore|Menore]]. The Bedford Ave/Williamsburg connection that began at this address became a supply relationship running, with a gap, into 2025 — fourteen years from origin to last documented activity, most of them after the apartment itself had been vacated. The connection is treated on [[wiki/mind/synthesis/dormancy-not-exit|the dormancy page]] as the proof that the no-exit rule applies to places and not only to people, and it is the reason this page cannot honestly close in March 2012 even though the tenancy does. A supply line originated at a Williamsburg address in 2010–12 kept producing through the Au Za'atar years and beyond. The apartment's longest consequence was not domestic. It was logistical.

## The location trail

What the message record withholds, the self-published location record partly supplies. Dan's 2010 Foursquare check-ins form a location trail around the address — the deli at 313 Bedford, the Marcy Ave J/M/Z station platform, the C-Town supermarket at South 1st and Havemeyer — corroborating the tenancy from outside the message corpus and fixing the household's daily geography to a few Williamsburg blocks (`dat:1392`). The trail is worth stating for what it is: check-ins are assertions, made by the subject, at the time, for an audience. They corroborate presence and routine. They do not corroborate rent, leasehold, or who else lived at the address, and the corpus holds nothing that does.

The tenancy ends on March 29–30, 2012, in the same medium: "Goodbye, Brooklyn!" on March 29, then "Hello, Manhattan" on March 30, after which the check-ins move to the Upper East and West Sides. The household's third year ran from the sublet at 90th Street between 3rd and Lexington (March 2012 – April 2013), and the NYC-1 period closed with the return to Uniontown in May 2013. The corpus gives no account of the move's circumstances — no landlord dispute, no lease expiry, no stated reason survives for why a two-year Brooklyn household became a one-year Manhattan sublet a year before the period itself ended. That silence is one of this page's standing gaps, and it is typical: the record dates the departure precisely and explains it not at all.

## The unpapered address

424 Bedford is the first entry in a pattern the wiki has since documented across seven residences and sixteen years: 424 Bedford Ave, 90th Street, [[wiki/places/155-virginia-ave|155 Virginia Ave]], [[wiki/places/307-e-76th-st|307 E 76th St]], [[wiki/places/337-saratoga-drive|337 Saratoga Drive]], [[wiki/places/117-belmont-circle|117 Belmont Circle]], and [[wiki/places/463-morgantown-st|463 Morgantown St]]. Across all seven, the corpus contains no lease held by Dan, no contemporaneously recorded rent figure, no signatory, and no deposit (`dat:1394`). Tenancy ran by relationship — mother, grandmother, partner, great-grandmother — terminable by the person, with no instrument.

The synthesis page that names the pattern reads the mechanism at attention level, and this page adopts that reading without extending it: demanding a lease from a housing provider functions as an audit of whether they can be trusted to keep granting the tenancy, and across the seven relationships the audit was deferred in every case while the housing held. Whatever the interior account of that deferral is, the instruments cannot see it, and this page does not supply one. What is documented is the sequence: the pattern predates both the poverty floor of the later years and the Annie decade, because its first instance is a 2010 Brooklyn apartment taken at the start of a funded career push, with a father's $2,000-a-month offer standing behind it. The absence of paper at 424 Bedford cannot be explained by the circumstances that might explain it later. It was there first.

The pattern's later history sharpens the point. The one papered counterexample in the set, 337 Saratoga Drive, held its paper entirely in his mother's name and ended by court order in her Chapter 13. The 2026 arrangement at 463 Morgantown St — no lease, no power of attorney as of August 2026 — is the seventh consecutive instance. A rule first visible in a Williamsburg buzzer code and a father's standing offer is still running sixteen years later, which is why this address, the least documented of the seven, carries a major page: it is where the documentary void starts, and the void is the finding.

## Conflicts in the record

**Occupancy dates (page range vs tenancy; reconciled 2026-09-09).** This page's frontmatter historically carried a date range of April 2010 to May 2013. That is the NYC-1 period's range, not the apartment's. The tenancy itself ran April 2010 to March 2012, ending with the March 29–30, 2012 "Goodbye, Brooklyn!" / "Hello, Manhattan" tweets; the period's third year belongs to [[wiki/places/90th-st-manhattan|90th Street]] (`dat:1392`). Current standing: corrected in the frontmatter above; the period and the address are filed separately from here on.

**The sources' grade (standing limit, restated 2026-09-09).** The tenancy's core facts — the April 2010 arrival, the February 18, 2010 hunting trip, the $2,000-a-month offer, the buzzer code, the Irene evacuation and the Occupy night — rest on Gemini-session documents, context-core material, and the Twitter archive, none of which are held as primary files in this repository; the ingest carries them as page testimony (`dat:1392`). The Foursquare trail and the departure tweets corroborate presence and dates from a second medium, but they are the subject's own published assertions. Current standing: the chronology is consistent with [[wiki/people/alexis-armel|Alexis Armel]]'s two-year-run account and is adopted with its grade stated; no held lease, rent record, or photograph exists to raise it, and none of the claims above should be cited as primary-verified.

**Address of record vs address of occupancy (pattern-level, narrowed 2026-08-22).** The unpapered-address synthesis was narrowed by a later recheck: a Fayette County summons from April 2018 carries 337 Saratoga Drive as Dan's address of record seven days after an eviction notice at 155 Virginia Ave, forcing a distinction the original rule had not needed — paper can move without occupancy moving (`dat:1394`). The narrowing concerns later addresses, not this one, but it is recorded here because 424 Bedford is the pattern's origin case: for this address the corpus holds neither an address-of-record document nor an occupancy instrument, so the distinction cannot even be posed. Current standing: the rule survives in narrowed form; this address remains its purest instance precisely because there is no paper on either side of the distinction.

**Move-in month (this page: April 2010; Alexis page, corrected 2026-09-02: start of March 2010).** The tenancy evidence carried by `dat:1392` dates the occupancy from April 2010, and the frontmatter above keeps that date. [[wiki/people/alexis-armel|Alexis Armel]]'s page, corrected against the tweet archive in the same period, dates the Florida departure to February 28, 2010 and the Brooklyn move to the start of March 2010, with the "welcome to BK mix" dated February 25. The two records are a month apart, most likely because the move itself (early March) and the tenancy's formal start (April) are being dated by different instruments. Current standing: unresolved at the month level; the frontmatter keeps April 2010 per `dat:1392`, and the discrepancy is recorded here rather than silently harmonised.

**What is not known (standing gaps).** Rent, lease terms, and any signatory; whether anyone beyond Dan and Alexis lived at the address; any photograph or physical description of the unit beyond the buzzer code; and the circumstances of the move to the Upper East Side sublet. These are stated here, per the canonical template, rather than in a separate Gaps section. They bound the article: the household, work, and event chronology above is the full extent of the held record, and the Menore origin — the address's longest consequence — is documented as a connection and a duration, not as a set of transactions at this address, none of which the corpus itemises.

## Assessment

424 Bedford Ave matters less as a home than as a starting condition. Three of the record's longest lines begin inside its two years: the first sustained cohabitation, with Alexis, which set the household form the Annie decade later inherited; the studio-work era, the one period in which the trained profession was the actual job, run from this address for its whole occupancy; and the Menore supply connection, which outlived the tenancy by thirteen years and became the standing proof that in this corpus a place, like a person, is filed under dormancy rather than exit. Against those three lines, the documentary void — no lease, no rent, no photograph, one buzzer code — is not an archival accident to apologise for. It is the earliest dated instance of the housing pattern that runs unbroken to the present address, visible first where the usual explanations for it do not yet apply. The apartment is gone from the record in every way a residence can be gone. What it originated is still running.
