# Book Want-to-Read List

Dan's Goodreads "want to read" shelf, as captured in an image-transcription
export of his full Goodreads shelves — 150 unique titles in the page's Full
List table below (the page historically headlined 149; see Conflicts in the
record). The shelf had never been mined before this pass, and it is distinct
from the 120-book *read* corpus already covered in
[[wiki/interests/favorites/books]]. Where the read list is a record of what
Dan has actually consumed, this is a map of intent: what he considers worth
his time next, which is its own signal about the shape of his interests even
before a single page gets turned.

The shelf does not wander. It is a direct extension of the read corpus's
obsessions rather than a departure from them: Trump-era politics and
institutional collapse, ancient Rome, and intelligence/conspiracy history —
the same three magnets, now pointed at what comes next. That continuity is
the first finding, and Dan has named the mechanism himself. Describing his
own Rome shelf in the main chat on 2026-09-23, he said: "Plutarch (twice,
including the complete works), Goldsworthy's *Caesar*, Holland's *Rubicon*,
Parenti's *Assassination of Julius Caesar*, Everitt's *Cicero*, Dan Carlin's
*Death Throes of the Republic* (five stars). This is not a casual history
interest. It is a *specific* interest in the mechanics of republican decay
and institutional collapse — and it sits in obvious dialogue with the
Trump-era" reading. The want-to-read shelf is that interest's forward
operating base: the next targets of an already-defined inquiry, not a fresh
set of curiosities.

The second finding is about method. Dan does not only read books; he
processes them. A May 2025 ChatGPT session shows him uploading full ePubs —
the export's filenames are Anna's Archive rips, including *World Order*
(Kissinger) and *The Divider: Trump in the White House, 2017-2021* (Baker) —
and running Python (ebooklib plus BeautifulSoup) to extract the text before
asking for chapter-by-chapter summaries ("Please summarize each chapter as a
list and give detailed information about each 'event' covered in the book").
The working notes in that same session name *Shock Doctrine*, Naomi Klein,
Michael Parenti, Thomas Frank, and *Blackshirts* — shelf titles and shelf
authors, being worked through the pipeline. "Want to read," for Dan, can
mean "want to have extracted and summarized." He will also grind through the
un-summarized kind: on 2024-07-17 he sent a contact a PDF of *Linguistic
Inquiries Into Donald Trump's Language* with the verdict, "The whole thing
is a long and tedious read but it's SUPER interesting" — tediousness is no
obstacle when the payoff is there.

Some of the shelf's titles have decade-long intent trails. Dan added *A
Brief History of Time* to his Facebook profile on 2011-02-04 and tweeted
"BRIEF HISTORY OF TIME" in all caps on 2013-11-05; it is still on the
want-to-read shelf. He added Henry Rollins's *Get in the Van* to the same
profile on 2013-01-19; *The Portable Henry Rollins* sits on the shelf now. A
2010-11-18 Facebook comment ("carl sagan cosmos"), a 2011-02-15 status
update quoting Sagan ("In order to make an apple pie from scratch, you must
first invent the universe."), and a 2018-01-03 change of his profile
quotations to another Sagan line ("Our species needs, and deserves, a
citizenry with minds wide awake and a basic understanding of how the world
works.") bracket eight years of Sagan fandom behind the shelf's *Cosmos*.
And Sammy's 2026-09-24 order-history pass surfaced a Christmas Eve 2 a.m.
Amazon order for *The Laws of Human Nature* — still on the want-to-read
shelf, meaning the purchase preceded (or bypassed) ever logging it read.
The shelf is not a wishlist in the casual sense. It is an intake queue, and
some items have been in it for over a decade.

## Provenance and the count question

Everything on this page traces to a single source: `raw/self/dox-md/DAN_COMP.md`,
described as an image-transcription export of Dan's full Goodreads "Read"
and "Want to Read" shelves. That file is not held in this repository — the
source reference is unresolved, and the export cannot be re-checked. The
"Read" section (120 titles) duplicates the corpus already covered by
[[wiki/interests/favorites/books]]; this page covers only the "Want to
Read" section.

The page headlined 149 titles. Its own table carries 150 unique data rows,
zero duplicates — counted directly from the table, and independently
confirmed by a 2026-09-09 review (kb dat:0434). The 149 figure is therefore
superseded (dated pointer, 2026-10-07); the table is what the page can vouch
for. Because the underlying export is unheld, the live shelf's true count
cannot be settled from evidence, and neither 149 nor 150 can be confirmed
against Goodreads itself. The Dimensions metrics below (average rating
4.11, 19 titles at 4.4+, highest-rated *The Sinister Truth: Mk-Ultra* at
4.75) are likewise inherited from the prior wiki's pipeline and were not
recomputed against the 150 rows; they are carried forward as the page's
stated metrics, not as re-verified facts.

## The Trump-era politics cluster

The page's own approximation puts ~34 titles in Trump-era politics and
institutional collapse — an approximation, not an independently verified
count. The cluster breaks into legible sub-shelves. The fascism-comparison
line is the most deliberate: *How Fascism Works* (Stanley), *The Anatomy of
Fascism* (Paxton), *How Democracies Die* (Levitsky & Ziblatt), *Blackshirts
and Reds* (Parenti), *History as Mystery* (Parenti), and *On Democracies and
Death Cults* (Murray). A 2025-04-16 ChatGPT session's working notes name
*How Democracies Die*, consistent with Dan running shelf material through
his ePub pipeline. Two corrections apply to the prior telling of this
cluster (see Conflicts): *Wannabe Fascists* was named in the page's prose
but is not among the 150 rows, and the "already-read Finchelstein title"
the prose invoked has no Finchelstein entry in the read-corpus rows. The
cluster stands on the titles actually present — and it is a sustained line
of inquiry, not a one-off: six-plus titles asking, from different angles,
how democracies fail.

Alongside the theory sits the insider-account shelf: *A Very Stable Genius*
(Rucker & Leonnig), *Frankly, We Did Win This Election* (Bender), *The
Reckoning* (Mary Trump), *Where Law Ends* (Weissmann), *The Cult of Trump*
(Hassan), *The Dangerous Case of Donald Trump* (Lee), *Trump's Brain*
(Decker), *Revenge* (Isenstadt), *Butler* (Zito), *The Bitter End* (Sides &
Teres), *Why We Did It* (Miller), *Thank You For Your Servitude*
(Leibovich), *Romney: A Reckoning* (Coppins), and *The Art of Power*
(Pelosi). The appetite behind these is visible in Dan's own sessions: in a
mid-2025 ChatGPT exchange he had the assistant perform an autopsy on a
passage about Obama's 2020 primary maneuvering — "Obama's 2020 role wasn't
apathy — it was *influence cloaked in apathy*" — closing with the slogan
"*Hope* was 2008. *Control* was 2020." That is the sensibility the
insider-account shelf feeds: elite maneuver read as the real text of
politics.

The third sub-shelf is the left's own critique of the Democratic Party and
its machinery: Thomas Frank twice (*Listen, Liberal*, *The Wrecking Crew*),

Think* (Johnston), *Insane Clown President* (Taibbi), *The Current Affairs

fourth is election mechanics and systems: *Ratf**ked* (Daley), *Kill

*Parliamentary America* (Stearns), *The Hollow Parties* (Schlozman),

## The Rome cluster

The page's approximation is ~18 Rome titles; Dan's own framing, quoted
above, is the better guide — "the mechanics of republican decay and
institutional collapse." The shelf's Rome titles read as the next targets
of that inquiry: *The Storm Before the Storm* and *The History of Rome: The
Republic* (Duncan ×2), *The Punic Wars*, *Caesar's Civil War*, and *Antony
and Cleopatra* (Goldsworthy ×3), *Dynasty* (Holland), *The Roman Triumph*
(Beard), *The War That Made the Roman Empire* (Strauss), *The Rise of Rome*
(Everitt), *Rome's Last Citizen* (Goodman & Soni), *Carthage Must Be
Destroyed* (Miles), *Hannibal: Rome's Greatest Enemy* and *Alexander the
Great* (Freeman ×2), *Dictator* (Harris's Cicero novel), *The Ides of
March* (Wilder), *Julius Caesar* (Shakespeare), *Shakespeare and the Fall of
the Roman Republic* (Gray), *Caesar and Christ* (Durant), *The Babylonian
Woe* (Astle), *The History of the Ancient World* (Bauer), and *Fall of
Civilizations* (Cooper).

The interest is live, not archival. On 2025-09-11 Dan texted about Cicero,
mid-argument with a friend: "He TOLD THEM he would burn down the fucking
forum" — "And he kept his fucking word." That is someone whose Roman
Republic obsession has a pulse. The fiction titles on the shelf are the
exception that proves the rule: Sammy's standing profile synthesis notes
the "Gore Vidal historical-fiction exception" as "the single documented
fiction engagement" in an otherwise ~97.8% nonfiction Goodreads, and Dan's
own trail supports it — on 2022-12-20 he sent S. LeDonne a Gore Vidal
YouTube video, and *Burr* and *Lincoln* (Vidal) sit on the shelf. Rome here
is not antiquarianism. It is a mirror held up to the present: republics,
how they rot, and the men who set the fire.

## The intelligence and conspiracy cluster

The page's approximation is ~14 titles; the testimony behind them is some
of the densest on the page. *Dark Alliance* (Webb) is the CIA-crack-cocaine
thesis, and Sammy's 2026-09-22 conspiracy-verdicts working file records the
position plainly: "the major funding mechanism for the CIA's covert wars,
particularly after the Iran-Contra blowup" — her recorded position, filed
from their conversations. *The Sinister Truth: Mk-Ultra* (Ciaccia) is the
highest-rated title on the entire shelf at 4.75, and Dan's own words put
the interest beyond the rating: on 2026-09-22, reacting to the RFK
assassination in the main chat, he wrote, "oh a Christian Palestinian
assassinates the anti-corruption, anti-war assumed dem nominee? MK ultra" —
MKUltra as his live explanatory frame for a political assassination. Two
years earlier, on 2024-07-17, he tied it to his January 6 theory in a text:
"It does tie in nicely to my January 6 theory though where intelligence
agencies are just hatching whatever careless plan they think up to keep
Trump from getting any political power again." The shelf's MKUltra title is
the book-shaped version of a theory he already argues in his own voice.

The Kennedy-assassination sub-shelf runs four deep: *The Death of a
President* (Manchester), *Six Seconds in Dallas* (Thompson), *On the Trail
of the Assassins* (Garrison), *JFK and the Unspeakable* (Douglass) —
institutional-murder literature stacked next to the MKUltra book, which is
exactly where Dan's RFK text would file it. The 9/11 sub-shelf is similarly
stacked: *9/11 on Trial* (Thorn), *The New Pearl Harbor* (Griffin),
*Disconnecting the Dots* (Fenton), *Fall and Rise* (Zuckoff), *The Only
Plane in the Sky* (Graff). *Aberration in the Heartland of the Real:
Timothy McVeigh* (Painting) has direct testimony behind it: on 2026-03-26
Dan texted, "i'm just saying they didn't frame an innocent mcveigh" —
"thye just covered up others involvement." The rest of the cluster fills
out the institutional-suspicion map: *Operation Paperclip* (Jacobsen),
*The Ghost* (Morley, on Angleton), *The Assassination of Fred Hampton*
(Haas), *Perversion of Justice* (Brown, on Epstein), *The Achilles Trap*
(Coll, on Saddam and the CIA), *The Jakarta Method* (Bevins). Read as one
shelf, it is a single sustained question — what do intelligence agencies
actually do, and what do they get away with — asked across seven decades.

## The New York City thread

This thread barely appears in the read list and is distinctive to the
want-to-read shelf: *The Power Broker* (Caro), *Boss Tweed* (Ackerman),
*The Bowery* (DeVil), *St. Marks Is Dead* (Calhoun), *Lost New York*
(Silver), *Broadway* (Leadon), *New York for New Yorkers* (Greene &
Zimmerman). The page's prior telling ties it to the period Dan spent living
in New York with Annie, which fits the shelf's shape — a retrospective
reading list about a city he inhabited.

*The Power Broker* has the strongest direct testimony of any single title
on the shelf. On 2024-09-25 at 3:40 a.m. EDT, Dan asked ChatGPT: "Can you
give me a summary of the book 'the power broker' and a timeline and
explanation of why people are so interested in Robert moses." Minutes later
he had it outline a long-form video essay on Moses — the paradox of the
unelected power broker, the Triborough bond maneuver, the Jane Jacobs
clash, the fall. That is not idle curiosity; that is someone doing the
pre-production research for a video essay on the book's subject before
having read the book. At 4.52, *The Power Broker* is the shelf's
second-highest-rated title, and the one Dan has most visibly circled.

## Science, systems, and the self

The science titles carry the longest paper trails. *Cosmos* (Sagan, 4.40)
sits on eight years of documented Sagan engagement: the 2010 comment, the
2011 status, the 2018 profile quotation. *A Brief History of Time* (Hawking,
4.21) was added to his Facebook profile in 2011 and tweeted in 2013. These
are not new interests; they are old ones that never got read. The systems
titles are newer and closer to his daily practice: *The Signal and the
Noise* (Silver, 3.97) behind the documented Silver following; *The Laws of
Human Nature* (Greene, 4.35) behind both the 2022-08-01 Messenger exchange
with Tom Maison — "Have you read any Greene?", with Dan explicitly
distancing Greene from pickup-artist culture ("Not at all like 'ThE GaMe'"
/ "Or any weird 'pickup artist' shit") — and the Christmas Eve Amazon
purchase. Dan reads Greene for power dynamics, not seduction manuals, and
he says so himself.

*MindHub: Personality Profiler* (snapshot_systems, 4.34) is the shelf's
oddest entry and the most revealing about the profiler in him: a
personality-typology tool shelved next to the Rome and Trump titles. The
page's prior telling paired it with "*Personality Types* and *The INTP* in
the read list" — half of which checks out. *Personality Types: Using the
Enneagram for Self-Discovery* (Riso) is confirmed in the read rows at four
stars; no book titled *The INTP* appears in the read-shelf rows (the phrase
traces to the wiki's INTP profile material, not a logged book — see
Conflicts). The correction sharpens rather than weakens the point: the
typology interest is real and logged, and MindHub is its forward edge. The
remaining systems titles group without direct testimony but inside the same
sensibility: *Guns, Germs, and Steel* (Diamond), *A Short History of Nearly
Everything* (Bryson), *Through Two Doors at Once* (Ananthaswamy), *The
Brain: A User's Guide* (New Scientist), *What If?* (Munroe),
*Superforecasting* (Tetlock & Gardner), *Supercommunicators* (Duhigg),
*The Shallows* (Carr), *Blink* (Gladwell), *The Charisma Myth* (Cabane),
*The Delusions of Crowds* (Bernstein). Big-pattern explanations, forecasting,
and how minds fail — the toolkit of someone who treats reality as a
high-fidelity system and hunts where it breaks.

## Assessment

The want-to-read shelf is the intake queue for a reading practice that is
already running hot — the read corpus's dates skew hard to 2024, and this
shelf is where the next material comes from. Its three magnets (Trump-era
institutional politics, Rome, intelligence history) are not three interests
but one question asked three ways: how republics decay, who really runs
things, and what power does to people. Dan's own words confirm the frame —
"the mechanics of republican decay and institutional collapse" — and his
testimony density is highest exactly where the shelf is densest: the Rome
titles he argues about unprompted (Cicero, "he kept his fucking word"), the
conspiracy titles he deploys as live explanatory frames (MKUltra for RFK,
for January 6), the forecasting analyst he follows in real time (Silver),
the power-dynamics author he recommends with a boundary attached (Greene,
"not... any weird 'pickup artist' shit"). The method matters as much as the
content: ePubs pulled and machine-summarized chapter by chapter, decade-old
intent trails (Hawking 2011, Sagan 2010–2018, Rollins 2013) finally
approaching the front of the queue, and a 3:40 a.m. pre-production research
session on Robert Moses for a video essay on a book he hasn't read yet.
The shelf's highest-rated title is an MKUltra book at 4.75; its
second-highest is *The Power Broker* at 4.52. Between those two ratings sits
the whole sensibility: hidden machinery, and the men who operate it in
plain sight.
