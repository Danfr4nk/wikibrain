# Music Favorites

Dan listens to music the way a selector listens: as someone who makes it. This page documents his taste as a *listener* — what is on his own shelf, what he kept, what he kept coming back to — separate from the GRIPNOTIC production work, which has its own pages. The two are not parallel tracks. The library is the reference shelf the production sits next to: genres he works in, genres he audits himself against, genres he archived a decade ago and never threw out.

The FAVS MASTERLIST is the spine of the record here: 1,860 tracks across 1,477 unique artists. None of it carries a numeric rating — music entries are inclusion-only, mostly pulled from Spotify likes, unlike the rated books and art elsewhere in the favorites domain — so the signal is presence, not preference intensity: what made the list at all, not how many stars it got. The 1.26 tracks-per-artist average is a count of how the library is built, not a verdict on the temperament of the listener behind it.

There is one mandatory frame for everything on this page. Dan does not hear sung lyrics as language. Words in a song register to him as timbre — another textural element of the vocal performance, like a synth patch or a drum break — not as semantic content to be parsed while listening. Every interpretive claim on this page and its sub-pages about thematic or lyrical content should therefore be read as either contextual knowledge of what a record is *about*, or speculation about what the vocal *performance* — texture, strain, delivery — might be doing, never as a claim about real-time lyric comprehension. The full statement lives at [[wiki/mind/concepts/lyric-qualifier]].

What the library shows, read that way, is a taste that has been continuous since adolescence rather than rediscovered: three genres maintained in parallel for nearly twenty years, a manual archive of a decade-old attachment sitting next to a live feed of current electronic listening, and — since 2025 — the library itself being measured and mapped by Dan's own instruments, because he decided to understand what he likes with precision rather than just keep liking it.

## The listener, not the producer

The wiki keeps the two roles on separate pages for a reason. The producer side — SLOPPP through MOGZART and MOGGED-UP to GRIPNOTIC, the sub-bass signature running involuntarily across all of them, the drop-structure technique Dan describes having to relearn on the way back — lives under [[wiki/interests/music/aliases/gripnotic]] and [[wiki/interests/music/concepts/sub-bass-signature]]. This page is the other desk: what he chooses to hear.

But the desk divider is porous, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest about how the record works. The electronic/bass cluster here is the genre space GRIPNOTIC operates in; the JPEGMAFIA pages map his production sensibility onto a commercial artist with a matching sub-bass-forward philosophy; the September 2026 MusicTrainer project treats the library as training data for a keep/drop classifier. Wherever the listener's taste and the producer's craft share a claim, the cross-reference is named. The point of keeping the pages separate is that this page answers a different question: not what he *makes*, but what he *kept* — and what the keeping pattern says about the ear doing the keeping.

## The library in numbers

The masterlist is not one continuous accumulation. It is built from two distinct sources with different characters, and the difference is structural, not incidental.

"MUSIC LIST (start-2024)" is a manually curated list: 463 tracks, no release dates in the export at all. The entire emo/pop-punk cluster — Fall Out Boy, My Chemical Romance, Say Anything, Taking Back Sunday — comes from here, which means someone sat down and specifically chose these tracks rather than letting an algorithm surface them: a deliberate act of archiving a decade-old attachment, not passive nostalgia.

"SPOTIFY LIKED 2025-2026" is the live feed: 1,384 rows, of which 871 of the 1,383 dated rows are 2024 or later. Nearly all of the electronic/bass cluster — LYNY, Knock2, and the rest of [[wiki/interests/favorites/music/electronic-bass]] — comes from here: current and ongoing rather than retrospective. ## The lyric qualifier: how the listening actually works

Before reading any artist page on this shelf as a claim about "why these lyrics resonate": Dan's 2026-07-14 founding statement, restated byte-exact in the held 2026-09-11 MUSIC THREAD, is that he does not hear the lyrics of songs, and that even having a lyric sheet — knowing the words — makes interpreting them harder, not easier. The voice arrives as cadence, phonetics, tone, melody, processing, rhythm, texture, energy. The count of exceptions is about three songs.

This is not a metaphor and not a bit. It is the phenomenological substrate under the whole shelf, and it has been operationalized, not just stated: AUTOPSY's 16-driver WHAT DID IT? taxonomy carries "vocal as texture — voice, not words" as a scoring dimension and has no field for lyrical content at all, and the hook2piano/MELODY tools treat the qualifier as an engineering constraint, rendering melody as pitch-geometry with note names as text because the user does not parse lyrics as language. The qualifier also resolved one of the wiki's oldest open questions: the thirteen-year pattern of never producing sung original lyrics, which looks from outside like a songwriter's stalled career, is reframed as never having been in the possibility space — a selector's output, not a songwriter's failure.

The consequence for this page is a standing rule: when the emo-cluster pages talk about concept albums built around betrayal and mortality, that is contextual knowledge of what the records are about — the biography of the attachment — not a claim that he parsed it in the moment of listening. What got parsed was vocal performance and sound. If a reading can't survive that filter, it doesn't survive.

## Three clusters, one long tail

The top-count artists split cleanly into three roughly even clusters — experimental/abrasive hip-hop, emo/pop-punk/indie rock, and electronic/bass — each accounting for something close to 5% of the library, with a long tail of one- and two-track artists making up most of the rest. That balance is the tell: this isn't three genres competing for dominance, it's three genres being maintained in parallel, each with its own dedicated sub-pages and its own function.

### Experimental and abrasive hip-hop

JPEGMAFIA, Kanye West, and a longer tail including A$AP Rocky, Lil Wayne, Tyler the Creator, Atmosphere, and Lupe Fiasco: dense, confrontational, self-produced-adjacent work. JPEGMAFIA leads the whole library at 13 tracks — over ten times the 1.26-track average — and the [[wiki/interests/favorites/music/artists/jpegmafia]] page gives the fullest treatment of why this cluster in particular maps onto the production identity. He self-produces everything, with a methodology closer to noise music and sound art than to conventional hip-hop beat construction; the track is a space where every element fights for dominance and clarity is optional, and sub-bass is the one stable frequency underneath the chaos — the same "bass as foundation, not ornament" principle the sub-bass signature documents across Dan's own aliases. The political content is inseparable from the sonic content: the confrontational delivery and the refusal to soften are the same argument made twice simultaneously.

### Emo, pop-punk, and indie rock

My Chemical Romance, New Found Glory, Fall Out Boy, Taking Back Sunday, Say Anything, Elliott Smith, plus Bloc Party, Death Cab for Cutie, Manchester Orchestra, and The Mars Volta. This is the manually-archived, decade-old cluster — the one that arrived on the shelf as a deliberate act of preservation in the start-2024 list, not as an algorithmic drip. Five-plus artist pages now cover it in detail, and the pattern across them is consistent: concept albums and whole-catalog engagement, timed to a genre and era (2005–2010) that overlaps directly with the "romantic collapse fantasy" imprinting window named elsewhere in the profile ([[wiki/mind/psychosexual/developmental-origins]]). Elliott Smith is the quiet pole of the shelf — seven tracks, an August 2013 Facebook like, a January 2018 crowdfund-for-an-Elliott-Smith-tattoo impulse that is the only evidence the attachment was ever meant to be permanent, followed nine months later by a booked Radiohead bear and a Misfits skull instead. Read with the lyric qualifier attached: whatever Smith does for him is done by grain, phrasing, and melodic movement, not by the words.

### Electronic, bass, and modern dance

LYNY, Knock2, Effin, Mau P, PEEKABOO, Levity, Nikita the Wicked, rSUN, oskar med k, borne, and A.M.C. — roughly 46 tracks together, all logged under "SPOTIFY LIKED 2025-2026." This is the live, current-listening cluster and the one with the most direct tie to Dan's own work; see [[wiki/interests/favorites/music/electronic-bass]] for the full treatment. The case there is that this cluster functions as active calibration listening for the GRIPNOTIC-era production practice, not just consumption running in parallel to it: Mau P's "BEATS FOR THE UNDERGROUND" and the "-Mixed" and VIP-style entries across the cluster show engagement with how tracks get reworked and remixed, and the recurring physical-space and motion titles (oskar med k's "keep your feet," "On The Ground"; borne's "Go Again," "Hold Me Down") point toward attention on rhythm, drop placement, and groove architecture — exactly the "intro, build, drop reset, build, drop" structure Dan has described, in his own words, as the specific technical skill he had to relearn returning to production.

The genre is unusually honest about its own function in its naming conventions, and the titles are a tell worth keeping on the record: PEEKABOO's "All I Need Is Bass" states the thesis outright; Nikita the Wicked's "SHAKE," "DOOM," and "LOUDER," A.M.C's "Nightmare" and "Run!" are titled for physical and visceral effect — verbs and states, not stories. A genre built to be felt in the chest before it is parsed at all is the purest instance of the "lyrics as timbre" pattern on this shelf; even the titles gesture at sensation rather than meaning.

And the overlap that matters: every artist in this cluster operates in the same low-end-forward register as the involuntary sub-bass signature documented across all four of Dan's own aliases (63–85% sub-bass presence, SLOPPP through GRIPNOTIC). The signature was identified as structural — present regardless of stated genre target — which makes this cluster the genre space where his own involuntary sonic fingerprint and the current commercial center of the scene happen to be the same thing. That is more than coincidence: it is calibration listening, keeping the ear tuned to where the genre currently sits in a lane where his baseline sound was already sitting before he set out to match it.

## Continuity with the 2007–2014 Facebook record

The current library isn't a break from earlier taste — it's a direct continuation of it. The Facebook archive ([[wiki/self/facebook]], 2007–2022) shows the same artists surfacing more than a decade earlier: Elliott Smith liked in August 2013 (seven tracks in the current library), Fall Out Boy and Say Anything both name-checked in 2007 status updates ("currently swooning over the new FOB," "in love with the new Say Anything disc"), Radiohead on loop the same year. The electronic side has an equally long tail: 2012–2014 likes include ODESZA, JAUZ, Borgore, Flying Lotus, Diplo, and Zedd, and event/photo data places Dan at Electric Zoo in 2010 and a run of raves and DJ shows from 2014–2019 (High Tide, Freddie Gibbs, United Ravers, Space Jesus). The emo attachment and the bass attachment are both at least eighteen years old by the time the 2024 manual list and the 2025–2026 Spotify feed pick them back up — this library isn't discovering a taste, it's the latest layer of one that's been continuous since adolescence.

## Cultural framing: the rock-irrelevance thesis

Both major clusters here (hip-hop and electronic/bass) sit on the winning side of an explicit argument Dan has made about the current cultural moment: that guitar-driven rock lost its dominant position after 1963 and has never gotten it back. The case — assembled in a ChatGPT session as ammunition against a specific person, built on streaming, market-share, and instrument-sales data — is that rock accounts for ~11% of US streams against hip-hop's ~31% (234B vs 341B streams), that guitar sales have halved in a decade, and that guitar-driven tunes are nearly invisible on current charts while the biggest "rock" acts are catalog assets rather than new breaks. See [[wiki/interests/rock-irrelevance-thesis]] for the full case.

The point isn't nostalgia dressed as contrarianism — it's the opposite claim: that the electronic and hip-hop concentration here represents alignment with where the actual cultural center of gravity sits, not a retreat into a niche. And the thesis connects back to the shelf itself: the rock-irrelevance argument rejects guitar rock as the *dominant* form, and the shelf shows what filled the space it left — hip-hop and electronic/bass, maintained for nearly two decades, now also the production lane.

## September 2026: the taste gets instruments

In September 2026 Dan did something unusual for a listener: he started building machinery to measure his own taste. A 2026-09-22 personal-interest goal commissioned a definitive deep document mapping the taste, preceded by forensics on ~1,900 labeled tracks — 209 keeps, 498 explicit rejects from the November 2025 bulk-like-then-purge, ~1,200 like-only — with ReccoBeats audio, genre, recency, popularity, and artist-familiarity features. The key finding, restated across the record: audio features cannot separate a keep from a like-only skip. The promote decision rides on genre rates — bass and tech house keep at roughly 38%, drum-and-bass and liquid at 8–11% — and session context, not on the sonic fingerprint of the track itself. Taste, measured, turns out to be mostly a genre-and-moment question.

MusicTrainer operationalized it the same month: the week-1 seed (locked 2026-09-14) preloaded all 30 tracks of the September 14 Discover Weekly with per-track locked keep predictions. The eight predicted keeps were Damaio's "En:vy," Keys N Krates' "Double Dutch," Flozone's "Break Up Song," SNBRN's "Six Shooter," Mushroom Cloud and MontyCler's "Combat Mode," DLMT's "One Drink," Malaa's "Diamonds," and spüke's "REMEDY" at 0.39 — the model's highest-confidence keep. The scored result, 2026-09-20: accuracy 0.633, precision 0, recall 0. All eight predicted keeps were wrong. The three actual keeps — Jai Wolf and SAYAK DAS's "Bad Habit," Flava D and Stush's "Energy," Cymek's "Untouchable" — were all missed; the model carried no audio features on them (the blind-fallback group). Five more tracks were liked-but-not-added (atmosphere, "Dreaming," "OPAL," "Janice STFU – Dubstep," and Flozone's "Break Up Song" — like without playlist add). The labels carry a caveat: they assume the current crate is FUNDAMENTAL FLAW, unconfirmed by Dan. The week-1 flop is itself part of the record — the instrument's first real scorecard, kept because misses are evidence too.

September also produced the genre-taxonomy thread, which is worth keeping for what it says about how he listens now. On 2026-09-25 Dan asked Sammy to define stutter house properly, doubting whether the eight tracks he had just sent were actually it. The working definition landed as the Fred again.. blueprint: house/garage beats where the lead instrument is a chopped, glitching vocal sample — fragments of soul or R&B vocals repeating and stuttering, "Marea (We've Lost Dancing)" the textbook, "the stutter is literally the hook." His eight-track Friday list cleared as *not* stutter house. The same session logged Lonelysoul's "BAD 4 ME" as keeper #3 — melodic deep-house-adjacent, straight sung topline, no chop-stutter lead — and self-corrected an earlier "bedroom indie pop" mislabel to dance/electronic. The listener was doing the genre's homework in public, in real time, with the shelf open.

## Current standing (October 2026)

Three positions, in Dan's own words as of 2026-10-04, sharpen where the record stands now rather than reversing it.

**Guitar music is not cool anymore.** His words, stated as a standing update, tightening the rock-irrelevance thesis from a cultural argument into a personal aesthetic rule: the guitar-driven center he grew up adjacent to is done, for him, not just for the charts.

**The drum kit is likewise mostly not cool — with one carve-out.** Same window, his words: drum kits are mostly not cool, *except in live shows*. The exception matters because it keeps the position from being blanket instrumentalism; it's about where a real kit belongs in the cultural moment, not a denial that percussion works on stage. The recorded music he keeps is built on machines.

**Lonelysoul is out.** Also 2026-10-04, his words: he now *hates* Lonelysoul — the act he had four tracks from in his Chaos in October pool — calling them too slow, putting out too much material, and looking like a try-hard or a scammer. This supersedes their multi-track liked-act status outright. The arc is dated and documented both ends: keeper #3 on September 25, gone by October 4, nine days. What changed between the two dates is a matter of his stated judgment, not of the record being wrong the first time — "BAD 4 ME" was what it was on September 25, and the reversal is the new fact.

## Assessment

The record supports a listener whose taste is stable, layered, and instrumented rather than volatile. Eighteen years of continuity between the 2007 Facebook record and the 2026 Spotify feed rule out faddishness; the three clusters are maintained in parallel with distinct functions (confrontational hip-hop as production mapping, the emo cluster as archived attachment, electronic/bass as calibration listening). The September 2026 measurement program — the taste-mapping goal, the MusicTrainer scorecards, the genre-taxonomy homework — is the strongest signal of all: a listener turning his own library into training data to find out what he actually likes, and keeping the misses on the record. The October standing updates sharpen rather than reverse the long arc: guitar and drum kits demoted as a matter of stated taste, one act nine days from keeper to hated. The shelf is not a museum of who he was. It is a working instrument he is still tuning.
