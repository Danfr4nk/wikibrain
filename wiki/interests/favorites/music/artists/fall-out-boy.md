# Fall Out Boy

Chicago pop-punk band, formed 2001, the commercial flagship of the
mid-2000s emo-adjacent scene alongside My Chemical Romance and Panic! at
the Disco. Bassist/lyricist Pete Wentz wrote the words; frontman Patrick
Stump's melodic instincts turned Wentz's dense, self-aware, footnote-heavy
lyric sheets into radio hits without losing the density.

To Dan they are not a "top music list" entry. They are a 2007-era identity
artifact — a band he actively toured to see as a teenager, saw play a
secret show under a fake name, and watched premiere an album on live
television, and then, after the 2009 hiatus, came to regard as a band he
loved only up through 2008 and has effectively disowned since. The affection
is real but frozen: it stops at *Folie à Deux* and does not extend to
anything the reunited band released after 2013.

## The touring years (2005–2008)

Dan followed the band on the ground. The concert record he kept
(in a Facebook "Concerts I've gone to" note, preserved in the corpus)
lists three Fall Out Boy appearances in roughly eleven months of
2007 — one of them the *Infinity On High* release-day TRL taping. The
full researched log pushes the circuit wider, from summer 2005 to
Orlando in December 2008:

| Date | Show | City / Venue | Note |
|------|------|--------------|------|
| Aug 1, 2005 | Vans Warped Tour | Post-Gazette Pavilion, Burgettstown, PA | Lineup included Fall Out Boy, Alexisonfire, Gogol Bordello, Hawthorne Heights. |
| Sep 28 – Nov 23, 2005 | Nintendo Fusion Tour | Various (tour), multiple cities | Confirmed tour window. |
| 2007-01-11 | Fall Out Boy | Cleveland, Agora | Note-only: attested by the Facebook note, absent from the concert master table. |
| 2007-02-06 | Fall Out Boy — **TRL Studios** (*Infinity On High* release-day taping) | New York City | Times Square; same-day free shows also in Chicago & LA. |
| 2007-05-06 | Say Anything (also on the same touring circuit) | Baltimore, Rams Head | — |
| 2007-11-19 | Fall Out Boy **secret show**, billed as "Schrute Farms" | Buffalo, Mohawk Place | ~300-cap sellout under *Office* alias; full *Take This to Your Grave*; Keith Buckley guest on Pantera "Walk". |
| 2008-12-12 | Multi-act show (Fall Out Boy / The Academy Is... / We The Kings) | House of Blues, Orlando, FL | XL 106.7 "Electric Xmas"; corroborated day-of on Twitter. |

The 2007 Facebook status layer backs this up directly: posts from that
year read "swooning over the new FOB," and on Feb 5, the night before the
taping, "in New York City to see Fall Out Boy at TRL." This was not
passive fandom — it was an itinerary. A teenager in southwest Pennsylvania routing himself to
Cleveland, NYC, and Buffalo for the same band inside one year is the
opposite of the "appears on a top artists list" framing the wiki
previously gave this page.

The same 2007 promotional circuit is the dated setting for another
first: a 2017-12-28 tweet — "Smoked my first pack of cigarettes when
I went to this promotional fall out boy show in 2007" — makes this band
the setting for the corpus's only dated nicotine onset, the same year
the health record dates the first opiate (see
[[wiki/health/chemical-architecture]]).

The Feb 6 2007 TRL appearance was the rollout gimmick for *Infinity On
High*: the band taped the Total Request Live broadcast on the album's
release day, part of a 3-city / 3-show release-week stunt. Dan was in the
studio for it — and he is visible in the Getty crowd photograph from the
taping, the kid in the colorful BAPE hood front and center. He identified
himself in the photo on 2026-09-25, via the through-line of his own
pictures from the era: a BAPE shark-hoodie portrait (face covered by the
hood) and fan video from the Buffalo show nine months later showing the
same Baby Milo tattoo ink. He was still engaging the record twelve years later —
"i'm listening to infinity on high and its like a really good album"
(2019-09-27) — but note the title he reaches for is the 2007 album, not
anything post-hiatus.

## The Buffalo secret show

The Nov 19 2007 Buffalo date was a secret show. Fall Out Boy played it
under the alias "Schrute Farms" — a *The Office* reference (see
[[wiki/interests/the-office]]), which tells you something about the
in-joke register Dan was operating in at seventeen. Decades later he
still treated the night as a genuine anomaly worth verifying: on
2025-07-22 he texted "chatgpt just informed me that the secret fall out
boy show i went to is the first and ONLY time that [Take This To Your
Grave] was played in its entirety." Whether or not ChatGPT was right, the
instinct — to treat a 2007 basement show as a unique historical data
point worth running through an LLM — is exactly the forensic-archival
reflex the rest of the wiki documents. He was there; it was the only full
play-through of their debut album ever; he logged it. Fan-shot video of
the Mohawk Place set (circulating as "Secret Fall Out Boy Show 11/19
Buffalo") catches his tattooed forearm reaching toward the stage — the
Baby Milo ink confirming the same BAPE-headed teenager from the TRL
crowd photo nine months earlier, per his own 2026-09-25 identification.

## The borrowed language of 2007

The band supplied more than the soundtrack in 2007 — it supplied the
vocabulary. "The Patron Saint of Liars and Fakes" is a Fall Out Boy song
title, from *Take This to Your Grave* (2003), and Dan wore it as a 2007
persona: a Nov 25, 2007 Facebook status reads "is the patron saint of
liars and fakes," and the same phrase appears in the 2007-groups layer of
the Facebook export. It named the gap between the presented self and the
thing underneath — self-deception as an identity to try on in public —
and it sits in the same status layer as "swooning over the new FOB"
itself, alongside scam-detection groups posts that prefigure the forensic
disposition the rest of the wiki documents. The knowledge base holds a
correction here (see Conflicts in the record): the phrase was once
misattributed on another page as Elliott Smith's self-description and a
2013 handle, both unsupported. The corrected provenance fits better than
the error did — a Fall Out Boy song title used as a persona in the exact
year of the band's touring peak is not a coincidence to explain away; it
is the identity artifact wearing its own source material.

## The December 2008 Orlando tail

Active fandom did not end in Buffalo. The 2008 Twitter record places him
back in the circuit in Orlando — Full Sail years, December, with the
band's orbit intact. Three tweets, three weeks apart, make the run:

- 2008-12-02: "It's like 2005 again. Fobz and panic both in rotation in my car."
- 2008-12-11: "Fall out boy tomorrow. Absofuckinglutley."
- 2008-12-12: "fall out boy/TAI/WTK - @houseofblues. let me know if you're going!"

The Dec 12 show is the XL 106.7 "Electric Xmas" bill at the House of
Blues, Orlando — Fall Out Boy with The Academy Is... and We The Kings —
and the tweets corroborate it day-of and act-for-act. The attendance
timeline now runs roughly twenty-three months, January 2007 to December
2008, not the "roughly eleven months" the page once claimed. And the Dec
2 tweet dates his own nostalgia out loud: "It's like 2005 again" — the
Warped Tour / Nintendo Fusion Tour summer of three years earlier, named
as a loop he is consciously stepping back into.

## The fandom as social world: how he found Ally Lubin

The deepest line the record draws from this band is not musical at
all. On 2026-08-31, in a message to Annie explaining how he came to know
Ally Lubin, Dan wrote:

> "She was quasi online famous among the fall out boy fandom and knew
> them personally / So I knew who she was and followed her for years
> before she ever knew I existed."

Ally Lubin — the eighteen-year attachment he never met in person, the
first outsider to read his wiki — entered his life through the Fall Out
Boy fandom, as someone "quasi online famous" inside it who knew the band
personally. He followed her for years before she ever knew he existed;
he later sent her $2,000 unasked-for "when she sent me pictures back in
the day." The band was the scene, the shared context that made the
attachment legible to both of them — which reframes the fandom from a
taste entry into social infrastructure. The most consequential
relationship of his adult life, measured by duration and by the volume
of the record it generated, is a downstream artifact of being a Fall Out
Boy fan in the mid-2000s. That is the page's strongest claim and it is
made in his own words, quoted verbatim.

## The band as social currency (2018–2019, 2025)

After the cutoff hardened, the band did not disappear — it converted
from identity into currency. Three moments:

**The shared track (2018-08-22).** A sent iMessage is a truncated
Spotify share: "Spot p chgy Starship Suave Suarez Remix) by Fall Out
Boy." The fragment is all that survives of the shared link — a remix
crediting the band, passed to someone as a gesture. The music still
circulated as something to hand another person.

**The Buffalo brag (2019-09-01).** To Annie, a two-text telling of the
secret show twelve years on: "one time i went to a secret show in
buffalo where fall out boy performed as 'schrute farms'," then "it was in
a vfw every time i die four year strong (this was the announcement that
they signed to decaydance) fall out boy." The night had become a story —
and the retelling drifts from the researched record, which has the venue
as Mohawk Place (a ~300-cap sellout), not a VFW hall. The drift is small
and human: a twelve-year-old memory reshaped by the room it was told in.
See Conflicts in the record.

**The keyboard labor (2025-09-10).** "Or learn more fall out boy songs
on the keyboard as though this is impressing the girl who I can not keep
from getting mad at me." FOB songs as courtship labor, performed for
Annie, with the mechanism named out loud — he knows it is impressing
work, he does it anyway. The band he froze at 2008 is, in 2025, still
the material he reaches for when he needs something to play.

## Where the love cuts off

The affection has a hard edge — but the sentence that states it most
plainly in the record is not his. On 2019-09-09 a received message from
(redacted), Annie's 2019-era number, reads: "I can't be a hater because
I love most of the albums but only folie a deux and prior, I'm just being
a brat because I've had an awful day." It arrives one week after his own
two-text Buffalo-brag telling to the same number — the cutoff phrasing is
hers, offered inside an exchange where he was doing the remembering. The
page previously presented the line as his statement; it is not. What *is*
his is the stance, corroborated everywhere else: the favorites list holds
six deep cuts and no post-hiatus material, the concert history stops with
the reunited band untouched, and by 2025 the cutoff had hardened into a
joke of his own — a text listing hypothetical groomsmen ran "a bag of
cocaine, the first 3 fall out boy albums and the ghost of gore vidal"
(2025-05-05), and a separate 2025 message reads "And more frightening is
that I'm listening to old fall out boy and am like 'wow I really get it
now'" (2025-02-17) — "old," not new. *Folie à Deux* (2008) was the band's
last record before their 2009 hiatus. Everything after the 2013 reunion —
*Save Rock and Roll* onward — is absent from his favorites, his concert
history, and his retrospective commentary. The band he loves is the
2001–2008 band. The post-hiatus band is, for his purposes, a different
act he has chosen not to claim.

## In Favorites

Six tracks, all logged under "MUSIC LIST (start-2024)" — a manually
curated list, not a passive Spotify like-feed — and none of them is the
band's actual hit. No "Sugar, We're Goin Down," no "Dance, Dance." What's
here instead: "Tell That Mick He Just Made My List of Things to Do
Today," "Yule Shoot Your Eye Out" (a Christmas EP original), "Thriller" (a
Michael Jackson cover from that same Christmas release), "Our Lawyer Made
Us Change The Name Of The Song So We Wouldn't Get Sued," and "What A
Catch, Donnie."

> **Caveat:** Dan does not process sung lyrics as language while
> listening — words register as timbre, not semantic content (see
> [[wiki/mind/concepts/lyric-qualifier]]). The reading below,
> built around specific lyrical/thematic content, likely reflects
> contextual knowledge of what these songs are about rather than
> in-the-moment lyric comprehension driving the attraction. Treat the
> track-selection pattern (deep cuts over hits, whole-catalog engagement)
> as the solid evidence; treat the "why" as speculative.

## Why the deep cuts, not the hits

This is a completist's list, not a fan's — the kind of selection someone
makes after having already absorbed the singles years earlier and moved
on to the material that only a listener who cared about the *whole band*
would bother with. The title-as-joke habit (two of the six songs here are
built around bits — the lawyer-forced-a-rename premise, the "Tell That
Mick" non-sequitur) is Fall Out Boy's own signature: burying real
sentiment inside a title that's doing standup first, in the same register
as the self-deprecating, gallows-humor idiom already documented across
the rest of the corpus.

"What A Catch, Donnie" is the one worth stopping on. It's a *Folie à
Deux* track built as a mock eulogy for Patrick Stump — a dozen peer-scene
guest vocalists (Panic! at the Disco, Cobra Starship, Gym Class Heroes and
others) singing tributes to a man who isn't dead, framed as if the band
itself is already a legacy act being memorialized. It's a song about a
band writing its own eulogy in advance, sincerely, while still very much
alive — the same "personal myth" structure the wider profile names
directly: **"the Hyperaware Hero of a World Too Stupid to See the Trap,"**
someone who narrates his own downfall before it happens because getting
there first is the only control available. Choosing that song, specifically,
out of an entire discography reads as recognition, not coincidence.

## Where this sits in the cluster

Read alongside [[wiki/interests/favorites/music/artists/my-chemical-romance]],
[[wiki/interests/favorites/music/artists/say-anything]], and
[[wiki/interests/favorites/music/artists/taking-back-sunday]], Fall Out
Boy is the least thematically dark of the four but shares their
underlying habit: mining a genre built entirely on performed
devastation, in-joke self-reference, and mythologized suffering. The
mid-2000s emo/pop-punk era this whole cluster draws from lines up almost
exactly with the "romantic collapse fantasy" imprinting window (2005–2010)
already named as a developmental origin for the broader relational and
sexual pattern documented at
[[wiki/mind/psychosexual/developmental-origins]] — this is speculative
extrapolation, not a claim made anywhere by Dan himself, but the overlap
in both timing and content (grand, doomed, self-narrated intensity) is
hard to read as coincidental.

## Conflicts in the record

**The touring arithmetic (2026-09-09, kb dat:0616).** The page's
2007-07-16 expansion stated the Facebook note "shows three Fall Out Boy
appearances in roughly eleven months, plus the Infinity On High
release-day TRL taping" — but its own table listed exactly three FOB
rows, one of which *was* the TRL taping, implying four shows where three
were listed. Corrected in the 2026-10-05 pass. Current standing: two
table-confirmed 2007 appearances plus one note-only.

**The Cleveland Agora date is note-only (2026-09-09, kb dat:0616).**
The 2007-01-11 Cleveland Agora row exists only in the Facebook "Concerts
I've gone to" note, which this repository does not hold; the 36-row
concert master table carries the Feb 6 TRL and Nov 19 Buffalo shows but
no Cleveland entry. The page also cannot resolve whether the entry
records a show attended or tickets bought and unused — the concert
index's own Twitter cross-check shows the table is a purchase record,
not an attendance record. Current standing: flagged as note-only in the
touring table.

**The "folie a deux and prior" phrasing is Annie's, not Dan's
(2026-10-05).** The 2019-09-09 message ("I can't be a hater because I
love most of the albums but only folie a deux and prior, I'm just being
a brat because I've had an awful day") is a *received* message from
(redacted) — the 2019-era Annie number, corroborated by the
surrounding thread ("Miss u," "thanks daddy," "I got my lips done"). The
page previously presented the line as Dan's cutoff statement. Current
standing: the signature phrasing is hers; the stance itself remains
supported by Dan's own later behavior (the 2025 texts, six deep cuts and
no post-hiatus engagement in Favorites, zero reunited-band shows), and
the phrase arrived one week after his own Buffalo-brag telling to the
same number — it reads as a stance co-produced inside that exchange
rather than a misquote.

**The Buffalo retelling drifts on the venue (2026-10-05).** Dan's
2019-09-01 retelling of the secret show to Annie says "it was in a vfw";
the researched concert record has the venue as Mohawk Place, Buffalo, a
~300-cap sellout. Current standing: a twelve-year retrospective detail
mismatch, recorded as memory drift, not a dispute about whether the show
happened — the TRL/Buffalo identifications of 2026-09-25 rest on the
same record.

**The "patron saint of liars and fakes" provenance (2026-09-09, kb
dat:0572).** Another page (Elliott Smith's) once asserted the phrase was
Smith's self-description lifted as a 2013 Facebook username; both halves
were wrong or unsupported. The phrase is the title of a Fall Out Boy
song from *Take This to Your Grave* (2003); the only dated traces of
Dan's use are 2007 — the Nov 25, 2007 status and the 2007-groups layer.
Current standing: corrected; the phrase now anchors this page's
borrowed-language section, where the provenance belongs.

## Assessment

Fall Out Boy is the archetype case for the freeze-at-boundary signature
generalized on [[wiki/mind/synthesis/music-as-identity]]: affection
locked at *Folie à Deux* (2008) while the object itself continued without
him. But the record also shows the band was never only an aesthetic
preference. It was identity infrastructure in 2007 (the touring
itinerary, the borrowed persona, the in-joke register shared with *The
Office*), social infrastructure for a decade and a half after (the fandom
is the scene where he found Ally Lubin), the setting of the corpus's
only dated nicotine onset, and — past the cutoff — social and courtship
currency, the material he reached for when he needed a story to tell or
a song to play. The stance "only folie a deux and prior" turns out to
have been voiced in the record by Annie, which narrows the claim without
weakening it: his behavior at every later checkpoint agrees. Read whole,
this is the least dark of the emo-cluster pages and the most revealing —
a band that kept paying rent on his life long after he stopped claiming
to be a fan.
