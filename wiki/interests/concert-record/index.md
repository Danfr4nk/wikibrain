# Concert Record (2001-2019)

Eighteen years of evenings, reconstructed from the paperwork one of them left behind. Dan Frank's documented concert history runs from August 4, 2001 — Rolling Rock Town Fair 2, a Stone Temple Pilots / Deftones / Incubus bill at the Westmoreland Fairgrounds — to April 20, 2019, a Freddie Gibbs-headlined 4/20 cruise boarding at 353 West Street in New York. Between those points the record holds thirty-six rows: four Town Fairs, four Vans Warped Tours, two Nintendo Fusion Tour windows, two Bamboozle festivals a decade apart, a Fall Out Boy secret show played under an *Office* alias to three hundred people in Buffalo, and one tweet trail that ended with a kid flying back to Florida early, mid-Christmas, for a 250-capacity room.

The record is honest about what it is, and that is the story. The master table was assembled from ticket stubs, order confirmations and recollection, and a record built that way preserves what was *bought*. On 2026-09-04, ninety-eight days of the twitter archive — September 24 to December 31, 2008, the account's first year — were read against the table: seven Orlando shows named by the feed, only four in the table. Three shows had vanished: The Roots (he posted from inside it), a Cobra Starship package bill, and Trans-Siberian Orchestra — announced at 17:04, dismissed as *"SUPER lame"* at 23:07, never logged. A show somebody else bought the ticket for, a show walked into, or a show remembered as not worth remembering: the log cannot hold any of them, and the feed remembers all three. A concert log is a record of intentions; a contemporaneous feed is a record of evenings.

What survives the cross-check is stronger for it. The two hardest-to-fake rows — the in-joke secret show and a comedy-festival date — both verify cleanly against public records, and the log's May 2008 Meadowlands bill resolves, by public record, to The Bamboozle 2008's Sunday headliner day. The rows that cannot be checked are carried as the log's testimony, at stated confidence, with the open questions named rather than guessed at.

## What this record is

The unit of entry is the **artist**, not the event: a performer Dan saw may have a page under [[wiki/interests/favorites/music/artists]] (or [[wiki/interests/concert-record/performers]] for non-music performers), listing and discussing every date he saw them. Festivals and tours get their own pages under [[wiki/interests/concert-record/festivals]]. The master event list below is the complete record as the log preserves it.

**Not every performer has a page, and the master table below — not the page list — is the complete record.** On 2026-08-29 the operator removed 47 single-appearance artist stubs as unnecessary; each held nothing the master event list and the festival lineups do not already state. The performers are still named in both. See `log.md`.

The stated source of the table, `raw/self/concerts/table.csv`, is not held in the current corpus — the source reference is unresolved. Every row below is therefore relayed through the prior wiki's transcription of that log, checked where checkable against public records, the twitter archive, and the sibling two-source timeline at [[wiki/timeline/events/teen-concert-years]] (the contemporaneous Facebook "Concerts I've gone to" note held open against the later 36-row researched reconstruction, contradictions named rather than resolved by picking a winner). Where rows are single-source, the record says so.

## The Pennsylvania years (2001-2007)

The record opens at the Westmoreland Fairgrounds, and it keeps coming back. Three Rolling Rock Town Fair editions are documented — **2001** (Stone Temple Pilots, Deftones, Incubus, Live, Oleander, Staind, Tantric; Confirmed), **2002** (Alien Ant Farm cancelled after a bus accident; Tommy Lee filled in), and **2004** (heavy rain and mud, no postponement found). The 2002 Tommy Lee substitution is independently repeated on the Outkast and Default performer pages, making it the best-supported single claim in the fair's file. The Mike Cordaro people-entry separately notes he and Dan attended the fair together as kids.

The rest of the amphitheatre circuit is thinner-sourced and the record says so. The **Jul 24, 2002** Barenaked Ladies bill at Post-Gazette Pavilion is single-source — two public web searches found no independent listing of that date, venue and bill, so it stands on the log alone. The **Mar 7, 2003** Face2Face Tour at Mellon Arena is the only indoor-arena Pittsburgh date across all Face2Face years. The John Mayer entry refuses to resolve — three viable Pavilion matches (2003, 2004, 2008), none on a Coldplay bill — and stays a three-way open question rather than a forced answer.

The mid-decade ran on touring packages, all at the same Burgettstown pavilion: four Vans Warped Tours (2005–2008), Coldplay's Twisted Logic Tour with Rilo Kiley (**Aug 11, 2005**), and the Nintendo Fusion Tour's second documented window (**Sep 28 – Nov 23, 2005**) — explicitly a tour window, not a single attended date; the log makes no claim about which 2005 stop he attended. Three rows stay open from this era and are not closed by guessing: The Early November with Ace Enders (~2004), Gym Class Heroes at California University of PA (~2006, no primary campus listing located), and a Taking Back Sunday college-campus show (~2006, no verified campus date beyond the Jun 27 amphitheatre show).

The Pennsylvania years close with the record's most mythologized row: **Nov 19, 2007**, Fall Out Boy at Mohawk Place in Buffalo — a ~300-capacity sellout played under the *Office* alias "Schrute Farms," the full *Take This to Your Grave* front to back, with Every Time I Die's Keith Buckley guesting on a Pantera "Walk" cover. It verifies in every detail against the public record: Blabbermouth's November 26, 2007 report, Concert Archives ("Schrute Farms: Fall Out Boy Secret Show"), and setlist.fm's full-album setlist with the Buckley "Walk." Two months earlier, *Infinity on High* release day (**Feb 6, 2007**) was a free Fall Out Boy show at Times Square (TRL), with same-day free shows in Chicago and LA.

## The Orlando year (2008)

By 2008 the log has moved to Florida. The year's opening show is the record's best public-record join: **May 4, 2008**, six acts at the Meadowlands Sports Complex — Panic! at the Disco and Coheed & Cambria co-headlining, with Paramore, Gym Class Heroes, Cobra Starship and The Academy Is... below them. The log records it only as a multi-act show, but Wikipedia's festival article, NME's bill announcement and setlist.fm's Gym Class Heroes setlist independently identify it: this was The Bamboozle 2008's Sunday headliner day (the festival ran May 3–4; Snoop Dogg and Jimmy Eat World headlined Saturday). It is the strongest validation the log format gets anywhere in the corpus — and an extension, not a correction, since the log's own fields were accurate throughout. Two documented Academy Is... appearances and both documented Paramore appearances fall on this bill and the 2007 Warped Tour.

The Orlando autumn is where the purchase-record thesis was proven — seven shows in the feed, four in the table, three vanished — and the cross-check below tells it in full. The Jack's Mannequin Orlando show stays open: year and market consistent, the exact night unresolved.

The year's last show is the record's best miniature: two December 2008 tweets date the Hey Monday show to **28 December 2008** — *"Be back on the 28th for hey Monday :)"* on the 19th, *"Flying to Orlando for hey monday tomorrow. Wooo"* on the 27th — and the log already held the ticket, filed under a different band: "There for Tomorrow · Dec 28, 2008 · BACKBOOTH · Orlando, FL · Order #M7WKYAHQW." The full join argument, with its inference caveat, is in the cross-check below. Flight of the Conchords at the UCF Arena on **Apr 8, 2009** closes the Florida run, confirmed.

## The New York years (2009-2019)

Two photo-verified 2010 additions sit in the sibling two-source timeline but have never been propagated to this page's master list: Dan attended **Electric Zoo 2010** (Randall's Island, Labor Day weekend) and a **Diplo club set at R Bar NYC (~2010, year approximate)** — his video captures Diplo's tent set at Electric Zoo 2010, a separate Diplo sighting from the R Bar club set — written into the timeline as new rows on 2026-09-25. They are filed here as an open item, not a row, until this index absorbs them. The same write pass photo-corroborated the next entry: **Apr 2, 2011**, Madison Square Garden — billed as the last show ever, reunited 2016. The master list long hedged "(Artist/show identity unrecorded in source)," but the researched record names row 21 outright as LCD Soundsystem's "final" show, and the 2010s period page names the LCD Soundsystem MSG farewell as a dated attendance anchor in the Facebook likes record. Two different instrument classes converge on the same artist for the same dated event.

**Aug 7–9, 2012** was Bloc Party's three-night sold-out *Four* run at Terminal 5 — recalled in the source as "Terminal 9," Dan's own misremembering of the venue name, preserved rather than corrected away. **Aug 30, 2013** is the Oddball Comedy & Curiosity Festival at Post-Gazette Pavilion — Dave Chappelle, Flight of the Conchords, Hannibal Buress, Demetri Martin, John Mulaney, Kristen Schaal on the official itinerary, confirmed to the day. Attendance stands on two legs: the ticket, via the prior wiki's transcription, and the contemporaneous post — read directly in the corpus text, platform-timestamped 22:54 EDT: *"Frogs and Katie Fletcher at #oddballcomedyfest Pittsburgh,"* with a Vine link (the Vine itself is defunct and unrecoverable). The post names the festival, the city, and the companion — what no ticket records. The record's last row is **Apr 20, 2019**: High Tide 4, the Hornblower Infinity Yacht, boarded at 353 West St — the Freddie Gibbs-headlined 4/20 cruise, with Wiki and Kota the Friend on the bill.

## The twitter cross-check — the table is a purchase record, not an attendance record

Added 2026-09-04, from [[wiki/mind/synthesis/twitter-2008]].

Ninety-eight days of the twitter account — 24 September to 31 December 2008, the account's first year — name **seven** Orlando shows. The table holds four of them.

| Date | Show | In the table? | What the tweet adds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 Oct 2008 | The Roots | **No** | *"at the roots show. i can dig that."* — posted 16:29 EDT, from inside it |
| 18 Oct 2008 | All Time Low / The Maine | Yes, row 28 | *"all time lowww/the maine"* — same-day, exact-date corroboration |
| 13 Nov 2008 | Cobra Starship / Hit The Lights / Forever The Sickest Kids | **No** | *"cobras/hit the lights/ftsk"* — the full bill, in his own abbreviations |
| 15 Nov 2008 | Trans-Siberian Orchestra | **No** | *"trans-siberian orchestra tonight."* at 17:04, then *"wow that was SUPER lame."* at 23:07 |

| 12 Dec 2008 | Fall Out Boy / TAI / We The Kings | Yes, row 30 | *"fall out boy/TAI/WTK - @houseofblues"* — the three-act bill, day-of |
| 28 Dec 2008 | Hey Monday (BACKBOOTH) | Row 25, previously undated | see below |

**The three misses are not random, and the reason they are missing is the
point.** This table was assembled from ticket stubs, order confirmations and
recollection — every "Resolved" note in the source cites an order number or a
routing check. A record built that way preserves what was *bought*. It cannot
preserve a show somebody else bought the ticket for, a show walked into, or a
show remembered as not worth remembering — and **Trans-Siberian Orchestra is
the cleanest case in the corpus of the last kind.** He announced it at 17:04
and dismissed it at 23:07, and it left no trace in the record he later
compiled. A concert log is a record of intentions; a contemporaneous feed is a
record of evenings.

> **GAP NARROWED [2026-09-04]:** row 25 read *"Hey Monday · 2008 · Unknown ·*
> *Orlando, FL · Still open — band formed 2008 in West Palm Beach; exact Orlando*
> *show unresolved."* Two tweets date it without touching the billing question:
> **2008-12-19** *"I don't want to go to cold, lame PA. Be back on the 28th for*
> *hey Monday :)"* — posted from Orlando the day he flew home for Christmas —
> and **2008-12-27 19:40 EST** *"Flying to Orlando for hey monday tomorrow.*
> *Wooo"*, followed by *"Hate airport days."* at 10:38 on the 28th. He flew back
> to Florida early, mid-holiday, for this one show. **The date is 28 December**
> **2008.**
>
> **And the table already held the ticket, under a different band.** The log
> files a *"There for Tomorrow · Dec 28, 2008 · BACKBOOTH · Orlando,*
> *FL · 1 General Admission ticket; Order #M7WKYAHQW"* entry — same city, same
> night, a 250-capacity room, and There For Tomorrow were an Orlando act sharing
> the Florida pop-punk circuit Hey Monday had just formed on. **The most
> economical reading is that the Hey Monday entry and the There For Tomorrow
> ticket are one night**, filed twice: once under the band on the ticket and
> once under the band he went for.
>
> The date is settled by the tweets alone and does not depend on that join. The
> join itself is inference — nothing in the corpus states the BACKBOOTH bill —

**What this implies for every other "Still open" row**, and it is not a
suggestion to go and close them by guessing: the twitter archive runs from
September 2008 to 2026, the concert table stops in 2019, and the two have never
been read against each other except for these ninety-eight days. Rows 6, 16,

## Conflicts in the record

**[2026-09-09] The cross-check's row citations did not resolve against the printed master list.** The 2026-09-04 cross-check was written against an earlier row numbering: it cited row 26 for Jedi Mind Tricks (printed row 26 is the May 2008 Meadowlands bill; Jedi Mind Tricks is row 29), row 29 for the Fall Out Boy / TAI / We The Kings bill (row 29 is Jedi Mind Tricks; the FOB bill is row 30), and rows 36 and 40 for the Hey Monday / There For Tomorrow BACKBOOTH ticket (row 36 is High Tide 4, Hey Monday is row 25, and there is no row 40 — the BACKBOOTH ticket entry has no counterpart in the printed list). The tweet evidence itself verifies exactly against the corpus's own twitter-2008 rows, so the thesis is untouched; the citations have been repointed to the printed list in this restructure. Knock-ons applied: the narrowed Hey Monday date (28 December 2008) was propagated into row 25, and the cross-check's closing sweep — which claimed rows 27 and 32 were "undated or unidentified" — was corrected, since row 27 (Jul 29, 2008 Warped Tour) is fully dated and identified and row 32's identity is now resolved.

**[2026-08-02] HIM date contradiction (open).** The master list resolves HIM to Apr 21, 2004 at The Rock Club, Station Square (Love Metal Tour), with a November 18, 2004 return documented. The sibling two-source timeline's contemporaneous Facebook "Concerts I've gone to" note dates the HIM night to 2005-11-09 at Mr. Small's. Both kept; not resolved by picking a winner.

**[2026-08-02] Taking Back Sunday date contradiction (open).** The master list confirms TBS at the Chevrolet Amphitheatre on Jun 27, 2006 (corrected from 2007). The Facebook note dates Taking Back Sunday with Angels & Airwaves to 2006-07-04 in Columbus. Both kept; not resolved by picking a winner.

**[2026-09-04 / corrected 2026-10-06] Hey Monday date settled, bill join still inference.** Two December 2008 tweets date the show to 28 December 2008 independent of any ticket join; the master list previously still read "exact Orlando show unresolved," contradicting the evidence on the same page. Row 25 now carries the settled date. The join to the Dec 28 BACKBOOTH ticket (filed under There For Tomorrow, order #M7WKYAHQW) remains inference — no corpus source states the BACKBOOTH bill — and the There For Tomorrow ticket entry the cross-check cites has no counterpart in the printed 36-row list, which is itself an open item.

**[2026-09-09 / 2026-09-25] "Final Show" artist identity resolved: LCD Soundsystem.** The master list and festival page hedged "(Artist/show identity unrecorded in source)" — true only of the stated source, the concert table CSV.
