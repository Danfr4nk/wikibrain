# The Trap Era (2013–14)

For two years Dan Frank was a trap producer. Not in the sense that a hobbyist dabbles — in the sense that he released roughly thirty named pieces in 2013, ran weekly and summer-long mix series in 2014, entered a Beatport remix contest, posted a public stem-trade address, broadcast live sets on Ustream, and was pitching his demo to Pittsburgh promoters within months. The era has a sound (festival trap and its bootleg economy, worn over an involuntary sub-bass floor that never left his work under any name), two aliases ([[wiki/interests/music/aliases/sloppp]] then [[wiki/interests/music/aliases/mogzart]]), a dated peak (September 2013), a documented high-water year (2014), and a close whose causes are partly on the record and partly not. It is also the single most documented era of his production life — the 2013–14 Twitter archive carries 23.4% and 31.6% of his original posts on his own music — which makes it the one era where the "was he actually doing it" question is answerable, and the answer is yes, for about two years, and then he stopped.

This article treats 2013–14 as the *sound* era: trap and trap-adjacent production under both early aliases. The **name** boundaries don't coincide — SLOPPP runs from mid-2013 to the December 26, 2015 "Goodbye Demo," and MOGZART carries the alias forward into 2016 — so the period's edges get their own treatment below (see [What ended it](#what-ended-it) and [Limits](#limits)). The alias pages carry the discographies; this is the era read whole.

## Before: the failed New York and the room it left

The trap era does not start in a studio. It starts in the wreckage of the first New York attempt (2010–2013): the [[wiki/work/creative-license]] exit over a paycheck/W-2 dispute, the ishlab stint (2010–11), and the May 2013 return to Uniontown that opens [[wiki/timeline/periods/uniontown-return-2013-2015]]. The only first-person statement of the return anywhere in the record is from July 12, 2013, to [[wiki/people/ej-rags]]: *"i just moved back from NYC and i'm super output recently."* That's the era's opening line — not a manifesto, a relocation notice with the output already running.

The production seed is older than the return. [[wiki/people/jason-bermejo]] introduced him to Kid Cudi and to DIY production at [[wiki/timeline/periods/full-sail-2008-2010]]; the Recording Arts degree (graduated September 2009, top 5%, Pro Tools HD certified) supplied the engineering literacy. [[wiki/timeline/events/franki-fireworks-day-2013]] dates the production identity's physical origin: a DJ controller acquired at ishlab and never touched, picked up in the depressive interval after the failed New York attempt, and a daily occupation by the summer of 2013. Then, on November 10, 2013, the hardware event: the Numark NS7 — bought at his asking on top of [[wiki/people/fran-coldren]]'s annual cheque, the grandmother-gifted controller that seeded the whole production identity and, per the sub-bass analysis, likely trained the ear the signature rides on.

So the era's prehistory is five years of preparation — Full Sail, the NYC studio jobs, the untouched controller — compressed into one explosive year. The Harlem Shake year, at that: trap saturating festival culture, SoundCloud ascending as the producer hub, bootleg culture peaking. He arrived at exactly the genre moment that wanted him.

## The sound: trap as costume

The trap era's sound is best read through the taste-forensics doctrine the 2025 MASTER CRATE made measurable, because the crate finally put numbers on things the 2013–14 work was doing by instinct. Four measurements carry the argument.

**The energy floor.** The crate's primary filter is energy ≥ ~0.70, not genre (first-quartile energy 0.704, average 0.80). The 2025 export spans 30+ genre labels — dubstep, bass music, bass house, tech house, riddim, house, g-house, drum and bass, stutter house, UK garage — and the genre coherence dissolves the moment you drop the energy floor. Read backward onto the trap era, this says trap was never the *point*; it was the garment. In 2013–14, festival trap was simply the highest-energy, most culturally available costume in the room — the form that let a Uniontown bedroom producer participate in the dominant electronic conversation of the year. [[wiki/mind/synthesis/alias-as-periodization]] says the same thing about the names: the alias is a clock, not a style. The genre-as-costume doctrine says the same thing about the genre.

**The three BPM rooms.** The crate is bimodal-by-design in tempo: a 125–145 house corridor (146 tracks), a 165+ DnB wing (19 tracks), and a 15-track halftime basement. Trap, as a form, lives precisely in that halftime basement — the 70–85 BPM half-time feel over 140-ish BPM drums. The era's sound was the sub-100 room: 808s stretched over half-time hats, drop structures built for festival hands, twerk-adjacent tempos. The 2026 MOGZART revival lands in the 165+ wing (jump-up DnB), not the halftime basement, which is one way the revival differs from the era it cites.

**The voice as texture.** 96.1% of the crate carries no foregrounded vocal performance; the largest segment (47.4%) is voice-as-texture — chops and samples, not singers. [[wiki/mind/concepts/lyric-qualifier]] documents the underlying perceptual fact: he states he does not process sung lyrics as language; words register as timbre. Across thirteen years and four aliases the catalog is remixes, bootlegs, instrumentals and spoken AI drops, and never a sung original lyric. The trap era obeyed this from the start — the bootleg method (Baauer, Katy Perry, Drake x Cashmere Cat, 2 Chainz) works precisely because the vocal is raw material to be cut and pitched, never a message to deliver. This is also why the remixes of 2014 read as *reworks* rather than covers: the source track's cultural charge is the hook, and the voice is clay.

**The sub-bass floor.** The 63–85% sub-bass presence measured across every alias is already present in the 2013 SLOPPP material — the earliest confirmation in the corpus that the signature is architectural, not chosen. It predates the first rename and cannot have been adopted under any later name. Trap, as a genre, is a sub-bass delivery system; it was the costume that fit the skeleton he already had. The 2025 crate's genre-sprawl-around-an-energy-floor is the same architecture twelve years later: the floor never moved, only the clothes changed.

So: what the trap era sounded like, in one sentence, is a twenty-four-year-old's involuntary sub-bass obsession wearing the dominant festival genre of 2013, with voices cut into texture and bootlegged cultural objects as the source material. The darkness was already there too — the crate's median valence 0.322 (75% at or below neutral) has its 2013 counterpart in titles like *BULLET SVNDWICH* and *helterskelter: race riot music*.

## The names: SLOPPP, MOGZART, and what they don't tell us

SLOPPP was the first named production alias, run from `soundcloud.com/sloppp`. MOGZART followed (approximately 2014, on the May account rotation the migration analysis flags), becoming the primary identity as SLOPPP wound down, then closed in 2016 and revived March 4, 2026. The two names bracket the era the way the alias-as-clock thesis says names always do: as period markers, not style shifts. The sonic identity — sub-bass floor, voice-as-texture, bootleg method — does not turn over at the rename; only the period does.

The etymologies are undocumented. Neither name's origin is stated anywhere in the corpus — no tweet, no message, no caption explains what "SLOPPP" or "MOGZART" means, and the article does not guess. What *is* documented is the rework brand inside the first name: "SLOPPPED N SCREWED" (e.g. the December 2013 *2 Chainz – Netflix ft. Fergie* rework), riffing on chopped-and-screwed convention, and the pun-on-a-cultural-object titling habit — *Betty Draper*, *A BRIEF HISTORY OF TIME AND BASS*, *The Diary of Dan Frank* — that [[wiki/interests/the-office]] documents running on television too. The name might be a slurred joke; the record is silent; the rework brand is the closest thing to a definition the era leaves.

## 2013: the burst

The alias emerges in mid-2013 — undatable precisely, because the Twitter archive's source changes on August 17, 2013, and everything before is invisible. From that date to December 26, 2013, the record carries roughly thirty named SLOPPP releases. But the shape of the year is not even: it has a summit.

**September 2013 is the actual peak.** Sixteen distinct titles in thirty days — *THE KEEPSAKE FOR PETE'S SAKE MIX*, *THE GREENER GRASS MIX*, *All Done*, *SLOPPPED AND SCREWED: HOODIE WEATHER MIX*, *TUNE IN, DROP OUT, TURN UP*, *Blazin*, *RACK TO THE FUTURE*, *Snailed It*, *CUFFED AND CRUISED*, *BULLET SVNDWICH*, *THE LOW-END MIX*, *'THINK ABOUT IT! THURSDAYS'*, *Backwards*, *The Diary of Dan Frank*, *Betty Draper* and *helterskelter: race riot music*. Three of them on September 28 alone. The Facebook-derived discography records one release in that month; the Twitter archive carries sixteen. This is not a hobby posting cadence. It is closer to a release schedule, run by a twenty-four-year-old with no label, no audience to speak of, and no income from it.

The December 2013 close is a rework burst — *2 Chainz – Netflix ft. Fergie (SLOPPPED N SCREWED Rework)*, *Baauer – Good 2 U (SLOPPP Loc'd Out Rethink)* — framed in the Twitter record as "reclaiming producer identity amid 2013's trap saturation," with the year closed by *"Wrapped up for 2013. Head to my soundcloud for mega turn up."* The year-mix goes out as *$ⱢØⱣⱣⱣ - ΫЄ∆ЯMỊX 2013 (Ᵽ∆Я₮ 1)* in substituted Unicode glyphs — a deliberate act of making a title harder to read and search, which is worth noting because it is the opposite of promotion.

And at the exact center of the most productive month in the project's life, September 23, 2013, announcing a mix and an original in the same post, the self-undercutting arrives:

> *"New mix is a go, another original going up tonight. #overcompensating #poseur #fakefuture"*

Three hashtags, all self-accusations, attached to the announcement rather than to anything else. It is the same move as *"as a 'nobody' DJ"* in April 2014, posted in the month he was entering a Beatport contest. [[wiki/mind/synthesis/failure-to-launch]] should read this era in that light: the output was real, sustained and prolific, and the self-assessment attached to it was already a verdict. The verdict preceded the death of the era by two years.

## 2014: the operating practice

2014 is the year the alias becomes an operation rather than an identity — and it is the strongest counter-evidence in the corpus to the claim that the production life was only ever a self-concept. Read rather than pattern-matched (see `skills/corpus/vocabulary-drift.md`), the year shows:

- **A release cadence.** Roughly monthly output across remixes, originals and mixes, including the 2014-only run: *RNDM VADER – #FWTB (Absolute Zero Festival Trap Remix)*, *SAYMYNAME – Pew Pew Pew (Frostbite Me Refix)*, *heRobust – Get Busted*, *Katy Perry ft. Juicy J – Dark Horse*, *Tuff – Your Parents Hate Trvping*, *gLAdiator – Assembly Line (Snazzy Bounce Edit)*, *Drake x Cashmere Cat – No Lie ft. 2 Chainz (Bootleg)*, *Caked Up – Bang Your Head (Festival Trap Remix)*, *Zeds Dead – Collapse (Reflip)*, and originals *Buffalo*, *Lazarus*, *Braindrops Are Falling on My Head*.
- **Two running series** — *SLOPPP's Week in Trap* (at least eight episodes; Episode 3 is dated February 21, 2014, Episode 8 April 8) and the *Heatwave* summer mixes (Vol. 1 announced May 13, 2014 — *"1 hour of tweriffik plurntastic tracks"* — Vol. 2 July 26), plus the monthly *Wax, Stacks and Racks* wrap-up format.
- **Competition.** The *Major Lazer – Aerosol Can (SLOPPP VIP Beatport Mix)* went into Beatport contest voting; he canvassed for votes twice, April 22 and 29.
- **Public collaboration solicitation, with a business address.** May 8, 2014: *"Lets collab! danfr4nk@icloud.com for stems and patch trades."* Tracks emailed to @SirCalvinSwish, offered to fellow DJs — *"lets collab soon, I have tracks for you to scoop."* Splice signup for collaborative production (August 5). Stems requested from @weareGTA.
- **Broadcasting.** Ustream sessions in November and December (*"Turn up is live. 4am twerk life"*, December 8) and the TRAP TV video mix (November 15, streamed to YouTube).
- **Named peer recognition.** Thanking @cobrakrames and @whatdirt for support, and a shout-out from Lil Debbie (May 17).
- **The booking attempts.** Three documented: [[wiki/people/zachariah-harshman]]'s January 2014 pitch to a Pittsburgh rave-scene contact; Dan's own July 6, 2014 outreach to Pittsburgh DJ **Phil Lacher** ("*I asked some friends about getting DJ slots in and around Pittsburgh. They all pointed me your way... I spin a lot of trap, deep house*") — referred onward to Brandon Hooven, no further trace; and the March 2015 Frank Swaney near-management deal (below).

And the self-assessment that comes with it, April 25, 2014, on the Steve Aoki pre-recorded-set scandal: *"as a 'nobody' DJ, the aoki scandal is extra annoying. this is the kind nonsense that allows for EDM to be disregarded"* — followed by the argument that playing a pre-recorded set is *"vile if the only other performance you're offering is spraying drinks on the hornswaggled."* That is a working practitioner's complaint about professional standards, made by someone placing himself at the bottom of the field without pretending he is anywhere else.

What this does and does not change: it does not make SLOPPP a career — nothing here shows income, bookings beyond the undated gigs, or an audience of any size. What it removes is the reading that the production identity was *only* a self-concept. For at least one year it was a practice with output, distribution, a release cadence, competitive entry, outward solicitation and named peer recognition. The failure it illustrates is sharper than "he never really did it," and less flattering: **he did do it, for about a year, and then stopped.** Also on the record that year: *"So many hours DJing that I need daily inversion table therapy"* (May 17) and a hand-painted MIDI controller (December 1) — the physical ledger of the practice.

## The MOGZART handoff

Around 2014, on the May account rotation, the primary identity shifts from SLOPPP to MOGZART — bass house / trap-adjacent, described in the alias page as more stylistically refined and less exploratory than SLOPPP. The two names overlap into 2015–16; SLOPPP closes with the "Goodbye Demo" on December 26, 2015 while MOGZART carries the identity into 2016 (the Juicy J bootleg with its 1,661 plays on zero promotion, the Uffie flip, the MOGGED UP crossover imprint).

The handoff matters to this article for one reason: the sound does not change at it. The 63–85% sub-bass band, the bootleg/flip methodology, the never-sung instrumental catalog — all of it crosses the rename intact. The rename marks a period boundary (the [[wiki/timeline/periods/uniontown-return-2013-2015]] years closing out, the Annie relationship opening), not a sonic one. Which is the period boundary this article's 2014 date_range chooses: the trap-as-costume years specifically, before the costume changed.

## What ended it

The honest version first: **there is no documented statement anywhere in the corpus of a decision to end the trap era.** No "I'm done with trap," no announcement, no postmortem. The close is reconstructible only from the negative space — what stops arriving. That is the load-bearing caveat for everything that follows.

What the record does show, in order:

1. **Through 2015 the production signals weaken into gaming and consumption.** Twitter shows the SLOPPP shares thinning to remnant posts and demos while gaming content rises. The alias's energy drains before the alias closes.

2. **The near-management deal dies silently.** March 15–16, 2015: **Frank Swaney**, a Pittsburgh-scene promoter claiming a nine-DJ roster and teaching at "Spawn Academy in Portland Oregon," hears the *Spring 2015 Demo* (shared via SoundCloud, initially broken because Dan had left it set to private), has fellow DJ "Subterranean" (John Hanson) review it — relaying specific praise for "quick mixing, a good mix of electronic and hip hop elements, and some good track selections" — and pitches full representation: a flat management fee covering roughly four months of promotion (500 demo CDs, 3,000 stickers, a street team), two tentative bookings named directly (an "Illuminati" event and a "Homegrown" show in Braddock, PA), and a plan to grow Dan from promoted artist to signed act. Dan's response is unguarded: *"i'm abysmal with the networking end of this, but i fucking LOVE the process,"* and *"you could tell me i'm playing at 1 and i'd be out the fucking door."* The contract terms were to be finalized through a third party, "Joby," talking to Dan's parents — the only documented instance of his parents being brought into a professional negotiation over his music career. No further trace of the deal, the contract, or either booking survives. SLOPPP closes with the "Goodbye Demo" nine months later. Whether the Swaney deal died of neglect, of the Joby-parent channel, or of Dan's own self-assessed networking abysmalness is undocumented.

3. **The handover of the top happiness slot.** The corpus's only ranked comparison of happiness sources dates this directly. December 9, 2015, at the very start of the Annie relationship: *"Ruin the only thing that's ever made me happy"* — referring to making music. April 3, 2016: *"You're the only thing that's ever made me happier than making music."* Between those two messages, on December 26, 2015, the "Goodbye Demo" goes up. The era's final release lands in the exact window where, on his own accounting, the thing the music used to be for him got displaced by exactly one person. [[wiki/mind/synthesis/music-as-identity]] reads this as the strongest evidence that music was a primary structure of self rather than a hobby — and the close of the era reads, in that light, as the structure being set down rather than the costume wearing out.

4. **The genre moved on.** Festival trap's 2013 saturation peak was not going to repeat; the commercial wave crest passed and the bootleg economy it fed thinned with it. This is circumstantial — the corpus never states it — but the costume doctrine implies it: when the highest-energy costume in the room changes, the costume changes. MOGZART's bass-house direction is the next garment.

Then the silence, measured exactly: the last public post of his own music of any kind under his own hand is March 7, 2016 (a SLOPPP link shared to a correspondent, pointing at existing catalog, not new output). From there, nothing — no originals about his own music from 2016 to 2026 across 908 original posts — until the MOGZART revival on March 4, 2026. A ten-year gap, with the trap era as the thing the gap opens on.

## The mirror: 2025–26

The re-entry is the era's mirror, and the numbers make the comparison possible in a way 2013 never allowed. The **2025 MASTER CRATE** — a Spotify playlist export curated by `dfrank88`, 232 tracks, most additions logged October 31 through December 22, 2025 — is the first quantified, dated picture of what he is actually mixing. It is 59% 2025 releases (70.7% from 2024–25), assembled in a seven-week burst — the same burst shape as September 2013, only this time the burst is curation rather than production. The analysis's verdict: *"The crate has no canon-building function — it's ammunition."* The 2013 burst was also ammunition — free downloads, tracklisted, timestamped, ~10k self-reported SoundCloud plays, criticism solicited from one named peer.

Compare the architectures: the crate runs energy 0.80 average over 30+ genre labels with no label or artist loyalty (162 labels, 321 artists, max 6 tracks each; no single artist dominating; Fred again.. leads at 6 tracks) — the trap era ran one genre label over an involuntary sub-bass floor with no audience to speak of and bootlegs of the biggest names in the room. Both are wide, current-scene samplings. Both select from listening without being identical to it (42.3% of crate tracks appear in his Liked-songs log; 0% in the historical 5321-track dump — the pipeline is hear → Like → promote if dancefloor-functional; the 2013 equivalent was hear the trap saturation → flip it → post it). Both skew dark (median valence 0.322 in 2025; *BULLET SVNDWICH* in 2013).

The differences are where the mirror shows the change: the 2026 revival lands in jump-up DnB (the 165+ wing) rather than the halftime basement; the brand has consolidated to GRIPNOTIC with MOGZART running in parallel rather than superseding; and the output type has evolved from bootleg remixes to remixes plus AI voice drops (per the CATO bootloader, with the self-deprecating tagline "made with love and autism in his mom's basement"). The ten-year silence between March 7, 2016 and March 4, 2026 is the longest gap in the production record — and the revival, posted under the trap era's own successor alias, is the era's name outliving the era's sound.

## The obsession-lifecycle reading

The wiki's recurring shape for his deep engagements — **immersion → escalation → monument → death**, first read off the 2018 spending ledger ("the ledger has a narrative shape: immersion, escalation, monument. The monuments are all December") — fits the trap era with unusual precision, and the era may be its cleanest instance. Immersion: the Full Sail training, the NYC studio jobs, the untouched ishlab controller waiting through the depressive interval. Escalation: mid-2013 to the September peak, sixteen titles in thirty days, the practice becoming an operation in 2014 — the contest, the series, the collab address, the Ustream broadcasts, the booking pitches. The monument: the March 2015 Swaney deal, the closest the era came to becoming an enterprise — management, CDs, stickers, street team, named bookings. And the death: the "Goodbye Demo," December 26, 2015, nine months after the monument arrived and went nowhere, four weeks into the relationship that displaced the music's top slot.

The lifecycle reading also sharpens the era's verdict against [[wiki/mind/synthesis/failure-to-launch]]: this was not an unfinished attempt but a *completed* one — the author had already filed the verdict against it in his own hashtags two years before the close. #overcompensating #poseur #fakefuture, posted at the peak of the most productive month, is the lifecycle's self-awareness running ahead of the lifecycle's end. The 2025–26 revival is the doctrine's sequel section: the monument phase, a decade later, on a new costume.

- **What ended the era (LOW — and the record says so).** No documented decision exists. The contributory sequence (signal decay through 2015, the silent death of the Swaney deal, the Annie happiness-slot handover, the genre's commercial crest passing) is documented; which of these was causal is not.
- **The alias etymologies (undocumented).** Neither name's origin appears anywhere in the corpus. Stated, not guessed.

## Limits

This article does not know: the SLOPPP/MOGZART name origins (undocumented); the SoundCloud upload dates, play counts, or whether the pages are still live (no metadata in raw/); what the 2013 material before August 17 was (invisible to the archive); why the Frank Swaney deal died (undocumented); whether the March 7, 2016 catalog link implies any later private production (undocumented — the record is silent, not negative); and whether the 2014 costume-shift was a conscious genre decision or drift (no statement exists). The era's *sound* boundary at December 2014 is editorial — the name SLOPPP runs fourteen months further, and the trap-adjacent MOGZART material bleeds across the line; the boundary marks the costume, not the clock. The NYC years are the era's prehistory, not its location — the production happened in Uniontown. Verbatim quotes are labeled as such; everything else is the author's synthesis of the alias pages, the Twitter/Facebook archives, and the 2025 taste-forensics.
