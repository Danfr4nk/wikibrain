# Golf

Golf runs through three generations of the family and both ends of Dan's relationship with [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] — he met her on a golf course at 2 AM in the rain, and a bitter callback to walking courses together closes out the relationship's final year. In between, it was his actual sport: a real, moderately skilled amateur game (occasionally breaking 90), a four-year caddying career at three elite regional clubs that doubled as his main income during the hardest financial years, and a running attempt — never fully successful — to get Annie to share it with him.

## The family lineage

Golf at this level of the family predates Dan by decades. His great-grandmother [[wiki/people/fran-coldren|Fran]] was, per the record on her own page, genuine western-Pennsylvania golf royalty: **two documented holes-in-one** — 1959 at LaGorce Country Club in Miami Beach, then 1969 at the Uniontown Country Club (the 153-yard No. 11, struck with a No. 5 wood) — the **UCC Women's Golf Crown** (342 over 72 holes, won by seven strokes), a 200 game at Manor Lanes, and family photographs placing her beside Arnold Palmer's private plane — she was socially close with Palmer himself. She lived on the UCC fairway for over fifty years, at [[wiki/places/117-belmont-circle]], literally a "golf course house." That fairway is also where, on the night of **November 29, 2015**, at roughly 2 AM in the rain, Dan met Annie — the single most consequential event in the whole message corpus, and it happened on the same course his great-grandmother had dominated for half a century (see [[wiki/timeline/periods/2015-2016-annie-relationship-start]]).

The lineage matters because it explains the access. Dan didn't discover golf as an outsider sport — he inherited the terrain. The family rented and then owned at Seven Springs for the ski years; the UCC fairway was family ground for half a century before he ever played it. Golf in this record is not an adopted hobby. It is the family sport, skipped a generation in its serious form, and returned to through employment.

## Dan's own game

Dan plays, and by his own account is a competent but unspectacular amateur — *"That was a fluke today tho. Idk how I played as well as I did...and I still probably barely broke 90"* (May 9, 2018) is as good as his self-reported scores get; a June 2017 message logs a more ordinary *"Got a par 4 and then par 3 to finish."* He studies the game seriously rather than just playing casually: in June 2016 he watched a full broadcast of the 84 Lumber Classic specifically "to better understand course strategy for my low handicap loops" — treating tournament golf as professional development for the caddying job, not just entertainment. His closest documented brush with real skill is a near-miss rather than a triumph: on **November 5, 2017** — his birthday — playing as "the last player of the season" on Mystic Rock, he came "2 feet from an ace on 17 which is the last hole today."

The score record is thin but consistent: a player good enough to break 90 on a good day, self-aware enough to call the good days flukes, and engaged enough to study course strategy for professional reasons. There is no junior-golf origin story anywhere in the corpus — the record picks the sport up already in progress by the time messaging begins in 2015 — and no formal handicap is documented. Whether he played growing up inside the family's country-club world or took it up as an adult is unestablished (see Conflicts).

His own course criticism runs through the record with the same forensic register he brings to everything else. On **April 23, 2018**, comparing the two home tracks: *"honestly i find it more playable than mystic...but if you were playing for score rather than fun, i'd make a mystic rock ..."* — Shepherd's Rock the friendlier play, Mystic Rock the sterner test. And his coinage for Mystic Rock — *"widely considered to be 'The Wire' of Mid-Atlantic golf"* (May 12, 2018) — is the kind of canon-comparison he reserves for things he takes seriously. The fee structure is its own running metric: *"Haha bro $200 for 36 is a California 10 for mystic rock loops"* — loop fees as the yardstick every other number is measured against.

## The playing partners

Regular playing partners across the record include his caddying boss **Brad** (a $150 straight-stroke-play bet at Pikewood that ended with Brad drunkenly snapping a club and standing Dan up), and NHL forward [[wiki/people/christian-hanson|Christian Hanson]], a Nemacolin regular Dan both caddied for and played rounds with through 2017–2018 (full account on Christian's own page). **Jack Rusko** surfaces as a golf-adjacent friend of the era: *"say jack rusko and i went up to mystic rock to get our paperwork"* (March 9, 2018) — the season's administrative errands doubling as the social fabric.

He also floated bringing Annie into actual play rather than just spectating — *"I don't know let's take her Togo play nine holes"* (May 2018) and, more bluntly, *"I spend all this fucking money to try to get you excit[ed] about actually playing golf"* (June 2018) — and tried to get her out on the course directly (*"How about playing golf with Annie and I on the new course tonight,"* Aug 2018). The record suggests it never fully took: a 2020 message elsewhere shows Dan trying, without success, to get someone else to "come walk the golf course with me." Golf was his; it was never theirs, despite a decade of trying.

The Danny Matthews overlap adds a social layer the main Danny page dates later. On **July 3, 2017**, someone is *"getting ready to go play nine holes out Duck Hollow with Ted Anderson, Danny Matthews, and Shelley Johns.. grabbing dinner after"* — Dan playing a casual round with the future arrangement's third party seven months before the February 2018 thread that opens the documented record. And on **May 16, 2018**: *"One of the caddies we were training said 'you guys know Danny Matthews? That dude can hit the ball 400 Fucking yards'"* — Danny circulating in the yard's oral tradition as a physical specimen. Golf is the stratum in which the Uniontown male friendships and the arrangement's personnel overlap; the same men appear on both registers.

## The two home tracks

Nemacolin's two courses are not interchangeable in the record, and Dan's own criticism draws the distinction sharply.

**Mystic Rock** is the stern test and the object of reverence. It is the course he calls *"top notch and really hard"* in the first month of the job, the course of the birthday near-ace (*"2 feet from an ace on 17 which is the last hole today,"* November 5, 2017, *"the last player of the season"*), and the course that gets the canon-comparison: *"One more eval card and I can play THE mystic rock, widely considered to be 'The Wire' of Mid-Atlantic golf."* The eval-card system — playing privileges gated behind the resort's own credential ladder — is part of what makes Mystic Rock the professional object: it is earned access, not just a tee time. His fee metric runs through it too: *"Haha bro $200 for 36 is a California 10 for mystic rock loops"* — the loop fees as the yardstick every other number in his financial life is measured against.

**Shepherd's Rock** is the newer, friendlier play. The Post-Gazette's April 2017 writeup of Pete Dye's new Nemacolin course entered the corpus the week it opened (*"New-golf-course-Nemacolin-Woodlands-Shepherd-s-Rock-Pete-Dye"*), and by July Dan was up early for practice on it — *"hey dude, i came up here super early to hit some balls and try to pull off a miracle with a shepherd's rock ..."* — and getting others on: *"i got us on shepherd's."* His own comparison, April 2018: *"honestly i find it more playable than mystic...but if you were playing for score rather than fun, i'd make a mystic rock ..."* — Shepherd's the round you enjoy, Mystic the round you post. The distinction is a player's distinction, not a tourist's: playable versus scoring, fun versus test, and the employee access (*"we can play mystic rock and shepherds rock for free whenever u want,"* Feb 2018) as the social currency that made both courses his to offer.

The two-course structure matters because it is the material base of the whole golf era. The $15 employee rounds, the free access offered to friends, the eval cards, the early-morning practice — this is what it looks like when a luxury resort's golf operation becomes someone's actual life rather than their occasional recreation. The courses are not where he played. They are where he worked, studied, socialized, and measured himself, for four years.

## Golf as craft, not just play

The 84 Lumber Classic detail deserves more than the passing mention it usually gets. Watching a full tournament broadcast *"to better understand course strategy for my low handicap loops"* is not fandom — it is professional development, and it is the same move he makes everywhere else in the record: study the system, extract the strategy layer, apply it to the work. The caddying job supplied the motive; the broadcast supplied the curriculum; the loops were the exam.

The same craft register runs through the equipment and the study habits the record preserves in fragments: the borrowed golf shoes at the interview (starting without the uniform), the eval-card ladder worked deliberately (*"One more eval card"*), the transfer conversation with Carl framed as *"a much more beneficial [career] situation and a natural progression"* — the vocabulary of someone building a career, not killing time. Golf is one of the few interests in the record that Dan pursued with professional seriousness rather than dilettantism, and the four-year caddying career is the evidence. The sport was the job, the study, and the game, all at once — the [[wiki/mind/synthesis/interests-as-era-markers|interest that literally was the era]].

## The caddying career (2016–2019)

Getting hired at Nemacolin in April 2016 didn't just give Dan an income — it visibly reignited his own interest in playing: *"I really really dig it. Spending all day on the golf course is a blast and it's getting me back into playing in a big way. Also mystic rock is top notch and really hard"* (May 11, 2016), written days into the job, with an employee discount making rounds on Nemacolin's two courses — **Mystic Rock** and **Shepherd's Rock** — cheap enough to actually use (*"We can play mystic rock for $15 with my employee discount"*). The caddying itself ran across three elite regional clubs — Nemacolin, Laurel Valley Golf Club, and Pikewood National — and is documented in full detail, including the caddie-hierarchy politics, the pairing with caddie master Carl (also head of the TPC Scottsdale program), and its overlap with [[wiki/people/fran-coldren|Fran]]'s hospitalization and death, at [[wiki/work/nemacolin-caddying]]. The job years (2016–2019) run concurrent with the hardest financial stretch documented elsewhere in the wiki, making golf simultaneously his sport, his social world, and his primary paycheck for most of his twenties.

The professional-development register is worth isolating because it distinguishes this from a casual interest. The 84 Lumber Classic broadcast (June 2016) watched *"to better understand course strategy for my low handicap loops"* is someone treating the sport as a craft. The eval-card system — *"One more eval card and I can play THE mystic rock"* — is someone working a credential ladder inside the resort's own hierarchy. And the Scottsdale transfer he raised with Carl is someone who saw the yard as a career, not a stopgap. Golf, for four years, was not the hobby alongside the job. It was the job, the study, and the game, all at once — the [[wiki/mind/synthesis/interests-as-era-markers|interest that literally was the era]].

## Golf as relationship touchstone

Golf's role in the Annie relationship doesn't end with how they met — it recurs as a marker of the relationship's emotional temperature for a decade afterward. In April 2020, mid-pandemic, a friend's reaction to seeing Dan's Instagram (*"LOOK HE PLAYS GOLF TOO!!"*) shows it was still a visible part of his public identity years after he'd stopped caddying professionally. That August, missing a round near his mother's birthday reads as a small, wistful regret rather than a major event. By **December 13, 2025** — deep in the relationship's terminal decline (see [[wiki/timeline/periods/dec-2025-spike]]) — golf resurfaces as something much sharper: Annie tells Dan *"you would just have to walk out your door, i wouldn't make you go out on the golf course like when [we] used to, when you ACTUALLY liked me"* — invoking walking golf courses together as a specific, named memory of when the relationship was good, thrown back at him as evidence of how far it had fallen. The full arc closes a loop that opened on a golf course in the rain in 2015: golf as the site of the relationship's best-documented origin moment becomes, a decade later, the site of its own eulogy.

The eulogy register is precise. She doesn't say "when we were happy" — she says "when you ACTUALLY liked me," and the evidence she cites is that he used to make her go out on the golf course. The decade of trying to get her to play — the money spent, the invitations, the nine holes at Togo — is re-read in the terminal phase as the proof of a love that has since been withdrawn. Golf becomes the relationship's own named good era, and the accusation is that he stopped bringing her into it. Whether the accusation is fair is unestablished; that golf is the vocabulary the accusation is stated in is documented.

## The later years

Whether he golfed at all between the 2019 end of the caddying job and the 2020 Instagram/nostalgia references, or after 2020, is not documented in detail. What the record does show is the interest persisting as media consumption and self-image: 2025's only golf reference is a *"GOLF themed YouTube channel"* he watches, and a self-deprecating story about avoiding polo shirts since being told he *"dressed like a golfer's little brother."*

The polo-shirt story is doing more work than it looks like. "Dressed like a golfer's little brother" is an identity read delivered by someone else — the golf identity is legible to others even when he isn't playing, to the point of being teased — and his response (avoiding the shirts) is the self-conscious management of a legible identity rather than its abandonment. The sport recedes from activity to identity: still part of how he is read by others (*"LOOK HE PLAYS GOLF TOO!!"*), still part of how he reads himself, no longer something the corpus catches him doing. The YouTube channel is the same pattern in media form — the interest maintained as consumption when the conditions for participation (the job, the employee access, the yard) are gone.

And the decade of trying to get Annie to play never resolves. The May 2018 *"let's take her Togo play nine holes,"* the June 2018 *"I spend all this fucking money to try to get you excit[ed] about actually playing golf,"* the August 2018 *"How about playing golf with Annie and I on the new course tonight"* — each one is a bid, and the record never shows one accepted. By December 2025 she is the one invoking the golf course, but as evidence of what was lost rather than as an invitation back. The sport that opened the relationship on a fairway in the rain closes it as the vocabulary of the eulogy, and the ten years between are the story of a shared interest that never became shared.

## Conflicts in the record

- **No origin story.** The corpus picks golf up already in progress in 2015. Whether Dan played as a junior inside the family's country-club world or took it up as an adult is unestablished. The Fran lineage supplies access but not a documented start.
- **No formal handicap.** Self-reported scores (breaking 90, par finishes, the near-ace) are the only skill evidence. Nothing formal was ever recorded.
- **The Mystic Rock canon claim.** *"Widely considered to be 'The Wire' of Mid-Atlantic golf"* is Dan's coinage in a single message, not an attested consensus. It reads as his ranking, stated as the world's.
- **The 400-yard Danny Matthews.** The yard's oral tradition credits Danny with 400-yard drives. This is folklore reported by trainees, not a measured figure, and it predates the main documented Danny thread — which is what makes it interesting rather than what makes it true.
- **Post-2019 play.** The record goes quiet on actual play after 2019. The 2020–2025 references are identity and media, not rounds. Absence of evidence, not evidence of absence — but the corpus is message-dense in those years and the rounds aren't in it.

## Assessment

Golf is the only thread in the record that touches all three of the wiki's load-bearing structures: the family lineage (Fran's fifty years on the UCC fairway), the working life (the 43-month caddying career, the longest commitment after Au Za'atar), and the relationship (met on a fairway in 2015, eulogized on one in 2025). It is the [[wiki/mind/synthesis/interests-as-era-markers|era-marker interest]] in its purest form — for 2016–19 the interest literally was the era — and it is the one interest the corpus shows him pursuing with professional seriousness rather than dilettantism. The decade-long failure to get Annie to share it is the thread's one sustained defeat, and the terminal-phase callback suggests both of them knew what that failure meant before either of them said it.
