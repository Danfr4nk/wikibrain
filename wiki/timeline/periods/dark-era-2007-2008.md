# The Dark Era — Opiates and the Body (2007–2008)

The two years between the November 2005 family rupture and the September
2008 departure for Full Sail are when the
[[wiki/health/chemical-architecture|chemical architecture]] that has
run continuously since actually began. Two body-level facts, previously
scattered across a stat line and a handful of message references, belong
together as one period: opiate onset, and a documented eating disorder.

At age 18–19, in the direct aftermath of his father's rehab admission and
his parents' divorce, Dan moved from the [[wiki/health/cocaine|cocaine]] use already normalized in
the household ([[wiki/people/rick-frank]], [[wiki/people/suzanne-frank]])
to opiates — Roxicet, then oxycodone. Five months separated first use from
addiction. The self-profile material frames the shift precisely: opiates served "for
internal regulation," and the operative question at the time was "how to
function without getting sick" — not euphoria-seeking but a stability
problem being solved with the tools available.

The same year brought a second row of the stack online: nicotine, dated by
his own 2017 tweet to a promotional Fall Out Boy show in 2007. And running
underneath all of it, an adolescent bulimia — candy as the dietary staple —
that Dan himself would later link, first-person and unprompted, to the loss
of two adult teeth. The period has almost no contemporaneous paper trail: it
is reconstructed from the self-profile material, the 2008 tweet archive's
hard boundaries, and oblique references in later message threads. It has
exactly one first-person endpoint, written on the day it happened and to
nobody at all: on 24 September 2008, from an account with no followers,
*"Really feels like life has started over now. It's weird enjoying life*
*again. So stoked."*

## The November 2005 hinge and the year after

The period's load-bearing event sits just outside it, in November 2005. As
CATO's record has it — Dan's account, no documentary corroboration found —
Rick disclosed he was entering rehab for cocaine at 6am the next morning,
the first drug conversation the household had ever had, delivered to
seventeen-year-old Dan in the car on the drive home from getting his
learner's permit. The permit was delayed a month. While Rick was in rehab,
per Dan's account, Suz slept with their shared cocaine dealer; divorce
followed. That same winter Rick was diagnosed with stage-4 throat cancer,
took experimental radiation at UPMC, and survived. It is the hinge the
entire psychological record organizes itself around — including the
November 2005 hinge entry the psychosexual cluster names as the encoding
moment of "intimacy is a precursor to abandonment" — and it lands at the
age when the record's extreme-sports identity is at its peak.

Dan's own first cocaine use followed within a year of the rupture, at
17–18 (2005–2006), in the Seven Springs ski-crew context. A later self-audit
explicitly frames that choice as instrumental as much as social: cocaine as
a chemical means of weight control tied to the adolescent bulimia — the
eating disorder and the early drug use were serving overlapping functions
from the start, not separate threads.

And two years before the opiate arc, the corpus holds a first instance of
deliberate intoxication as something closer to engineering than accident.
On 15 June 2005 — the summer after eleventh grade — three 17-year-olds by
his own telling drove to a sold-out Tom Petty show at the Post-Gazette
Pavilion in Burgettstown, could not get in, and dosed 20–40 DXM capsules
each from a CVS run instead, then drove home down Rt 51 at 95 miles per
hour. The chemical-architecture page reads it as the word-for-word
prefiguration of the engineered-stack framing Dan adopts a decade later:
the first documented substance in the corpus taken deliberately to
"shatter the space-time continuum." Full account on
[[wiki/timeline/events/robotussin-s-last-dance]].

## The opiate onset, 2007

In 2007, at age 18–19, in the post-rupture years, Dan moved from cocaine
to opiates: Roxicet, then oxycodone. The arc's key figure is five months —
first use to addiction — and the framing that arrives with it. The
self-profile material on record states the shift precisely: opiates served
"for internal regulation," and the operative question became "how to
function without getting sick" rather than "how to be cool." The
distinction is the pivot point of the whole period: from instrumental use
— cocaine as a weight-control tool, DXM as a boundary-dissolver — to
maintenance use. A stability problem being solved with the tools
available.

Cocaine use stopped entirely during this window. It would not resume until
2012, two years after the Suboxone stabilization — returning then as a
deliberate "voltage" layer added on top of an already-secure base rather
than a relapse.

The one specific, dated event that makes the dependence undeniable is
November 2007. Dan traveled to Las Vegas with his father for his nineteenth
birthday (November 1, 2007) — his father freshly out of rehab — and on that
trip recognized his own first opiate withdrawal, leaving Vegas knowing he
was addicted. The symmetry is worth stating plainly because the record
carries it plainly: the man who had disclosed his cocaine rehab at 6am two
years earlier is now traveling with a son who is doing the same recognition
work about a different drug, and the son does it alone.

The five-month first-use-to-addiction window is stated in the profile
material but not dated to specific months within 2007 — the record does
not supply them.

## Heroin, considered and rejected — winter 2008

One specific choice is worth recording exactly because it complicates the
period's name: the following winter (2008), he tried heroin once and
rejected it on aesthetic grounds — it "felt dirty." The
chemical-architecture page reads the preference as consistent with a
broader "chemical connoisseur" disposition rather than pure hedonism: the
method and purity of a substance mattering as much as its effect. The
standing reading is that this is a "heroin and opiates" era in the sense
that heroin was on the table and turned down, not one where it was used —
and the preference held. The stabilization did not arrive until Suboxone
day-zero, February 17, 2010, at the tail end of the Full Sail window
([[wiki/timeline/periods/full-sail-2008-2010]],
[[wiki/health/chemical-architecture]]). The date's original derivation did
not hold — see Conflicts in the record.

## A second row of the stack: nicotine, the same year

2007 did not bring one substance. A tweet of 28 December 2017 supplies an
onset date the health pages never had: *"Smoked my first pack of cigarettes
when I went to this promotional fall out boy show in 2007."* The
chemical-architecture page narrows the candidate to the likeliest —
**6 February 2007, Times Square, the Infinity On High release-day free
show** — stated as likeliest, not settled, because he never names the
venue. What is settled is the year, and the year's significance: nicotine
and the first opiate arrive in the same twelve months. The stack does not
begin as one substance arriving alone.

The same archive supplies the row's first dated first-party evidence at
the other end: already a smoker at nineteen, Dan announces a quit on
16 October 2008 — *"I think I'm gonna stop smoking cigarettes. Another
problem solved."* — and is back on them by 22 December, sixty-seven days
later. The chemical-architecture page reads the announcement sentence as
containing the whole later architecture in miniature: the decision is
treated as the solution, and the behavior is not consulted.

## The eating disorder

A second, previously undocumented body-history fact from the same broader
window: Dan had an eating disorder — specifically adolescent bulimia —
referenced obliquely but repeatedly in the
[[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] message corpus rather than in any
dossier. Annie's own messages confirm both a shared history and lasting
physical effects — "Two people with previous eating disorders together is
something else" (Jan 9 2016) and, describing herself, "I know that I
fucked my stomach up from having an eating disorder" (May 31 2016). A
later message (Jul 15 2024, from a different contact) has someone
recognizing the same pattern in Dan directly: "She's like you had an
eating disorder didn't you? Bc I did too and I can see myself in you."

The clearest first-person link between the teenage bulimia and a specific,
dated adult physical outcome came two decades later. In a mid-2025 AI-chat
session, Dan — unprompted, adding a fourth compounding stressor to three
already named — said he had recently lost two teeth, one a visible
"vampire" tooth, and supplied his own causal chain without hedging:
*"bulimic as a high schooler candy as a staple of my diet terrified of
dentist (aka haven't gone) suboxone cigarettes."* The chain is his, not a
clinician's — five factors with no weighting and no medical adjudication,
and the dentist-avoidance parenthetical is itself contradicted by the
message record (see Conflicts in the record). What is first-person and
dated is the outcome, and the wound he assigns it: he scores the loss not
as a health event but as an image event — damage registering as exposure,
exactly what the [[wiki/health/hyperreflexivity|hyperreflexivity]]
mechanism predicts.

The food-and-diet page adds the register question. Against the bulimia
sits the adult composition regime — an absolute, no-questions-asked
dietary rule in which cheeseburgers are meat-cheese-bread, tacos are
meat-cheese-shell, only corn and lettuce are accepted vegetables, onion
and tomato are refused outright, and pickles are "the mortal enemy."
Whether the regime predates or follows the bulimia is unknown, and the
answer decides whether the adult specification habit is a control behavior
that grew out of a disordered one or an independent lifelong trait.
Both pages agree on the register: the bulimia, the candy staple and the
two lost teeth are the only place in the corpus where food is a subject
in its own right — and it enters through the body, as damage, never
through taste.

No primary source gives an exact date for the eating disorder's onset,
duration, or any point of resolution — the reconstruction is built
entirely from oblique references in later message threads, not from any
direct account.

## The interrupted baseline: the extreme-sports years

What the 2007–2008 onset interrupts is a total-obsession adolescence. From
roughly age 11 through 17 (approximately 2000–2006), Dan's primary identity
was in two extreme sports: aggressive inline rollerblading in summer,
slopestyle terrain-park skiing in winter. Seven Springs is the physical
anchor — the family's ski-in-ski-out condo in the Villages, the north-face
pipe, the grom park — and Dan is framed as a product of the Tanner Hall
era, the skill-threshold window when slopestyle went from basic rotations
to corked 450s onto flat-down rails. By the 2006 season Seven Springs had
become the "premiere terrain park of the mid-Atlantic," with a full
dedicated slopestyle run containing several dozen features. Summers ran
through weekend-long Vans Skatepark birthday trips (~2001–2003) and four
summers at Camp Woodward. The capture is explicit that the economics were
class-signaling — "most people can't afford to do that" — the same
instinct visible in the Abercrombie accumulation and the bought-his-way-in
social position of the 2006–07 school year.

The sequence reads as an interrupted trajectory rather than a gradual
fade: the resort's peak park build coincides with the hinge year, and
then the opiate onset arrives at 18–19 and the skiing identity is gone
from the record. The body that had been trained toward a performance
ceiling for six years becomes the body that is being chemically managed.
Nothing in the corpus narrates the transition — no farewell to the sport,
no injury, no decision. It simply stops being the subject.

## Schooling and the drift years

Operator testimony, 2026-09-24: he did a few semesters at Penn State
Fayette's Eberly campus during the post-rupture window, and did **not**
attend Duquesne University — an earlier wiki entry asserting Duquesne
attendance (a brainstorm item commissioned without premise-checking) was
deleted and retracted the night it shipped. No dates, major, or further
detail on the Fayette semesters is on the record; the semesters sit
somewhere in the window between Laurel Highlands graduation and the
September 2008 Full Sail departure.

## The period's exit, in his own words, on the day it happened

On 21 September 2008 Dan drove south — *"Moving to Florida. Leaving PA to
live on my own for the first time."* — and the day he reached Winter Park
he opened the `@danfrank` account. The first tweet, 24 September, is *"i
fucking looove winter park. this town is unreal."* **Forty-five minutes
later, at 05:37 UTC:**

> *"Really feels like life has started over now. It's weird enjoying life*
> *again. So stoked."*

Three things in one sentence, all of them useful here.

**"Started over"** is him treating the move as a discontinuity rather than
a next step — which is what this page argues the Full Sail departure was,
now stated by the subject on day one instead of inferred from later
silence.

**"Enjoying life *again*"** is the load-bearing word. It presupposes a
period of not enjoying it, and dates the end of that period to the move
rather than to anything that happened before it. This page's
date_range_end of 2008-08-01 is a reasonable boundary for the
*conditions*; the **experienced** exit is seven weeks later and is tied
to a change of place.

**It is written to nobody.** The account has no followers on 24 September
2008 — it is being started that day. This is not a status performed for
an audience, which is the standard objection to reading social posts as
testimony, and it does not apply to the first hour of an empty account.

**What it does not do.** It says nothing about opiates, and nothing about
the eating disorder. Neither subject appears anywhere in the 95 recovered
tweets of 2008. The relapse this wiki dates to Thanksgiving 2009
([[wiki/people/alexis-armel]]) is fourteen months after this tweet, so the
"started over" is not durable in the way he meant it — but that is a fact
about what followed, not a reason to discount what he said at the time.

Two outside confirmations fix the era's boundaries from years later. A
July 2013 exchange with [[wiki/people/elizabeth-eleanor|Elizabeth Eleanor]],
a fellow recovering addict, has Dan stating "i'm still on 2mg of suboxone
a day, it's been almost 5 years since i touched an opiate and i'm still
too terrified to get off" — backing into a last-opiate-use date of roughly
2008–2009, independently consistent with this window, and the corpus's
only concrete dosage figure. And the 2010-02-17 day-zero tweet — "this is
the most stressful day/decision of my life. nothing is making it easier."
— anchors the Suboxone stabilization at the far end, fourteen months
after the move.

## Conflicts in the record

**DUQUESNE UNIVERSITY — retracted the night it shipped [2026-09-24].**
An earlier wiki entry asserted that Dan attended Duquesne University
during the post-rupture window — a brainstorm item commissioned without
premise-checking. Operator testimony the same day: he did a few semesters
at Penn State Fayette's Eberly campus and did **not** attend Duquesne. The
entry was deleted and retracted that night. Current standing: Fayette is
the recorded schooling for this window; no dates, major, or further
detail are on record.

**NO FIRST-PERSON EXIT MARKER — gap closed [2026-09-02].** Until this
date the page stated, in its Gaps section, that no first-person source
for the period's break existed — the whole reconstruction was built from
oblique references in later threads. The recovered 2008 tweet archive
supplied the contemporaneous first-person marker: *"Really feels like
life has started over now. It's weird enjoying life again."* What the
recovery did **not** close: the eating disorder and the 2007 first-use
months appear nowhere in the 95 recovered 2008 tweets. That gap is still
open.

**SUBOXONE DAY-ZERO — footing changed, conclusion unchanged [2026-09-03].**
This page cites 17 February 2010 as the Suboxone day-zero. Its actual
origin is a Gemini session in which a model told Dan that he and his
father "boarded an Amtrak train" to Penn Station on Thursday 18 February,
and Dan replied: *"NO no - if the train left on feb 18 that means that
the first day i began my successful suboxone streak was feb 17, 2010."*
The date was computed, not recalled — the day before a train — and the
anchor came from a model reading email logs. **That anchor is wrong:**
the 2010 tweet archive shows the 18 February train running the other way
(on the 17th he is already in New York — *"off to Williamsburg"* — and
on the 18th he is *"On the train back to PA"*). The date survives on
better evidence than the reasoning that produced it: 17 February 2010
carries a contemporaneous tweet at 15:07 New York time — *"this is the
most stressful day/decision of my life. nothing is making it easier."*
— followed within seventy-two hours by *"planning the next 3 years of my
life all day in muh head"* and *"put my life on a treadmill. shape up or
bust."* He never names the decision, so this remains a dated alignment
rather than a proof — but it is first-party, same-day, and independent
of the derivation it replaces. Full accounting on
[[wiki/health/chemical-architecture]].

**THE DENTAL PARENTHETICAL — his 2025 account contradicts the message record [2026-08-19].**
Dan's 2025 tooth-loss chain quotes him as "terrified of dentist (aka
haven't gone)." The message dump contradicts the parenthesis directly:
**2017-10-09** — *"went back to the dentist today and i've got a lot of
work to get done. all those years of being a delinquent junkie catching
up to me."* **2017-11-30** — a post-surgical follow-up: *"When I went
back for a 1wk checkup on the second, they noticed a hole that hadn't yet
begun to develop tissue so they put in a membrane and restitched it"* —
which means a graft. Further appointments are recorded in 2020 and 2024.
Current standing: the maintenance is episodic and reactive, not absent —
he goes when something has already broken, which is why the 2017 visit
opens with *"a lot of work to get done"* rather than with a cleaning.

**"STARTED OVER" WAS NOT DURABLE — the Thanksgiving 2009 relapse.**
The 24 September 2008 tweet's implicit promise broke fourteen months
later. Over the Thanksgiving 2009 weekend, per a 2026-08-27 operator
capture, Alexis brought thirty Roxicet pills and "the opiate addiction
that Dan had truly kicked while in college is back in force." A friend,
Spetch, drove from Winter Park to Ocala daily to keep a supply running;
the pattern continued through a Christmas trip home and resolved seven
weeks after the couple's return to Orlando on the already-dated
February 17, 2010 day-zero. This is a fact about what followed, not a
correction to what the tweet says at the time — the tweet's evidentiary
value as a contemporaneous exit marker is untouched.

## Assessment

Two coping mechanisms — a chemical one and a body-control one — both
trace to the same hinge point: the collapse of the family's stable
narrative in November 2005. Neither is framed anywhere in the source
material as resolved through treatment; the opiate arc resolves into
permanent chemical maintenance (Suboxone, 2010–present, zero relapses,
measured from the 2010-02-17 day-zero), and the eating disorder's
resolution, if any, is undocumented — it surfaces only as something
Annie and at least one other contact recognized in him years later,
never as its own worked-through history.

The period's shape is discontinuity without recovery. The move to Florida
is experienced as an ending, the tweet says so, and the conditions it
ended were back fourteen months later. What "started over" was the
geography and the public voice — the first durable public record of his
life begins at the arrival — not the chemistry. The architecture that
began in 2007 (opiate, nicotine) kept running; the maintenance dose
arrived in February 2010, not September 2008. The eating disorder never
gets its own resolution date at all.

The record's honesty constraint is worth restating: nearly every date in
the 2007 first-use window is missing, the eating disorder has no onset or
duration, and the one thing the period's own subject said about it — the
tweet — says nothing about the substances. This page is a reconstruction
with one contemporaneous sentence in it, and the sentence is about
leaving.
