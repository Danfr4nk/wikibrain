# 2021–2023 Employment Block

For three years — invisible in Dan's self-narrative until the location history exposed it — Dan held one sustained job: busser, then server, at [[wiki/work/au-zaatar|Au Za'atar Midtown]], the Lebanese restaurant at 58th & 1st. It is the longest continuous employment of his adult life on the year-round accounting (the full tenure runs March 2021–August 2024, forty-one months), and the period's texture survives in his own retrospective voice: the two "STORYTIME: AU ZA'ATAR" Gemini sessions, where he narrated the shed, the staff, and the alibis in memoir register. The location record logs ~690 shifts across the tenure — a 2026-09-14 recount of 121,733 GPS pings raises the visit count to 712 — making this the one place the whole middle of the second NYC era reduces to.

The period is the corpus's single most important work episode, and not because the job was the point. It is the only assigned commitment Dan ever sustained at cost for a multi-year run, which makes it the episode the mind-model syntheses have to account for rather than around. What the record keeps making visible is a split: the sensor layer and the message record corroborate the frame — the shifts, the hours, the warm threads, the pay figure, the involuntary end — while the texture lives almost entirely in his telling. That asymmetry is not a defect of the page; it is close to being the page's subject. A three-year block with a five-event LIFE calendar, a held message record nearly silent about the restaurant, and one plywood shed holding almost everything.

## How it started

By early 2021, two years into the second NYC period, "the money just ran out." Dan's own narration names the motive for seeking work with characteristic candor: not rent but continuity of supply — he wanted to keep being able to call his Dominican dealers daily (the [[wiki/people/menore|Menore]] service line, running since 2018). With no food-service experience he charmed owner [[wiki/people/tarik-fallous|Tarik Fallous]] at the interview, was hired on the spot, and started the next day as a busser — into the structural quirk that made everything after possible: an equal front-of-house tip split, bussers paid the same as waiters, unusual for the industry. Dan's account is that he talked himself into that split in the interview itself — a day-one invention that became the arrangement's structural hook for the entire tenure. His general claim about job beginnings ("I've turned every internship i've ever had into a paid gig — I'm REAL good at the beginning of a job I like") is borne out here: within months the turnover-churned restaurant's opening protocol was simply *his*.

The owners' style set the culture in ways Dan documented with dry contempt: Tarik and his brother Ghassan rented an apartment above the restaurant for no purpose but constant surveillance, micromanaged trivia, and — per Dan — never actually worked. Staff churned fast, which by default made Dan the protocol-keeper. He also notes he was the only white front-of-house employee, and that with the owners' prejudices this cut in his favor — his own accounting of the mechanism, recorded here as his claim about how he read the room, not as fact about anyone else.

A month in, he got [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] hired as hostess — he was the employment vector, and they worked different locations: her at the original East Village flagship, him at the Midtown shed. (The 2026-07-13 revision corrected the earlier page version that had conflated the two locations; see Conflicts in the record.)

## The Shed economy

The owners opened outdoor-only in spring 2021 — nice sheds, BYOB, food "to die for" — planning to build the interior later with nine months of runway. They never did: November 2021 arrived with construction "not even technically started," all four seasons of 2022 passed with none, and the restaurant served its third consecutive outdoor winter in 2023–24, after COVID ended, as the only outdoor-dining holdout on the block — neighbors hostile, customers furious, Tarik unbothered. Winter service meant an apron over a ski jacket, space heaters mounted over each table, and a two-tier seating taxonomy of Dan's coinage: "INDOOR OUTDOOR" (inside the shed) versus "OUTDOOR OUTDOOR" (on the sidewalk, minimal heat), the latter offered to freezing customers who had reserved actual tables. Tableside shawarma — the signature vertical rotisserie carved at the table — meant hand-carrying what was effectively an oven across an icy February sidewalk.

Because everything moved inside nightly, rebuilding the restaurant was 2–3 hours of work every day, and Dan owned it as the opener: sleep to 1pm, arrive at 2, build until 5, work the first service hours, first cut at 9–10pm — a slot he held the entire tenure, never once closing. That slot is not just his narration: the 2026-09-14 GPS recount independently timestamped the pattern across 33 months — arrivals cluster at 14:00 (340) and 15:00 (275), departures at 21:00 (262) and 22:00 (131), median visit 6.2 hours, Mondays dark (16 visits against 99–127 every other day). The 2pm arrival and the 9–10pm first cut he narrates are confirmed by the pings.

Dan's own verdict on the dysfunction is the period's key self-insight: he was "kinda happy about it," because the brokenness preserved the "weird" conditions he thrives in. The sentence is doing more work than it looks like — it is the same man who, everywhere else, builds institutions only to be the one who doesn't fit inside them, telling you that for once the institution's failure to institutionalize was the feature.

## The night-class alibi

To hold the peak hours and dodge the closing shift, Dan ran a fictitious 9pm "night class" — "night classes... of some unspecified sort" — for over eighteen months. It was never exposed. The alibi secured peak-hour shifts and the equal tip split while exempting him from closing duty, and it doubled as cover for the [[wiki/people/valeria-iglesias-cid|Valeria]] affair, one unfalsifiable fiction serving labor extraction and concealment simultaneously. It even survived a one-star review for leaving mid-meal that asked, verbatim, **"WAS HE HIGH?"** ("uh, clearly") — survived it because Tarik and Ghassan liked him and "ALL of the neighbors and regulars" insisted he wait their tables. The live page files this as the stated origin of the "DAN CYCLE OF JOB ENTROPY," and the synthesis layer files it as the fullest documented instance of the institutional-out protocol ([[wiki/mind/concepts/institutional-out]]): the pre-installed escape, deployed on hire "before the ink is even dry," held for eighteen months as absolutely as any stated rule he himself runs on.

## The promotion and the caddie method

Manager [[wiki/people/dimitri|Dimitri]] — "the only boss or manager or supervisor who i have ever actually really loved" — insisted Dan become a server. Under the equal split the promotion carried zero economic gain; its perks were purely positional (iPads, authority over the Honduran and Guatemalan bussers), and Dan took it anyway. Promotion day: a mass staff exodus, a Boosted-board ride to the 5th Avenue Uniqlo for a white oxford shirt, and a same-day start with no training. What he brought instead of training was the caddying philosophy: a caddie is "an ADVOCATE OF THE CUSTOMER," paid to bend the club's rules for his group — so he ran tables the same way, steering customers off dishes ("it's come out like half cooked a bunch of times"), staging dropped beer bottles to comp replacements, and openly badmouthing the construction delay to seated guests, noting himself that this is "frowned upon in food service in a way that it is not as a golf caddie."

It worked: the post-buzz clientele was mega-repeaters — Middle Eastern family groups, neighbors, TikTok parties filming the shawarma — and as the only long-tenured recognizable face, Dan was the one regulars would "GUSH to tarik" about. The cost was equally real: as an introvert, fifteen-table freezing Saturday nights took "literally EVERYTHING i had in me." That is the drain the record names as the stated origin of the job-entropy cycle — the exhaustion that needed the alibi, and the alibi that made the exhaustion survivable for eighteen months.

## The social world: Lebanese and Senegalese

The job installed two peoples permanently in his esteem — "LEBANESE and SENEGALI." The Lebanese owners' humor ("incapable of not making fun of everyone and their flaws") matched his own register — "they liked me because i, too, am both an asshole and a sonofabitch." The Senegalese half is [[wiki/people/ismaila-barry|DJ]], brought in by Dimitri, who became one of his best friends — "smart and curious and funny" — one of the few friendships the record ranks that high. DJ made the same busser-to-server climb Dan did, in parallel, and the friendship was built on the floor, in the shared work, rather than on any prior history.

Alliance with the kitchen was engineered directly: cocaine bumps offered to the cooks and stolen Takis brought in for everyone, buying friendships he reports keeping "today" (2026). The BOH bond has a specific genesis in his telling: he describes shedding an admitted prejudice about Mexicans — "turns out i fucking LOVE mexicans" — and keeps the respect of the Mexican kitchen squad as the one credential he carried out. The period also logged a candid prejudice datum, carried here as his stated self-accounting: he dropped his self-described "unjustifiably bigoted" view of Mexicans and relocated rather than dissolved it ("it's the guatamalens that actually suck"). That relocation is ugly and it is on the record in his own voice, so it stays in as his own accounting of how his prejudices moved, not as a finding about anyone else.

## The wider floor

Around the Triad of Dimitri, DJ, and Dan, the documented cast fills in, and the pattern is that almost every tie the restaurant produced got tested by the job's end — some survived, most didn't.

**The audit war.** The staff-side economy ran on mutual suspicion. Employees held a settled "radical skepticism" that the owners were siphoning the digital tip pool — the pool had zero transparency and paychecks were suspiciously identical every week — and Dan ran what he called a "decentralized audit" (pocketing cash tips) as self-help against it. For over a year he told staff and customers alike that the owners were siphoning tips ("because they 100% were"). The tip-audit war and the affection for Tarik coexisted for the whole tenure; both are on record and neither cancels the other.

**De facto manager.** After Dimitri's first quit — about six months in, per the STORYTIME telling, under the misery of working for Tarik — there was simply no manager, and Dan became the de facto one: "except i don't do anything the manager should other than advocate for the customers and neighbors." Then Dimitri returned ("WITH A STEEL CHAIR") as co-manager alongside the 22-year-old Tunisian the owners had hired "because he speaks arabic." Factions formed, and the owners consistently favored "the more seemingly muslim option" — even Dimitri taking short shrift. The six-month stint and the steel-chair return are Dan's stylized telling (memoir, not deposition); the held record gives the harder anchors elsewhere.

**Thanksgiving 2023.** The Tunisian manager took Dan's open accusations to Tarik, and on Thanksgiving 2023 Dan was sat down and "brought right up to the line of being let go." He held on — the 58th Street regulars and neighbors demanded he stay. By the later telling the two were "kinda chill."

**Sergio.** A 53-year-old Miami service veteran ("I've served celebrities" since 2000) whose war with Dan over the word "dude" escalated into a "nonsense meeting" with Tarik and Ghassan, conducted in tooth-loss analogies, recorded and auto-transcribed. Sergio accused Dan of acting "like he owns the place" and refused to be called "dude"; Dan countered that Sergio shoved him twice, that "dude" was "a term of endearment," and that "kid" was "a term of criticism." The mediator was Ghassan — Tarik joined toward the end — per Dan's own 2026-07-14 follow-up, which corrected the page's earlier Dimitri attribution. Sergio "came through and left quickly": a short-tenured hire, not a lasting cast member.

**Felipe.** His "unbelievably gay Colombian bff," who joined as a server — shattering the old glass door with a table leg on day one, in Dan's telling. But per Dan directly (2026-07-14), contact with Felipe did not survive past the job; the record corrected the early framing of a lasting friendship. The friendship was real and it died with the shed.

**Lou Bahet.** An Au Za'atar hostess and the era's burst-that-meant-nothing: 1,602 Instagram messages over 21 days (August 31–September 20, 2023), then nothing. Not a friendship — a flare of contact with a coworker, documented and gone.

**Menore.** The supply network wrapped around the workday: Menore delivered mid-shift to the Shed block through taxi windows, his legit-taxi cover making the sidewalk handoffs trivial — primary-confirmed for May–Aug 2024 (deliveries to 1st Ave between 57th and 58th), with the earlier years no longer resting on the storytime alone: third-party threads show him running deliveries throughout 2023 and January–May 2024. This page's independent description of him as Dan's "Dominican contact" is confirmed from the message record.

## Annie and Valeria

A month after starting, Dan got Annie hired as hostess at the **original East Village location** — a separate restaurant from the Midtown shed ("the restaurants ran totally differently and with different people"). In a turn Dan flags as "truly weird," Annie — "prim and proper... reliability IS her strong suit and is my antithesis" — was fired from that East Village hostess job with no notice and no reason, while Dan held onto the Midtown job for years longer. The contrast sits in the record unexplained.

The [[wiki/people/valeria-iglesias-cid|Valeria]] thread is the era's other human fact and the one that breaks the frame. She was the Au Za'atar host-stand hostess — trained as a hostess by Annie herself ("hey i trained a new," Annie's text to Dan; Dan's later framing: "the perfect handoff in the spirit of Alexis>annie"). The earliest dated evidence is an August 31, 2021 TikTok DM to Alexis, in Dan's own words: "I've got a new gf. She's 20 and an illegal immigrant... I'm still with Annie but the Mexican girl is making a fast ascent up the ranks." (His framing self-corrected from "Mexican girl" to "Chilean girl" by August 2022.) Then a nine-month forensic absence — no recovered messages September 2021 through April 2022, likely WhatsApp, no export held. The affair peaked in person over winter 2021–22, covered by the night-class alibi, and its message shadow is the May–June 2022 Instagram burst: 4,882 records total — 1,038 messages May 28–31, 3,518 June 1–13, 308 June 14–30, then 11 in July and 3 in August. June 9: the basement hug (Tarik's "You talk to valeria a lot" came three hours before). First ILYs: hers June 11, his June 13 — "the color has left my world." The Central Park kiss ("Did u like my kiss?") is verbatim in-thread. Annie's name appears zero times in all 4,882 records. Dan's own measure of it: "would have literally moved to chile for her i swear to god." She is the one infidelity in the decade he names as unauthorized. The post-mortem tags the breach as the period's "CORE ANOMALY," and its collapse taught him a social law he stated as doctrine: the norteño *server* poached her, but "my mexi cook buddies... would NEVER have done that shit to me" — floor staff betray, kitchen allies don't.

## Transition out

The block ends past this page's boundary: the city-mandated shed tear-down of August 2024 erased the "Outside Guy" territory. The ending came in two stages, and Dan is precise about the order. First, the employer cut his hours through June–August 2024 in a pattern he read in real time as maneuvering to force a quit and dodge unemployment obligations ("lowering my hours so much that it's not even worth staying," August 6, 2024); he dropped resumes from July 9 onward and filed for unemployment on August 8. The DanAnnie dossier amendments rule the loss **involuntary** (Amendment 2, ACCEPTED) — the fact that reframes Annie's late-2024 "you never work" grievances as locally valid but historically partial. Second, the territory itself died: the finished interior opened in 2024 — glowing dome ovens, green velvet booths, geometric floor tiles, chain curtains and mood lighting, with blue painter's tape still on the counters while service ran — and demand for the sidewalk tables died first; then the city mandated the winter shed teardown, and that was that. "Once the inside opened, they just didn't have enough interest in outdoor dining" — the interior made the shed obsolete before the mandate finished it. By the final stretch he was serving "like 3 tables per week total and would just hang," pocketing all the cash tips. His own epitaph for the era: "it was a long run and it was fun and good food and cool mexicans and... valeria." He came in "a novice in a red apron" and left "a legend in a white Uniqlo shirt," the Mexican squad's respect intact. (The famous "outside bread," he confirms, "obviousLY WAS NOT from outside.") The 2024 wind-down feeds directly into the [[wiki/timeline/periods/2025-collapse|2025 collapse]].
