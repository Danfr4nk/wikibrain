# 2015-2016 Annie Relationship Start

**Date range:** Nov 2015 – 2016

In late November 2015, Dan ended a six-year relationship with [[wiki/people/alexis-armel|Alexis Armel]] and began the relationship with Anne Ulmer ("Annie") that would run for eleven years, 2015–2026 — the first relationship to fully activate his attachment system, and the pair that carries the overwhelming share of his entire relational message volume. The handoff took roughly a week: an introduction by Alexis herself around November 24, an in-person meeting on a golf course at 2am in the rain on November 29, Alexis's eviction from the Virginia Avenue apartment on December 1, and a bond that fired at a speed and intensity with no precedent anywhere in the corpus.

The story of the period is the shape of that firing. December 2015 carries the new-life declaration on the afternoon of December 1 — "Annie Ulmer from now on its just you and me" — the December 2 family crisis over Dan's reputation, the first public appearance on December 3, explicit marriage declarations on December 10, and the onset message flood: 728 messages sent on December 10 alone, then 682, 363, 679 across the next three days — the single highest four-day sent-volume in the entire record outside the relationship's own 2025–26 termination window. 2016, measured by the dossiers, is the relationship's happiest year — love-language rates peak for both parties (Annie 5.3%, Dan 5.7% of all messages, the decade highs), the combined message volume of 2016 is the pair's highest of any year — and it is simultaneously a dense rehearsal of every mechanism the later years document: the trust deficit stated on New Year's morning, the abandonment schema named in January, Annie's drug escalation to intravenous use, the first major work fight, the jealousy kink named in February, the marriage frame restated within weeks of the start.

Why this period matters is what the dossiers say about it deliberately: it is the genuine-love baseline against which the whole decade is measured — "the later failures were trajectory, not character." And mechanically, it is the wiki's cleanest instance of the [[wiki/mind/synthesis/bond-switch-2015|single-bond switch]]: the attachment slot vacated and reoccupied with no unattached gap between them, inside seventy-two hours, with no precipitating betrayal — the event the corpus's attachment model is built on.

## Before: Uniontown, late November 2015

The period opens inside the closing weeks of the [[wiki/timeline/periods/uniontown-return-2013-2015|Uniontown-return period]]. Dan is living at the Virginia Avenue address — the apartment leased during the Alexis years, known in the corpus as "the Alexis lair" — in a six-year relationship whose public trace had gone silent two years earlier but which was still the household's actual state. [[wiki/people/casey-bondarenka|Casey]] is in the house, acquired into the friend circle days before the switch. An indoor cannabis grow — "Dream Queen," Dan's name for his favorite strain, an August 18 – October 4 EXIF-dated photo series — ended roughly four weeks before the switch window.

**Annie enters through Alexis herself.** The corpus anchors the introduction to roughly November 24, 2015, from Annie's own December 1 message: "HAPPY ONE WEEK SINCE LEX HANDED YOU TO ME." Dan's 2026 account of the origin week (testimony, September 12, 2026, sent with the Morgantown-trip photos) supplies the mechanism the corpus never had: about a week before Thanksgiving, Alexis told him to go meet Annie to pick up drugs for the two of them while Alexis worked. He and Annie clicked immediately; she asked him to go with her to Morgantown for the pickup. They went together, and on return they had sex — seven hours later he brought Alexis her drugs, Annie still beside him, and all three sat down at the bar together. "That was the true minute Alexis and I split up." Alexis, in his telling, "knew what was going on just not HOW MUCH" — and "no one thought I could get Annie." The origin week of November 19–27 holds four iMessages total; that week is textually dark, so the rupture layer is testimony-only. (The dating is held open against the corpus: Dan says ~November 19 for the meeting; the back-computation gives ~November 24.)

Between the introduction and the in-person meeting, on Thanksgiving Day — Thursday, November 26, 2015, 14:33 EST — Annie sent the first nude Dan ever got from her: a black-and-white bathroom selfie taken on her own iPhone 6s front camera. The timestamp rests on EXIF; the "first" rests on Dan's 2026 testimony. (A 2026-09-12 supersession note withdraws the older reading that no physical contact preceded the photo — per the origin account, they had already had sex the previous week.)

## The switch: November 28 – December 1

On November 28, already texting Annie intimately, Dan narrates the 2009 origin of the Alexis relationship to her as a contrast case — "I love this it's the exact opposite way I started my last relationship" — directly before the line about Alexis cheating "2 weeks in after I moved her to fla." The message is retrospective; the episode is from 2009, at Full Sail, online-only. (See Conflicts in the record — the old chronology read it as a 2015 betrayal and built the switch's causation on it.)

**November 29: the golf-course meeting.** Dan meets Annie in person at roughly 2am on a golf course, in the rain; their grandmothers were neighbors for fifty years. The record opens that night at 23:47:54 the previous evening ("Fuck my friends. Fuck birthday dinner. Fucking going drinking. I wanna be with you"). What follows, in the hand-read's exact timings, is the fastest commitment sequence in the corpus:

- **01:35** — Annie: "my fam and dude are here and are going to force me to leave with them." She is not unattached: a partner is with her that night. The man is never named in this window; Dan identifies him in 2026 as Emilio — testimony, not a corpus derivation.
- **02:49–02:52** — Dan calls him "turd boy"; Annie: "I don't like him. At all." / **"I am going to get rid of him just like you just did."** The switch is mutual and simultaneous — both slots vacated and reoccupied inside the same seventy-two hours.
- **03:14** — both parties' first "I love you."
- **03:23** — "You are my full life."
- **03:24** — "YOU ARE MY EVERYTHING."
- **03:28** — "I met someone that instantly changed my life" — said *to* Annie, not about her to a third party, as earlier quoting had it.

That is ninety minutes from the end of the meeting to the declarations, before either had slept. The correct statement is not "day two" but *inside two hours of first physical contact*.

The same night, an outbound message reads: "Things are collapsing with lex. You might be seeing a lot more Annie very soon." The recipient is almost certainly [[wiki/people/casey-bondarenka|Casey]] — it lands inside his documented same-day thread, one minute before his reply about a woman he's warning Dan off of — but this cannot be mechanically confirmed (sent messages in the master CSV carry an empty contact field), so the recipient is inferred from timing and content. With that caveat, it is the earliest dated primary-source text of the pivot itself, sent the same night.

**The switch was brokered, and paid for.** The message record shows a fourth party operating the transfer. On November 29 [[wiki/people/suzanne-frank|Suz]] phones to say Dan *should* date Annie, and the reason she gives is that the name fits the family — "I.e. Anne Dan Suzanne Fran Diane Van" (13:48–13:49). Roughly fifteen hours later, at 05:02 on November 30: "Suz just came to check on me, brought me a line, and told me she'd get me a car this week if I get Alexis out." Cocaine and a vehicle, from the mother, as consideration for completing the eviction — the earliest and least ambiguous instance in the corpus of maternal leverage over the romantic bond. The slot was not only Dan's to reassign.

The eviction runs as procedure rather than confrontation — which is why no scene appears anywhere in the record. The key is confiscated on November 28 ("She left her key out yesterday and I snagged it," 11-29 14:39); property is removed from the guest bedroom "so she knows I could actually call the cops" (11-30 00:35); Alexis's parents are summoned to collect her (13:42). Each step is reversible and none is a rupture. Alexis was physically in the house throughout — "Alexis is sloshed" on November 30 — which falsifies the earlier reading that placed her in Florida that month.

November 30 also carries the cover layer. Around 4:56 PM Dan sends Alexis the cover text — "Hey I remembered that you and Anne are supposed to work together tonight. Please leave her out of all of our problems, she really isn't involved" — asserting the opposite of the truth while Annie was its entire cause; Alexis answers with cold disengagement ("I couldn't care less about anne"). That night at 11:36 PM Dan clears the Facebook relationship field and captions the screenshot "11/30/2015 official break up" — his retrospective dating on a contemporaneous screenshot. And in the overnight hours of the 29th: "Alexis doesn't know that you have any part in this. She thinks I'm just creeping you" (0:01), consistent with the "knew what was going on just not HOW MUCH" account — then by 8:33 the "It's done I'm yours" vows.

**December 1: the formalization.** "Helping lex leave" — Alexis moves out of the Virginia Avenue apartment. On the afternoon of December 1 (screenshot EXIF 13:38 EST), in the hours after the eviction, Dan declares the bond's founding rule: "Because I didn't sleep and I want to bask in my new life with you" — "Brace yourself" — "Annie Ulmer from now on its just you and me" — "Because she's gone" — "We made it lol." She answers: "You're adorable" — "This is wonderful you give me the best feeling." His last visible line: "I wanna spend forever smothering you in love." The eviction is framed as a joint victory — *we made it* — and "Annie Ulmer from now on its just you and me," issued in her full name, is the earliest primary-source instance of the explicit-verbal-commitment architecture: positive verbal expression as absolute rule. The same afternoon's photos are the household being made theirs hour by hour — she holds lit Christmas lights over a mattress (16:54), he pulls a face in a beanie while she leans on his shoulder (19:43), a Post-it advent calendar goes up on the door (22:52, "Post it....Adventi calendar"). On December 3 Annie co-authors the follow-up text to Alexis live at her workplace, quoting the November 30 line back as the template. Formalization is a November 29–30 two-day process, not a single timestamp.

The lair continuity is the physical proof of the switch's structure: Annie moved into the same apartment — the Virginia Avenue address that had been the Alexis lair. Dan did not leave the shared space and bring Annie into a new one; the new bond occupied the physical shell of the old one. The MOGZART era, the couples-camming period, the 2017 poverty floor, and the 2018 deep cycle would all then play out *inside the Alexis lair*, with Annie now its co-occupant.

## December: the bond at full flood

**December 2: the family crisis.** Annie's mother learns Annie's car was at Dan's house — "someone told me your car was at Dan Frank's this morning" (18:44) — and the informant is never named, though both parties blame Casey. (Annie's own message that evening names a different vector: "because fucking Emilio texted her about me" — the displaced partner contacting her *sister* Claire, not her mother. The two vectors must not be collapsed: the corpus identifies the person who told Ellen as nobody, and Emilio's documented route is Claire.) The objection her mother raises is not the affair but Dan's reputation: "he has a bad past do you know what he's done to his family" (18:47), escalating to "am I going to have to treat you like a 12 year old" (18:49). Annie's answer: **"I'd rather lose all of them then to not be with you"** (19:00). Within the hour Dan is calling it "Romeo and Juliet," and by 22:41 he is drafting the letter to her parents that no copy of survives. The reputation gets its only first-person accounting that night: "I took $500 from my grandma one time and got caught / But that's the worst thing I've ever done in my life" (19:17–19:18). Whatever her family had heard, the gap between their version and his own is the relationship's first structural problem — stated on day five, by both of them, in writing. [[wiki/people/rick-frank|Rick]] intervenes that night: "Call me tomorrow. I will help you fix this" (23:56), and the crisis resolves through the fathers rather than between the couple.

The same night carries Dan's abandonment fear, present from literally the third day of the documented record, before there was anything to betray: "I'm really really scared that you won't want this anymore" (Dec 2, 2015). It became the organizing force of the next decade — and the hand-read gives it a concrete cause recovered from Rick's side of the corpus: Annie's parents had just found out "the way they did" and moved to squash the relationship, and Dan told his father he planned to write them a letter asking permission. Her fear that night was responding to a real external threat, not free-floating anxiety.

**December 3: first time in public.** Four days after the formalization, six nighttime selfies (23:41–23:51 EST) outside a bar or restaurant — string lights, a lit window, a partial EXIT sign — Annie smiling at the camera, kissing his cheek, laughing mid-motion. EXIF timestamps and a live GPS fix (~39.9004, -79.7255 — Uniontown, PA area) match capture to the second; the "first" rests on Dan's 2026 testimony. The read is the hidden-to-public transition: the first ~ten days ran on cover — Harshman alibis, "she thinks I'm just creeping you," the golf course at night. These frames are the cover coming off.

**December 6: Shumar's confrontation, and church with her family.** [[wiki/people/zac-shumar|Zac Shumar]] — the man who had wanted Annie himself and had poured his heart out to Dan about her — confronts Dan by text over taking her: "Like I poured my heart out to you about her then you go and fuck her the next weekend. Like it just fucking hurts, I trusted you man" (2:57–3:41 PM, EXIF-dated; the sole record, absent from the message exports). Shumar names the transfer in real time — "you bail on lex and jump on Annie" — an outsider reading the single-bond switch eight days into the documented record. Dan defends the relationship as family integration rather than conquest: "I went to church with her and her family today" — December 6 was a Sunday, so the claim is weekday-consistent. And the switch is already public enough in Uniontown that Shumar had "found out through other people." Four days later the corpus confirms the argument was real and still live — Dan texts her on December 10 that Shumar "didn't get back from philly in time" with his PA system and that "he's still mad at me." (The separately documented Zach Clingan intervention — "I have to talk to you" — is December 9, not the same afternoon.)

**December 7: settled.** A week after the public debut, the photographic register has moved from cover to public to domestic: a laughing selfie — her hand over her mouth, him in a red shirt leaning on her shoulder — and two bed photos under white sheets (EXIF 14:07–14:35, iPhone 6 and 6s, Uniontown-area GPS). Two weeks into the relationship.

**December 10: the commitment goes explicit.** Two exchanges from that day survive as screenshots and are instances of the explicit-verbal-commitment architecture. In the evening Dan wrote, as received on Annie's phone (8:53 PM): "I'll keep rambling about how perfect you are and how happy it makes me that this (what I'm doing right now) doesn't seem to annoy you… I love you forever and will always be here to make sure you smile." Later the same night (18:01–18:03 EST): Dan — "I want to marry you" / "And spend forever feeling this happy" — Annie — "I can promise you" / "That you will marry me." Her "Save it forever" and his "Nobody has ever made me as happy as you just did" extend the exchange. The public register corroborates the timing independently: Dan's Twitter handle **@Lo_weez** debuts as his "new primary" relational tag in December 2015 ("worlds best girlfriend"), continuing through 2016 with matching personal texture ("Girlfriend drinks too much Cherry Coke"), in the same window that @alexisarmel goes silent.

December 10 is also the first day of the **onset flood** — 728 messages sent, then 682, 363, and 679 across December 11–13, recomputed directly from the master CSV. The highest daily sent-volume anywhere in the 2011–2026 record except the termination window. The same shape — maximum output under maximum attachment activation — appears at both ends of this relationship and nowhere else in Dan's corpus with comparable force: the activation made visible as a daily-volume time series.

The month's remainder is fallout on both sides of the swap. On December 13 the displaced partner persists: Annie is "gonna go off on him about throwing shit at me the other night," and his own message that night is "Honestly I just miss having fun with you!" On December 23, in Facebook Messenger — a channel the iMessage corpus does not cover, and the hand-read records zero Annie-corpus messages across December 20–24 — Dan explodes at [[wiki/people/zachariah-harshman|Zach Harshman]] for pulling away; Zach's defense names the switch's social mechanics from the outside: he had become Alexis's "best friend other than you for the past year," spending regular time with her after the breakup, and had backed off to avoid the appearance of impropriety. A third party, inside Alexis's own post-breakup orbit, independently placing the visible social fallout in the same one-month window.

**December's data profile.** The mined calendar counts 43 events for 2015, 39 in December — breakup, met Annie, hired, panic. The relationship category is high throughout (266 total). In the master message CSV, Annie is Dan's top handle with 31,000+ rows. Early comms align with the LIFE REPORT profile: anxious-preoccupied, rapid fragmented cadence, information dumps. The 2015 volumes: Dan 7,242 / Annie 6,394; love-language frequency 3.6% for both parties; Annie initiated contact on 62% of days.

Three December facts seed the later decade. Annie entered as a former MyFreeCams model — disclosed; Dan knew via his ex and was drawn to it — and the first camming discussion between them came within weeks, seeding the later couples-camming era. Dan's panic attacks are dated in the first weeks. And on December 29 Annie references "one of the last times I was over at j's" — a man who "literally gave me a chunk of ice" (crystal meth) without her asking — a distinct, earlier episode to keep separate from the MyFreeCams history.

## January 2016: the decade in miniature

The relationship's first full month reads like a compressed version of the whole relationship. Every pattern that later defines the bond is already present: Dan's trust deficit, Annie's drug use escalation, the marriage frame, the abandonment anxiety, the work-fight oscillation, and the supply-chain dependency.

**The trust deficit, stated outright.** On New Year's morning: "Because I've never been able to trust anyone before tbh" (2016-01-01 01:30) — the earliest explicit statement of a trust deficit the attachment model treats as structural. Not a response to betrayal; stated on day one of the new year, in a weeks-old relationship, before there is anything to betray. The fear follows in the same exchange: "I just see how much happiness you give me and I'm scared to lose that" (01:28). The attachment system is already operating at full activation.

**The abandonment schema, named.** On January 3: "it would be so typical of my entire life / to find something that makes me TRULY happy / and find the most beautiful girl in the world / who for whatever reason loves ME for who i am / and have everything be perfect / until it's just gone" (00:01–00:02). Stated as a worldview, not a reaction — a standing expectation that happiness is temporary and loss is inevitable.

**The marriage frame, revisited.** The same day, in a discussion about children: "I would marry you today. I promise you" (20:52) — "I would do that today with you...because i really can see what life with YOU would be and I want that more than anything I've ever wanted" (20:44). The contrast with Alexis is explicit: "I never wanted or even imagined having kids with her" (20:42). The singular-slot mechanism operating in the marriage domain, stated within weeks of the start.

**Annie's drug use escalates to intravenous.** From snorting/smoking in December, to pricking herself with a needle on January 2 ("I pricked myself / I know I said I would stop / I'm trying Dan," 23:29), to attempting to shoot up on January 5 ("I did try to shoot this morning.. I couldn't get a vain," 10:45). Each step documented in real time, each met with Dan's concern — "I just don't think you realize how likely it is to destroy what we are working for" (13:10) — and his own admission that he is fighting the temptation to try it himself: "i'm fighting MYSELF from wanting to try this" (23:47, Jan 2).

**The work fight, and the distance problem.** On January 7–8 the couple's first major fight is about work: Annie wants to work every other day in an area she would like to move to; Dan is upset that this means seeing her 50% less. "I do want that! But I don't understand why the only way to do that is to work so far away that we'll see each other 50% less" (23:20, Jan 7); "I told you... I cannot work in this town" (23:20). The underlying pressure is her parents: "my parents are convinced I'm not working cause they know you aren't working" (23:30). Dan says he'll get a job around here; Annie: "I'm over this. Seriously over it" (11:33, Jan 8). The oscillation — one party's independence threatening the other's togetherness — repeats across the decade.

**Dan's illness, and Annie's care.** January 7: "Annie I slept for 18 hours / And I think I'm dying" (09:07) — dry heaving every hour on the hour the night before, "the worst night of my life." Annie cares for him, brings him medicine, and is scared of getting sick herself: the care dynamic that will define the relationship — Annie as Dan's primary caregiver, Dan as the one who needs to be cared for.

**Annie quits CT's.** January 5: "Wow. I quit / I literally fuckig quit" (21:38) — taken off the schedule again; told she'd work later in the week, but the rest of the schedule posted without her. Dan is furious on her behalf. Her father yells at her for quitting — "Saying I didn't go to school to be waiting around to see if I get on a schedule to bartend" (21:59) — the parents' anger at her not working becoming the structural pressure that keeps her in a cycle of unemployment and dependency.

January's smaller entries round out the picture. On January 18 Dan texts Annie about needing money for a tux and having gone to "grams" for a work list, explaining he needs the paid work done now: "I know but she leaves for fla this week" — "she" is **Fran**, leaving for her Florida winter (an earlier pass misread the pronoun as Alexis; Alexis's only Florida residency was 2009–2010). On January 21 Annie tells Dan that "bimel" is bringing her drugs "more addictive than the ones we're already dealing with" — a new name in the record, never identified, absent from every contact list.

## 2016: the statistically happiest year

The dossiers' 2016 numbers are the relationship's decade highs. Love-language frequency: Annie 5.3%, Dan 5.7% — the peaks for both parties, both years. Message volumes: Dan 13,954 / Annie 14,395 — the pair's highest combined year. Dan initiated contact on 54% of days. 2016 is the baseline the rest of the decade is measured against: the year the love in the earliest weeks was real by any plain measure, before it became trajectory.

In February 2016 Dan names the kink he would name for the rest of the relationship: "Jealousy is like the biggest kink" — the explicitly pursued, openly discussed piece of his own sexual identity that the later arrangement architecture (2018–2024) would be built around. The mined calendar's 2016 samples fill in the texture: a Valentine's engagement tease ("crossing paths ring"), church, "you've changed my life" framed as a major decision, road-trip preferences, a cousin Caitlin Christmas Eve, Fran hospitalized, a move and a job step out of town. The calendar counts 78 events for 2016. The relationship never splits across this period — the first actual split in the eleven-year record comes in February 2025; the physical-only arrangement and the 2026 closure belong to a later period entirely.

## Conflicts in the record

**2026-08-15 — "cheating revelations" was in this period and does not belong there.** The LIFE_EVENTS_CALENDAR row `2015-11-28: Lex cheated on me 2 weeks in after I moved her to fla` was generated by tagging a message on the date it was *sent*. The message is Dan narrating the **2009** beginning of the Alexis relationship to Annie, directly after "I love this it's the exact opposite way I started my last relationship." Per the operator the episode was online-only and unconnected to 2015. Alexis was in the Uniontown house all week and left for Florida never — she moved out December 1 and stayed in Pennsylvania. **Every 💔 Cheating/Affair count in this page's data profile inherits this defect** and should be treated as an upper bound until re-derived — the calendar classifies by message date and keyword, so any retrospective mention of a past event lands in the wrong year. With the betrayal removed, nothing Alexis did in November 2015 explains the ending: six years closed in roughly seventy-two hours with no precipitating grievance, and the introduction came from Alexis herself.

**2026-08-02 — the twenty-four-hour switch is Dan's, not Annie's.** This page previously stated that *Annie* left her existing relationship within twenty-four hours of the introduction. Per the operator, that is backwards: **Dan** left Alexis inside twenty-four hours of meeting Annie. The corpus corroborates the corrected version and never corroborated the old one. ## Assessment

The dossiers' verdict on this period is the one the whole decade is measured against: both people genuinely present — the later failures were trajectory, not character. The numbers support it independently of anyone's memory: the love-language peaks of 2016 (Annie 5.3%, Dan 5.7%), the decade's highest combined volume that same year, the 62%/54% initiation rates, the onset flood whose four-day shape has no equal in the corpus except at the relationship's own end. Nothing about the bond in these weeks required managing him — "YOU ARE MY EVERYTHING" lands before there is anything to be managed for.

The period is also the sharpest available instance of the single-bond switch, and the record gained on it since 2026-08 strengthens rather than weakens that standing: a switch executed with no precipitating grievance, with the introduction coming from the departing partner herself, with the slot vacated and reoccupied on both sides inside seventy-two hours, with the lair held constant across the transfer, and with the family supplying the means. If the attachment model's claim — the slot is never left voluntarily empty, the replacement sourced before the vacancy — has a type specimen, this is it.
