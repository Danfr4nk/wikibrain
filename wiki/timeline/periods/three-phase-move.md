# The Three-Phase Move

He has lived in two places, in three phases, in fifteen years. Uniontown first — childhood, school, [[wiki/timeline/events/november-2005-rupture|the November 2005 rupture]] that made staying unlivable. Then New York, spring 2010 to May 2013, the audio-engineering ambition with his name on it. Then Uniontown again, 2013 to February 2019 — the producer-alias years, the Alexis endgame, the Annie genesis. Then New York again, February 9, 2019 to February 2025 — the longest stretch of adult stability in the record, including the 41-month Au Za'atar tenure, and the most expensive obsession years in the corpus. Then Uniontown, February 2025 onward, where he still is, at an address with no signed lease.

The pattern is a metronome: ambition goes north, trouble brings him home. Every northbound move is a beginning that costs money; every southbound move is an ending that costs more. What follows is the four chapters — trigger, cost, ending — for each move, with the gaps stated plainly where the record goes silent, and a pattern chapter on why the geography keeps doing this.

---

## Chapter 1: Uniontown → New York, spring 2010

### The move

The record here is unusually good, because it was made in public, in real time. Dan graduated Full Sail's recording-arts program in September 2009 — top five percent of his class, Pro Tools HD certified — and within four months he was aiming north. The @danfrank archive narrates the decision in a sequence tight enough to date every step:

- **January 25, 2010** — the Pro Tools exam failed once.
- **January 26, 2010, 19:40** — the retake passes: *"EAT UR HEART OUT BITCH IM PRO TOOLS CERTIFIED."*
- **January 26, 19:51** — eleven minutes later: *"Brooklyn here we come!!"*
- **February 17** — *"off to Williamsburg"* (the apartment hunt) and, seven hours on, *"this is the most stressful day/decision of my life."*
- **February 19** — *"COME STAY. be moved in by march 1."*
- **February 20** — *"moving to brooklyn in 9 days."*
- **February 27** — *"last day in errrlando…spending it how I always imagined: hangin in a u-haul store."*
- **February 28, 20:30** — *"peace out, florida. it's been real."*
- **March 24** — *"a new work email address. big day. update your contacts: dan@ishlab.com."*

Twenty-eight days from the credential to the departure; one minute of that mattered most, because the certification and the move decision are the same moment, eleven minutes apart. Dan left Florida on **February 28, 2010** and was living and working in Brooklyn by the third week of March. This closed a standing gap in the timeline: context-core's residence table had Brooklyn starting in April 2010 — a month late — and an earlier note had claimed a January 3 move with the Florida tail running through March. Both were wrong; the public archive corrects them.

### The trigger

The credential is the trigger and the ambition is the reason, but the motive underneath — why New York rather than staying — is undocumented as a stated decision. What is documented is the wound behind the direction. He was twenty-one, four years out from [[wiki/timeline/events/november-2005-rupture|November 2005]] — the paternal rehab admission and the maternal affair with the family dealer, delivered to him as a seventeen-year-old on the drive home from getting his learner's permit. The household record calls that event his life's hinge, and the first move is the first time he put physical distance between himself and it. The certification explains the *where*; the ambition explains the *why now*; the wound explains why the direction was away. The record supplies two of the three; the third is inference, and is labeled as such.

### The cost

The financial cost of the 2010 move is undocumented in dollar terms. There is a U-Haul implied by the Orlando tweet and no receipt anywhere on record. What it cost him in trajectory is clearer: the audio-engineering career he moved to build never stabilized. The ishlab stint produced a dan@ishlab.com address and an idle DJ controller; [[wiki/work/creative-license-exit|Creative License]] (2011–2012) produced a whistleblower disclosure, a payroll dispute, and a retaliatory false LinkedIn claim — capability entering an institution through the vertical axis and exiting through a fight about the record. The New York years were also the long cohabitation baseline: Alexis was present the entire NYC-1 stretch, her Bridge Cafe job running until Hurricane Sandy erased the Seaport outpost in October 2012. That cohabitation ends with the era, not after it.

### The ending

NYC-1 ends with the **May 2013** departure back to Uniontown — dated by context-core and memory, not by a primary record; the last post placing him in the city is an internship pitch from **November 2012**, and the first-person confirmation is a July 12, 2013 exchange where he writes *"i just moved back from NYC."* That is a statement of having moved, not a date for it. What ended the era is not documented as a single trigger — no eviction notice, no firing letter, no stated decision anywhere in the corpus. Say that plainly: the thing that ended the first New York phase is a gap in the record. The DJ controller from ishlab came home with him untouched, and *"by summer, I was dedicating my time to that."* The career didn't come back; the equipment did.

### Gaps

- The **dollar cost** of the 2010 move is undocumented. No moving receipts, no security-deposit figures, no first-month rent.
- The **why New York** beyond the ambition is unstated. The November 2005 reading is inference, not testimony.
- The **May 2013 departure's trigger** is unknown — nothing in the corpus explains the decision or the mechanism.
- The **May 2013 date itself** rests on memory and context-core; the dated record only confirms presence in Fayette County from mid-August 2013 and a first-person statement of having moved by July 12.

## Chapter 2: New York → Uniontown, May 2013

### The move

He moved back into 337 Saratoga Drive, the family home, and stayed through January 2015 before the move to 155 Virginia Avenue. The first and only first-person statement anywhere in the record is that July 12, 2013 line — *"i just moved back from NYC and i'm super output recently"* — which says, in eleven words, the two things this period is about: the geography changed, and the output restarted immediately. The return is the most-corrected fact in the timeline: an earlier framing filed 2013–2014 under "NYC" and described the producer years as a metropolitan chapter. The correction, forced by context-core and the tweet census (New York references fall from 10.2% of 2012 originals to 0.3% in 2013), places them in a Uniontown bedroom, which changes what they mean.

### The trigger

Unknown, as above — the trigger for the 2013 return is the same undocumented gap as the trigger for ending NYC-1, because they are the same event. One candidate texture, not a cause: by November 2012 the public record stops placing him in the city; by summer 2013 he is picking up the idle DJ controller. The shape suggests a career that ran out rather than a decision that happened — attrition, not rupture. But nothing in the corpus says that in so many words, and this page will not supply the sentence he never wrote.

### The cost

No dollar figure exists for the 2013 return either. The costs are legible in kind, not in currency:

1. **The career form.** The recording-arts ambition survived only by migrating medium — from the studio-floor audio-engineer path the first New York phase attempted to the bedroom-producer path of [[wiki/timeline/periods/uniontown-return-2013-2015|SLOPPP]] (2013–2015, at least 29 documented 2013 releases, sixteen titles in September 2013 alone) and the ~2014 handoff to MOGZART. The ambition kept its output and lost its institutional shell.
2. **The Alexis relationship.** The six-year relationship ran its endgame entirely inside the middle Uniontown interlude — the July 2013 Franki Faris detonation, the five-month concealment of 2014, and the November 2015 single-bond switch to Annie — and ended with *"11/30/2015 official break up"* captioned on a cleared Facebook relationship field. The NYC-1 cohabitation baseline did not survive the return.
3. **The drug architecture.** Per the later operator record, the $450-a-day cocaine spend runs "through this whole era" — the move put him back in the town where the supply network's earliest nodes were, not in a city where he had to build one.

### The ending

The middle Uniontown interlude ends on **February 9, 2019**, when he leaves for New York a second time — and like the first departure, the trigger for the exit is less documented than the exit itself. What is documented is that the Annie relationship, which the interlude incubated from Thanksgiving 2015, was the vehicle: they moved north together. What is not documented is why the timing landed on that February, or what broke the inertia of a household that had held him for nearly six years — through the poverty floor of 2017, the deep cycle of 2018, the entirety of the ledger years.

### Gaps

- The **2013 return's trigger** — same gap as Chapter 1's ending, stated twice because it matters twice.
- Any **dollar figure** for the return's cost: none on record.
- The **February 2019 departure's trigger**: undocumented. Nobody wrote down why *then*.

## Chapter 3: Uniontown → New York, February 9, 2019

### The move

His words, spoken the night the receipts were being read: *"We moved to New York in February, like early February, February 9th, 2019. Anything purchased from like January 1st up until that date was like getting ready to move to New York."* This is the one move in the whole sequence with a contemporaneous price tag — not because a moving company sent a bill, but because Dan's stated framing turns five weeks of Amazon receipts into a move-prep ledger, and the clusters confirm the framing.

This is the entry point to [[wiki/self/2019-spending-analysis|the 2019 ledger]], so the chapter states the items; the ledger carries the full per-order log:

- **January 1** — GoPro HERO7 Black ($443.08), 128GB SanDisk Extreme ($37.09). Day one of the year, the vlogging rig.
- **January 9, 3:06 AM** — Boosted Mini X, $1,076.95. The fleet's second board.
- **January 10** — three more Bern helmets ($96.40, $94.71; the collection reaches six across the year).
- **January 11** — 47-piece mechanic's tool set ($113.29), Nike sportswear ($179.39), chalk markers, art supplies.
- **January 13, 1:34–1:51 AM** — a BLACK+DECKER drill ($107.19), a bicycle helmet ($42.40), a longboard ($22.89), in seventeen minutes.
- **January 16** — two Meowijuana Purrple Passion catnip pouches ($21.18).
- **January 18** — a pet litter tray ($49.77).
- **January 25** — a longboard wall rack ($25.42): the fleet mounted on a wall that didn't exist yet, in a city he hadn't moved to.

The window holds 57 orders and **$4,757.24** — nearly half the year's entire Amazon total ($10,981.37) in roughly seven weeks. The 2019 analysis's verdict is the finding this chapter restates: the duplicates stop looking compulsive and start looking like planning — two Mini X's is redundancy, six helmets is a helmet for every mode of riding, the drill and the tool set and the wall rack are a man assembling an apartment in advance of arriving in it. The January binge is the most rational spending in either ledger; it just looks insane because it happened at 3 AM in bursts.

### The trigger

Undocumented. His framing explains the purchases, not the decision. What was documented within weeks of arrival: forty-five days after the move, on a weekend back in Pennsylvania, the Shelbie sequence began — the threesome, the infatuation, the April 18 refurbished MacBook ($793.94, *"I BOUGHT HER A MACBOOK LOL"*), the $4,000-of-exotic-drugs trip, the car-keys walk-off that ended it. That the move's trigger is not on record while its aftermath is documented in such detail is itself the finding: the obsession arrived before the address did, and the January receipts are its packing list.

### The cost

The documented move-prep cost is $4,757.24 across the seven-week window. The fuller ledger context: the 2018 receipts ($22,931.99 across five and a half months) are the same era's visible fraction, and underneath both years runs his own stated $450-a-day figure — *"through this whole era"* — on the order of $164,000 a year, while unemployed, in a year that also included a cross-state move. What ended the era's spending was not the move but the glide: after March 2019's second Boosted Mini X ($1,105.65), the receipts fall off a cliff — seven months at gift-card prices. New York did not get cheaper; he got quieter.

The second NYC era was also the era of the only long job in the record: [[wiki/work/au-zaatar-41-months|Au Za'atar]], March 2021 to August 2024 — 41 months, his longest tenure anywhere, the one boss he ever loved. The first New York phase had the ambition; the second had the employment. Both ended the same way: back south.

### The ending

The second New York era ends with the **February 22, 2025** forced exit from the apartment — and this one is documented to the mechanism. After the affair came to light in January 2025, the apartment went on a "shot clock": his account names roughly **$10,000** already owed plus a **$7,000 ConEd bill** — financial realities that made continuing to argue about staying in New York *"not really even worth the time it would take to debate."* The exit itself, per the later-record correction, was half of an arrangement he initiated: Dan called John Paci by phone in January, and Paci performed a landlord eviction. What the period page called a "forced exit" was his half of a choreography he authored. He landed alone at his mother's house — the same 337 Saratoga Drive he had moved back into in 2013, now with Suz's 2024 personal bankruptcy as the household's standing condition and the house itself listed for sale the same week.

### Gaps

- The **trigger for the 2019 move** is undocumented anywhere in the sources assembled. No stated decision, no job offer, no eviction from Uniontown.
- Whether the **March 25 Mini X** was a second concurrent board or a return-and-rebuy is inferred, not proven; returns are invisible to the receipt dataset.
- The **$4,757.24** is one mailbox's view (ihatedanfrank only); Dan has said dfrank88 carried purchase history too. The real move-prep figure is at least this, possibly more.
- The exact **February 22, 2025 exit date** comes from the period page's framing of the "forced exit"; the shot-clock address itself is not named in the ChatGPT self-analysis.

## Chapter 4: New York → Uniontown, February 2025

### The move

He landed at his mother's house in late February 2025 — the third time he has moved back to Uniontown in his adult life, the second time into 337 Saratoga Drive specifically. The landing was precarious from the first week: the childhood home — the one he called *"the single 'safe' place that existed in my life that i could crash land in"* — was listed for sale the same week the April 27 ChatGPT analysis was written, a full year before the sale process closed around June 2026 (~$465,000, contingent on the buyers' own bankruptcy approval). That listing was driven by Suz's 2024 personal bankruptcy — a full year earlier than the housing-arc section otherwise documented, meaning the house went through at least two listing attempts across 2025–2026 before it finally sold.

He is currently at **463 Morgantown Street**, with no signed lease — housing unsettled as of the writing of this entry, with the contractor exposures (the Arnu lien, John Carney unreachable since March 2026) sitting on top of it.

### The trigger

The affair discovery of January 2025 plus the apartment's financial reality — the documented trigger, for once. Annie moved unilaterally to her parents' house rather than accepting his open-ended offer to fund an apartment anywhere she chose; he left alone. The same pursuit/withdrawal pattern the terminal-phase statistics later measured numerically was already fully present in the April representative week — the pattern was stable across the whole terminal window, not something that developed gradually.

### The cost

In dollars, his stated numbers: ~$10,000 owed on the apartment, $7,000 to ConEd, the eviction theater with Paci. In kind: the second New York era — the longest stretch of adult stability in the record, the 41-month job, the 2019–2025 cohabitation that was the last long form of the Annie relationship before its terminal phase — ended in a choreographed eviction and a crash-landing in a house that was itself on the market. The relationship's terminal-phase record (Aug 2025 onward) sits entirely in the Uniontown frame: the move did not reset the pattern, it relocated it.

### The ending

None. This chapter is open. The housing situation — 463 Morgantown St, no signed lease — is the present tense of the article. The middle interlude's ending was a departure north; this one has no departure on the horizon that the record names.

### Gaps

- The **exact landing date** at 337 Saratoga Drive in late February 2025 is not pinned by a primary record; it follows from the February 22 exit date plus the ChatGPT timeline's account.
- The **463 Morgantown Street arrangement** — when he moved there, from where, on what terms — is undocumented in the sources assembled. The "no signed lease" fact is the only housing-term detail the record gives.
- The **dollar cost of the physical move itself** (trucks, movers, deposits) is undocumented, as with every other chapter.
- Suz's **2024 bankruptcy filing** is corroborated only by Dan's own statement in the ChatGPT conversation, not by a legal-record source.

## The pattern: oscillation as the geographic form of the obsession lifecycle

Read the four chapters as one motion and the move sequence is not four decisions. It is one cycle, executed four times: **accumulate north, collapse south, regroup south, accumulate north again.** The geography is the shape the obsession lifecycle takes when it needs a bigger container than a timestamp can hold.

The [[wiki/self/two-year-synthesis|two-year synthesis]] names the cluster as the unit of purchase — something gets decided, usually at night, and then it gets executed completely, immediately, across as many carts as it takes. The three-phase move is the same unit at a larger scale. The January 2019 move-prep binge is literally the same behavior as the sex-toy clusters of the same month (the January 10 run: $189.17 in eighteen minutes, 12:53 to 1:11 AM) — the 2019 analysis's thesis is that impulsivity and planning share a timestamp, and the move-prep window is the proof: drill, tool set, helmets, wall rack, the most rational spending in either ledger, bought at 3 AM in bursts. The two Dans of January, side by side.

Each northbound move coincides with an ambition that needs new infrastructure. 2010: the Pro Tools certification and the audio-engineering career, eleven minutes apart. 2019: the vlogging rig on January 1, the board fleet, the wall rack for a wall that didn't exist yet. The ambition is real each time — this is not the failure-to-launch story told carelessly; the Full Sail credential was earned, the Au Za'atar tenure was the longest job in the record, the board fleet got ridden. What the moves show is not that the ambition fails but that it has a geographic half-life: about three years the first time, about six the second, and each southbound return is the ambition being carried home to be re-bodied — as SLOPPP in 2013, as the glide in 2025.

The southbound moves share a different signature: none of them has a documented trigger except the last one, and the last one only has one because Dan wrote it down for a chatbot. The 2013 return's trigger is a gap; the 2025 return's trigger is an affair, a shot clock, and an eviction he arranged himself. The asymmetry is the pattern's thesis: the northbound moves are documented because they are *projects* — announced, receipted, clustered — and the southbound moves are undocumented because they are *collapses*, and collapses don't keep ledgers. The receipts record what was wanted; the silence records what couldn't be borne.

And the final observation is the ledger's, not the wiki's: the $450-a-day figure he stated himself — *"through this whole era"* — means every one of these moves happened inside an economy of roughly $164,000 a year that no receipt dataset can see. The documented costs — $4,757.24 of move-prep, $10,000 owed plus $7,000 to ConEd — are the visible tips. The three-phase move is also the shape of the habit's hardware: the drill and the tool set were 2019's logistics the way the stash safes and spy bolts were 2018's, and the ledger can't tell the difference. The geography moved; the architecture didn't.

## Limits of this page

- **No physical-move dollar figures exist for any chapter.** The move-prep ledger is the only priced move in the sequence; it prices the packing, not the moving.
- **Two of the four moves have no documented trigger** (2010's stated decision beyond the ambition; 2013's return). The 2019 trigger is his alone and unstated; the 2025 trigger is his and documented.
- **The February 9, 2019 date is operator testimony**, given thirteen days before this entry was written — reliable by his standard, not by a receipt's.
- **The 463 Morgantown St situation** is reported from his standing context as of September 2026; the arrangement's terms are otherwise unexamined.
- This page does not re-litigate the alias years, the bond switch, or the terminal phase — those have their own entries ([[wiki/timeline/periods/uniontown-return-2013-2015]], [[wiki/timeline/periods/feb-apr-2025-return-and-rupture]], [[wiki/self/2018-spending-analysis]], [[wiki/self/2019-spending-analysis]]) — it frames them as the interiors the moves bracket.
