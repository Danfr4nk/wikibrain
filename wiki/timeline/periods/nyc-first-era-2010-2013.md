# NYC, First Era (2010–2013)

Three years, two apartments, the audio-engineering career attempt, and the
first long cohabitation of his adult life. Dan's first New York City period
ran **March 2010 to May 2013** — it opens directly off the [[wiki/work/full-sail-2008-2009|Full Sail]]
tail, with a Pro Tools certification and a one-line announcement, and it
closes not with a New York event but with a **departure**: the May 2013
return to Uniontown, which the old timeline framing misfiled as "later NYC"
for years. This is the container era. Inside it: the [[wiki/work/creative-license-exit|Creative License]] job and its
whistleblower ending, the birth of the trap-era production impulse that
would become [[wiki/interests/music/trap-era|SLOPPP]] at the era's tail, the
start of the sixteen-year Suboxone chassis, and the Twitter voice — the
weaponized-irony online persona that seeds the "ihatedanfrank" identity —
taking its mature shape.

The era is also the wiki's **coverage-asymmetry exhibit**. The second NYC
era (2019–25) is documented to the dollar — an Amazon order ledger shows
the 2019 move-prep down to Boosted boards, a wall rack, helmets, a drill,
and cat gear ([[wiki/self/2019-spending-analysis]]), and the
[[wiki/self/two-year-synthesis]] can reconstruct years almost
week-by-week. The first era has **no message corpus at all** — the earliest
real-text rows in any on-disk export begin November 12, 2015, two and a half
years after NYC-1 ended. Everything here is reconstructed from **764 dated
public tweets** (a live-scrape floor, not a complete export), the Facebook
record (posts, likes, message threads that reach back into 2011), Dan's own
Gmail from the Creative License dispute, and testimony he gave years later
in context-core and the Gemini-_58 session. That is the honest shape of
this article: a period told from outside the head, from what was posted,
checked in, and filed — with the gaps named rather than papered over.

## The move in — dated to the day

The relocation is narrated in real time by the @danfrank archive, and the
sequence is tight enough to date every step. It begins with a credential,
not a plan. On **25 January 2010**, Dan failed the Pro Tools certification
exam: *"ughhhhhhhhhhh missed my 210m by 3 questions."* On **26 January at
19:40 UTC**, the retake passed: *"EAT UR HEART OUT BITCH IM PRO TOOLS
CERTIFIED."* **Eleven minutes later**, at 19:51: *"Brooklyn here we come!!"*
The credential and the decision to move are one moment — the period's
causal hinge is a test score, and it is timestamped.

What follows, in the tweets:

| When (UTC) | The tweet | What it fixes |
|---|---|---|
| 2010-02-17 13:32 | *"off to Williamsburg"* | The apartment hunt — a scouting trip, not the move |
| 2010-02-17 20:07 | *"this is the most stressful day/decision of my life"* | Same day, seven hours on (see below) |
| 2010-02-19 04:38 | *"COME STAY. be moved in by march 1."* | The target date, stated to a friend |
| 2010-02-20 03:15 | *"moving to brooklyn in 9 days."* | Counts to 1 March independently |
| 2010-02-27 19:02 | *"last day in errrlando…spending it how I always imagined: hangin in a u-haul store."* | The last Florida day |
| 2010-02-28 20:30 | *"peace out, florida. it's been real."* | **The departure** |
| 2010-03-24 18:35 | *"a new work email address. big day. update your contacts: [email redacted]"* | First day on the ishlab address |

Dan left Florida on **28 February 2010** and was living and working in
Brooklyn by the third week of March — the first day on the ishlab studio
address dates the start of the only window in his life where the
recording-arts training was actually the job. The corroboration matters
more than the correction: Facebook's own **work** history starts ishlab in
**March 2010**, produced years apart by a different mechanism, and agrees
with the tweets against Facebook's own self-entered *places lived* field,
which claims January 3, 2010 and is wrong by eight weeks — on 20 February
2010 Dan was still counting down nine days to a move that had not happened.
Where a self-reported field and a timestamped one disagree here, the
timestamp has won — worth remembering the next time a `places lived` date
is the only source for something. The move is the second of the
[[wiki/timeline/periods/three-phase-move]] — the first documented
leave-and-return in a life that would make the pattern a habit.

Two things about the move are still open, and they are stated as open
rather than filled. The **trigger** at the level of *why Brooklyn, why
now* — the deeper motive behind the eleven-minute celebration — is
undocumented: the tweets give a credential and a declaration, not a plan,
a lease, or a roommate arrangement. And the **financing** — who paid for
the U-Haul, the first month's rent at 424 Bedford Ave, the deposit — is
never named in the record. Context-core carries a later parallel: in 2009
Suz bought the Winter Park condo Dan lived in outright rather than renting
it, the earliest documented instance of her using real property rather than
cash to support him. Whether the same mechanism underwrote the Brooklyn
move is unconfirmed. The move's machinery is visible; its funding is not.

**The 17 February mystery.** Three tweets on the 17th — the apartment-hunt
scouting trip — land on the day the wiki dates day zero of the Suboxone
regimen that has run without a break ever since. At 13:32: *"off to
Williamsburg."* At 16:44: *"@alexisarmel welcome home"* with a photo. Then
at **20:07 UTC** — 15:07 New York time:

> *"this is the most stressful day/decision of my life. nothing is making
> it easier. ugh laying down for a little bit."*

The next morning he is *"On the train back to PA... can't wait to get home
and see babygirl"*, posting *"smart has plans, stupid has stories."* Two
days later: *"planning the next 3 years of my life all day in muh head"*,
*"laying down because thats the only thing i cant mess up"*, *"put my life
on a treadmill. shape up or bust."*

What that decision was, he never says — and the obvious candidate is ruled
out by the record: the Brooklyn move had been settled twenty-two days
earlier, and every mention of it is unambiguously good news. Nobody calls a
decision already made and celebrated the most stressful of their life three
weeks later. What remains is a decision he was unwilling to name in public,
taken on the exact day the wiki dates the start of sixteen years of opioid
maintenance, in the same week his own account describes as the last of
active use. The date's provenance is itself a finding: it is **computed,
not remembered** — a model reading email logs told Dan the 18 February
train was the outbound leg, Dan derived day zero as the day before, and the
tweet archive shows the 18 February train was actually the **return** leg.
The derivation is broken; the dated alignment — a same-day tweet about the
most stressful decision of his life — survives its own reasoning as the
stronger evidence. See the forensic appendix for the full accounting.

## Two apartments, three years

The residence timeline is **424 Bedford Ave (Williamsburg, Brooklyn),
March 2010 – March 2012**, then **Manhattan Upper East Side, 90th between
3rd and Lexington, March 2012 – May 2013**. The second move is dated by two
consecutive tweets that need no interpretation:

> **2012-03-29 19:40** — *"Goodbye, Brooklyn!"*
> **2012-03-30 19:55** — *"Hello, Manhattan."*

The check-ins follow immediately and confirm the neighbourhood: Osso Buco
UES on 31 March, then New York Sports Club, Josie's West and Le Pain
Quotidien through the first ten days of April, all Manhattan — after two
years in which every check-in had been Brooklyn.

The Williamsburg years have a geography of their own, and it is precise
because Dan was posting Foursquare check-ins with street addresses between
March 2010 and January 2011 — **fifteen of them**, the only address-level
location data the corpus holds for any year before the Google Timeline
export begins in April 2014. They cluster in a few blocks of south
Williamsburg: the Bedford Ave deli at S. 2nd, the c-town at South 1st and
Havemeyer, the Walgreens at 210 Union Ave, the Marcy Ave J/M/Z platform.
Ako Japanese at 205 Bedford and Peter Luger on Broadway are the
neighbourhood's restaurants; DuMont Burger recurs as the one he names
before arriving (*"zomg i can taste the dumont burger already"*, 11 March
2010, written from a studio session in Florida three days before the train
north). Beyond Williamsburg the pins are Brooklyn Bridge Park, Grimaldi's
in DUMBO, **Electric Zoo on Randall's Island** (5 September 2010, "w/ 115
others") and Hibernia on W 50th.

The cultural texture of the era is dense in the Facebook likes record: the
chiptune-to-EDM progression (Anamanaguchi → Flying Lotus →
ODESZA/JAUZ) — the indie and electronic vocabularies run side by side
through 2010 rather than in sequence, Grizzly Bear and Arcade Fire in the
same months as Diplo, Electric Zoo, Ableton warping, and a published
dubstep mix — and the **LCD Soundsystem MSG farewell (April 2011)** as a
dated attendance anchor. The Twitter voice of these years is the mature
form of the weaponized irony and aesthetic signaling that becomes the
linguistic fingerprint — the same online persona that seeds the
"ihatedanfrank" identity later. It runs at full volume with old friends
too: a March 2010 Office in-joke to Eric Jester — *"remember when he stole
'shut up Oscar!' from us in s5 of the office? do sumthan bout it"* — that
neither man explains because neither has to, seven years before the
message thread with Eric begins.

Three firsts fall inside the opening weeks. On the night of **23 January
2010**, three tweets date Dan's **first tattoo**, identify it as Alexis's
initial, and catch the plan moving from *"monday"* to *"NOW!"* in sixteen
minutes. In December the nicotine arc opens: *"today is day 1 with no
tobacco"* (13 December) announces a switch to an e-cigarette as a quit —
*"(un) hooked"* corrected mid-sentence by the man writing it — and
*"1 week smoke free!"* on 10 January 2011 dates a fresh day one to roughly
the 3rd, so the attempt broke inside three weeks and the relapse appears
nowhere except as a restarted counter. And in August 2010, in the recovered
Dan↔Suz SMS batch, Dan hears that **Bill Kubia** — a drug-era acquaintance
— has died of an overdose, and replies *"so glad I'm not there."* He is six
months into the maintenance chassis, twenty-one, two boroughs from the life
that killed him. The relief is the honest register; there is no eulogy.
The same SMS window carries **Kenney**, Suz's 2010 boyfriend — present
enough in the family orbit to have met both Fran Coldren and Diane Moore —
and two names still open: an **"Alexa"** (Jan–Aug 2010) and a **"Zach"**
(possibly Zachariah Harshman, unconfirmed), both identity-unresolved.

## The household

**Jamie Mohler** — the Full Sail friend — is confirmed by a 2011 Gchat log
as a regular presence in the Brooklyn household during the Occupy Wall
Street autumn, the primary-source anchor for the operator's account of who
was there. Per the operator, Jamie was also present the day Dan first met
**Menore** — the original introduction to the dealer who becomes the supply
network's most reliable node, years before the documented 2018
reconnection. That specific claim remains operator-attested; no message
thread documents the meeting itself. The Full Sail cohort follows into the
era in reduced form — Jason Bermejo, Eric Jester, Matt Dunn, the annual
$5 birthday Venmo bit already running — and **Ally** surfaces as a 2013
message burst, a channel from the 18-year attachment reopening inside the
era's last months.

## The audio-career attempt — and its ending

Professionally this is the studio-work era, the one window in Dan's life
where the recording-arts training was actually the job, and it did not
convert into a durable career. The thread runs **ishlab** (2010–11) —
an internship/engineer role at Ishlab Studios under Jamin Gilbert, 10 Jay
Street, DUMBO, still undocumented beyond the mentions — into
**[[wiki/work/creative-license-exit|Creative License]]** (April 2011 – February 2012), a small
celebrity-talent and licensed-music brokering company at 71 8th Avenue in
the West Village, where Dan worked as "Special Projects Coordinator"
under company president **Kevin McKiernan**. (The résumé later flatters the
title to "Executive Assistant / Office Manager"; LinkedIn's own 2012
notice uses the contemporaneous title, which settles it.)

The exit is this era's most documented event after the move — a four-act
sequence reconstructed from ~35 real 2011–2012 Gmail threads the operator
knew existed and a dedicated pass recovered. **Act zero:** Dan's stated
reason for quitting, in his own later words — *"the thing i cited when i
quit had to do with some kind of small fraud with airplane tickets that i
blew it up into a much much bigger thing but maybe i was just
desensitized."* The airfare citation is confirmed as cited; its specific
mechanism is unverified, and Dan's own self-doubt about whether he blew it
up is part of the record, not a later gloss. **Act one — the payroll
dispute (Feb–Apr 2012):** last day on or before 15 February 2012, flagged
by a $104 MetroCard charge Dan says he never requested, on a check that
arrived unsigned; six weeks with accounting officer Marty Jackson —
*"It's a trust issue. We don't trust you."* — escalated by Dan to the IRS
and the NYS Department of Labor, with his father Rick operating in the
corpus's clearest instance of tactical-coach register: *"You sound so
desperate. That is what kevin wants. We'll talk about a strategy for
this."* **Act two — the catharsis (April 26, 2012):** a one-line email to
a former coworker with an attachment titled `BEST OF KTM VOICEMAILS.mp3`.
**Act three — the whistleblower disclosure (May 2012):** unprompted,
months after the dispute closed, Dan wrote to former CL contact Renae
Holland that McKiernan had used Dan's own account to pressure former
employees and had inserted a CEO-approval gag clause into departing
interns' contracts — *"I think that the two interns should be aware of
this for their own legal protection and because it's just disgustingly
wrong"* — with Renae corroborating independently from her own tenure:
*"in my time there I was Kevin falsify more documents that I can count —
I know what he is capable of."* **Act four — retaliation (June 2012):**
McKiernan filed a formal LinkedIn intellectual-property complaint against
Dan's profile, backed by two documents notarized by Director of Business
Affairs Katherine Palakovich asserting Dan had never been a Creative
License employee at all; Dan filed a counter-notice offering *"paystubs,
tax documentation, or any other official documentation"* and named the
motive: *"Mr. McKiernan has and continues to attempt to smear my name
because of my actions as a whistleblower while under his employ."*

The full account belongs on [[wiki/work/creative-license-exit]]; the
period-level fact is the shape: a trusted vertical figure's undocumented
financial claim met with exit and documentation rather than confrontation —
the founding case of the vertical-authority pattern that recurs fourteen
years later at BFS Foods, the corpus's own naming of the first and most
recent instances of one shape. And the postscript that neither cancels the
other: Dan kept McKiernan's contact information for close to a decade, and
in December 2014 Palakovich — by then leaving the company herself — bcc'd
Dan on a mass "Ex-Creative License Happy Hour" invitation. Two and a half
years after a conflict serious enough to involve the IRS and a notarized
false statement, he was still on the alumni list. Both are true.

The post-February 2012 employment trail is thin. The Gemini-_58 session
(unconfirmed against email) describes Craigslist applications to EA/Office
Manager roles across Manhattan — which means the departure was probably
also an ordinary job change in progress, not purely a dramatic break,
consistent with his own later doubt about whether the airfare complaint was
proportionate. No audio work of comparable seriousness recurs until the
much later GRIPNOTIC production reactivation. The thread simply ends with
the 2013 departure.

## Alexis, the cohabitation, and the Sandy shock

**Alexis was there for all of it** — *"with me the whole time we were in
nyc,"* per the Gemini-_58 reconstruction — the relationship begun at Full
Sail (Thanksgiving 2009) running its middle chapters through Brooklyn and
the Upper East Side. This is the first long cohabitation of his adult life,
and the baseline the 2015 single-bond switch is later measured against.
Alexis worked at the historic **Bridge Cafe** (279 Water St, the city's
oldest continuously operating tavern) — a job that started in the Bedford
Ave years and continued into the 90th Street year — until **Hurricane
Sandy's** October 2012 flooding of the Seaport district closed it, ending
her employment: a real economic shock to the household roughly six months
before the return to Uniontown, and worth noting as landing during the
Manhattan address's occupancy rather than the earlier Brooklyn years,
where it is sometimes loosely attributed.

A brief **2013 Franki blip** — *"I really didn't have the romantic feelings
for franki that i said i did at the time"* — resolved back to Alexis,
rehearsing the slot mechanics the 2015 single-bond analysis makes
explicit. (The July 2013 Franki fireworks interlude itself belongs to the
[[wiki/timeline/periods/uniontown-return-2013-2015|Uniontown return]], weeks
after the departure, not to this era.)

## The two throughlines that start here and do not stop

Two throughlines of the whole corpus originate in this window. The
**Suboxone regimen** begins at its front edge — and this era is where the
record is thinnest and the convergence most interesting. The wiki dates
day zero to **17 February 2010**, on the broken-derivation alignment
described above. But two independent channels say the year without
reaching for it. A Facebook message dated **2011-08-04** has Dan writing
outbound: *"I was supposed to go to my Suboxone appointment today, so I
haven't gotten anything since Saturday night. I got one of those At-Home
test kits just to make sure I'd pass the test that they give me there,
and apparently I did just a liiittttle bit too much"* — and asking the
recipient for clean urine. In 2011 the regimen involved **appointments and
urine screening** — monitored treatment, not the pharmacy-handoff shape
the later record describes. And on **2013-07-10** he writes *"i've been on
maintainence for 3.5 years,"* *"i was on the strips when i had a doctor in
nyc"* (past tense — the NYC doctor is already over), *"and i'm down to
2mg/day."* Counting back 3.5 years from July 2013 gives roughly January
2010. The honest form of the claim is temporal: **a prescriber in New
York, monitored in 2011, 2mg/day by July 2013** — and the transition to
the logistics-only shape documented in the 2019–2025 messages is undated.

The second throughline is the **producer identity** — and its placement
requires care, because this is the finding the old timeline got wrong by
filing under "later NYC." **SLOPPP**, Dan's first named production alias,
launches in **2013** — the same year as the May departure — and peaks in
2013–14: weekly mix series ("SLOPPP's Week in Trap"), the signature
"SLOPPPED N SCREWED" reworks, bootleg reworks of the trap-saturation
era's biggest names (Baauer, Major Lazer, Zeds Dead, Caked Up), the
involuntary 63–85% sub-bass signature already present from the earliest
material. The 2013 Twitter archive carries 29 distinct titles, sixteen of
them in September 2013 alone, with the self-undercutting that came with
them — *"#overcompensating #poseur #fakefuture"* posted on an announcement
at the peak. The September burst is **post-return** — September 2013 is
Uniontown — so the canonical placement of the alias development is the
[[wiki/timeline/periods/uniontown-return-2013-2015|Uniontown return]], and
this page holds that correction rather than quietly benefiting from the
era's glamour: the recording-arts ambition survived the NYC-career failure
by migrating to a Uniontown bedroom and a free hosting platform. But the
**birth** of the identity straddles the seam — 2013 material begins in
the era's last months — and the trap era's 2013–14 run sits at this
period's tail in the honest, overlapping sense: the impulse is NYC-1's,
the output is Uniontown's.

## The departure — and what isn't known about it

The period closes with the **May 2013 departure to Uniontown** — the
return that the Twitter record's "2013 systemic rupture" actually marks
(the rupture was the leaving, not a New York event). New York references
run 8.8%, 8.9% and 10.2% of originals across 2010, 2011 and 2012 and then
fall to **0.3% in 2013**; the last tweet placing him in the city is the 18
November 2012 internship pitch (*"living in NYC"*). That is the right
shape for a spring 2013 departure, and it is not a date — **May 2013 rests
on context-core**, and on the July 2013 line to EJ Rags: *"i just moved
back... and i'm super output recently."*

The **reason is undocumented.** Unlike the move in — credential, eleven
minutes, U-Haul, day-stamped — the move out has no mechanism on record.
The Sandy shock (October 2012) is a documented household-economic event
six months before the departure and a natural candidate explanation, but
the corpus never connects it to the leaving in any row, and the wiki does
not make the connection for it. The post-Creative-License job trail is
thin but present (Craigslist EA/Office Manager applications in 2012); what
it looked like by spring 2013 is unknown. The return goes back into **337
Saratoga Drive**, the family home — the same address the
[[wiki/timeline/events/november-2005-rupture|November 2005 rupture]] had
made the life's hinge — and three years later the geography gives way to
the [[wiki/work/nemacolin-caddie-years|Nemacolin caddie years]] (2016–19),
the Uniontown arc's other pole: the studio career's failure landing in the
golf course's three-year tenure.

The old framing's error is worth one more sentence, because it is the
kind of error this wiki exists to kill. For years the timeline carried
"NYC Round One" as **2010–2014** and described Alexis as "met through
webcam work." Both are corrected against context-core: NYC-1 ended **May
2013**, not 2014 — the alias development and the Alexis endgame happened
in Pennsylvania, not New York — and Alexis was in-network from the Full
Sail era, not met later. The prior "NYC-1 2010–2016" framing was an
inference error from undated Twitter content. Geography changes what a
period means; getting it wrong moved four years of creative and
relational reorganization into the wrong city.

## The mirror: 2019–25 and the coverage asymmetry

The second NYC era (2019–25) is the control against which this era's
silence can be measured — and the comparison is a finding, not a lament.
The 2019 move-prep is documented **to the dollar in the Amazon order
ledger**: Boosted boards, a wall rack, helmets, a drill, cat gear, all
dated in the order log that the [[wiki/self/2019-spending-analysis]]
reads as move-prep, with ~$450/day in concurrent unstated cocaine spend
behind it. The 2010 move-prep is documented to the **tweet** — the U-Haul
store, the nine-day countdown — and the financing behind the tweets is
never named. One era's relocation is a line-item; the other's is a vibe
with a truck.

The asymmetry is not an artifact of carelessness; it is an artifact of
**which channels survived.** The 2019–25 era has the iMessage exports, the
Google Timeline (from April 2014 — a year after NYC-1 ended), the Amazon
ledgers, the Voice record. The 2010–13 era has the public tweet archive,
the Facebook export (which reaches back into 2011 in messages, unlike the
iMessage corpus), and the operator's own email and testimony. What changed
the era's coverage on 2026-09-02 is the lesson: until then the page said it
rested on *"context-core, the Facebook record, and the Gemini-_58
reconstruction, not on primary message data"*; the 764 dated originals
closed the move-date gap the page had carried for six weeks and moved both
apartment dates earlier. The next coverage jump is sitting in the open:
Google Voice history back to 2009 — Dan's own number — is the live lead
for the missing voice of this era, and the operator has greenlit a full
export. If it delivers, NYC-1 gets its first private channel; until then,
the tweets are the only first-party record it has, and this page repeats
rather than quietly benefits from the two limits the archive states about
itself: 2010–2012 are **live-scrape reconstructions**, not a complete
export (the operator spreadsheet does not begin until 2013-08-17, so the
yearly counts are floors, not totals), and the archive is
**originals-only** (pure reposts excluded), so a quiet stretch means quiet
in things Dan wrote, not silence on the account.

## What the era means

NYC-1 is the first complete cycle of the life's central shape: the
leave-and-return. Dan leaves Uniontown on a credential and a declaration
("Brooklyn here we come!!"), attempts the career the degree pointed at,
fails to convert it, and comes back to the family home — carrying the
ambition in a new form. The [[wiki/timeline/periods/three-phase-move]] reads
the moves as grammar; this is the move where the grammar is first written.
Everything after is a rewrite: the 2019 return to New York carries the
same studio ambition in a bedroom rather than a studio, the same drug
regimen in its logistics-only shape, the same Twitter voice now filed
under a corporate handle.

The era's most human fact is the smallest one. Six months into the city,
Dan gets the news about Bill Kubia and writes *"so glad I'm not there."*
He is twenty-one, on Suboxone, in Williamsburg, at the start of the only
job his degree ever got him. He does not go back. He does not eulogize.
He stays. That is the whole era in one line: he got out — of the town,
of the life that was killing people he knew — and stayed out for three
years, and then went home anyway.

   Both ends of the 90th-st claim are therefore stale; the Twitter-dated
   version is the one this page carries. No row decides between April and

2. **The Suboxone day zero.** The wiki carries **17 February 2010** as
   pinned, from a same-day tweet about the most stressful decision of
   Dan's life. The date's provenance is a broken derivation (the 18 Feb

   survives as stronger evidence than the derivation. Independent
   brackets: a 2011-08-04 monitored appointment (Facebook) and *"3.5
   years"* in July 2013, both consistent with January–February 2010.
   Legacy docs once said January 2011; context-core flags the one-year

3. **The Creative License chronology.** Dan's own résumé gives "Jan 2011 –
