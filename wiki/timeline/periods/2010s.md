# NYC Round One (2010–2013)

Dan's first New York City period ran **March 2010 to May 2013** — three
years, two apartments, the audio-engineering career attempt, and the
first long cohabitation of his adult life, with [[wiki/people/alexis-armel|Alexis]].
It opens off the [[wiki/timeline/periods/full-sail-2008-2010|Full Sail]]
tail — he left Florida on **28 February 2010**, not "through March" — and
closes not with a New York event but with a **departure**: the May 2013
return to Uniontown that context-core flags as the timeline's most
load-bearing correction. Two things start here and never stop: the
Suboxone maintenance chassis, and the Twitter voice — the weaponized
irony that hardens into the linguistic fingerprint and seeds the
"ihatedanfrank" identity.

The period is told from outside the head. No message corpus reaches back
this far — the earliest real-text rows in any on-disk export begin
November 12, 2015, two and a half years after NYC-1 ended. What exists
instead is **764 dated @danfrank originals** (265 from the February 2010
departure to year end, 225 in 2011, 205 in 2012, 69 in January–May 2013),
written contemporaneously and in public, plus the Facebook export (posts,
likes, message threads that reach back into 2011), Dan's own Gmail from
the Creative License dispute, photographs he supplied in September 2026,
and testimony he gave years later in the Gemini-_58 session and
context-core. That is the honest shape of this article: a period
reconstructed from what was posted, checked in, filed, and photographed
— with the gaps named rather than papered over.

## The move in — dated to the day

The relocation is narrated in real time by the @danfrank archive, and
the sequence is tight enough to date every step. It begins with a
credential, not a plan:

| When (UTC) | The tweet | What it fixes |
|---|---|---|
| 2010-01-25 18:41 | "ughhhhhhhhhhh missed my 210m by 3 questions." | The Pro Tools exam is failed once, on the 25th |
| 2010-01-26 19:40 | "EAT UR HEART OUT BITCH IM PRO TOOLS CERTIFIED" | The retake passes — the certification is **26 January 2010** |
| 2010-01-26 19:51 | "Brooklyn here we come!!" | **Eleven minutes later.** The credential and the decision to move are one moment |
| 2010-02-17 13:32 | "off to Williamsburg" | The apartment hunt — a scouting trip, not the move |
| 2010-02-17 20:07 | "this is the most stressful day/decision of my life" | Same day, seven hours on (see below) |
| 2010-02-19 04:38 | "COME STAY. be moved in by march 1." | The target date, stated to a friend |
| 2010-02-20 03:15 | "moving to brooklyn in 9 days." | Counts to 1 March independently |
| 2010-02-27 19:02 | "last day in errrlando…spending it how I always imagined: hangin in a u-haul store." | The last Florida day |
| 2010-02-28 20:30 | "peace out, florida. it's been real." | **The departure** |
| 2010-03-24 18:35 | "a new work email address. big day. update your contacts: dan@ishlab.com" | First day on the ishlab address |

The corroboration matters more than the correction. Facebook's own
**work** history starts ishlab in **March 2010** — produced years apart
by a different mechanism — and agrees with the tweets against Facebook's
own self-entered *places lived* field, which claims January 3, 2010 and
is wrong by eight weeks: on 20 February 2010 Dan was still counting down
nine days to a move that had not happened. Where a self-reported field
and a timestamped one disagree here, the timestamp has won — worth
remembering the next time a `places lived` date is the only source for
something.

Two things about the move are still open, and they are stated as open
rather than filled. The **trigger** at the level of *why Brooklyn, why
now* — the deeper motive behind the eleven-minute celebration — is
undocumented: the tweets give a credential and a declaration, not a plan,
a lease, or a roommate arrangement. And the **financing** — who paid for
the U-Haul, the first month's rent at 424 Bedford Ave, the deposit — is
never named outright in the record. What exists is a partial lead: a
2011 Gchat exchange on the Bedford line has Dan's father Rick floating a
standing offer of $2,000 a month "while you intern," pitched as the
alternative to an Entourage-style West Hollywood move, and Rick's
physical presence at the lease signing (see below) is consistent with a
parent in the guarantor's seat for a 21-year-old's first New York lease.
But an offer in a later chat and a pen in a hand are not the same as a
money trail, and the wiki does not make that jump. The move's machinery
is visible; its funding is not.

The move is the second of the [[wiki/timeline/periods/three-phase-move]] —
the first documented leave-and-return in a life that would make the
pattern a habit.

## The 17 February decision

Three tweets on 17 February 2010 — the apartment-hunt scouting trip —
land on the day the wiki dates day zero of the Suboxone regimen that has
run without a break ever since. At 13:32: *"off to Williamsburg."* At
16:44: *"@alexisarmel welcome home"* with a photo. Then at **20:07 UTC**
— 15:07 New York time:

> *"this is the most stressful day/decision of my life. nothing is making
> it easier. ugh laying down for a little bit."*

The next morning he is *"On the train back to PA... can't wait to get
home and see babygirl"*, posting *"smart has plans, stupid has
stories."* Two days later: *"planning the next 3 years of my life all
day in muh head"*, *"laying down because thats the only thing i cant
mess up"*, *"put my life on a treadmill. shape up or bust."*

What that decision was, he never says — and the obvious candidate is
ruled out by the record: the Brooklyn move had been settled twenty-two
days earlier, and every mention of it is unambiguously good news. Nobody
calls a decision already made and celebrated the most stressful of their
life three weeks later. What remains is a decision he was unwilling to
name in public, taken on the exact day the wiki dates the start of
sixteen years of opioid maintenance, in the same week his own account
describes as the last of active use.

The date's provenance is itself a finding and has to be stated plainly.
It is **computed, not remembered** — a model reading email logs told Dan
the 18 February train was the outbound leg, Dan derived day zero as the
day before, and the tweet archive shows the 18 February train was
actually the **return** leg. The derivation is broken; the dated
alignment — a same-day tweet about the most stressful decision of his
life, written at 15:07 New York time on the only date in the window that
carries an unnamed decision — survives its own reasoning as the stronger
evidence. The full accounting lives in the forensic layer of the
[parallel era page](wiki/timeline/periods/nyc-first-era-2010-2013.md); the
period-level fact is the alignment, not the arithmetic.

## The lease signing, photographed sixteen years later

Two photographs supplied by Dan on 2026-09-25 capture the lease signing
itself — the first images the corpus has ever carried of the move's
machinery. The first shows the Zazza building in Williamsburg under its
"Premium Rental Residences" leasing banner; the second shows his father
**Rick** inside the unit, writing on a clipboard at the kitchen counter.

Rick's presence at the signing is new. The article has always noted the
financing as the move's one unnamed element — who paid for the U-Haul,
the first month, the deposit — and his being physically in the room, pen
in hand, is consistent with a parent in the guarantor's seat for a
21-year-old's first New York lease. That is as far as the photos take
it: they document his involvement, not the money's source. (The young man
at the edge of the building-exterior shot is the leasing agent — Dan
recalled the name as Robin and described him as a limey, i.e. British,
the agent they spent the scouting day with; the name is his uncertain
recall, carried as testimony, not fact.)

Five more photographs from the same 2026-09-25 batch — captioned only
"Brooklyn era" — are the first interior views the corpus has ever
carried of 424 Bedford Ave. The living room sits under corner windows
looking over Williamsburg rooftops: black leather couch, a TV on a small
stand, a wooden radio cabinet, and moving boxes still out — the
just-moved-in months. The couch shot catches a young man cross-legged
with a game controller, a small tattoo on the calf. The face is a profile
view and face-tag came back inconclusive (unknown, runner-up Dan at
0.34), so the wiki records him as presumed Dan on provenance — his
photos, his apartment — not on a verified ID. The bathroom shot is the
same story from behind: teal towel, green flip-flops, double-sink vanity,
no face visible.

The fifth photo is the batch's dated artifact. Etched into wet sidewalk
concrete: **"DAN ♡ ALEXIS"** and **"8-8-10"** — August 8, 2010. It
independently anchors the couple in Williamsburg in the summer of 2010,
five months into the Bedford Ave tenancy — the same season the record
elsewhere has Dan tweeting from the apartment and Alexis in the frame of
the era. Nobody etches a date into concrete for a visit.

These photographs supersede the longstanding claim on the residence
page that no images of the Bedford Ave unit survive. (See Conflicts in
the record.)

## Two apartments, three years

The residence timeline is **424 Bedford Ave (Williamsburg, Brooklyn),
March 2010 – March 2012**, then **Manhattan Upper East Side, 90th between
3rd and Lexington, March 2012 – May 2013**. The second move is dated by
two consecutive tweets that need no interpretation:

> **2012-03-29 19:40** — "Goodbye, Brooklyn!"
> **2012-03-30 19:55** — "Hello, Manhattan."

The check-ins follow immediately and confirm the neighbourhood: Osso
Buco UES on 31 March, then New York Sports Club, Josie's West and Le
Pain Quotidien through the first ten days of April, all Manhattan, after
two years in which every check-in had been Brooklyn.

The Williamsburg years have a geography of their own, and it is precise
because Dan was posting Foursquare check-ins with street addresses
between March 2010 and January 2011 — **fifteen of them**, the only
address-level location data the corpus holds for any year before the
Google Timeline export begins in April 2014. They cluster in a few
blocks of south Williamsburg: the Bedford Ave deli at S. 2nd, the c-town
at South 1st and Havemeyer, the Walgreens at 210 Union Ave, the Marcy
Ave J/M/Z platform. Ako Japanese at 205 Bedford and Peter Luger on
Broadway are the neighbourhood's restaurants; DuMont Burger recurs as
the one he names before arriving ("zomg i can taste the dumont burger
already", 11 March 2010, written from a studio session in Florida three
days before the train north). Beyond Williamsburg the pins are Brooklyn
Bridge Park, Grimaldi's in DUMBO, Electric Zoo on Randall's Island (5
September 2010, "w/ 115 others") and Hibernia on W 50th.

The cultural texture of the era is dense in the Facebook likes record:
the chiptune-to-EDM progression (Anamanaguchi → Flying Lotus →
ODESZA/JAUZ) — the indie and electronic vocabularies run side by side
through 2010 rather than in sequence, Grizzly Bear and Arcade Fire in
the same months as Diplo, Electric Zoo, Ableton warping, and a published
dubstep mix — and the **LCD Soundsystem MSG farewell (April 2011)** as a
dated attendance anchor. The Twitter voice of these years is the mature
form of the weaponized irony and aesthetic signaling that becomes the
linguistic fingerprint — the same online persona that seeds the
"ihatedanfrank" identity later. It runs at full volume with old friends
too: a March 2010 Office in-joke to Eric Jester — *"remember when he
stole 'shut up Oscar!' from us in s5 of the office? do sumthan bout
it"* — that neither man explains because neither has to, seven years
before the message thread with Eric begins.

Three firsts fall inside the opening weeks. On the night of **23 January
2010**, three tweets date Dan's **first tattoo**, identify it as Alexis's
initial, and catch the plan moving from *"monday"* to *"NOW!"* in sixteen
minutes — the tattoo predates the Brooklyn departure by five weeks. In
December the nicotine arc opens: *"today is day 1 with no tobacco"* (13
December) announces a switch to an e-cigarette as a quit — *"(un)
hooked"* corrected mid-sentence by the man writing it — and *"1 week
smoke free!"* on 10 January 2011 dates a fresh day one to roughly the
3rd, so the attempt broke inside three weeks and the relapse appears
nowhere except as a restarted counter. And in August 2010, in the
recovered Dan↔Suz SMS batch, Dan hears that **Bill Kubia** — a drug-era
acquaintance — has died of an overdose, and replies *"so glad I'm not
there."* He is six months into the maintenance chassis, twenty-one, two
boroughs from the life that killed him. The relief is the honest
register; there is no eulogy. The same SMS window carries **Kenney**,
Suz's 2010 boyfriend — present enough in the family orbit to have met
both Fran Coldren and Diane Moore — and two names still open: an
**"Alexa"** (Jan–Aug 2010) and a **"Zach"** (possibly Zachariah Harshman,
unconfirmed), both identity-unresolved.

## The household

**Jamie Mohler** — the Full Sail friend — is confirmed by a 2011 Gchat
log as a regular presence in the Brooklyn household during the Occupy
Wall Street autumn, the primary-source anchor for the operator's account
of who was there. Alexis's own August 12, 2011 Gchat (*"is Mohler
supposed to come over? or is he house bound bc of his knee?"*)
independently places Jamie inside the household's regular social orbit,
not just Dan's individual friendship. Per the operator, Jamie was also
present the day Dan first met **Menore** — the original introduction to
the dealer who becomes the supply network's most reliable node, years
before the documented 2018 reconnection. That specific claim remains
operator-attested: no message thread documents the meeting itself. The
residence page carries the same claim as a cause-edge — Menore's supply
operation traces its origin to a Bedford Ave/Williamsburg connection,
placing the start of a long relationship at this address — which makes
the Brooklyn apartment the origin point of the supply line that outlasts
the lease by more than a decade.

The Full Sail cohort follows into the era in reduced form — Jason
Bermejo, Eric Jester, Matt Dunn, the annual $5 birthday Venmo bit
already running — and **Ally** surfaces as a 2013 message burst, a
channel from the 18-year attachment reopening inside the era's last
months. [[wiki/people/charles-davenport|Charles Davenport]] samples the
graph-expansion phase immediately after the Brooklyn relocation — one of
the earliest-dated exchanges in the Facebook message corpus. The
peripheral threads whose only real activity dates to this window —
[[wiki/people/marc-charles|Marc Charles]], [[wiki/people/sean-teets|Sean
Teets]], [[wiki/people/katie-fletcher|Katie Fletcher]] — mark the shape
of a social graph being rebuilt in a new city: active here, quiet after.

## The audio-career attempt — and its whistleblower ending

Professionally this is the studio-work era — the one window in Dan's
life where the recording-arts training was actually the job, and it did
not convert into a durable career. The thread runs **ishlab** (2010–11)
— an internship/engineer role at Ishlab Studios under Jamin Gilbert, 10
Jay Street, DUMBO, still undocumented beyond the mentions — into
**Creative License** (April 2011 – February 2012), a small
celebrity-talent and licensed-music brokering company at 71 8th Avenue
in the West Village, where Dan worked as "Special Projects Coordinator"
under company president **Kevin McKiernan**. (The résumé later flatters
the title to "Executive Assistant / Office Manager"; LinkedIn's own 2012
notice uses the contemporaneous title, which settles it.) What the job
actually was, per his own résumé: assisting the company president with
correspondence, travel logistics, calendar management and personal
projects; graphic design elements for the website and business
development; and "oversight of office and employee expenses" — the last
item being the one that matters, because it is the job description that
put him in a position to notice the thing he later quit over. Client
accounts on the record are Walmart (contact Simona Rabsatt) and Molson
Coors (Lori Estabrooks).

The exit is this era's most documented event after the move — a four-act
sequence reconstructed from ~35 real 2011–2012 Gmail threads, recovered
2026-08-10 at the operator's direction. **Act zero:** Dan's stated
reason for quitting, in his own later words — *"the thing i cited when
i quit had to do with some kind of small fraud with airplane tickets
that i blew it up into a much much bigger thing but maybe i was just
desensitized."* The airfare citation is confirmed as cited; its specific
mechanism is unverified, and Dan's own self-doubt about whether he blew
it up is part of the record, not a later gloss. **Act one — the payroll
dispute (Feb–Apr 2012):** last day on or before 15 February 2012, flagged
by a $104 MetroCard charge Dan says he never requested, on a check that
arrived unsigned; six weeks with accounting officer Marty Jackson —
*"It's a trust issue. We don't trust you."* — escalated by Dan to the
IRS and the NYS Department of Labor, with his father Rick operating in
the corpus's clearest instance of tactical-coach register: *"You sound
so desperate. That is what kevin wants. We'll talk about a strategy for
this."* **Act two — the catharsis (April 26, 2012):** a one-line email to
a former coworker with an attachment titled `BEST OF KTM VOICEMAILS.mp3`.
**Act three — the whistleblower disclosure (May 2012):** unprompted,
months after the dispute closed, Dan wrote to former CL contact Renae
Holland that McKiernan had used Dan's own account to pressure former
employees and had inserted a CEO-approval gag clause into departing
interns' contracts — *"I think that the two interns should be aware of
this for their own legal protection and because it's just disgustingly
wrong"* — with Renae corroborating independently from her own tenure:
*"in my time there I was Kevin falsify more documents that I can count
— I know what he is capable of."* **Act four — retaliation (June 2012):**
McKiernan filed a formal LinkedIn intellectual-property complaint
against Dan's profile, backed by two documents notarized by Director of
Business Affairs Katherine Palakovich asserting Dan had never been a
Creative License employee at all; Dan filed a counter-notice offering
*"paystubs, tax documentation, or any other official documentation"* and
named the motive: *"Mr. McKiernan has and continues to attempt to smear
my name because of my actions as a whistleblower while under his
employ."*

The full account belongs on [[wiki/work/creative-license-exit]]; the
period-level fact is the shape: a trusted vertical figure's undocumented
financial claim met with exit and documentation rather than
confrontation — the founding case of the vertical-authority pattern that
recurs fourteen years later at BFS Foods, the corpus's own naming of the
first and most recent instances of one shape. And the postscript that
neither cancels the other: Dan kept McKiernan's contact information for
close to a decade, and in December 2014 Palakovich — by then leaving the
company herself — bcc'd Dan on a mass "Ex-Creative License Happy Hour"
invitation. Two and a half years after a conflict serious enough to
involve the IRS and a notarized false statement, he was still on the
alumni list. Both are true.

The post-February 2012 employment trail is thin. The Gemini-_58 session
(unconfirmed against email, carried as testimony) describes Craigslist
applications to EA/Office Manager roles across Manhattan — which means
the departure was probably also an ordinary job change in progress, not
purely a dramatic break, consistent with his own later doubt about
whether the airfare complaint was proportionate. No audio work of
comparable seriousness recurs until the much later GRIPNOTIC production
reactivation. The thread simply ends with the 2013 departure.

## Alexis, the cohabitation, and the Sandy shock

**Alexis was there for all of it** — *"with me the whole time we were
in nyc,"* per the Gemini-_58 reconstruction (carried as testimony) —
the relationship begun at Full Sail (Thanksgiving 2009) running its
middle chapters through Brooklyn and the Upper East Side. This is the
first long cohabitation of his adult life, and the baseline the 2015
single-bond switch is later measured against. Alexis worked at the
historic **Bridge Cafe** (279 Water St, the city's oldest continuously
operating tavern) — a job that started in the Bedford Ave years and
continued into the 90th Street year — until **Hurricane Sandy**'s October
2012 flooding of the Seaport district closed it, ending her employment:
a real economic shock to the household roughly six months before the
return to Uniontown. It is worth noting where it lands — the shock hits
during the Manhattan address's occupancy, not the earlier Brooklyn
years, where it is sometimes loosely attributed. The corpus never
connects Sandy's economic damage to the May 2013 departure in any row;
the wiki records the proximity and refuses the inference.

A brief **2013 Franki blip** — *"I really didn't have the romantic
feelings for franki that i said i did at the time"* — resolved back to
Alexis, rehearsing the slot mechanics the 2015 single-bond analysis
makes explicit. (The July 2013 Franki fireworks interlude itself belongs
to the [[wiki/timeline/periods/uniontown-return-2013-2015|Uniontown
return]], weeks after the departure, not to this era.) Dan's own
account of Alexis's public register has an odd later footnote: the
Twitter account's public register on her stops 29 November 2013 and stays
at zero for the last twenty-four months of the relationship, across
1,132 further posts, in the years his distinct-handle count was peaking
— the archive's first contemporaneous, non-testimonial evidence that the
attachment slot was occupied without being live.

## The two throughlines that start here and do not stop

Two throughlines of the whole corpus originate in this window.

The **Suboxone regimen** begins at its front edge — and this era is where
the record is thinnest and the convergence most interesting. The wiki
dates day zero to **17 February 2010**, on the broken-derivation
alignment described above. But two independent channels say the year
without reaching for it. A Facebook message dated **2011-08-04** has Dan
writing outbound: *"I was supposed to go to my Suboxone appointment
today, so I haven't gotten anything since Saturday night. I got one of
those At-Home test kits just to make sure I'd pass the test that they
give me there, and apparently I did just a liiittttle bit too much"* —
and asking the recipient for clean urine. In 2011 the regimen involved
**appointments and urine screening** — monitored treatment, not the
pharmacy-handoff shape the later record describes. And on **2013-07-10**
he writes *"i've been on maintainence for 3.5 years,"* *"i was on the
strips when i had a doctor in nyc"* (past tense — the NYC doctor is
already over), *"and i'm down to 2mg/day."* Counting back 3.5 years from
July 2013 gives roughly January 2010. The honest form of the claim is
temporal: **a prescriber in New York, monitored in 2011, 2mg/day by July
2013** — and the transition to the logistics-only shape documented in
the 2019–2025 messages is undated. The archive names no medication in
this window at all; the tweets do not settle the start year, and the
one-year discrepancy against legacy docs (January 2011) stands
unresolved pending a first-strips corpus row that has never been
located.

The second throughline is the **producer identity** — and its placement
requires care, because this is the finding the old timeline got wrong by
filing under "later NYC." **SLOPPP**, Dan's first named production
alias, launches in **2013** — the same year as the May departure — and
peaks in 2013–14: weekly mix series ("SLOPPP's Week in Trap"), the
signature "SLOPPPED N SCREWED" reworks, bootleg reworks of the
trap-saturation era's biggest names (Baauer, Major Lazer, Zeds Dead,
Caked Up), the involuntary 63–85% sub-bass signature already present from
the earliest material. The 2013 Twitter archive carries 29 distinct
titles, sixteen of them in September 2013 alone, with the
self-undercutting that came with them — *"#overcompensating #poseur
#fakefuture"* posted on an announcement at the peak. The September burst
is **post-return** — September 2013 is Uniontown — so the canonical
placement of the alias development is the
[[wiki/timeline/periods/uniontown-return-2013-2015|Uniontown return]], and
this page holds that correction rather than quietly benefiting from the

## The departure — and what isn't known about it

The period closes with the **May 2013 departure to Uniontown** — the
return that the Twitter record's "2013 systemic rupture" actually marks
(the rupture was the leaving, not a New York event). New York references
run 8.8%, 8.9% and 10.2% of originals across 2010, 2011 and 2012 and then
fall to **0.3% in 2013**; the last tweet placing him in the city is the
18 November 2012 internship pitch (*"living in NYC"*). That is the right
shape for a spring 2013 departure, and it is not a date — **May 2013
rests on context-core**, and on the July 2013 line to EJ Rags: *"i just
moved back... and i'm super output recently."* The EJ Rags exchange
itself — prompted by Ej seeing Dan's art on Instagram and being startled
to learn Dan had destroyed it — draws out Dan's stated philosophy of
deliberately destroying his own creative work (*"that was so I can
create something new"*), dated July 12, 2013, days into the Uniontown
return.

The **reason is undocumented.** Unlike the move in — credential, eleven
minutes, U-Haul, day-stamped — the move out has no mechanism on record.
The Sandy shock (October 2012) is a documented household-economic event
six months before the departure and a natural candidate explanation, but
the corpus never connects it to the leaving in any row, and the wiki does
not make the connection for it. The post-Creative-License job trail is
thin but present (Craigslist EA/Office Manager applications in 2012);
what it looked like by spring 2013 is unknown. The return goes back into
the family home — the same address the
[[wiki/timeline/events/november-2005-rupture|November 2005 rupture]] had
made the life's hinge — and three years later the geography gives way to
the [[wiki/work/nemacolin-caddie-years|Nemacolin caddie years]]
(2016–19), the Uniontown arc's other pole: the studio career's failure
landing in the golf course's three-year tenure.

## The coverage mirror

The second NYC era (2019–25) is the control against which this era's
silence can be measured — and the comparison is a finding, not a lament.
The 2019 move-prep is documented **to the dollar in the Amazon order
ledger**: Boosted boards, a wall rack, helmets, a drill, cat gear, all
dated in the order log that the [[wiki/self/2019-spending-analysis]]
reads as move-prep, with concurrent unstated cocaine spend behind it.
The 2010 move-prep is documented to the **tweet** — the U-Haul store, the
nine-day countdown — and the financing behind the tweets is never named.
One era's relocation is a line-item; the other's is a vibe with a truck.

The asymmetry is not an artifact of carelessness; it is an artifact of
**which channels survived.** The 2019–25 era has the iMessage exports,
the Google Timeline (from April 2014 — a year after NYC-1 ended), the
Amazon ledgers, the Voice record. The 2010–13 era has the public tweet
archive, the Facebook export (which reaches back into 2011 in messages,
unlike the iMessage corpus), and the operator's own email and testimony.
What changed the era's coverage on 2026-09-02 is the lesson: until then
the page said it rested on *"context-core, the Facebook record, and the
Gemini-_58 reconstruction, not on primary message data"*; the 764 dated
originals closed the move-date gap the page had carried for six weeks and
moved both apartment dates earlier. The next coverage jump is sitting in
the open: Google Voice history back to 2009 — Dan's own number — is the
live lead for the missing voice of this era, and the operator has
greenlit a full export. If it delivers, NYC-1 gets its first private
channel; until then, the tweets are the only first-party record it has,
and this page repeats rather than quietly benefits from the two limits
the archive states about itself: 2010–2012 are **live-scrape
reconstructions**, not a complete export (the operator spreadsheet does
not begin until 2013-08-17, so the yearly counts are floors, not totals),
and the archive is **originals-only** (pure reposts excluded), so a quiet
stretch means quiet in things Dan wrote, not silence on the account.

No *message* corpus survives from this period; the earliest real-text
rows in any on-disk export begin November 12, 2015. That limit is real
and unchanged. What changed is that it is no longer the same thing as
having no primary source: 764 dated public originals cover these three
years, and the page now rests on them for the move, the two apartments,
the Williamsburg geography, the tattoo, the quit attempt, and the ishlab
start.

The wiki-pinned 17 February 2010 day-zero rests on a same-day tweet ("this is the most stressful day/decision of my life") whose derivation is broken but whose dated alignment survives. The résumé is a later, sloppy document; the emails are contemporaneous. The post-Creative-License job trail thins to unconfirmed Craigslist applications in 2012. Nothing on record says why he left.

## Assessment

NYC-1 is the first complete cycle of the life's central shape: the
leave-and-return. Dan leaves Uniontown on a credential and a declaration
("Brooklyn here we come!!"), attempts the career the degree pointed at,
fails to convert it, and comes back to the family home — carrying the
ambition in a new form. The [[wiki/timeline/periods/three-phase-move]]
reads the moves as grammar; this is the move where the grammar is first
written. Everything after is a rewrite: the 2019 return to New York
carries the same studio ambition in a bedroom rather than a studio, the
same drug regimen in its logistics-only shape, the same Twitter voice
now filed under a corporate handle.

The era's most human fact is the smallest one. Six months into the city,
Dan gets the news about Bill Kubia and writes *"so glad I'm not
there."* He is twenty-one, on Suboxone, in Williamsburg, at the start
of the only job his degree ever got him. He does not go back. He does
not eulogize. He stays. That is the whole era in one line: he got out —
of the town, of the life that was killing people he knew — and stayed
out for three years, and then went home anyway.
