# Robotussin's Last Dance

June 15, 2005 — the summer after eleventh grade, age 17 by his own telling, with a license, a car, a father whose marriage was collapsing and a mother whose affair had already hollowed the household into something Dan could leave without looking back. The day opens at Frank's Auto Supply, the family business, where he spent the afternoon as a delivery driver, four-x-ing the drive time to every stop and going home to jerk off twice on the clock. At 4pm, less than an hour left in his shift, Jimmy Shaffer called with comically enthusiastic excitement: he and Rob Orange would pick Dan up at 5:30 and drive him to the Tom Petty concert at the Post-Gazette Pavilion in Burgettstown, PA.

They drove to Burgettstown with no tickets, learned at the will-call window that the show was sold out, and heard Tom Petty already on stage — "Runnin'" playing live — drifting out of a venue they could not get into. Jim had brought them there to wing it, and in 2005 that did not read as a genuinely stupid thing to do. What they did instead: drove to the nearest CVS, bought enough dextromethorphan to cook themselves, and dosed 20–40 red gel capsules each — the largest single DXM dose Dan had taken to that point. By the time the drug hit, geography had betrayed them: the closest CVS was in the direction of Uniontown, not away from it, so they were ninety minutes and roughly ninety-five miles in the wrong direction, sitting incapacitated at a Ruby Tuesday's near Pittsburgh airport, near-ejected for their condition, agreeing to leave voluntarily. Then they drove home down Rt 51 at 95 miles per hour, passing cars and smacking into orange traffic cones — more intoxicated at the wheel than they had been at the table, because the DXM was still coming up when they left.

This is the episode's significance in one frame: the first documented instance of Dan taking a substance deliberately to "shatter the space-time continuum" — his own words — two years before the opiate arc begins, and the night the freedom he had been feeling all summer collided with actual impairment on a highway. It is also, in retrospect, the last great outing of a trio whose third member, Rob Orange, would die in April 2014 — the one loss in the cohort that both surviving friends describe as the one that actually hurt. The outing lived on as a named shared memory: in December 2018, four years after Rob's death, Jim Shaffer and Dan reconstructed it in a text thread as pure adventure, and the 2026 captures are what finally attached the drug and the drive to the story.

## The day

The capture anchors the afternoon at Frank's Auto Supply, the family auto-parts business, where Dan was working as a delivery driver that summer — four-x-ing the drive time to every stop, going home to jerk off twice on the clock, the mundane boredom of the last hour of a shift. At 4pm Jimmy Shaffer called. The capture renders the excitement as "comically enthusiastic": Jimmy and Rob Orange would pick Dan up at 5:30 and drive him to the Tom Petty concert at the Post-Gazette Pavilion in Burgettstown, PA. There were no tickets. The plan was to wing it.

Burgettstown is north of Uniontown, out by the West Virginia border — the Post-Gazette Pavilion (later the KeyBank Pavilion, later still the Pavilion at Star Lake) is the big amphitheater on Route 22, and on June 15, 2005, it was hosting Tom Petty & the Heartbreakers' Summer 2005 tour with the Black Crowes as special guests. That date is real: the May 3, 2005 tour announcement listed "15 Post Gazette Pavilion, Pittsburgh, PA" on the confirmed itinerary, and setlist.fm holds a listing for the Black Crowes / Tom Petty at Post-Gazette Pavilion, Burgettstown on Jun 15 2005 (`kb/data/0360-robotussin-s-last-dance-date-and-trip-corroborated.md`, filed 2026-09-09). The page carried that as an open gap for a year — "The Post-Gazette Pavilion's sold-out Tom Petty date of June 15 2005 has not been independently verified against tour records" — and the verification closed it. The show being sold out is the capture's own claim, corroborated indirectly by the same machinery: a sold-out lot is why three ticketless teenagers were turned away at will-call.

By the time they arrived, Tom Petty was already on stage. They learned about the sellout at the will-call window while "Runnin'" — presumably "Runnin' Down a Dream," though the capture just says "Runnin'" — was audible live from the parking lot. The image is exact: standing outside a sold-out amphitheater at dusk in June, close enough to hear the show they had driven an hour to not see. The capture frames Jim's wing-it plan as era-appropriate — "in 2005 that did not read as a genuinely stupid thing to do" — and the whole day has the loose, consequence-free texture the capture keeps insisting was real and not retrospective projection: a kid with a license and a car and nobody checking what he did with either.

## The drug

The plan they arrived at instead: go to the nearest CVS and buy enough DXM to cook themselves. The source specifies "20–40 red gel capsules of pure dextromethorphan" per person, three people — which would have been the largest single dose Dan had taken to that point. The phrasing matters: "pure dextromethorphan" marks the red gel caps as a deliberate choice, DXM-only product rather than the combination cold medicines that carry acetaminophen and other adulterants — this was a kid who knew what he was after.

The capture places this session inside a small arc: Dan would go on to do DXM roughly 8–10 times in the six months following, before graduating to what he calls "more professional and mature narcotics" — meaning the opiate arc that begins the following year (2006), two years out from the documented opiate start. Among those 8–10 sessions, this is the one he names as the marquee experience: not the most intense chemically, but the one where the freedom he had been feeling all summer collided with actual impairment on a highway.

And the language: DXM is, per `wiki/health/chemical-architecture`, the first documented substance in the corpus that Dan took deliberately to "shatter the space-time continuum" — his word-for-word prefiguration of the engineered-stack framing he adopts a decade later. That sentence does the real structural work on this page. The 2005 kid eating cough medicine to shatter the space-time continuum is recognizably the ancestor of the adult who would describe his own neurochemistry as engineered architecture. The pharmacological intent was already there — consciousness as a material to be deliberately broken and remade — before the pharmacology got serious.

The cheapest part of the whole affair is the capture's dryest line: DXM was the cheapest, closest version of that feeling. It was already in the medicine cabinet.

## The wrong direction

The red flag the capture flags for the reader is geography. The closest CVS to Burgettstown was in a direction that required driving from Burgettstown toward Uniontown, not away from it. The three of them were chasing the drug purchase and discovered, too late, that the drug was taking them home — or rather, that by the time the DXM hit, they were ninety minutes and roughly ninety-five miles in the wrong direction from where they had intended to be (a lot, not the show, still), and very far from the bed Dan would eventually sober up in.

The Ruby Tuesday's near Pittsburgh airport is the night's center of gravity. They sat there, per the capture, so incapacitated they would have been kicked out had they not agreed to leave voluntarily. The details hold the whole scene: three boys on 20–40 DXM capsules each, at peak onset, in a chain restaurant off the airport corridor, negotiating their own ejection. There is no corroboration from the staff or the other two participants — this is single-source testimony from Dan, and the page has always said so.

What the record adds, from the 2026-08-17 gap answer, is the pharmacological inversion (see Conflicts in the record): they were not winding down at Ruby Tuesday's. The DXM was still coming up. They left more impaired than they had arrived, and the drive happened at or near peak. The capture is Dan's own answer to a question about his own culpability — asked whether any of the three were sober enough to make decisions by the time they left, he volunteered the fact that makes the story worse, not more explicable. That answer is filed as first-person self-incriminating testimony, and it is the only correction to the record that makes it worse rather than more defensible — which is worth noting, because the capture it comes from is Dan's own retelling of a story he and two friends have always told as adventure.

## The drive back

The capture's most vivid memory is the Rt 51 stretch: 17-year-old boys, 95 miles per hour, passing cars and smacking into orange traffic cones. The capsule count, the distance from home, the highway speed and the cone impacts are all presented as a single motion — not a decision followed by an outcome, but a single continuous event whose internal logic was the drug and the friendship and the summer.

Route 51 is the spine of that part of Pennsylvania — the southbound run from the Pittsburgh airport corridor back through the river towns toward Uniontown, two lanes for long stretches, construction zones where the orange cones live. The capture's "90 minute drive" and "roughly ninety-five miles" sit on top of a 95 mph claimed speed; the numbers are Dan's retrospective estimates, uncorroborated, and the page has never pretended otherwise. What the numbers assert is not precision but a feeling: they were going much too fast, in much too much traffic furniture, while much too high, and none of it interrupted them. Nothing in the record suggests any of the three were pulled over, injured, or otherwise interrupted.

The drive's terminus is Uniontown, not a house or a bed — which is what makes it a story rather than a cautionary footnote. The destination was the place he would sober up in, and the place that had already, by that June, been partially emptied of the family that used to live in it. He drove 95 miles home, at 95 mph, on the peak of a DXM dose, toward a house that was no longer really a home in the way the word had meant the year before. The symmetry the capture reaches for is deliberate: the night ended where the era already was.

## The era

This is the summer the family's stable narrative actually broke. The capture names Rick as a "non-factor" — not absent, but no longer able to constrain what Dan could or could not do on any given night. The prior two years had already normalized household cocaine use: the substance the capture frames as the "adult" drug already present in the house before Dan ever tried it himself (`wiki/health/chemical-architecture` tracks the household normalization; the rupture sequence of 2004–05 is documented at `wiki/timeline/events/2005-11-parental-rupture` in `kb/events`). The divorce had not yet been absorbed into a new family structure — it was still raw enough to feel like permission rather than loss.

The Abercrombie & Fitch accumulation that dominated eleventh grade Dan's self-concept — best-dressed senior superlative, bought his way in — is the surface layer of a kid who felt, for the first and only time, that the usual rules did not apply to him and that the consequences would be someone else's problem. The capture's phrase for the summer's texture is worth keeping exact: freedom colliding with impairment. The freedom was the summer; the impairment was the drug; the collision was Rt 51.

Summer 2005 sits in the master timeline as the hinge between two documented states: the household-cocaine normalization Dan names retrospectively, and the opiate arc that begins within two years. This episode is the hinge's sharpest single point — the same summer he first uses DXM deliberately as a consciousness-altering tool rather than a casual experiment, the first instance of the "shatter the space-time continuum" language, and the first time the record shows him treating his own nervous system as an instrument to be played rather than a condition to be endured. Everything after — the engineered stack, the deliberate pharmacology — is a change in materials, not in intent.

## The memory's two lives

The Tom Petty minivan story is a named shared memory that surfaces in the held iMessage corpus on December 9, 2018 — four years after Rob Orange's death, eighteen months into Jim Shaffer's re-established friendship with Dan. The contact writes, at 16:05 UTC (11:05 a.m. EST): *"Do you remember when me, you and rob orange took that suped up mini van to the Tom Petty concert."* It is the handle's locator in the corpus and the independent corroboration of the trip itself — the outing was real, the three participants are named by one of them, and the memory predates the 2026 captures by eight years.

Here the two retellings differ in a small, telling way. Jim's is adventure — *"All I remember is how hard we would fucking puke"* — and Dan's, in the same thread at 16:42:12 UTC, is two short sentences: *"We were on DXM"* (28 seconds later, 16:42:40 UTC): *"we ALL ate a bottle of roburussin, each"* (his spelling, preserved). Then the familiar register: *"We would TRIPLE the speed limit driving to Tom petty concerts which we had no tickets to, nor would we attend"* (16:49 UTC). So the December retellings name the drug; what they omit is the dose and the drive-specific detail the 2026 capture adds. That distinction matters because an earlier formulation of this page — and `kb/data/0360` itself — overstated it as "both retellings leave the substance context blank." The record is more precise: Jim's omits the drug; Dan's names it. (See Conflicts in the record, 2026-09-09.)

Then the register drops, and the memory stops being a bit. Dan: *"But really...rob is the only one that I get sad about."* Then: *"Doesn't it feel like...all of this should have been a bigger deal / Half of our friends died lol."* Jim, asked what year Rob died, first agrees to 2013 and then corrects himself — *"No. 2014"* — consistent with the April 2014 date on `wiki/people/rob-orange`. Jim's one unguarded sentence in eighteen months of texting: *"Rob and I would talk on the phone and laugh till we cried for 2 hours at a time, 5 days a week. I'd trade everyone in that photo for one more call."* Dan: *"You and I might be descended from Roaches. We somehow went the hardest and survived."*

The thread picks the memory up again on March 26, 2019: *"We'll start from the convenience story parking lot leaving Tom Petty with Rob Orange and just go from there."* The DXM vocabulary persists in the held corpus beyond the retellings — Dan writing "DXM/robo-tripping" on July 20, 2018, and, on June 4, 2019, *"i'm really sorry dude i took some robotussin earlier and i was knocked out."* Those corroborate the vocabulary, not the 2005 dose — but they establish that the drug was a standing reference point in his adult life, not a one-night novelty excavated for a story.

The master timeline files the December correction explicitly: *"The December retellings name the drug. dat:0360 says both December 2018 retellings 'omit the substance context entirely.' Dan's 'we ALL ate a bottle of roburussin, each' names it"* — pointing to `wiki/people/new-jim-shaffer`, Appendix B, corrections this rewrite makes. This page adopts the corrected reading: the retellings name the drug, omit the dose and the drive.

## What the corroboration changed

The September 2026 datum (`kb/data/0360`, filed 2026-09-09) strengthened this page in an unusual way: the weakest legs got corroborated, and the corroboration made the remaining single-source material more conspicuous, not less.

First, the date. The page carried its own gap note — "The Post-Gazette Pavilion's sold-out Tom Petty date of June 15 2005 has not been independently verified against tour records" — and the gap is now closed: the May 3, 2005 press release for Tom Petty & the Heartbreakers' Summer 2005 tour lists "15 Post Gazette Pavilion, Pittsburgh, PA" (with the Black Crowes as special guests) in the confirmed itinerary, and setlist.fm holds a listing for the Black Crowes / Tom Petty at Post-Gazette Pavilion, Burgettstown on Jun 15 2005. The tour date was public all along.

Second, the trip. The held iMessage corpus contains the shared memory years before the 2026 captures existed: the December 9, 2018 "suped up mini van" exchange and Dan's "no tickets... TRIPLE the speed limit" reply independently confirm the page's no-tickets and reckless-drive elements. Dan's own December 2018 telling confirms the outing; Jim's names it as a shared adventure.

What stays single-source: the 20–40 capsule estimate, the Ruby Tuesday's incapacitation, the 95 mph Rt 51 run, and the 2026-08-16 capture's "MUCH less sober" answer — all from the unheld 2026 captures (`raw/timeline/captures/2026-08-16_185413_gap-robotussin-s-last-dance.md`, `raw/self/captures/2026-08-09_143916_robotussin-s-last-dance.md`, neither held in this repo). The datum's framing of this is worth keeping verbatim: *"The page's honesty about this is the datum: it files the intoxication answer as first-person self-incriminating testimony and notes explicitly that this is 'the only correction to the record that makes it worse rather than more explicable.'"* The corroborated trip is not corroboration of the DXM.

## Conflicts in the record

**2026-08-17 — They were peaking, not coming down.** This page asked whether any of the three were sober enough to make decisions by the time they left Ruby Tuesday's. The operator:

> **"on the contrary, we were all MUCH less sober and subsequently even more intoxicated before a 90 minute drive"**
> (`raw/timeline/captures/2026-08-16_185413_gap-robotussin-s-last-dance.md`).

That inverts the assumption the drive section originally rested on. The implicit reading was of a night winding down — incapacitated at the restaurant, then a drive home. The pharmacokinetics ran the other way: DXM was **still coming up** when they left, so the ninety-minute, ninety-five-mile-per-hour run down Rt 51 was made at or near peak intoxication by a seventeen-year-old who was *more* impaired at the wheel than he had been at the table he was nearly ejected from.

The answer is also the only correction to the record that makes it **worse** rather than more explicable — which is worth noting, because the capture it comes from is Dan's own retelling of a story he and two friends have always told as adventure. Asked to fill a gap about his own culpability, he volunteered the fact that makes the story less defensible. Weigh that against the in-attack claims elsewhere in the corpus that do the opposite.

**2026-09-09 — The tour-date gap is closed.** The page's own gap note — "The Post-Gazette Pavilion's sold-out Tom Petty date of June 15 2005 has not been independently verified against tour records" — is now superseded. The May 3, 2005 press release for Tom Petty & the Heartbreakers' Summer 2005 tour lists "15 Post Gazette Pavilion, Pittsburgh, PA" with the Black Crowes as special guests; setlist.fm holds the Jun 15 2005 Burgettstown entry. Standing: the date is verified; the sellout itself remains the capture's claim (the turn-away at will-call is single-source, consistent with a real sold-out date). The gap note is retired by this entry, not deleted from the record — the master timeline still carries the original wording as the receipt.

**2026-09-09 — "Both retellings leave the substance context blank" is overstated.** `kb/data/0360`'s "What it is not" section claims the December 2018 retellings "omit the substance context entirely." The held-corpus rows contradict this for Dan's half of the exchange: *"We were on DXM"* (2018-12-09 16:42:12 UTC) and *"we ALL ate a bottle of roburussin, each"* (16:42:40 UTC) name the drug explicitly. Jim's retelling does not name it — his is *"All I remember is how hard we would fucking puke"* — so the datum is correct about Jim and wrong about Dan. The master timeline (December 2018 row) flags this correction and points it at `wiki/people/new-jim-shaffer`, Appendix B. Standing: the retellings name the drug and omit the dose and the drive-specific detail; the 2026 captures are what add the capsule count, the Ruby Tuesday's incapacitation, and the Rt 51 run.

**2026-10-06 — "Age 17" against the birthdate.** The capture, and this page following it, gives Dan's age that night as 17. Dan was born November 1, 1988 (`wiki/self/lineage/family-tree.md`; `wiki/self/index.md`) — which puts him at 16 years and 7.5 months on June 15, 2005, five months short of 17. This is not a dispute with a source; it is an internal arithmetic tension. Standing: the page retains the capture's "17" as the participants' own framing of the night (the summer after eleventh grade, a licensed driver, treated as 17 by everyone in the car), and records here that the calendar says 16. No correction to the body is made — the rounding is the era's, not the wiki's.

**Standing limits (not disputes).** The capsule count is Dan's retrospective range ("20–40"), not a counted inventory. The CVS is named only as "the closest CVS to Burgettstown"; the Ruby Tuesday's is specified as near Pittsburgh airport but not pinned further. No other participant has corroborated or contested the capsule count, the Rt 51 speed, or the cone impacts — single-source testimony, first-person and self-incriminating, which is the strongest form single-source testimony takes, but still one witness. Rob Orange, the third member, died on or around April 11, 2014 (`wiki/people/rob-orange`), so the trio's account can never be completed from his side; Jim Shaffer has never been asked the drug questions on the record. The two 2026 captures that carry the substance detail are not held in this repo (both source references unresolved); what survives of them here is quoted through the staged page and the 2026-09-09 datum.

## Assessment

The record supports three judgments. First: this is the earliest documented instance of the pattern the corpus keeps finding at both registers of Dan's life — maximum engagement, no plan for landing, the escape hatch chosen at the moment the plan fails. The sold-out show becomes a CVS run becomes a Ruby Tuesday's becomes a 95 mph drive; the structure reappears in adult form at lower amplitude, with the same friendship circle present at both. Second: the corroboration history is itself the point. The date was public all along, the trip was a named shared memory in the held archive eight years before anyone attached the drug to it, and the one part that cannot be corroborated — the capsule count, the drive — arrives only through the single source that also volunteers the fact making himself look worse. The page's honesty layer is load-bearing here, not decorative. Third: in the friendship record, this is the trio's last great outing. Four years after Rob Orange's death, the two survivors reconstructed it in a thread that slides from adventure to grief in six messages — *"rob is the only one that I get sad about"* — and the 2005 night is the last entry in the shared file they ever made together.
