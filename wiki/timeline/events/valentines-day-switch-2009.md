# The Valentine's Day switch (February 2009)

On a night in early February 2009, Dan drove to Jacksonville with his
girlfriend Danielle Onesi to work the merch table at a gig by After Play
Rewind — a band made up of his own Full Sail classmates. He came home
having started the process of leaving Danielle for Kelly Mulroy, a
classmate from Baltimore who would drop out of Full Sail in month five.
The entire Kelly relationship that followed exists in the record as two
letters and a mayday: hers on March 19, his on May 28. Everything about
the switch — the venue, the band, the girl, the dropout, the letters —
was reconstructed in a single night of testimony on 2026-09-24, and the
contemporaneous paper trail backs the shape of it even where the exact
date stays hedged.

## The night, as told

The pieces, in Dan's words from the night he told it:

- The band was **After Play Rewind**, and "they were my fs classmates."
- Dan's job that night: **"I would sell merch."** He wasn't a punter; he
  was working the table.
- The show was in **Jacksonville**, and in his memory it fell on or
  around **Valentine's Day** — "I believe, the famous Danielle Kelly
  Valentine's Day switch event show in Jacksonville." The "I believe" is
  his, and it stays on the record.
- He went with **Danielle Onesi**, his first girlfriend, the relationship
  he'd been in since roughly 2005.
- He connected that night with **Kelly Mulroy** — "this new girl I met
  from Baltimore" — who was also a Full Sail classmate: "No she was a fs
  classmate too."
- Kelly was a **month-5 dropout**: she left Full Sail after five months
  and went home to Baltimore, which is why the whole romance plays out
  at long distance.

The photo he sent that night is the only known artifact of the gig
itself: After Play Rewind on a small club stage, red-haired frontman,
keys and drums, theatrical lighting. Its EXIF timestamp reads
**2009-02-01 07:13:38** — early February, inside the Valentine's window,
two weeks before the holiday. The camera date and his memory don't match
to the day, but they agree on the month, and the month is what the rest
of the paper trail confirms.

The Jacksonville circuit mattered here. Jack Rabbits — the room APR
would play that April with Blood On the Dance Floor — was the anchor of
the northeast-Florida all-ages scene, the kind of 300-cap room where the
Orlando bands drove up I-95 for a Friday and the crowd was half
classmates. Dan working the merch table wasn't a casual favor; in that
scene the merch table was the social switchboard, the place you stood
all night while everyone you knew cycled past. He spent the evening
selling his classmates' shirts and EPs, and somewhere in that foot
traffic the evening rearranged his life. The photo he took is a stage
shot, not a crowd shot — the camera was pointed at the band because the
band was the assignment. What happened off-camera is the part that
stuck.

## The band: After Play Rewind

After Play Rewind (APR) were an Orlando electronica/dance pop/screamo
quintet, and their surviving bio reads like a Full Sail admissions
brochure: "David and Chris originating from Germany, Bailey from the
mitten state, Corey from the lone star state and Jason from the windy
city. The quintet formed after individual departures from previous
regionally successful bands." Dan named three members from memory on
2026-09-24 — **David Keller, Chris Smith, Corey Brown** — which maps
cleanly onto the bio's David (Germany), Chris (Germany), and Corey
(Texas). Bailey (Michigan) and Jason (Chicago) complete the five.

Their calling card was a manifesto: **100% loop free.** Where 99.9% of
electronica acts ran loops and leaned on a DJ, APR played everything
live — synths, keys, drum pads, guitars — and said so loudly. In the
Orlando scene of 2008–2009, at the tail end of the bloghouse years and
the peak of the crunkcore moment, that was a line in the sand.

What survives of them online, still findable:

- A **Last.fm artist page** carrying the full bio.
- A **Concert Archives band page** (no tour dates logged — small-club
  coverage of the era is Swiss cheese).
- An artist page on the old **music.inparadise.net** aggregator.
- A documented **April 3, 2009** bill at **Jack Rabbits in Jacksonville**
  with Blood On the Dance Floor and The Riff Raff (FL) — two months
  after the switch night, same city, same circuit.
- A **February 2, 2013** appearance at The Box Office in Las Vegas on a
  pop-punk package bill — they were still a going concern four years
  later.
- The track **"Hit And Run"** floating around the mp3 mirrors.

The corpus holds two direct traces of Dan's own connection to the band,
and they bracket the story nicely. First, his **blog note of February
17, 2009** — three days after Valentine's Day: "So I'm about 99% sure
nobody reads this blog at all (even my friends, fuck em'.) But i'll use
this for some shameless promotion. Check out After Play Rewind,"
linking afterplayrewind.com. He was hyping his classmates' band in the
exact week the switch happened. Second, an **August 2010** Facebook
exchange with David Keller in which Keller sends Dan a mediafire link —
`APR+chem+3+songs+mixed.zip` — "fer sher man. those are three of five
that will be on the EP." The friendship, and the band, outlasted the
romance that started at their gig.

## The people

**Danielle Onesi** — the girlfriend he arrived with. His first
girlfriend, together since roughly 2005, the relationship that carried
him from Uniontown to Florida. By 2009 she was in online fashion school
through the San Francisco Art Institute (his words, 2026-09-24) — a
detail that says something about the household: two nineteen-year-olds
in Winter Park, both in school, one on campus and one online, building
the adult life in parallel. A calendar photo from the era shows "Dee's
Birthday" marked on the 4th (month unknown), the kind of domestic
artifact that survives when the relationship doesn't. She never left
the picture afterward — she is now Suzanne Frank's closest friend and a
Christmas fixture — which makes the Jacksonville night the hinge of her
story too: the night the romantic relationship ended and the lifelong
family-friend one began. That she stayed, through the switch and
everything after, is its own statement about who she is in this family.

**Kelly Mulroy** — the girl he left with, in the emotional sense.
Full Sail classmate, from Baltimore, gone by month five. The record
holds exactly two messages between them, and they are the whole
relationship. They are quoted at length in the next section because
they deserve the complete-log treatment: there is nothing else.

**David Keller** — the APR member the corpus actually knows. His
Facebook thread with Dan runs from 2009 to 2017 and reads like the
afterlife of the Full Sail cohort: the August 2010 EP tracks, a May
2017 exchange about "the final bounce of our final project from full
sail" (they built it together), then an August 2017 catch-up where
Keller is managing a Panera Bread ("paying me way too little for the
50 hours scheduled a week on my feet") and Dan, back in PA, is
caddying and talking up Vegas golf. Two classmates, eight years out,
comparing notes on jobs that have nothing to do with audio. The thread
is warm the whole way through — Dan pitching the caddy life ("I get to
smoke weed all day, run around in the sun on a championship golf
course"), Keller asking how to get in, both of them laughing about
where the Full Sail dream landed. It is the only post-2010 trace of
the APR circle in the corpus, and it reads like a reunion that never
needed a occasion.

**Chris Smith and Corey Brown** — named by Dan as APR members, with no
corpus hits anywhere: not in the FTS index (779,358 documents), not in
the Facebook export, not in Gmail. They are recorded here on his word
alone, with the seam showing.

## Reading Kelly's letter

The complete-log doctrine applies with unusual force here: the two
messages are not excerpts from a larger correspondence. They *are* the
correspondence. Kelly's letter of March 19, 2009, sent at 2:57 in the
morning, is quoted in full:

> Sunnnyyy D! This may seem like a random little note that I'm writing
> you, but I've been thinking about you a lot and I just wanted to make
> sure that you knew how important your friendship is to me. I want to
> thank you for helping me in ways that I can't even begin to explain
> over the past month that I've known you. You have helped me realize
> that it's okay to just take a deep breath and go through with
> something that you've been scared to do for a long time. It takes a
> lot of courage to do that and you are one of the few people that I
> have met in my lifetime that possess that quality. You have helped me
> to realize that you should never just settle for something because
> life is too short to be unhappy. You have shown me that it's okay to
> go out of your comfort zone and start to forge a path of your own.
> Even though it's scary, you have helped show me that this is possible
> and that sometimes the things that you're scared of end up being the
> most worthwhile. I think since I've been home I've had some time to
> reflect and focus on the lessons that I've learned from the people
> that I've met these past couple months.
>
> I admire you in a lot of ways Dan. I want to thank you for allowing
> me to be myself around you and I also want to thank you for helping
> me find a new found faith in trying to forge a path of my own. Thank
> you for letting me know that it's okay to be scared..as long as you
> have good friends and people in your life, you can get through
> anything.
>
> I also want to say that I'm sorry for not being there for you as much
> as I should over these past two weeks or so.. I don't want you to feel
> as if I've abandoned you or that I'm not here for you anymore. I've
> just still been trying to get used to all of the changes in my life.
> I want you to know that I think about you often and wonder how you're
> doing. Let's keep eachother updated more so than we have been. I
> don't wanna lose touch.. You're amazing dan! I feel so lucky to know
> you and have you in my life. Thank you for being the person that you
> are. Don't ever change! I miss you...
>
> - Kelly

Three things in the letter only make sense with the facts Dan supplied
on 2026-09-24. First, "over the past month that I've known you" —
written March 19, it dates their actual connection to mid-February,
the Jacksonville window, even though they'd shared a classroom for
months. Second, "since I've been home" — home is Baltimore; she is
writing from the place she retreated to after dropping out in month
five, and the whole letter is a dropout's letter, full of forging
paths and comfort zones and not settling. Whatever Dan helped her "go
through with" that she'd "been scared to do for a long time," the
leaving-Full-Sail decision is sitting right there in the subtext.
Third, the apology for "not being there for you... over these past two
weeks" — the pullback rhythm of a long-distance relationship already
cooling six weeks in.

Dan's reply, May 28, 2009, at 7:41 in the evening, is the entire
other half of the correspondence:

> sooo....i was closing windows on my comp yesterday, and i realized i
> totally left you hanging when you messaged me the other night, and
> you said something was wrong. just wanted to say sorrrry/make sure
> everything is good. miss you SO much, come visit me....like...NOW?

The "come visit me....like...NOW?" is the Baltimore-trip pitch, and it
is the last word in the thread. Then silence. By September he is in
the six-week long-distance relationship with Stephanie Nalbone
(September 25 – November 10, 2009), and by the end of 2009 he is with
Alexis Armel, who stays six years. Kelly is the brief, bright,
long-distance interval between the four-year first love and everything
after — a relationship that lived entirely in the mail because she was
already gone.

The February 17 blog note is the load-bearing contemporaneous document:
it puts Dan publicly promoting After Play Rewind three days after
Valentine's Day, in the same month as the photo's EXIF. The
Valentine's-Day-exact memory is his and hedged; the February is
corroborated twice over.

## Where it sits

The switch night closes the Danielle era and opens the strangest year
of Dan's romantic life. The sequence, in his words from the same night:
**Danielle → Kelly (brief) → gap → Steph → Alexis.** The corpus dates
it: Kelly's letters March–May 2009, Stephanie Nalbone September 25 –
November 10, 2009, Alexis by year's end. Between Kelly's last letter
and Steph's first message is a three-month gap the record doesn't fill.

It also sits at the end of the great fall-2008 concert streak — the
two months in Florida where Dan photographed everything, identified
band by band on 2026-09-24 with his EXIF timestamps doing the dating:
Cobra Starship (November 23, Sassy Back Tour), All Time Low (October 18,
House of Blues Lake Buena Vista, with Mayday Parade, The Maine, and
Every Avenue), Jack's Mannequin (October 17, The Social downtown —
Dan's correction of an initial wrong guess, and the brick walls in the
photo are what gave away that it wasn't House of Blues), Gym Class
Heroes and The Roots (October 10, the co-headline bill, two Roots shots
to one GCH backdrop), We The Kings headlining the Bill & Trav's Bogus
Journey Tour bill with The Academy Is..., Carolina Liar, and Hey Monday
(October 3 — one of the two guys in the afternoon's posed photo was
wearing a Hey Monday jersey, opener's merch, hours before doors). The
Jacksonville night is the coda to that run: the show he worked instead
of photographed, the one where the camera captured the band but the
real subject was in the crowd.

And it is a Full Sail story all the way down. The band were classmates.
The girl was a classmate. The merch table was the job. The dropout is
why the relationship is letters. Even the blog promotion is a classmate
doing promo for classmates. The whole episode fits inside the
September-2008-to-March-2010 Full Sail window and barely touches the
world outside it — except for Baltimore, which is where Kelly took the
story when she left, and where the letters went.

## Limits

- The **exact date** is hedged by Dan himself ("I believe"). EXIF says
  February 1; his memory says Valentine's Day. Concert Archives has no
  February 14 APR Jacksonville show on record, but its small-club
  coverage of the era is incomplete — the absence proves nothing either
  way. February 2009 is corroborated; the day is not.
- The **venue** is unconfirmed. Jack Rabbits hosted APR in Jacksonville
  that April and was the room for that circuit, but no listing ties the
  February show to a room.
- **Chris Smith and Corey Brown** have zero corpus hits. Their membership
  in the band rests on Dan's word alone.
- **Kelly Mulroy's thread** predates the corpus index — it lives in the
  legacy Facebook export, which is why the FTS sweep (779k docs) returns
  nothing for her name. The two messages are archived with this page.
- Dan's "famous" is doing affectionate work. Nobody outside the cohort
  called it that. Inside the cohort, apparently, everybody did.
- **Gate note (2026-09-24):** the corpus-sweep gate ran before this
  article. The FTS leg has a phrase-query bug — multi-word terms return
  "0 hits across 0 corpora" (single-word terms work fine) — so the
  phrase searches for "David Keller," "Chris Smith," "Corey Brown," and
  "Kelly Mulroy" were re-run as direct FTS5 phrase queries against
  `~/workspace/corpus-index/corpus.db` (779,358 documents): zero hits
  for all four phrases. The single-word "Keller" search returns only
  Helen Keller and Keller Williams (real estate) hits. The legacy
  Facebook export tree — where the Kelly and David Keller threads live
  — is not covered by the FTS index, which is why the sweep misses
  them; both threads were read in full from the export and archived
  with this article. Both Gmail accounts were swept live via the sweep
  tool with no relevant hits. Coverage: complete with the stated
