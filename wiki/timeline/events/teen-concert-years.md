# Concert Record (2001–2019)

For three years of his teens, live music was not background — it was the itinerary itself. Dan Frank kept a Facebook note titled "Concerts I've gone to," a literal list of the shows he had routed himself to across Ohio, Pennsylvania, New York, Maryland, and New Jersey, and the list reads like a travelogue of the mid-2000s emo and pop-punk boom: three Fall Out Boy shows in eleven months plus a release-day taping inside MTV's TRL studios, Taking Back Sunday, Say Anything, Panic! at the Disco, Vans Warped Tour four summers running. This is not inferred from a favorites list — it is an itinerary he kept.

The note was the second chapter, not the first. A later operator pass reconstructed the itinerary against venue routing, tour histories, and support-act billing, producing a list four times the size of the Facebook note and extending it in both directions: back to August 2001, when Dan was twelve, at Rolling Rock Town Fair in Latrobe, Pennsylvania, and forward to April 2019, boarding a Freddie Gibbs 4/20 cruise yacht at 353 West Street in Manhattan. Eighteen years, thirty-six researched entries — and then a twitter cross-check that proved the neat table was a record of what he bought, not of every evening he was in the room.

This page walks the record in order: the festivals before the scene found him, the 2005–2008 years when he toured it himself, the off-record night at Tom Petty, the Orlando autumn the ticket stubs missed, and the long tail into comedy bills and festival headliners. Where the sources disagree, the disagreements are kept — they are part of the record's honesty.

## The record he kept

The Facebook note survives verbatim across the corpus — preserved in Dan Profile.txt, the Gemini Activity export, and the failed-wiki log — and reads as follows:

| Date | Artist(s) | Venue / City |
|------|-----------|--------------|
| 2005-04-23 | Green Day / My Chemical Romance | Mellon Arena |
| 2005-11-09 | HIM | Pittsburgh, MR Small's |
| 2006-07-04 | Taking Back Sunday / Angels & Airwaves | Columbus |
| 2007-01-11 | Fall Out Boy | Cleveland, Agora |
| 2007-02-06 | Fall Out Boy — **TRL Studios** (Infinity On High release-day taping) | New York City |
| 2007-05-06 | Say Anything | Baltimore, Rams Head |
| 2007-11-19 | Fall Out Boy **secret show** (billed "Schrute Farms") | Buffalo, Mohawk Place |
| 2008-02-11 | Will Ferrell / Demetri Martin / Zach Galifianakis | Penn State University |
| 2008-05-03 | Bamboozle | Meadowlands, NJ |

The 2007 Facebook status layer corroborates the intensity of that year: posts read "swooning over the new FOB," "in New York City to see Fall Out Boy at TRL," and a Say Anything obsession — the same window the profile names as the "romantic collapse fantasy" imprinting period ([[wiki/mind/psychosexual/developmental-origins]]). The same status layer names "slaying on halo" and Super Mario Galaxy in the same breath — the concert devotion and [[wiki/interests/video-games|gaming]] were coordinates of one identity cluster, not separate hobbies.

The note is contemporaneous self-reporting: residue of who he was in 2007 and 2008, not a document he assembled later. That is what makes it the anchor — and what makes its disagreements with the later reconstruction interesting rather than dismissible. The reconstruction is stronger on dates; the note is stronger on what he believed he did.

## Before the scene: 2001–2004

The first two entries are Rolling Rock Town Fairs — regional nu-metal festivals at the Westmoreland Fairgrounds in Latrobe / Mt. Pleasant, Pennsylvania, four years before the emo/pop-punk itinerary the Facebook note captures. August 4, 2001, confirmed: Stone Temple Pilots, Deftones, Incubus, Live, Oleander, Staind, Tantric. He was twelve. July 27, 2002: Godsmack, Outkast, Nickelback, P.O.D., Sevendust, Default, Injected — with Alien Ant Farm cancelled after a bus accident and Tommy Lee filling in. July 31, 2004: Staind, Disturbed, Velvet Revolver, Three Days Grace, Finch, Finger Eleven, Hoobastank, N.E.R.D, Sevendust — played through heavy rain and mud, with no postponement found. The full per-edition lineups live at [[wiki/interests/concert-record/festivals/rolling-rock-town-fair]].

Between the fairs the record already shows range. July 24, 2002, at Post-Gazette Pavilion in Burgettstown: Barenaked Ladies with Guster, Vertical Horizon credited as the opener that night. March 7, 2003, at Mellon Arena in Pittsburgh: the Billy Joel / Elton John Face2Face Tour — and the researched note matters, because that was the only indoor-arena Pittsburgh date across all Face2Face years. April 21, 2004, at The Rock Club in Station Square: HIM, resolved as the Love Metal Tour, with a November 18, 2004 return also documented. June 17, 2004, at the Chevrolet Amphitheatre: The Darkness, date nailed down through Wildhearts support routing. October 10, 2004, at the A.J. Palumbo Center: Incubus with Ben Kweller — confirmed, and the only Pittsburgh stop of that era.

The fall of 2004 plants the first scene flag: the Nintendo Fusion Tour at Rostraver Ice Garden in Belle Vernon, dated to roughly October 22–23, 2004, confirmed via routing (immediately before the tour's October 24 Philadelphia date), with My Chemical Romance and Anberlin on the bill. The 2004 Fusion is the hinge — the nu-metal fairs of 2001–2004 give way to the world the Facebook note belongs to.

## The core years: 2005–2008

This is the itinerary at saturation. April 23, 2005: Green Day and My Chemical Romance at Mellon Arena — the note's first entry, and the emo/pop-punk era opening at full volume. July 5, 2005: Saosin with Anberlin, Acceptance, Terminal, and Codeseven at the Charleroi VFW, resolved as the All Dudes All the Time Tour — a VFW hall, not an arena, which says something about how deep the commitment ran. August 1, 2005: Vans Warped Tour at Post-Gazette Pavilion in Burgettstown, with Fall Out Boy, Alexisonfire, Gogol Bordello, and Hawthorne Heights. Ten days later, August 11, 2005, at the same pavilion: Coldplay with Rilo Kiley, on the Twisted Logic Tour — the only confirmed outdoor Coldplay date at the venue. Then the fall Nintendo Fusion Tour window, September 28 to November 23, 2005: Fall Out Boy, Motion City Soundtrack, Panic! at the Disco, The Starting Line, Boys Night Out, across multiple cities.

The summer of 2006 is the amphitheatre run. June 27, 2006, at the Chevrolet Amphitheatre in Pittsburgh: Taking Back Sunday with Angels & Airwaves, Head Automatica, and The Subways — confirmed, and explicitly corrected from 2007 in the reconstruction. July 18, 2006, at the same amphitheatre: Panic! at the Disco headlining over The Dresden Dolls and The Hush Sound. July 27, 2006: Warped Tour back at Post-Gazette Pavilion, this time AFI, Rise Against, Thursday, Motion City Soundtrack, NOFX. Two rows stay open from this stretch: a Gym Class Heroes show at California University of PA around 2006, with no primary campus listing ever located, and a Taking Back Sunday college-campus date in Pittsburgh that never resolved to anything beyond the June 27 amphitheatre show.

2007 is the Fall Out Boy year. January 11 at the Agora in Cleveland, per the note. February 6 at TRL Studios in New York City: the *Infinity On High* release-day free show, the 3-city / 3-show release-week stunt (same-day free shows also in Chicago and Los Angeles), and he was in the studio for it. May 6: Say Anything at Rams Head in Baltimore. August 8: Warped Tour at Burgettstown again — Paramore, Coheed & Cambria, All Time Low, Bad Religion. And November 19: the secret show at Mohawk Place in Buffalo, billed "Schrute Farms," a ~300-capacity sellout where the band played *Take This To Your Grave* in full — per a 2025 ChatGPT cross-check he ran himself, the first and only time the album was ever played straight through — with Keith Buckley guesting on the Pantera cover "Walk." This is the one dated, verifiable instance where his *Office* fandom enters the concert record directly ([[wiki/interests/the-office]]).

2008 closes the era. February 11: Will Ferrell, Demetri Martin, and Zach Galifianakis at Penn State University — a comedy bill in the middle of the music itinerary. May 3 (per the note; May 4 per the researched list — see Conflicts): Bamboozle at the Meadowlands in New Jersey, Panic! at the Disco and Coheed & Cambria headlining with Paramore, Gym Class Heroes, Cobra Starship, and The Academy Is... July 29: the last Burgettstown Warped — Anberlin, All Time Low, Against Me!, 3OH!3. Then the move south, and the record changes shape.

## The off-record night: Tom Petty, June 15, 2005

The concert record contains one entry that is not a show at all. On June 15, 2005 — the summer after eleventh grade, age seventeen — Dan, Jimmy Shaffer, and Rob Orange drove to Post-Gazette Pavilion in Burgettstown for Tom Petty. They had no tickets. The show was sold out; they heard "Runnin'" playing live from the parking lot as they learned this at the will-call window. What followed is documented at [[wiki/timeline/events/robotussin-s-last-dance]]: twenty to forty DXM capsules per person from the nearest CVS, incapacitation at a Ruby Tuesday's near Pittsburgh airport, and a 95-mph run back down Rt 51 at or near peak intoxication, smacking orange traffic cones.

It belongs here because it is the concert record's off-grid entry — the only concert date in the corpus where the destination is reached and the show is missed simultaneously. The Tom Petty minivan memory had always circulated in the corpus without the substance context; the 2026 capture fills in what was actually inside the adventure. The pavilion's sold-out Tom Petty date of June 15, 2005 has not been independently verified against tour records — that gap stands (see Conflicts).

## Orlando: what the ticket stubs missed (2008–2009)

The Florida entries start in the researched table: October 18, 2008, House of Blues Orlando — All Time Low, Mayday Parade, The Maine, Every Avenue, resolved as the joint fall 2008 package. November 17, 2008, The Social in Orlando — Jedi Mind Tricks with Outerspace and Reef the Lost Cause, resolved. December 12, 2008, House of Blues — Fall Out Boy, The Academy Is..., We The Kings, the XL 106.7 "XL-ent Electric Xmas" show. Two more 2008 Orlando rows stay open: Jack's Mannequin (year and market consistent, exact night unresolved) and Hey Monday.

That is the table. The twitter archive from the same autumn says the table is a purchase record, not an attendance record. Ninety-eight days of tweets — September 24 to December 31, 2008 — name seven Orlando shows. The table holds four. The three it misses: The Roots on October 7 (*"at the roots show. i can dig that."* — posted 16:29 from inside it); Cobra Starship with Hit The Lights and Forever The Sickest Kids on November 13 (*"cobras/hit the lights/ftsk"* — the full bill in his own abbreviations); and Trans-Siberian Orchestra on November 15 (*"trans-siberian orchestra tonight."* at 17:04, then *"wow that was SUPER lame."* at 23:07). The reason they are missing is the point: the table was assembled from ticket stubs and order confirmations, and that kind of record preserves what was bought. It cannot preserve a show somebody else bought the ticket for, a show walked into, or a show remembered as not worth remembering — and Trans-Siberian Orchestra is the cleanest case in the corpus of the last kind. He announced it at 17:04, dismissed it at 23:07, and it left no trace in the record he later compiled. A concert log is a record of intentions; a contemporaneous feed is a record of evenings.

The archive also corroborates the table's entries to the day, from inside the venues: *"all time lowww/the maine"* on October 18; *"holy fuck, jedi mind tricks are so fucking scary."* at 01:50 on November 17, walking out; *"fall out boy/TAI/WTK - @houseofblues"* on December 12. And it settled the Hey Monday row: on December 19, from the airport, he posted *"I don't want to go to cold, lame PA. Be back on the 28th for hey Monday :)"*; on December 27 at 19:40, *"Flying to Orlando for hey monday tomorrow. Wooo"*, followed by *"Hate airport days."* at 10:38 on the 28th. He flew back to Florida early, mid-holiday, for this one show. The date is December 28, 2008 — and the table already held the ticket, ten rows later, filed under the opener's name: "There for Tomorrow · Dec 28, 2008 · BACKBOOTH · 1 General Admission ticket; Order #M7WKYAHQW." The most economical reading is that the two rows are one night, filed twice — but that join is inference, and nothing in the corpus states the BACKBOOTH bill.

April 8, 2009, at UCF Arena: Flight of the Conchords — the last Orlando entry, and the last entry for two years.

## The tail: 2010–2019

September 3–5, 2010: Electric Zoo at Randall's Island, Labor Day weekend — his stage-at-dusk photo and his video of Diplo's tent set corroborate the 2010 edition. Around 2010: a tiny Diplo club set at R Bar in New York, his photo showing Diplo on decks with a MacBook. April 2, 2011, at Madison Square Garden: the LCD Soundsystem "final" show, billed as the last ever — they reunited in 2016 — with his arena photo corroborating attendance. April 29 to May 1, 2011: Bamboozle at the New Meadowlands Stadium in East Rutherford, with Lil Wayne, Mötley Crüe, Taking Back Sunday, and A Day to Remember. August 7–9, 2012: Bloc Party's three-night sold-out *Four* run at Terminal 5 in New York — which he recalled, in the record, as "Terminal 9."

August 30, 2013: the Oddball Comedy & Curiosity Festival at Post-Gazette Pavilion (then First Niagara Pavilion) in Burgettstown, with Dave Chappelle, Hannibal Buress, and John Mulaney — and with Katie Fletcher. At 22:54 Eastern, from inside the venue, he posted a Vine: *"Frogs and Katie Fletcher at #oddballcomedyfest Pittsburgh."* It is the best-evidenced single evening in the entire concert record: a ticket, a platform-timestamped public post, and his own 2026 retelling all name the same night, and two of the three name the person he was with, which no ticket can. Full verification of the three records lives at [[wiki/interests/concert-record/festivals/oddball-comedy-curiosity-festival]].

The record ends April 20, 2019: Freddie Gibbs's "High Tide 4" 4/20 cruise aboard the Hornblower Infinity Yacht in New York, with Wiki and Kota the Friend, boarded at 353 West St. From a regional nu-metal fairground at twelve to a yacht off Manhattan at thirty.

## Conflicts in the record

> **CONTRADICTION [2026-08-02] — the self-kept note and the researched list disagree on HIM.** The Facebook "Concerts I've gone to" note dates HIM to **2005-11-09 at Mr. Small's**; the researched list places the Love Metal Tour at **2004-04-21, The Rock Club at Station Square**, with a separate November 2004 return. Neither source is discarded. The note is contemporaneous self-reporting and is residue; the researched list is a later reconstruction with routing evidence behind it and is stronger on dates but is not primary. Both readings are held.

> **CONTRADICTION [2026-08-02] — the note and the researched list disagree on Taking Back Sunday / Angels & Airwaves.** The note dates it to **2006-07-04 in Columbus**; the researched list gives **2006-06-27 at the Chevrolet Amphitheatre in Pittsburgh** and explicitly flags it as *"corrected from 2007."* Same standing as above: contemporaneous residue against a routed reconstruction.

> **DATE DISCREPANCY — Bamboozle 2008.** The Facebook note dates Bamboozle to **2008-05-03**; the researched list places Panic! and Coheed's co-headlined edition on **2008-05-04** at the Meadowlands Sports Complex. One day apart; the sources are held side by side.

> **THREE-WAY AMBIGUITY — John Mayer.** Three dates are individually viable — **2003-08-14**, **2004-08-11**, or **2008-08-20**, all at Post-Gazette Pavilion — and none of them sits on a Coldplay bill, which is how the memory had filed it. Recorded as open rather than guessed at.

> **COMPLETENESS CLAIM RETRACTED [2026-09-04] — the table is a purchase record, not an attendance record.** The master table's implicit claim to completeness is false: one autumn of the twitter archive names three Orlando shows it does not contain — The Roots (October 7, 2008), Cobra Starship / Hit The Lights / Forever The Sickest Kids (November 13, 2008), and Trans-Siberian Orchestra (November 15, 2008). Trans-Siberian Orchestra is the mechanism's cleanest demonstration: announced at 17:04, dismissed as "SUPER lame" at 23:07, absent from the later record entirely. Any show somebody else bought the ticket for, walked into, or chose not to remember is invisible to a stub-built log. The twitter archive runs from September 2008 to 2026 and the concert table stops in 2019; the two have never been read against each other except for these ninety-eight days, so rows 6, 16, 17, 27 and 32 — undated or unidentified, four of them inside years the archive covers — remain specifically checkable rather than closed.

> **INFERENCE FLAG [2026-09-04] — the Hey Monday / There for Tomorrow join.** The tweets settle Hey Monday to December 28, 2008 without touching the billing question. The BACKBOOTH ticket filed ten rows later under There For Tomorrow — same city, same night, 250-capacity room, Order #M7WKYAHQW — is the most economical single-night reading, but it is inference: nothing in the corpus states the BACKBOOTH bill.

> **ARTIST FIELD UNRECORDED — the 2011 "final" show.** The master table's April 2, 2011 Madison Square Garden row carries no artist name; the operator-pass note identifies it as the LCD Soundsystem "final" show. The identification rests on the note and the corroborating arena photo, not on the table itself.

> **UNVERIFIED [2026-08-17] — the Tom Petty date.** The Post-Gazette Pavilion's sold-out Tom Petty date of June 15, 2005 — the off-record night — has not been independently verified against tour records. The capsule count, the Rt 51 speed, and the cone impacts are single-source testimony: Dan's own retelling, self-incriminating in the one detail that matters (the drive happened at or near peak intoxication, per his 2026-08-17 correction), and therefore the strongest form single-source testimony takes — but still one witness.

## Assessment

Three things are true at once here that the wiki had flattened into "top music artists":

1. **He toured the scene, he didn't just like it.** Three Fall Out Boy shows in eleven months plus a release-day television taping is fandom as logistics, not a playlist. The Rolling Rock fairs start at age twelve; the itinerary was already a habit before the scene found him.
2. **The TRL taping was an industry gimmick he was physically inside of.** The Feb 6, 2007 appearance was the 3-city / 3-show *Infinity On High* release-week stunt; he was in the studio for it.
3. **The secret show was a deliberate in-joke he was let in on.** Buffalo was billed "Schrute Farms" and, per a 2025 ChatGPT cross-check he ran himself, was the first and only time *Take This To Your Grave* was played in full.

This is the same collector's-completeness instinct that shows up later in the message-corpora forensics and the wiki itself: a teenager who logs every show he attends and remembers the fake name on the marquee is already running the archival reflex the rest of the corpus documents. And the 2026-09-04 twitter finding sharpens the instinct's shape: he kept the stub record of what he bought, and the feed recorded what he actually did — the log is a record of intentions, and intentions were the identity.

This record is the itinerary/devotion facet of the four-mode thesis at [[wiki/mind/synthesis/music-as-identity]] — fandom run as logistics, the mid-2000s scene followed city to city.
