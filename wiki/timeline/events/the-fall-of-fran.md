# The Fall of Fran (night of March 7–8, 2018)

On the night of March 7–8, 2018, two things Dan Frank had been building toward for the better part of a year arrived in the same house within minutes of each other. The first was the opening encounter of the non-monogamy arrangement he and [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] had been discussing since roughly early 2017 — a third party, [[wiki/people/danny-matthews|Danny Matthews]], due at the door for "a real quick and low stakes romp." The second was the fall that broke [[wiki/people/fran-coldren|Fran Coldren's]] hip, removed her from [[wiki/places/117-belmont-circle|117 Belmont Circle]] on a stretcher after five decades, and started the four-week collapse that killed her. They did not merely coincide. Fran's first fall happened while Danny was en route; the encounter went ahead anyway; and the second, worse fall happened in the minutes after Dan closed the door behind him.

This page is the night's own record. The four weeks that follow it — the surgery, the nursing home, the eviction letter, the pediatric-wing vigil and the death — are at [[wiki/timeline/events/fran-death-vigil]]. What matters here is the collision itself: a plan a year in the making executing on schedule through a catastrophic injury to the person Dan was being paid fifteen dollars an hour to protect, in the house she had instructed both him and his mother never to let take her. The corpus's own contemporaneous name for the fall is **"Down Goes Frazier."**

Three anchors in the held message corpus date and bound the story. The arrangement's negotiation opened on **February 16, 2018** — "you guys would be the first couple we were with," Dan's own message, verified verbatim — which resettles the arrangement's documented start eight months earlier than the November 2018 date the wiki had carried. The fall itself is dated by the next morning's caregiver-thread messages, Dan to [[wiki/people/vicki|Vicki]] at 13:29 EST on March 8: "she was screaming so loud when they were putting her on the gurney it was just awful." And the cost is dated by her death exactly four weeks later, April 4, 2018. What happens between those anchors — the two impacts, the decision to let the evening proceed, the encounter's deliberate blank — comes from Dan's 2026-capture retellings, attributed testimony that the corpus cannot corroborate because the people who could confirm it never recorded it.

## The lead-up: six months of paid caregiving under a standing order

For the last six months of Fran's life, Dan was her paid full-time caregiver at $15 an hour — not the wiki's inference but Dan's own contemporaneous summary, sent April 6, 2018, two days after her death and verified verbatim in the held iMessage corpus: *"she was a trip dude but i spent the past 6 months looking after her full time ($15 an hour to play video games with gram is a good deal) and we had a lot of fun. got good closure and finally did something in my life that wasn't completely selfish haha."* That sentence back-computes the paid arrangement's start to roughly October 2017 and corrects the wiki's earlier multi-year caregiving inference. Dan's own 2018-03-30 reading puts the estate's trustee in the payroll chain — "fred would have put the kaibosh on me being paid to watch gram," i.e. [[wiki/people/fred-adams|Judge Fred Adams]], Ira's old law partner — though that row survives only in the unheld dump, not the held corpus, so it is Dan's account rather than a verified document. [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] worked alongside him, and in his own assessment she was "10,000× more responsible" — the changing, the day-to-day survival, the actual nursing. His contribution was the opposite kind: he refused to sanitize a 97-year-old with dementia and treated her as a peer — video games, the bong she had complimented as "a lovely vase," the vape, the carnage of *GTA V* run in stage mode until she delivered the line the family kept: "WHAT. THE FUCK. IS HAPPENING IN THIS WORLD ANYMORE."

The standing directive governing all of it was categorical, and it was given to [[wiki/people/suzanne-frank|Suz]] and Dan jointly, in the same terms: **keep me at home no matter what, do everything you can to not let me get taken from this house.** The rationale everyone understood was that if she entered a facility once she was never getting out. She turned out to be right, which is why the directive governs the reading of every decision made in her last year — including the one at the center of this page. The directive itself has no held-corpus hit; it is the page's account from unheld captures, and that absence is worth keeping on the record because the whole defense of the night's decision rests on it.

By the winter of 2017–18 the directive was under active pressure from two directions at once: Fran's decline and the family's campaign. The decline had already produced a filmed crisis that was **not** the fall that ended her residency. On the keno morning — roughly late 2017, the exact date unestablished — Dan woke at 8:00 AM to a "bump," found Fran collapsed on the carpet wrapped in a white faux-fur blanket, screaming in agony and unable to stand, the record preserving her "I don't want to die." Suz arrived around 10:00 AM, having spent the whole night into the morning in a video-keno marathon. Dan told his mother "we have to just call an ambulance, this is crazy," and Suz — in Dan's account of her words — said **"nope. i'll be back in a little bit"** and left. At 2:00 PM Fran was still down, "barely making any sense and screaming whenever she tries to stand" while Annie worked a full shift; Suz got back at 4:00 PM and performed the manual lift, captured in `IMG_0569.MOV`. Dan sustained himself through the hours of solo triage, by his own later account, with cocaine bumps. Fran recovered and made it a few more months after this spill; the formal shift rotation dates from roughly this period, [[wiki/people/marla|Marla's]] first message arriving December 8, 2017.

What kept him from overriding Suz that morning, on the record, is not squeamishness — it is that he was, in his own words, terrified that if the situation went sideways his grandparents would use it to strip his inheritance "or worse." The pair was carried on the page as "Diane and George" until Dan's operator answer of 2026-08-17 settled it in two words: **"Dian and Dave"** — Diane, his maternal grandmother, and her second husband, not Suz's father. It makes the fear a forecast rather than a mood: that same couple served the eviction notice, took the hospital's calls, and sent the exclusion letter. An eight-hour delay in treating a fallen 97-year-old is thereby explained by an estate, which is the family's whole architecture in one morning.

The autumn leaves a second, smaller marker. On the evening of **October 7, 2017**, Dan reported to an outside contact, the unidentified urpaaa-at-yahoo correspondent: "no relaxing. gram is bleeding. hit her arm on the chair. i'm only one here." Casual caregiving bulletins about Fran went only to the innermost circle; the assumed shared referent is the giveaway that this contact already knew the shape of the year. And the threat the family posed was already live as a written fact by **November 26, 2017**, when Dan wrote — at 10:57 EST, verified in the held corpus — that certain people must be kept away from the house, *"especially and most specifically not people directly connected to my grandparents who are looking for any excuse to throw her in a nursing home."* Four months before the fall, five before the letters, the conflict the spring would formalize was already understood as an active campaign while Fran was still at home. In December 2017 he described her, in a message that survives only in the unheld dump, as "a 97 year old woman with advanced dimentia" — the age row the held corpus cannot hit under any spelling, but which arithmetic sustains against the dated birth (1920-08-15) and death (2018-04-04) regardless.

## The plan: three weeks of negotiation

The arrangement had been "tossed around for almost a year" before it was executed. The choice of third party was deliberately unthreatening: Danny Matthews, a local residential realtor, a grade above Dan at school and remembered from then as a dork — someone with no standing to displace anyone. The framing Dan gives it is logistical rather than romantic: "a real quick and low stakes romp," an in-and-out.

The corpus preserves the negotiation itself, and it is unusually clean. The Danny Matthews thread opens on **February 16, 2018** with Dan proposing a Sunday, Danny answering with his and his girlfriend Nicole's own experience ("you guys gotta be gentle though... like I said we're not pros... we've had our own experiences before we got together, and a few since, but it's always been a good time for everyone"), and Dan replying with the line that dates the whole architecture: **"you guys would be the first couple we were with."** The line is verified verbatim in the held corpus (2018-02-16 23:32:34 UTC, from Dan), and seven minutes later in the same thread (23:39:36 UTC) he discloses the camming operation — "we make like $150 an hour" — matching the page's "within twenty minutes" framing. The arrangement was opened, negotiated and disclosed in a single evening, three weeks before it was consummated.

That single sentence resettles a date the wiki had wrong by eight months. [[wiki/mind/psychosexual/arrangement-history]] had the November 2018 Alexis reunion as "the earliest well-documented instance"; it is not. This night is, and Dan says so himself in writing at the time. The Danny thread is also what kills the 2017 misdating of the whole sequence from the inside: in March 2017 the corpus holds no contact with Danny at all. The arrangement could not have been executed a year before it was negotiated.

Danny would turn out to be the only person who appears at 117 Belmont Circle in both of its endings. Inside it the night Fran's residency ended; fifteen months later, the agent who listed it for sale — the house "finally went pending" by July 11, 2019, the sale closing in summer 2019 for $250,000. His thread runs 349 messages from 2018-02-16 to 2024-12-26, an intermittent seven-year connection with no apparent awkwardness between its two halves: real-estate logistics in one message, swinger logistics in the next.

## The night: March 7–8, 2018

Dan and Annie had taken the overnight — "we can stay tonight, annie didn't have to work" — and the next morning's messages document the ambulance. The account of the night itself comes from Dan's 2026-capture retellings and is attributed throughout, because the one held-corpus anchor inside the choreography is the gurney quote, and everything else rests on the person who lived it.

Fran had been put to bed several minutes earlier. The first impact came while Danny was still en route — loud enough that Dan's own retelling renders it as a detonation. He reached her and knew immediately that this one was different from the previous spills, including the keno morning with Suz that had ended with a manual lift and no ambulance. She was hurt. She said, characteristically, "No, I'm... I'm okay," and the doorbell rang on the same beat.

The decision Dan made in that moment is the part of the night he defends rather than apologizes for, and he defends it with her own instruction. The standing directive Fran had given him and Suz was categorical: **keep me at home no matter what, do everything you can to not let me get taken from this house.** She said she wanted to go back to bed. Calling an ambulance meant overriding the one thing she had asked of both of them. He put her back to bed, and the evening went ahead.

He does not remember the encounter. His account is explicit about the gap: "I don't remember any of it. It happened" — the next thing in memory is the door closing behind Danny and the walk back to the bedroom. What he does remember is the function it served, which he states in mechanical terms: the exposure filled "the masochistic emotional pinch I was looking for (satiating the jealousy by basking in jealousy)," with the intended payoff still to come — sex with Annie while the jealousy was live. This is the most direct first-person statement of the two-stage procedure that [[wiki/mind/concepts/erotic-architecture]] theorizes from the outside.

The second impact came before that payoff. It was louder than the first, and this time Fran was screaming. Dan called 911 with the physical residue of the preceding hour still on him — a detail he keeps in every telling, because the juxtaposition is the point of the story to him. The next fixed image in his memory is Fran being carried out of her own house on a stretcher. That image is corroborated the next morning in the held corpus: Dan to Vicki at 13:29 EST — "she was screaming so loud when they were putting her on the gurney it was just awful." Marla asks "What time did ambulance take her?"; Vicki notes "it's a good thing Marla wasn't there, she would have been really scared." And Dan's same-morning message names what the night meant to him as a caregiver: "so much relief that we didn't drop the ball last night. i was always worried something would happen and i wouldn't hear her and she would lay there suffering." This time even Suz said "we don't have a choice."

## What it cost: the four weeks after

Her hip was broken. By **March 9** she had been through surgery — Dan's 21:29 EST message: "sorry dude just had a lot going on with gram and all. she got through the surgery and supposed to go to a nursing home on monday" — and by about **Monday, March 12**, she was in one: the precise outcome the standing directive existed to prevent, and the one she had predicted would finish her. It did. Her mind did not come back. What remained was a single fixed question that she asked without stopping: *when am I leaving?* Dan reads that fixation as being about three things at once and says so — the house, the autonomy, and a specific contempt for being absorbed into "the institutional sludge that defined 'normalcy.'" She deteriorated, got sick, and was dead four weeks later. Footage from the nursing-home interval (`IMG_4361.MOV`) shows her aphasic in a wheelchair, working at the lap belt, still trying to get free — and Dan marks it as the last time he actually spoke with her; she was nonresponsive for everything after.

The stretcher is the hinge. She never re-entered 117 Belmont Circle. A fifty-seven-year residency — the GEDCOM fixes her tenure from about 1961, a return from Miami Beach with her daughter married inside the house that February, which makes the residency fifty-seven years rather than the round fifty every retelling uses — the physical seat of everything on her page, ended on one night. Not by sale, not by decision, but because a plan was in motion and a fall arrived in the middle of it. The sale came fifteen months later, with Danny as the agent.

While she was dying, the estate moved on the living. On **March 29** — mid-collapse, three days before the final admission — Dan was served an eviction notice terminating his residency at the Virginia Avenue address and demanding the keys (`IMG_4647.jpg`). The server was listed as "Dian V. Moore" — identified 2026-08-18, on the wiki's own record, as **Diane Moore herself**, Fran's only child and Dan's maternal grandmother. The notice is the concrete instance of the pattern the record names everywhere: the functional sentinels doing the work while the institutional actors managed the assets.

On **April 1** — the first day of caddie season, Dan having worked two loops at Nemacolin in the freezing cold before getting the call — Fran was moved to a wing of the Uniontown Hospital pediatric section to die, a room secured by the donations she and Ira had made. Judge Fred Adams mandated that someone be with her 24 hours a day, and the rotation held it in shifts: [[wiki/people/vicki|Vicki]] arriving at 7:00 AM ("I wouldn't want to be anywhere else, this lady is like my grandmother I never had"), [[wiki/people/marla|Marla]] continuing nights ("you've been doing an amazing job for gram, especially since she's been in the hospital"), Dan and Annie taking their turns. The hospital cut off her water as comfort care — "they cut WATER which i thought was weird" — and she fought through roughly four days of dehydration, nonresponsive, at times seizing, "calling for Ira last week" per Vicki. On the same day, April 1, the social workers were, in Dan's contemporaneous account, "talking to dave moore about the day-to-day stuff" while Diane "hasn't been able to be reached by anyone" — Dave speaking on her behalf, a detail that matters for what came next.

The exclusion order landed in the terminal week. On **April 3**, the day before Fran died, Dan was reading, forwarding, and lawyering a letter from his maternal grandmother setting rules for his presence in Fran's house, demanding the keys, and closing with the sentence *"any violation of the rules will be considered trespassing."* His objection to it was never that it inconvenienced him — in Dan's account, "nothing would have made Fran more upset and furious than this." His read of the document that night was that it wasn't really Diane's writing: *"i think dave wrote it — read the last paragraph."* Annie, contrary to the earlier account, received no letter of her own; Dan's message that evening is explicit — *"lol also if they are writing such professional correspondence why wouldn't Annie get her own letter"* — and she was upset about that one. The letter's operative effect is on the record: the two people who had kept Fran at home for three years held no shifts in the rotation Adams had mandated. They attended anyway, off the roster. Dan's own note on being left out is four words long: "I wasn't really complaining."

Fran died on **April 4, 2018**, with Dan and Annie the two in the room at the moment itself. The death date is fixed by two independent held-corpus messages: Marla's "She made it thru the nite" at 07:23 EST — the last one, a message that only gets sent while someone is still alive the morning after — and [[wiki/people/davey-fitzpatrick|Davey Fitzpatrick's]] unprompted "Sorry for your loss bro" the next afternoon at 14:47 EST, corroboration from outside the family. Dan's contemporaneous verdict: "fran was happy when she died SPECIFICALLY because annie and i were there with her." Three minutes later it began to snow outside Uniontown Hospital. His longer verdict: "it really was about as quick, painless and easy of a death as one could wish for. if anyone deserved it, it was her. she grew up DIRT fucking poor and went on to live an absolute batshit crazy life." A memorial celebration followed at the Uniontown Country Club on May 6, 12–4 PM, deliberately framed as "a happy occasion and something that gram would have approved of," not a conventional funeral.

The room had one more event in it before the end. During the four-day shutdown Dan stepped into the room's bathroom, lost an argument with a smoke detector, and brought a building-wide fire alarm — four fire trucks in the docket-era account, six in his 2026 telling, the conflict preserved on [[wiki/timeline/events/uniontown-hospital-vape-alarm]] — into her death watch. A Fayette County summary case, docket MJ-14101-NT-0000082-2018, was filed April 5, the day after she died, on a single local-ordinance count of smoking in a hospital. The fall and the fire alarm bracket the same four weeks and share one structure: an urge executed on schedule inside a room where something catastrophic was already happening.

The death moment itself got a story and a retraction within twenty-four hours, both verified verbatim in the held corpus. The story (April 5, 20:10, to a friend): Annie asleep at the bedside, Dan silently asking — "i said to myself 'if you can hear me give me some kind of sign'" — and Annie waking him immediately after to say Fran had blinked for the first time in a week; he told her he loved her, she smiled, and she slowly passed. The retraction (April 5, 23:26, three hours later, to his sister): "upon further review of the video, it looks like a monitor went off alerting us that her pulse was dropping. neither of us remembered it but the video helps correct the timeline. def isn't as 'supernatural' knowing that fact. but doesn't take away from the cool moment we had with gram." Both halves belong on the record, and the full account of the vigil lives at [[wiki/timeline/events/fran-death-vigil]].

The money ran alongside the grief and the page keeps both. In **January 2018**, with her alive and declining, Dan was writing about what comes "after gram goes and we have a chance to get above water with the inheritance... i want to seriously get out of here." On **April 4**, the day she died, he texted an acquaintance: "also.....,.inheritance will help with the mourning proce$$." Two days later: "now that i'm not saddled here with gram anymore....and that inheritance money coming...ya boy is getting out of PA." And the same day as the caregiving summary: "thanks buddy. gram did aaaaaanything i asked her to, so the least i could do was be there for her" — the closing verdict on the six months, verified at 2018-04-06 19:22. A first cheque arrived **April 27, 2018** — "i got my inheritance check from grandma today." The mourning was not performed and the calculation was not performed. They ran concurrently, which is a more honest description of how inheritance actually works than the reverent version.
