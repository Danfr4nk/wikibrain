# Caddying — Nemacolin, Laurel Valley, Pikewood National (2016–2019)

Between the [[wiki/legal/2015-possession-arrest|possession arrest]] and the deep 2017–18 poverty years, Dan
held a caddying job at **Nemacolin Woodlands Resort** (Farmington, PA)
titled "Experience Associate – Golf," **April 2016 – November 2019** per
his resume — borrowing golf shoes for the interview on April 6, 2016. The
role covered guest golf-round assistance, strategic course advice across
Nemacolin's two courses, pace-of-play management during corporate and
charity outings, and equipment logistics. His manager was Brad Hubeaut.
The job was not just income: it reignited his own playing and anchored
the era in which [[wiki/interests/golf|golf]] was at once his sport, his
social world, and his primary paycheck.

At **43 months** this is the longest assigned commitment in the corpus after
[[wiki/work/au-zaatar|Au Za'atar]] — and the caddying years run concurrent with the
darkest financial stretch documented elsewhere in the wiki, evidence that the
2016–2019 window, often treated as background to the early
[[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] relationship, had its own steady, physically
demanding income source running the whole time.

## The hire (April 2016)

The starting condition is worth recording exactly: he **borrowed golf shoes for the interview** on April 6, 2016 — a hire into a luxury resort golf operation made without owning the uniform of the trade. His manager was **Brad Hubeaut**, and the resume title was "Experience Associate – Golf," the resort's euphemism for caddie.

The job took hold fast. In **June 2016** he watched a full broadcast of the 84 Lumber Classic specifically "to better understand course strategy for my low handicap loops" — tournament golf consumed as professional development for the caddying work, not entertainment. And by **May 11, 2016**, days into the job, he wrote: *"I really really dig it. Spending all day on the golf course is a blast and it's getting me back into playing in a big way. Also mystic rock is top notch and really hard."* The hire didn't just supply income; it reignited his own playing, with the employee discount making rounds on Nemacolin's two courses — **Mystic Rock** and **Shepherd's Rock** — cheap enough to actually use (*"We can play mystic rock for $15 with my employee discount"*).

The first season's texture is family-adjacent. On **May 10, 2016** he texts, *"Hey when you see gram tomorrow can you let her know we made it back safely. We got back from Nemacolin and took her ..."* — Fran, 98 years old, being taken up to the resort for her birthday dinner that August (*"We are going to take gram to dinner tomorrow night at Nemacolin for her birthday"*). The job's orbit included the family's matriarch before it ever became a social world of its own. By **August 17, 2016** there is a 401k enrollment letter (*"I'm now enrolled in the Nemacolin 401k plan, i believe that means I'll be eligible for health ..."*), and by November the family is treating the job as the plan: *"You need to call Nemacolin. From now until the beginning of January you could be making a lot of money this is big time ..."* (Nov 11, 2016). The one documented miss of the season is also familial — a September 2016 golf-and-dinner plan that collapsed because, per Suz's testimony, *"Dan couldn't get his shit together. He says, 'I feel terrible.'"*

## The looper hierarchy and day trips

Caddying at a resort of Nemacolin's caliber runs a visible status ladder — "the real loopers" get called up for off-property day trips to prestige courses, and Dan's May 2018 messages capture his first such call-up as a milestone (*"first time getting called up with the real loopers to take a day trip and work at another track"*). On **May 21, 2018** he made his first off-property day trip to **Laurel Valley Golf Club**, leaving Uniontown at 4:30 AM alongside named coworkers Brad (his manager there), Trevor, Brooke, "cuzzy," Will, and Chad — single-bag that day, paired two caddies to a group, $100 cash for the second loop. He rated the outing directly against his home track: "an impressive lack of golf knowledge for how long I've done the job." Two days later, on **May 23, 2018**, he was paired at Laurel Valley with **Carl** — "the head of the caddie program there and also the head of the program in Scottsdale" (TPC Scottsdale) — for what he explicitly calls his actual first time carrying two bags simultaneously ($90 flat for the pair): *"getting out with Carl was a huge blessing (it could have been a disaster)."* He used the pairing to ask directly about transferring to Laurel Valley full-time, describing it as "a much more beneficial [career] situation and a natural progression from looping at [Nemacolin's] mystic [rock course]" — the transfer never happened, but the ambition is the clearest documented evidence he saw caddying as a career rather than a stopgap.

The season's own mythology grew around Mystic Rock. On **May 12, 2018**: *"One more eval card and I can play THE mystic rock, widely considered to be 'The Wire' of Mid-Atlantic golf"* — his own coinage, comparing the course to the prestige-drama canon. On **May 21**: *"Haha bro $200 for 36 is a California 10 for mystic rock loops"* — the loop fees as the yardstick everything else is measured against. And on **April 12, 2017** he forwarded the Post-Gazette's writeup of Nemacolin's new Pete Dye course, **Shepherd's Rock**, the second half of the home track: *"New-golf-course-Nemacolin-Woodlands-Shepherd-s-Rock-Pete-Dye."* By July he was up early for practice on it (*"hey dude, i came up here super early to hit some balls and try to pull off a miracle with a shepherd's rock ..."*), and that same day: *"i got us on shepherd's"* — the employee access functioning as the social currency it was meant to be.

## The yard cohort

The caddie yard was a social world with its own management layer, its own hierarchy of regard, and its own folklore — and the corpus names it person by person.

**Davey Fitzpatrick** is the yard's management layer in the record: assistant caddie master for 2018, the source of every documented report time and training assignment in Dan's season, and the coworker who picked Dan to train the new caddies. On **October 1, 2017** someone asks, *"So it's like caddie master?"* — the yard's internal vocabulary surfacing in the message record. Davey's standing is the one institutional fact the page has: he is the person the season's choreography flowed through.

**Vaughn** is the cohort's paycheck-to-paycheck edge: a fellow caddie coordinating rides to the yard and check pickups, who doubled as a small-time weed middleman between loops. The carpool-and-report-time choreography — *"I'm on my way up the mtn, late report time"* (Sep 28, 2017) — is the season's daily texture, and Vaughn is the name attached to its most precarious version.

**The unidentified "urpaaa" thread** runs entirely on the yard's clock: report times, a $100 Venmo tip, and Dan looping **5.5 hours the day after a tooth extraction** because the yard culture punished sick days. It is the single clearest datapoint on the physical cost the job extracted — the show-up-or-lose-your-spot economy of resort caddying, documented in one body.

**Zach Clingan** runs on the yard's clock too — getting cut, the offered two-bag round, the end-of-season bonfire invite, the next April's opening-day ping. He is flagged in Conflicts below, because the 2015–16 corpus shows a Zach Clingan in a very different register: *"Zach clingan just texted me saying 'I have to talk to you'"* (Dec 9, 2015), *"He pointed out that Clingan said 'I HAVE THE POWER!'"*, *"Zach Clingan is their undercover sleeper agent"* (Dec 11, 2015), and *"Zach Clingan and I were best friends"* (Jan 7, 2016, past tense). Whether the yard-cohort Clingan is the same person reconciled, or a different contact sharing the name, is unestablished — the wiki treats them as one pending evidence either way.

The cohort's own social events were Dan's, not the club's: **Christo Coan's** October 31, 2017 party was open to *"every caddie, staging, and pro shop employee"* and thrown at Dan's house. **Steve Kezmarsky** asked to be gotten into the yard in July 2017 and never submitted the resume — a documented near-member, the one who didn't make it in. The periphery includes a **Bill** who is a caddying peer distinct from Bill Ulmer, and **Joey Superick**, a brief golf-and-music friendship from June 2018 adjacent to the same scene.

And the cohort had its own folk heroes. On **May 16, 2018**: *"One of the caddies we were training said 'you guys know Danny Matthews? That dude can hit the ball 400 Fucking yards'"* — the local realtor ([[wiki/people/danny-matthews]]) circulating in the yard's oral tradition as a physical specimen, months before the corpus's main Danny Matthews thread had fully developed.

## The season's texture: money, sickness, and VIPs

The job's texture in the corpus is not the golf — it is the money management and the call-for-backup culture. *"I'm driving to Nemacolin to get money"* (Aug 1, 2016): the resort as the physical place the paychecks lived. The *"call for backup"* register runs through the record: a caddie manager fired mid-tenure for a no-call-no-show (*"apparently... not getting along with narcotics of some sort,"* per a 2019 message), VIP loops staff refused to caddie for (*"we're not taking caddies"*), and the constant carpool-and-report-time choreography. The pattern matches the same "reads every interaction for the tell" [[wiki/mind/concepts/forensic-method|forensic instinct]] the rest of the corpus documents in professional settings — the yard as another institution being read for its real rules rather than its stated ones.

The seasons also mark the calendar the operator's memory misdated. Caddie-season opening day is a tag both April 2017 and April 2018 carry, which is the likeliest mechanism behind the operator's one-year misdating of the whole Fran sequence ([[wiki/timeline/events/the-fall-of-fran]]). The job was the metronome of those years; when the memory slips a year, the slip runs on the yard's clock.

## The seasons, year by year

The corpus lets the four seasons be told as dated texture rather than summary.

**2016 — the hire and the family orbit.** April 6: borrowed golf shoes for the interview. May 5: *"Matt crushing it today at mystic rock. No wind haha."* May 10: the "we got back from Nemacolin and took her" message about Fran. May 11: *"I really really dig it."* August: the 401k letter; the birthday dinner for Fran at the resort. August 1: *"I'm driving to Nemacolin to get money"* — the resort as the place paychecks live. November 11: the family's *"You need to call Nemacolin... you could be making a lot of money this is big time"* — the job being treated as the plan by everyone around him. December 24: *"Not to busy but Nemacolin I've heard is close to full so that should bring some people in"* — the resort's occupancy as household small talk. The first season is legible as a family project as much as an employment: Fran taken up the mountain, Suz coordinating rides to Mystic Rock, the 401k letter forwarded with pride.

**2017 — the second course and the yard's social peak.** April 12: the Post-Gazette Shepherd's Rock piece forwarded — Nemacolin's new Pete Dye course entering the record the week it opened. July 16: the early-morning practice session and *"i got us on shepherd's"* — the employee access functioning as social currency. September 28: *"I'm on my way up the mtn, late report time"* — the season's daily choreography in one line. October 1: *"So it's like caddie master?"* — the yard's internal vocabulary. October 18 and 27: the bonfire invitations (*"Just gonna be a bonfire and hangs kinda thing"*), the season's social calendar. October 31: Christo Coan's party for every caddie, staging, and pro shop employee, thrown at Dan's house. November 5: the birthday near-ace on Mystic Rock's 17th — the season's personal high-water mark, recorded as a golf fact rather than a work fact, which is the point: by year two the job and the game are the same life.

**2018 — the professional peak.** March 9: *"jack rusko and i went up to mystic rock to get our paperwork"* — the season's administrative errands as social fabric. April 23: the Mystic Rock vs. Shepherd's Rock course criticism. May 12: *"One more eval card and I can play THE mystic rock."* May 16: the Danny Matthews 400-yard folklore from the trainees. May 21: the Laurel Valley day trip, 4:30 AM departure, *"first time getting called up with the real loopers."* May 23: the Carl pairing, the first two-bag round, the Scottsdale transfer raised and not taken. The season reads as a career being built in real time — eval cards, call-ups, a transfer conversation with the head of two programs — and then the transfer doesn't happen, and the record never says why.

**2019 — the wind-down.** The year's texture is institutional decay rather than personal arc: a caddie manager fired mid-tenure for a no-call-no-show (*"apparently... not getting along with narcotics of some sort"*), VIP loops the staff refused (*"we're not taking caddies"*). The resume says November 2019. Nothing in the corpus stages a departure scene. The job that generated the bulk of his twenties' friendships ends the way the corpus usually ends things: not with an event, but with the messages about it stopping.

## Fran's house as the yard's staging point

Across the 2018 season, [[wiki/places/117-belmont-circle|Fran's house]] — on the UCC fairway, the golf-course house — served as the caddie yard's informal staging point: a coworker put up there to save a hotel bill in April, a golf meet-up called from it in May, and a September pharmaceutical handoff, running five months past her death. The convergence is worth stating plainly: the family golf lineage (Fran's house on the fairway) and the working golf life (the yard) shared an address for a full season. It is also the same house where [[wiki/people/danny-matthews|Danny Matthews]] — the arrangement's third party and the yard's 400-yard folk hero — appears as the only person present at both of the house's endings.

## The end (November 2019)

The resume dates the job **April 2016 – November 2019**. The exact end circumstances are undocumented — voluntary departure versus displaced — and no message record narrows when the caddying work actually stopped versus when Au Za'atar began (March 2021), leaving a roughly 16-month unaccounted gap. The gap itself is a finding about the corpus's coverage: a 43-month job that generated the bulk of a decade's friendships leaves no departure scene, no farewell thread, no "last day" message. The Scottsdale transfer conversation with Carl is the last documented ambition in the record; whether the job ended because the ambition died or the ambition died because the job ended is unestablished. What is established is that the next documented work era — Au Za'atar, March 2021 — begins with no bridge between them, and the wiki does not invent one.

The pattern the cohort documents is the one that matters for the rest of the wiki: **caddying was the last era in which work generated Dan's friendships in bulk** — nothing after it (Au Za'atar's Triad excepted) produced a comparable social layer.

## Conflicts in the record

- **End-date ambiguity.** Resume says November 2019; `date_range_end` here is 2019-11-01. Whether that is the last day worked, the last paycheck, or the resume's rounded month is undocumented, and the 16-month gap to Au Za'atar (March 2021) has no message coverage.
- **Zach Clingan identity.** The yard-cohort thread (getting cut, two-bag round, bonfire invite, opening-day ping) may or may not be the same Zach Clingan who appears in December 2015–January 2016 messages as an antagonistic figure and a former best friend. Same-name status is unestablished; the connections entry flags it.
- **The urpaaa handle.** The $100 Venmo tip and tooth-extraction loop come from a thread keyed to an unidentified `urpaaa@yahoo.com` address. The person is unnamed in the corpus; the yard-clock reading is circumstantial but strong.
- **The December 2015 plane crash.** A reported plane crash at Nemacolin Resort (Dec 11, 2015) appears in the corpus four months before the hire and has no connection to Dan's employment — recorded here only so the date doesn't get misread as season context.
- **"Experience Associate – Golf."** The title is the resort's; the work described in the corpus is caddying. No contradiction, but the euphemism is the resort's branding, not Dan's description.

## Assessment

This is the corpus's second-longest assigned commitment and its second case of an assignment hosting someone else's goal ([[wiki/mind/synthesis/the-embedded-objective]]). What the record shows him tracking across 43 months is not the assigned duties — guest assistance, pace-of-play, equipment logistics — but a private progression the employer never set or measured: the call-up to "the real loopers," the 4:30 AM Laurel Valley departure, the first round carrying two bags, and the Scottsdale transfer raised directly with Carl. That ladder is the payload, and it is the weakest of the rule's three positive cases because it is inferred from what Dan chose to record rather than from any statement of intent. It is also, with the failure-to-launch frame, the configuration Assertiveness 5 with Submissiveness 1 predicts is the only kind of commitment he can sustain: the tenure held because the ladder was visible and peer-ranked, not because anyone managed him.
