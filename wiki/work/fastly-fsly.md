# Fastly (FSLY)

Fastly (NYSE: FSLY) is the edge-CDN stock that opened Dan's 2020–2021 market era — the first real trade of a roughly year-long episode in which his pattern-recognition engine, starved of its normal inputs by the pandemic's "micro-radius siege," turned itself on retail markets. The whole position began as four text messages on September 20, 2020: childhood friend [[wiki/people/jerad-friedline|Jerad Friedline]] pitched FSLY as the future of web hosting and edge CDN, claimed a little over $200k in gains since April, and closed with "Buy FSLY and do not touch for 2-3 years." Dan's reply came twenty-three seconds later: "just buy FSLY?"

He bought in — 112 shares, roughly $15k of his portfolio — and rode the steep tail of FSLY's vertical run through early October, skimming a $600 day on October 6 and a quick ~$4k profit before the meme-stock eruption widened the game in January 2021. He was out before the late-2020 earnings guidance blowup that collapsed the stock in a single session. Months later, watching the aftermath, he named the lesson the trade taught him: momentum over conviction. Sell strength; don't marry the thesis; never let "don't touch for 2-3 years" turn a good window into a bag to hold.

FSLY matters because it is the hinge: the one tip that converted a pattern-recognition habit — normally spent on politics, music, and relationships — onto retail markets, and the cleanest documented instance of the asymmetric echo pattern that defines the era. Jerad supplies conviction and scale; Dan supplies a smaller, hedged echo.

## The tip: four messages, September 20, 2020

The entry was a tip from Jerad, not Dan's own research — and the corpus shows the conversion happened in under a minute. At 19:40 EDT on 2020-09-20, Jerad sent two messages. The first was the credential and the thesis: *"FSLY... busy at the moment but will send my research tomorrow. I made a little over $200k from April - now from this stock alone, future of web hosting and edge CDN."* The second was the pitch-closer, a single fact meant to end all diligence: *"Amazon is using Fastly on Amazon.com over their own AWS hosting, that's all you need to know."*

At 20:03 EDT Dan replied, *"just buy FSLY?"* — twenty-three seconds later, per the timestamps, Jerad closed the sale: *"Buy FSLY and do not touch for 2-3 years."* No research was exchanged that night; the promised research would arrive, in effect, seventeen days later, after the position was already green (see below). What moved the money was not a model but a friend with a number: $200k since April. The asymmetry that defines the whole market era is already visible in miniature here — Jerad operates from conviction and scale, Dan operates from social proof and a smaller, hedged echo.

The "don't touch for 2-3 years" line is the buy-and-hold religion in one sentence, and it is the exact advice Dan would later name as the trap. Held for 2–3 years, the position would have ridden FSLY up a little further and then straight down through the late-2020 guidance collapse. Dan didn't follow it literally. He followed the first half — buy — and ignored the second, which is the whole story of the trade.

## Entry: capital, timing, and the stake behind it

The position went in fast. On September 26 at 00:56 EDT — six days after the tip — Dan wrote, *"i can't believe i caiught you when i did on that fastly tip."* The gratitude is doing double duty: thanks for the money, and thanks for the timing. The word "caught" is the giveaway — he already understood, before October, that what he'd caught was a wave, not a value. The typo is preserved verbatim from the corpus; the meaning survives it.

By September 30 he was reporting back: *"112 shares my dude"* and *"fastly killing it."* One hundred twelve shares at the late-September FSLY price is the right order of magnitude for the ~$15k portfolio allocation the page has always carried — the share count is the corpus-anchored figure; the dollar size is the page's long-standing figure from the unheld dump, and the two agree. He had, in his own framing, dumped a large fraction of his portfolio into a single name on a friend's text. This is the intellect/impulsivity paradox the market-era page documents at full amplitude: portfolio-scale bets on tips and vibes, self-aware the entire time.

Where the stake came from is a temporal join the record supports but never states: the estate check of September 1, 2020 — the inheritance landing — sits nineteen days before the tip, and the [[wiki/mind/synthesis/estate-money-spine|estate-money-spine]] page flags it as the probable source of both the ~$15k FSLY position and the later ~$25k Robinhood peak. That join is inference, flagged as such on the spine page and kept flagged here. What the corpus does establish is the sequence: money in, tip arrives, position opens, within weeks.

## The vertical tail: October 6–9, 2020

October is the ascent, and the corpus record for these four days is unusually rich — the only stretch in the trade where Jerad acts as an ongoing analyst rather than a one-time tipster, and where Dan's own messages show the pitch mutating as he repeats it.

On October 6 at 15:37 EDT, the skim: *"dude i made $600 just from FSLY today."* That evening at 21:29 EDT, Jerad wrote in with risk management — the friend, not the trader, doing the worrying: *"Very solid day for FSLY... there could be some volatility the rest of the week with Trump saying no more stimulus (unless he is reelected)... I expect the whole market to drop. Hoping FSLY holds up but just throwing it out there if everything drops tomorrow. Long term it will be a small blip."* Macro fear, micro reassurance, and the long-term framing in one message — the "don't touch" doctrine restated as comfort.

October 7 was the news day. At 20:45 EDT Jerad reported: *"Fastly partnered with Google Cloud, tomorrow could be a great day as well. I don't know what to expect from here. Didn't see it breaking all time high this quick!"* The narrative catalysts were stacking: the TikTok-hosts-Fastly story and now a Google Cloud partnership, both landing on the same vertical run. Three minutes later came the second idea — *"My other play right now is RGR, Ruger Firearms... this election will almost certainly be settled by the courts, I expect Ruger and Smith and Wesson to run like wild leading up to or after the election"* — with the conviction hierarchy stated outright: *"Not as confident as I am with Fastly (because I actually understand Fastly's business model)."* The Ruger pitch never became a Dan position in the corpus; it survives here as the measure of how much bigger the Fastly conviction was.

Then the business-model explainer, the research that "tomorrow" had promised on September 20, arriving October 7 at 20:49 EDT, after the position was already green: *"Fastly is web hosting, paid per usage... so more bandwidth = more $... they also are faster than Amazon AWS and Microsoft Azure, so will continue to win business, while data usage is only going to keep going up."* And the retail-era toolchain pointer at 20:52: *"Lots of good info on Twitter, just search $FSLY."* This is the operating manual's thesis in the wild, weeks before the manual named it: the skill is sensing the crowd, and the crowd is on Twitter under a cashtag.

Dan's own messages that night, 21:10 and 21:11 EDT, show the telephone game running in real time. First the single word *"FSLY"* — a pulse check, a touchstone. Then: *"for fucking real. my buddy works at a rival company and apparently this company Fastly just got bought out by AWS to handle a bunch of logistics."* Compare with what Jerad actually said on September 20: "Amazon is using Fastly on Amazon.com over their own AWS hosting." In seventeen days, "using Fastly on Amazon.com" became "bought out by AWS." The pitch inflated in transmission — and Dan was already transmitting it onward, citing a buddy at a rival company as his own source. This is the evangelism pattern the market-era page documents — Jerad to Dan to a friend who "started calling in daily gains" — caught here at the moment of mutation. The instinct to broadcast is already stronger than the instinct to verify.

On October 9 at 10:16 EDT, Jerad supplied the cleanest statement of the cultural-trend thesis the trade actually ran on: *"I think this is driven on news TikTok passed Instagram in millennial users... Fastly hosts TikTok on a usage based billing platform."* One sentence, no balance sheet, no multiple — millennial user counts as the fundamental. The operating manual would later state the era's insight plainly: "The skill here is the ability to sense cultural trends. The technical trading info and fundamentals reports aren't nearly as important now that retail traders have complete access." The TikTok line is that insight before it had a name.

## Out before the collapse

The entry landed in the steepest part of the run, not early. FSLY had already gone from ~$15 in April to ~$30 by mid-September on Jerad's own "$200k from April" gains; Dan bought in late September and rode the vertical tail. Jerad's October 7 note — "Didn't see it breaking all time high this quick!" — is the sound of the tail end. Dan skimmed the $600 day on October 6 and was out before the exit.

The trap in "don't touch for 2-3 years" is visible in hindsight: FSLY's late-2020 earnings report guided margins down on TikTok uncertainty, and the stock dropped sharply in a single session — one of the worst large-cap drops of that earnings season. The exact date and percentage are market history, not in Dan's message corpus, and are flagged as external context here as on the prior version of this page. What matters for the record is the sequence: Dan's later lines are him watching the aftermath, not the ascent. He caught the exact window between the all-time-high candles and the guidance blowup. The entry was good luck on timing as much as a good pick — and Dan knew it, which is why the September 26 message says "caught," not "chose."

## The afterlife: January–February 2021

FSLY stayed in the message stream long after the position closed, as a touchstone word and then as a lesson.

On January 27, 2021 at 20:44 EST, in the middle of the GME frenzy week — the week of the 100 AMC shares, the Robinhood peak near $25k, the DOGE bought on a tip — Dan sent Jerad a single word: *"Fastly."* The corpus gives no surrounding context for what the word meant: a touchstone, a half-typed thought, a prompt, a comparison point for the meme eruption. It is recorded here as unexplained, because the record does not explain it. What it does establish is that FSLY, the first trade, was still the reference frame against which the meme-stock eruption was being measured.

February 4 is the evening the lesson got named — and it arrived in a pair of messages that belong together. At 20:59 EST: *"So I was really choomed this morning and moved a bunch of stock that I probably shouldn't have just because I was too high."* Eighteen minutes later, at 21:17 EST: *"I learned a lot from that big fastly dip about the importance of momentum trading. I never get FOMO about stuff I've sold that has gains later, but you feel it hard when you miss a good opportunity to get out."* The thesis and the behavior in the same night: an admitted impaired trade, then the clearest statement of the era's central insight. This is the LOSE IT principle the market-era page dates to this period — impulsivity managed by joking about it, self-aware the entire time. The momentum lesson wasn't a conclusion reached in calm retrospect; it was typed eighteen minutes after confessing to moving stock while too high.

On February 17 at 00:47 EST: *"FSLY earnings tomorrow."* Still tracking, post-lesson — watching the aftermath, not the ascent, exactly as the prior version of this page had it.

And on February 23, the bloodbath that put FSLY's modest scale in perspective: *"I bought fucking calls on CCIV yesterday,"* then two minutes later, *"But my $25k just turned into 17k."* The FSLY position — ~$15k in, ~$4k profit, a $600 day — is the floor under the later, larger GME/AMC/CCIV swings. The first trade was the small one. Everything after was bigger, faster, and less lucky.

## The asymmetry, stated once

The Jerad page names the era's defining trade structure, and FSLY is its cleanest instance: Jerad supplies conviction and scale — $200k since April, the business model he says he understands, the multi-day advisory thread — and Dan supplies a smaller, hedged echo: 112 shares, in and out in weeks, profit skimmed before the collapse. The echo is not a lesser version of the conviction; it is a different instrument. Jerad held the thesis. Dan held the window. The window paid; the thesis, held 2–3 years as advised, would have paid and then some, then collapsed. Dan's later verdict — momentum over conviction — is the echo-holder's verdict, and it is honest about what it is: the lesson of the dip, not the lesson of the run.

## Assessment

The record supports one judgment, and it is Dan's own: momentum over conviction. The trade worked because the timing was lucky and the exit was early; the advice that came with the tip — "do not touch for 2-3 years" — would have converted the win into a loss. Dan named the lesson himself on 2021-02-04, eighteen minutes after admitting to moving stock while too high, which is the era in miniature: self-aware impulsivity producing a durable thesis. The asymmetric echo — Jerad's $200k conviction, Dan's $15k skim — became the repeating structure of the 2020–21 market era, and FSLY is where it first appears. No final P&L exists in the corpus; the market-era page's standing gap (no exit accounting, unknown whether any position survived into the 2021–23 low-income stretch) applies here as well.
