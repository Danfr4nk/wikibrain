# The Nemacolin Caddie Years (2016–2019)

Forty-three months carrying golf bags across two of Pennsylvania's most
expensive fairways. From the spring interview Dan walked into in borrowed
golf shoes (April 6, 2016) to the November 2019 end date on his resume, this
is the longest sustained commitment in the record before Au Za'atar — and
the last era of his life that was principally *physical*. Outdoor work,
weather, walking, a body used for something other than a desk or a screen.
Everything after it — the restaurant years, the 2021 second NYC move, the
obsession era of agent pipelines and stylometry — happened indoors. The
caddie years are the final chapter of the physical life, and they ended
without a funeral anyone attended, which is why the record shows golf dying
not with a decision but with a fade: zero post-2019 golf signal beyond
nostalgia, media consumption, and one self-deprecating story about polo
shirts.

This was also the era in which work last generated Dan's friendships in
bulk. A whole social layer — a manager, an operations hand, a social
organizer, a weed middleman, a genuine friend, a marquee NHL regular —
grew out of the caddie yard, and nothing after it produced a comparable
one. The [[wiki/work/au-zaatar|restaurant years]] produced the Triad; the
caddie years produced a yard. The difference in density is worth holding,
because it is the measure of what the job was beyond the paycheck.

## Arrival: the Uniontown return

By the time Dan applied, he had been back in Uniontown for years — the
first NYC era ([[wiki/timeline/periods/nyc-first-era-2010-2013|2010–2013]],
per the wiki's current periodization) already behind him, with a second NYC
stint in between (the [[wiki/work/duquesne-university|Uniontown-return era]]
frames the whole window). The long-ago cause of the returns traces back to
[[wiki/timeline/events/november-2005-rupture|November 2005]], the hinge that
sent the biography reeling back toward Uniontown whenever the city chapters
closed. He was 27 at the hire, turning 28 that November — the age at which
the rest of the Uniontown cohort had long since settled into adult routines,
and he had a borrowed pair of golf shoes and a resume that ran through
Ishlab, Creative License, and the SLOPPP/MOGZART production aliases.

The job title on the resume was **"Experience Associate – Golf"**, which
does what resort titles do: it puts a hospitality frame around the oldest
unskilled-skilled labor in golf. The real job was looping — carrying bags,
reading greens for guests who couldn't, managing pace of play across
corporate and charity outings, running equipment logistics, and learning
the two courses: **Mystic Rock** and **Shepherd's Rock**. He borrowed the
shoes for the April 6 interview; he got the job.

Five days into it, he wrote to Annie the message that is the era's founding
document:

> *"i really really dig it. Spending all day on the golf course is a blast
> and it's getting me back into playing in a big way. Also mystic rock is
> top notch and really hard"* (2016-05-11)

That last clause is doing two things at once. It is a new hire's enthusiasm,
and it is the reactivation of a lineage: his great-grandmother
[[wiki/people/fran-coldren|Fran]] had spent fifty years as western-PA golf
royalty on the UCC fairway — two documented holes-in-one, the UCC Women's
Golf Crown at 342 over 72 holes, a social circuit that included Arnold
Palmer himself. The grandson carrying bags at Mystic Rock was not
discovering golf. He was re-entering it from the bottom of its service
ladder, at a course Fran had golfed royalty-wise never needed to work. The
employee discount — *"We can play mystic rock for $15"* — made the lineage
usable: he could afford to play the game his family had played for
generations, for the price of a movie ticket.

## The body at work

What distinguished this job from everything before and after was the
physical ledger. A caddie loop at Nemacolin is not a shift on one's feet
behind a counter; it is miles of walking steep terrain in weather, carrying
a bag that weighs what a bag weighs, reading a course out loud to people
who pay to be there. The corpus carries the physical texture directly:

- **4:30 AM departures from Uniontown** — the May 21, 2018 Laurel Valley
  day trip left before dawn, with six named coworkers, single-bag that day,
  $100 cash for the second loop.
- **Freezing April openings** — April 1, 2018, *"the first day of golf at
  Nemacolin, which means the first day of caddie season,"* meant two loops
  in the cold before the call about Fran came.
- **The two-bag threshold** — carrying two bags simultaneously was the
  professional milestone; his first time was May 23, 2018 at Laurel Valley
  ($90 flat for the pair), paired with the program's head.
- **The 5.5-hour post-extraction loop** — Dan looped a full round the day
  after a tooth extraction because, per the unidentified
  urpaaa-thread's evidence, the yard culture punished sick days.

This is the continuity with [[wiki/places/seven-springs-childhood|the ski
identity]] the page needs to name plainly: the same body that spent
childhood winters on Seven Springs — the mountain his family treated as a
second home, the ski-identity years — now spent springs and summers walking
a mountain resort's golf courses in all weather. Different sport, same
physics: terrain, exposure, weather, legs. It is the one thread of his
life where the body was an instrument rather than a container, and it ran
continuously from the childhood hill to November 2019. After that, nothing.
The BFS counter years, the Au Za'atar years, the 2026 pipeline years — all
of them are indoor standing, indoor walking, indoor staring. The caddie
years are the last time the record shows him working under an open sky.

And the record shows him taking the physical work seriously in a way that
sits oddly beside his own self-assessments. In June 2016 he watched a full
broadcast of the **84 Lumber Classic** specifically *"to better understand
course strategy for my low handicap loops"* — tournament golf as
professional development, not entertainment. This is a 27-year-old
caddie doing homework. The embedded-objective synthesis
([[wiki/mind/synthesis/the-embedded-objective]]) reads the whole
43-month stretch through this lens: the longest tenure in the record was
held not by the assigned duties but by a private status ladder the employer
never set or measured.

## The ladder nobody posted

Caddying at a resort of Nemacolin's caliber runs a visible hierarchy, and
Dan's messages across 2017–18 track his movement up it with the precision
of someone logging ranks in a game. The rungs, in his own vocabulary:

**"The real loopers."** The off-property day trips to prestige courses
went to the established core, not the roster at large. His May 2018 message
captures his first call-up as a milestone in full: *"first time getting
called up with the real loopers to take a day trip and work at another
track."* On May 21, 2018 he made it real — Laurel Valley Golf Club,
departing Uniontown at 4:30 AM alongside Brad, Trevor Bevins, Brooke,
"cuzzy," Will, and Chad. Two caddies to a group, single-bag that day,
$100 cash for the second loop. And his assessment of the prestige track's
own loopers was characteristically dry: *"an impressive lack of golf
knowledge for how long I've done the job."* He had arrived and found the
residents wanting.

**The two-bag round.** Two days later, May 23, 2018, paired at Laurel
Valley with **Carl** — *"the head of the caddie program there and also the
head of the program in Scottsdale"* (TPC Scottsdale) — Dan carried two bags
for the first time. $90 flat for the pair. *"getting out with Carl was a
huge blessing (it could have been a disaster)."* He used the pairing the
way he used every interesting pairing: he asked directly about
transferring to Laurel Valley full-time, describing it as *"a much more
beneficial [career] situation and a natural progression from looping at
[Nemacolin's] mystic [rock course]."* The transfer never happened. The
ambition is the clearest documented evidence he saw caddying as a career
ladder rather than gig income — which is also why the Scottsdale thread
matters: the head of the TPC Scottsdale program, in person, hearing Dan
pitch himself for the full-time track. A Uniontown caddie angling toward
Arizona's flagship golf operation. It died there, on the fairway.

**Pikewood National.** Rated by the coworkers as superior to Laurel
Valley, Latrobe Country Club, and even Oakmont, Pikewood (Morgantown, WV)
was where Dan looped in a tournament setting in late June 2018, coordinated
directly by caddie master **Ryan Sensenig** for a player named Andy Decker
(June 29, 10:00 AM tee time, #1). He had asked to be kept in mind for
Pikewood and Potomac assignments weeks earlier. The tournament loop is the
third rung: resort guest play → off-property day trip → tournament
coordination by another club's caddie master.

None of this was posted anywhere. The employer measured report times and
loop counts; Dan measured himself against the unwritten ladder, and the
corpus is his scorecard. This is the configuration the profile work flags
as the only sustainable one for him — a peer-visible status ladder rather
than a managerial one — and 43 months of 4:30 AM departures are the
evidence it held.

## The yard: the last bulk friendship

The caddie yard produced the most documented social layer of any job in the
record. Each of these names carries a thread:

- **[[wiki/people/davey-fitzpatrick|Davey Fitzpatrick]]** — fellow caddie in
  2017, assistant caddie master in 2018. Davey is the yard's management
  layer in the record: the channel through which every documented report
  time and training assignment of Dan's final season arrived, and the
  coworker who picked Dan to train the new caddies. The operations log of
  the job.
- **Vaughn** — fellow caddie, coordinating rides up the mountain and
  check-day logistics, doubling as a small-time weed middleman between
  loops. The yard's paycheck-to-paycheck edge.
- **[[wiki/people/christo-coan|Christo Coan]]** — golf-course coworker and
  the yard's social organizer. Christo co-organized Dan's **October 31,
  2017 Halloween/birthday party** — open to *"every caddie, staging, and
  pro shop employee,"* thrown at Dan's house rather than the club. His
  thread also carries the passing reference to Dan's otherwise
  undocumented DUI, which is how the corpus's only mention of that event
  survives.
- **Trevor Bevins** — the "Trevor" of the May 21 Laurel Valley trip, the
  era's closest work friendship inside the yard itself, later departed for
  **Sand Valley**. The departure is the type case of how these
  relationships ended: the yard dissolved them by geography, not by
  argument.
- **Chad Johnson** — the "Chad" of the same trip, fellow caddie and social
  contact beyond the yard.
- **Mike Zollinger** and **Jimmy** — fellow caddies from the 2018 season.
- **Bill** — a caddying peer, documented as distinct from Bill Ulmer.
- **[[wiki/people/jack-rusko|Jack Rusko]]** — fellow caddy and the one
  genuine friendship of the cohort. His section below.
- **Joey Superick** — a brief golf-and-music friendship from June 2018,
  adjacent to the same scene.
- **Zach Clingan** — a previously undocumented member of the cohort whose
  entire thread runs on the yard's clock: getting cut, the offered
  two-bag round, the end-of-season bonfire invite, and the next April's
  opening-day ping.
- **urpaaa** — an unidentified contact whose thread runs entirely on yard
  time: report times, a $100 Venmo tip, and the post-tooth-extraction
  5.5-hour loop.
- **Steve Kezmarsky** — asked Dan to be gotten into the caddie yard in
  July 2017 and never submitted the resume. The documented near-member:
  the cohort Dan could have expanded and didn't.
- **Bruce Burish** — the friendship originates in the caddying era and
  goes dormant until 2018; Bruce's opening register is nostalgia for
  Nemacolin, which is what re-establishes the channel.

The pattern the cohort documents is the page's load-bearing social claim:
**caddying was the last era in which work generated Dan's friendships in
bulk.** Nothing after it produced a comparable layer — not BFS, not the
2026 freelance years. Au Za'atar produced its Triad, but the Triad was a
unit; the yard was a *world*: managers, trainers, drivers, organizers,
middlemen, genuine friends, and marquee guests, all within one job's orbit.

### Jack Rusko

The thread is 202 messages, and the record kept it because the friendship
was real rather than circumstantial. Jack was a fellow caddie across
2017–18, and the corpus shows the two of them operating as a unit inside
the yard: *"say jack rusko and i went up to mystic rock to get our
paperwork"* (2018-03-09) — the administrative errand done together, the
small tell of a genuine peer.

The signature material is comic. Jack was the yard's chaos agent in Dan's
telling, and Dan turned him into a running character across two years of
messages:

- *"i don't start worrying until we lose jack rusko. i don't know that the
  resort will survive"* (2017-10-01) — deadpan catastrophe framing.
- *"You might just get presented with the prestigious jack Rusko 'rookie of
  the year' award hint hint"* (2017-10-26) — the invented ceremony.
- *"i had jack rusko piss for me lol"* (2018-03-19), followed by: *"how
  much do you think a glass of jack rusko piss is worth at auction?"* —
  the March 2018 drug-test scare, defused into a joke. He followed up days
  later: *"i didn't know i wasn't being drug tested so i spent the whole
  time maintaining the temperature of my bottle of jack rusko pee in my
  armpit."* (2018-03-27)
- *"Rusko said he's gonna hit tomorrow. That will be a real treat for
  everyone"* (2018-05-14), and twenty-five minutes later, flat: *"The world
  needs a few jack rusko's."*
- *"Well if you catch a jack Rusko 9 hole combo meal give me a shout"*
  (2018-06-17) — Jack as a unit of weather: *"We made it 9 holes today and
  I did the ol' jack Rusko 'oh wow the forecast looks BADDDDDDD'"*
  (2018-06-23).
- *"That's the jack Rusko DREAM"* (2018-06-28); *"it's the jack rusko
  answer"* (2018-04-20); *"jack rusko bitmoji"* (2018-05-01).

The received side is thin but warm — *"Rusko style"* (2018-06-09),
*"No you're not. You're mini Jack Rusko"* (2019-03-06) — and the 2019
tail of the thread shows the friendship surviving the job's end: Annie's
messages in August–September 2019 relay that Jack kept messaging her on
Facebook about a drone video he'd sent Dan (*"will you please let him know
you got it so he'll stop messaging me lol"*), and about *"his cars and
1957 wooden boat."* The caddie-yard peer, a year after the last loop,
still pinging through any channel that worked.

And then there is the **two-month laptop-loan saga** — the friendship's
most-documented episode by duration, and the record's thinnest by content.
What the corpus preserves is the shape, not the substance: a laptop lent,
two months of negotiation over its return, the low-grade friction of a
friend holding something of yours that you need back. The particulars are
not on the record; the existence of the saga, and the friendship's
survival of it, are.

Which is why the signal moment matters more than the saga. After
[[wiki/people/fran-coldren|Fran]] died on April 4, 2018 — in the middle of
the caddie years, five days before the season's real business — Jack sent
**unprompted condolences**. The corpus records a cluster of April 5
condolence texts from various numbers; the memory record identifies Jack's
as the one that marked him out, and the yard connection as the reason it
landed: this was a coworker, not family, not an Annie-adjacent obligation,
choosing to reach out. In a relationship documented mostly through jokes
about piss and weather forecasts, the unprompted condolence is what the
record kept, and what the wiki keeps. It is the single strongest evidence
that Jack Rusko was a genuine friend and not just a funny coworker —
nobody sends condolences to a bit.

## The friction: Brad Hubeaut

Every yard has its manager, and this one's was **[[wiki/people/brad-hubeaut|Brad Hubeaut]]** —
caddy manager across the 2017–18 seasons, the dispatcher who assigned
report times, ran hiring paperwork, and managed the roster across
Nemacolin's courses (Mystic, Shepherd's, Laurel Valley, occasional trips
to Oakmont). The 163-message thread (October 2017 – October 2018) is
dominated by routine scheduling — daily "X:00 report" texts, availability
checks (*"Hey buddy can you work today?"*), carpool logistics for early
loops at Oakmont (*"leave from Uniontown super early"*), Social Security
card reminders for new paperwork. Brad's tone is casual and collegial
(*"buddy,"* *"gents,"* *"boys"*) — a shift-lead managing a crew of young,
informally organized workers, and a man Dan put down as a job reference
in April 2017 (*"Put Brad hubeaut as a reference"*).

The friction episode came in **April 2018**, on top of the Fran vigil's
first two weeks — which is either the explanation or the excuse,
depending on whose reading you take. On April 20, Brad relayed a complaint
from another staffer (Tyler) that Dan hadn't cleaned his carts, and
separately told another recipient: *"Dan Frank booked it and didn't clean
his carts at all. Just left them at the bottom of the ramp."* Five days
later, after Dan apparently declined a shift, Brad snapped:

> *"Wow man so all that texting about helping out was bullshit... I ask
> you do the same. Let's keep it strictly professional,"*

and two days after that listed the specific violations — turning down work
four times, turning down a "hitting" assignment, the uncleaned carts —
before telling Dan to *"come talk to me"* once conditions improved.

The relationship cooled noticeably but did not end: report-time texts and
shift offers resumed within days and continued through the 2018 season.
Brad closed out the corpus thread in October 2018 with a friendly aside
about Dan's "drone gig." The friction, in the end, was a flare rather than
a severance — which is the difference between a yard manager and a boss
in the corporate sense. There was no HR, no write-up, no file. There was
Brad being pissed, Dan presumably being Dan, and the season continuing.

Two more Brad datapoints, for the complete picture. First, the June 2017
remark, preserved in the story-time pilot session (Dan's own testimony):
Annie told Dan on June 24, 2017 that *"Brad said we could write off the
procedure as an occupational expense lololol. He was there a few weeks
ago"* — Brad, her caddie-manager colleague, having been at the same
abortion provider recently with someone else. It is a coarse joke between
coworkers in a coarse-joke workplace, and it tells you more about the
yard's register than about anyone's character. Second, the golf-page's
record of a **$150 straight-stroke-play bet** between Dan and Brad at
Pikewood that ended with Brad drunkenly snapping a club and standing Dan
up. The manager was a playing peer too — the line between boss and
coworker ran through the yard the way it runs through every good one.

And the yard had its institutional churn on top of the personal friction.
A 2019 message records a caddie manager fired mid-tenure for a
no-call-no-show — *"apparently... not getting along with narcotics of some
sort"* — and the VIP loops the staff refused to caddie for (*"we're not
taking caddies"*). The job's texture, across the whole stretch: a resort
that paid caddies to be charming and knowledgeable while the caddies ran a
carpool-and-report-time choreography around the edges of it.

## Golf as obsession, golf as death

The caddying job did not just give Dan an income; it **reignited his own
playing**, and for the 2016–19 stretch [[wiki/interests/golf|the sport]] was
simultaneously income,
social world, and game — the cleanest instance in the record of the
[[wiki/self/two-year-synthesis|obsession-lifecycle doctrine]] (complete
immersion, then total death; zero post-2019 golf signal beyond nostalgia
and media consumption).

The playing record is a competent amateur's, self-scored without illusion.
Best reported round: *"That was a fluke today tho. Idk how I played as
well as I did...and I still probably barely broke 90"* (2018-05-09). A
more ordinary day, June 2017: *"Got a par 4 and then par 3 to finish."*
The closest brush with real skill is a near-miss rather than a triumph:
on **November 5, 2017** — his 29th birthday — playing as *"the last player
of the season"* on Mystic Rock, he came *"2 feet from an ace on 17 which is
the last hole today."* Two feet from a hole-in-one, on his birthday, on
the season's last day. The record doesn't say whether he holed the putt.
The record's silence on that detail is its own kind of honesty.

The marquee playing partner of the era was retired NHL forward
**[[wiki/people/christian-hanson|Christian Hanson]]**, a Nemacolin regular
Dan both caddied for and played rounds with through 2017–18 — the kind of
access that makes the yard's status ladder legible from the outside. And
Dan spent real money and real effort trying to pull Annie into the game
with him: *"I don't know let's take her Togo play nine holes"* (May 2018),
more bluntly *"I spend all this fucking money to try to get you excit[ed]
about actually playing golf"* (June 2018), and direct invitations (*"How
about playing golf with Annie and I on the new course tonight,"* Aug
2018). The record suggests it never fully took.

The end of golf-as-life is undated and unceremonious, which is the point
the two-year-synthesis doctrine makes about obsessions: they die by fade,
not by decision. No message records a last round, a sold set of clubs, a
conscious quitting. The resume ends the job in November 2019. A 2020
message shows Dan trying, without success, to get someone else to *"come
walk the golf course with me."* By 2025 the only golf references are a
"GOLF themed YouTube channel" he watches and a self-deprecating story
about avoiding polo shirts since being told he *"dressed like a golfer's
little brother."* The interest persisted as media consumption and
self-image, the way a religion persists in someone who stopped attending.
The December 2025 callback — Annie's *"like when we used to, when you
ACTUALLY liked me"* — weaponizes walking golf courses together as the
relationship's named good-era memory, the loop that opened on the UCC
fairway in the rain in 2015 closing as its eulogy.

## The Fran intersection

The caddie years and the [[wiki/people/fran-coldren-caregiver-years|caregiver years]]
overlap, and the overlap is where the era's human weight sits. The paid
caregiving arrangement (≈October 2017 – April 2018) ran concurrently with
the caddie seasons — $15 an hour to keep Fran out of a nursing home, and
a caddie's wage to cover everything else. Two jobs, one winter, both
documented in the same message threads, sometimes the same day.

The intersection's sharpest point is dated precisely. **April 1, 2018** —
*"the first day of golf at Nemacolin, which means the first day of caddie
season"* — Dan worked two loops in the freezing cold, and then came the
call: Fran needed to go to the hospital. The opening beat of the vigil
documented in full at [[wiki/timeline/events/fran-death-vigil|the death
vigil]] arrived as a caddie came off his second loop of the day. The
season opened and the four-week terminal sequence opened on the same
afternoon. He kept working through it — the Brad friction of April 20–27
is a man grieving through shifts he was declining — and the yard kept
scheduling him, because seasons don't pause.

Fran's house on the UCC fairway served as the yard's staging point across
the 2018 season in a way the record makes almost casual: a coworker put up
there to save a hotel bill in April, a golf meet-up called from it in May,
a September pharmaceutical handoff — running five months past her death.
[[wiki/places/117-belmont-circle]] was the family house on the fairway and
also, functionally, a caddie's crash pad and meeting point. The two worlds
the wiki keeps separate — the matriarch's estate and the yard's logistics —
shared an address all year.

And the death itself arrived inside the working life, not outside it. The
April 5 condolence texts came to a man in the middle of a season. Jack
Rusko's unprompted one is the one the record kept. The vigil's closing
weeks — surgery, the nursing home, the April 1 pediatric-wing admission,
hospice, the April 4 death — all sit inside the caddie-years' date range,
which is why this period article carries the Fran connection rather than
ceding it entirely to her page. The biggest event of his life happened
while he was employed as a caddie, and the job absorbed it the way jobs
absorb everything: shifts declined, carts uncleaned, a manager's snapped
patience, and then the season continuing.

## The end, and the sixteen-month gap

The resume dates the Nemacolin job to **November 2019**. He was 31. The
corpus does not record the leaving — no farewell message, no stated
reason, no documented final loop. The exact end circumstances (voluntary
departure vs. displaced by the job's November end) are undocumented, and
no message record narrows when the caddying work actually stopped versus
when Au Za'atar began (March 2021). Between them sits a roughly
**sixteen-month unaccounted gap** — the run-up to the second NYC move,
during which the record is thin on employment and thick on other things.

What the tail of the era does document is institutional churn rather than
personal closure: the 2019 firing of a caddie manager for a no-call-no-show

The physical-life reading of the ending is the one that survives the

## Why the job matters

At 43 months, this is the longest assigned commitment in the record before
Au Za'atar, and the two together falsified the claim that Dan never
absorbs sustained cost for a goal somebody else set. What the record shows
him tracking across those years is not the assigned duties — guest
assistance, pace-of-play, equipment logistics — but the private
progression the employer never set or measured: the call-up to the real
loopers, the 4:30 AM Laurel Valley departure, the first two-bag round, the
Scottsdale transfer raised directly with Carl. That ladder is the payload.

Three things about the era deserve to be stated flatly, because they are
the reasons the period article exists rather than the event stub:

**First, it was the last physical life.** Outdoor work, weather, walking,
a body with a job to do that wasn't typing. The skiing identity's
descendant and its final instance. Everything the wiki documents after
November 2019 happens under a roof.

**Second, it was the last bulk friendship.** The yard produced a manager,
an operations hand, a social organizer, a middleman, a genuine friend,
marquee guests, and a whole weather of casual peers — in one job's orbit.
No later era matches the density. The social world contracted after this:
the Triad, then the pipelines.

**Third, it was the cleanest obsession arc.** Golf entered as lineage
(Fran's fifty years), became profession (43 months), became identity
(income, social world, and game at once), and died by fade — no decision,
no funeral, just a 2025 polo-shirt joke where a life used to be. The
[[wiki/self/two-year-synthesis|two-year-synthesis]] doctrine could have
been written from this era alone.

The era's epitaph is already in the record, written five days into the
job, before any of it had happened: *"Spending all day on the golf course
is a blast."* It stayed true for 43 months. Then it stopped being true,
and nothing in the corpus says why — which is the most honest thing the
record has to say about how obsessions actually end.
