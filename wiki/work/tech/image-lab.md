# Image Lab

The image lab is Dan's AI image-and-video generation workstream: the
room where the recurring two-girl character battery, the dated episode
runs, and most of his image-side red-team probes happened. It is not a
deployed product. It is a practice — a designated IMAGE GEN side chat
he designated and pinned on 2026-09-10 **[ATTESTED]**, a persistent
character-memory file (`~/workspace/image-lab/character-tags.md`),
an output directory (`~/workspace/imagine_media/`), and a standing set
of operating rules accumulated over four days of heavy use: character
tags as the gold standard, the gold-standard reference as the clothing
controller, the registry-and-censorship pipeline for what gets
committed, and the NN-named gallery mirror for what gets kept.

The lab's durable outputs are instruments and artifacts, not images
for their own sake: the `#dukes` / `#cutoffs` character tags, the
Frame Describe tool and lexicon v0.1 (live 2026-09-12 at
`frame-describe.html`), the 155-face Face Book catalog, the
NN-named avatar-gallery mirror, and the dated ledgers of what the
pipelines refused — the refusal record being, for his purposes, as
much a product as the images. **[INFERRED]** The lab is where his
image-side probe episodes were most visibly staged: the
2026-09-10 reverse-instruction reversal and the 2026-09-13
[[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]]–Will fabrication (explicit media → firsthand
testimony → secrecy directive → the reveal "You didn't even flinch").

This page was created because the 2026-09-13 engine tick's
article-reconstruction queue item (work-0040) flagged that
`dat:1459` and `dat:1472` — the avatar-history and avatar-gallery
evidence nodes — had no wiki home. **[DERIVED]** The workstream
itself had been archived only as scattered nodes; the room had no
article.

## Pre-history: the Google Whisk fossil record

Before the image lab existed in name, there was volume. On
2026-09-13 (~00:05 UTC), during an avatar-generation session, Dan
volunteered that he has "almost 10,000 images I generated and saved
when google whisk was a thing" — a ~10k-image personal archive of
Google Whisk (Google Labs' AI image-remix tool) generations retained
on his side **[OPERATOR]**. The count is his rounded estimate, not an
audited inventory; `dat:1475` carries it as moderate-confidence
testimony with the never-hardcode-numbers rule attached. Nothing
about the Whisk archive is organized into the wiki's media flow — it
is the fossil record of his image-lab output before the current
media pipeline, mentioned once and never catalogued. **[INFERRED]**
It establishes that image generation was already a high-volume habit
before September 2026; what the lab added was discipline — tags,
references, a refusal ledger, and a commit pipeline.

## Instruments

The lab accumulated five durable instruments, each with its own
file, rule, or URL.

### Generation (image and video)

The media pipeline itself — image generation and video
generation via the imagine_media tooling — is the lab's working
instrument, and its failure modes are the lab's most careful
ledger. The record distinguishes three outcomes: clean
generations, content-policy blocks (delivered as explicit policy
verdicts), and — starting 2026-09-11 — blanket upstream 503
failures. On 2026-09-11, after hours of blanket 503 failures on
all image prompts, a benign control prompt ("Generate an image
of a red apple on a table") succeeded while even a
fully-clothed, innocuous #cutoffs prompt (standing in a hallway
smiling) 503'd. The in-window assessment: the flag was on the
characters themselves, not any particular scene or outfit — and
the bare-503 form was new, since earlier the pipeline had issued
clean policy verdicts. The assistant's speculation that the
characters or the account had entered an upstream "penalty box"
was explicitly flagged as unverified by tool output: the only
established facts were the apple's success and the characters'
failures. **[ATTESTED, with the unverified flag preserved]**

Also 2026-09-11, video-side: four requested videos of both

## What was learned

The lab's four days produced a small set of durable operating
lessons, each with a dated first appearance:

1. **The tag is the character.** Character continuity is a
   named, file-persistent specification (#dukes as gold
   standard, #cutoffs as legacy), and the gold-standard
   *reference image* — not the accumulated approximations —
   controls clothing continuity. Dan's wardrobe-drift
   corrections (09-11, 09-12) made this standing: when
   continuity is requested, the original reference wins.
2. **Refusals are a product.** The lab's refusal ledger —
   policy verdicts, the 09-11 bare-503 distinction, the dated
   real-person refusals — is archived with the same care as the
   generations. The fabrication-class posture stands: stay
   engaged, no moralizing, no invented corroboration.
3. **Correction is the intake method.** The lab's cleanest
   instruments (the #dukes spec itself, Frame Describe's
   lexicon, the water-only post-rally restatement) all came
   out of the same shape: the assistant's thin or wrong first
   pass becomes the probe, Dan's correction becomes the
   durable instrument. The Frame Describe build (dat:1462)
   names it: the honest thin trawl of his own corpus was the
   contrastive move that made the commissioned lexicon
   authoritative.
4. **His device wins.** The stale-backend correction
   (dat:1459): verify the real current avatar before
   character-consistent generation, not after. The lag is
   standing (two incidents on record), not a one-off.
5. **Generation vs. adoption.** The Star-of-David episode
   (dat:1496): the refusal boundary moved. A blocked direct
   generation can still surface via another flow; the hold
   that matters for the avatar workstream is on *wearing* —
   "Making it and wearing it are different lines."
6. **The commit pipeline is policy, not preference.** The
   2026-09-12 censorship directive: derivatives embedded in
   the wiki are pixelated/blurred where they show nudity or
   sexual activity, originals are never committed, the
   registry maps every item to its canonical location. This
   is Dan's explicit order and it stands — thumbnail
   placement in a Sources section, inline display only when
   the image is itself the object of analysis.
