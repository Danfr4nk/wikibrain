# Claim Validity

*Generated by `bin/wiki-claims`. A hand-edit fails the gate; rerun the
tool. This page is **evidence, not a claim** — it records what the wiki
asserted and for how long, and states no finding of its own. A finding
drawn from it reaches an ordinary page through the normal operations.*

Every other date in this wiki is about a *page*. `date_created` and
`date_modified` say when a file moved; `date_range_start` and
`date_range_end` say what span of evidence it covers. None of them says
**when an assertion stopped being true** — so nothing could notice when
one did, and a conclusion built on it kept reading as current.

## Three ways a claim stops being usable

| | What it means | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Retracted** | It was never true | `RETRACTED.md`, gated by `bin/wiki-lint` |
| **Corrected** | It was wrong when written | Correction blockquotes, STYLE_GUIDE rule 9 |
| **Expired** | It was true, and then it stopped | Here |

The third is not a weaker version of the first two. *Dan lives in
Morgantown* was **correct** in 2019. Retracting it says the wiki got it
wrong; leaving it says he lives there now. Both are false.

## What this ledger cannot see

**It is sparse and opt-in by design.** The unit is a claim somebody
chose to put a clock on, not every sentence in the wiki. A claim absent
from this page is not thereby timeless — it is unexamined. Reading the
counts here as coverage of the corpus would be a category error.

**`lapsed` is the honest band and the useless-feeling one.** It is also
the commonest true answer, because this corpus is built from archives
that each stop on a date. Wanting a cleaner number is exactly the
pressure that would produce a false one.

**The standing directive filters the record.** Claims naming the person
under the moratorium in `CLAUDE.md` are refused outright, so this is a
record of a filtered corpus and any rate drawn from it inherits that.

**Nothing here is a finding.** The ledger records windows. What a window
*means* is synthesis, and synthesis happens on an ordinary page.
