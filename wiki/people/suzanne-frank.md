# Suz (Suzanne Frank)

Dan's mother, and the parent who stayed. The corpus holds more of her than of
anyone except [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] — a correspondence that
opens in January 2010 with her asking her son *"How are you?"* and then, three hours
later, *"Are you alive?"* — and that is still running sixteen years later, in
September 2026, as a 2 a.m. exchange about reconnecting a downspout pipe
before the rain. Between those two messages sits the whole relationship: a
woman who decided, against the example of her own parents, that she would
never lose contact with her children, and who then kept that decision daily —
through a death vigil she administered alone, through her son's decade with
Annie, through a bankruptcy and the sale of his childhood home, and into a
shared Uniontown house where the contact is now literal: a nightly checklist
of fridge, garbage, windows and laundry, sent by text from inside the same
walls.

She is a licensed Pennsylvania realtor in Fayette County, the daughter of
[[wiki/people/diane-moore|Diane Moore]] and the granddaughter of
[[wiki/people/fran-coldren|Fran Coldren]] — the two women who between them
define her: Diane by rejection, Fran by unconditional love, in her own
account of both. She was married to [[wiki/people/rick-frank|Rick Frank]]
for twenty years, and the contrast between the two parents is the corpus's
standing control case: he withdrew to roughly two thousand messages across
the same years; she accumulated more than thirty thousand and writes more
of it than Dan does. Availability is not a mood in this record. It is a workload, and
she carries most of it.

Money passed through this relationship constantly, and for years this page
treated the flow as the relationship's definition. It is documented below,
in its place, because the facts matter — she really was poor, the bankruptcy
really happened, the transfers really were disputed. But the transfers were
the weather of the relationship, not its climate: they rose and fell with
emergencies, while the thing that never varied, in any year of the record,
was that she was there, in the thread, that day. What follows is organised
by that constancy.

## The principle: never lose contact

The fullest statement she makes anywhere in the corpus is a single message
sent at 19:51 on 8 July 2019, in the middle of an argument, which is where
her principles tend to be stated:

> Part of this, you already know, but I'd like you to remember that I have
> loved you deeply since the day you were born. I am pretty sure you know
> that. I have always felt that it was important to make sure that no matter
> what, I would never lose contact with either of my children the way it
> happened with your Dad and me and our parents. It's wrong and hurtful and
> I really wish i had parents who cared about me. You will always have
> parents who care about you.
>
> However, that love and concern should be mutual. It is not…

The message goes on into grievance — it is also a complaint, and the two
registers sharing one paragraph is characteristic — but the principle is
stated as a principle: contact is not conditional on the relationship going
well. It is the refusal of the thing that was done to her. The record of
what was done to her is in the next section; the record of the keeping is
the rest of the page. She writes more than Dan in every year of the thread
but its first fourteen months. The thread's single busiest day — 224
messages, 27 June 2024 — is not a crisis but the first Biden–Trump debate,
watched in separate rooms and narrated to each other in real time. And when
the thread does break, in July 2026, the break is legible precisely because
the baseline is daily: fourteen messages in a month that should have held
three hundred (see §2025–2026).

Read against the July 2019 letter, the posture stops looking like a trait
and starts looking like a verdict: the total availability is the sentence
she passed on her own upbringing. [[wiki/people/diane-moore|Diane's page]]
carries the same edge from the other side — her rejection is the documented
cause of her daughter's organising principle.

## Fran, and what she says about being the one who stayed

Both mother and son skip a generation in naming who raised them. Dan names
[[wiki/people/fran-coldren|Fran Coldren]] his biggest life influence. In
May 2018, six weeks after Fran's death, Suz wrote this to a family member
who had not come to the house — the message survives in her Messenger
thread with Dan, which is the only reason the corpus has it:

> I have barely any support group to help me or to talk to. As a result, I
> really haven't had time to mourn her loss because I immediately had to
> start the process of getting rid of things and cleaning and preparing
> the house to be sold. I had to plan her memorial service by myself and
> basically handled everything alone. One thing I can say is that anytime
> I had a decision to make, I thought, what would Gram do? That made it
> easy because I probably knew her better than anyone and I usually knew
> what she wanted. **She was the one and only guiding force in my life.
> The one person who loved me unconditionally** and I will live the rest
> of my life honoring her by doing the things I know she would want me
> to do.

That is the same generation-skipping attachment Dan describes, in the same
maternal line, one rung up — and it makes the vigil's division of labour
legible as something other than an accident. The person who did the
administrative half of Fran's death alone was the person for whom Fran was
the only unconditional relationship on record. Her dog
[[wiki/people/lucy|Lucy]] was euthanised within days of Fran's death, by
deliberate scheduling — *"because we wanted to get all the awful-ness out
of the way at once"* — the corpus's one documented case of batching grief
rather than sequencing it, and it is hers.

### The keno morning

The sharpest documented instance of the gap between the family's *intended*
caregiver and its *available* one is the first of Fran's major falls (~late
2017 — the filmed spill, not the final March 2018 fall). Dan found Fran on
the floor at 8:00 AM. Suz arrived at 10:00, having played video keno
through the night; the two of them tried to lift Fran, she screamed, and
Dan's account has Suz declining to call an ambulance — *"nope. i'll be back
in a little bit"* — before returning at 4:00 PM to lift her physically.

The account is Dan's, recorded in `DANSYNTH.txt` and in two 2026 captures;
no independent record of the morning exists, and the two-hour interval is
the only hard datum in it. What the corpus supplies as counterweight, in
the same period and also in Dan's words, is her conduct at the end of the
vigil: on 2 April 2018 she visited the pediatric wing — where Fran had
been given a private room on the strength of her and Ira's donations to
the hospital — and *"really held herself together and made it very light
spirited and happy."* Both are hers, and the wiki states the interval
rather than the verdict, because the person who could supply the other
half of it has never been asked.

## The witness and the broker

Staying, in her case, was never passive. Across the decade she is the
family member who intervenes in her son's relationships, keeps their
secrets, and — once — names their violence in writing while it is still
deniable. Three episodes carry the role.

**November–December 2015: brokering the switch.** The
[[wiki/timeline/annie-record|two-sided December 2015 hand-read]] shows her
taking an active part in the transition from
[[wiki/people/alexis-armel|Alexis]] to [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]],
on a compressed timeline. On 29 November 2015 she telephones Dan to say he
*should* date Annie, and the reason she gives is that the name fits the
family — *"I.e. Anne Dan Suzanne Fran Diane Van"* (13:48–13:49). Dan
confirms in the same window that *"She srsly offered me a car last
night."* At 05:02 the following morning:

> **"Suz just came to check on me, brought me a line, and told me she'd
> get me a car this week if I get Alexis out."**

Read at face value, that is a mother arriving before dawn with cocaine and
a vehicle conditional on completing an eviction. What the record does not
contain is her account of what she thought she was doing, and the single
line is the whole of the evidence for the conditional. The rest of the
month is warmer and equally documented. She calls Annie his girlfriend for
two weeks before he uses the word himself — Annie's reaction, *"SHE CALLED
ME YOUR GIRLFRIEND / IM IN LOVE IM IN LOVE"* (16 December, 16:46), is the
strongest response to a family-approval signal anywhere in the first
month. She pays him in cash for cleaning cars at her office (*"she gave me
two 50's"*). She builds Annie a purple hair chair as a Christmas gift,
asks Annie to cut her hair, and on 3 January 2016, over a thank-you note:
*"don't fuck this up! I love her."* It is why
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/bond-switch-2015|bond-switch-2015]] records the
switch as brokered rather than private. From `DANSYNTH.txt`, prompted by
two photographs Dan supplied: **Suz dated Annie Ulmer's father in high
school** (late 1970s / early 1980s). Dan's decade with Annie therefore
repeats a pairing his mother and Annie's father had already run, one lot
away from where both families' grandparents lived side by side for fifty
years ([[wiki/places/117-belmont-circle]]). The source is AI-secondary in
form but the claim originates with Dan and the photographs; the wiki
records it as context rather than cause.

**June–July 2017: the abortion.** For two weeks in the summer of 2017,
Suz was the only steady adult in the abortion story — and her archive is
the only place the Dan–Suz half of it survives. Verbatim probes from her
thread return zero hits in Dan's own iMessage export; his export is
partial (or another device) for this window, and the whole conversation
exists only here. Full account at
[[wiki/people/annie-ulmer#junejuly-2017-the-abortion|June–July 2017: the
abortion]]. June 24, evening — Dan tells her the news: *"Didn't mean to
just drop that on you so blindly. I just didn't know when or if she would
need someone to talk to, and I wanted you to be able to process it a
little before she reaches out."* Her answer to his dark jokes: *"That's
enough! This will come back to haunt you if you don't deal with it."* And
the stakes speech: *"If she changes her mind and things don't work out
between you, you will have an emotional, financial and physical
responsibility to her and the child for the rest of your life."* June 27,
appointment day — she gave him the one piece of hard information anyone

## The realtor

She has been a licensed Pennsylvania real estate agent for the whole span
of the corpus, and the work is the single most frequent subject in the
thread after the household itself: showings, listings, lockboxes,
multi-list settings, closings that do and do not happen. It is also the
closest thing she has to a public identity, and the record treats it as
craft as much as income: continuing-education classes squeezed in at
night, a running interest in writing about it — notes toward *"something
about my real estate experience to publish or submit to a magazine"*
(March 2019), an enquiry about a non-fiction writing class (October
2019), and, of one particularly difficult counterparty, *"This is
definitely one for my book!"* (May 2024). Nothing in the corpus indicates
the book was started.

She moved from Coldwell Banker to **Park Place Realty Group** in
**October 2019** — *"Park Place Realty. It's a boutique agency that my
buddy Barry Crumrine started. He left Remax"* (28 September 2019);
*"Park place realty… here I come!"* (2 October 2019); by 13 November
2019 Coldwell Banker had cut her access. The strongest documented month
of her career, **August 2020** — five closings against four in the
preceding eight months, in the middle of the pandemic housing surge —
came ten months after the move; Dan attributed the shift to the agency
change, she attributed it to bought leads that were *"costing a lot."*
Both readings survive. In August 2024 she named a different exit: she had
done student teaching decades earlier, and on 24 August 2024 she wrote:
**"I do everything backwards. It looks like, at age 62, I'm going to
start teaching. wtf?"** The corpus does not record what came of it, and
she was still taking listing photographs in June 2026.

### The ankle, February 2024

The injury that reorganised her working life is documented in real time,
in her own thread. On **17 February 2024** at 11:58: *"On way to hospital
in ambulance. I either broke or sprained my ankle."* She had been showing
a property in Gibbon Glade in eight inches of snow: *"I slipped on a rock
that was under the snow and either sprained or broke my ankle. Fire dept
had to come get me. Took me out in a stokes basket."* The verdict, that
evening: *"I broke both the inside and outside ankle bones"* — surgery
the next morning, rods and pins, six to eight weeks non-weight-bearing.
A realtor who cannot walk a property cannot work, and she was still
citing the ankle as a practical limit a year later (*"Driving there is
going to be hard because of my ankle"*, February 2025). Eight months
after the fall she filed Chapter 13. Dan, on 5 October 2026, named the
broken ankle the filing's main cause. The bankruptcy section below is
written on that basis: the filing's paperwork is tax debt, but its
precipitating event is a body failing at work.

## Houses she held for other people

Property, in her record, is how care was made physical — she held houses
the way other people hold opinions, and twice she held one for Dan at
ruinous cost to herself.

**The Winter Park condo, 2008–2010.** When Dan left for
[[wiki/timeline/periods/full-sail-2008-2010|Full Sail]] in September
2008, Suz bought a condo near campus rather than renting one — the
earliest documented instance of her using real property to support him.
Per the operator capture of 10 August 2026, the tenant she later let it
to never paid a month's rent and was arrested a few months into the lease
for running a human-trafficking scheme; the address was supplied the same
day: **2924 Antique Oaks Circle, Winter Park, FL**, directly across State
Road 436 from Full Sail. A search anchored to the confirmed address found
no matching arrest, court record or news story; the trafficking arrest
remains uncorroborated. The loss does not. On 14 November 2024, listing
her losses unprompted, she wrote: **"You have no idea how much money I
lost between antique oaks and Virginia Ave. I try not to think about
it"** — and, minutes later, her own estimate of the combined figure:
*"It's easily over a half million."* The two named property losses —
Antique Oaks and [[wiki/places/155-virginia-ave|155 Virginia Ave]], the
latter at roughly $135,000 by her tax accounting — are the two largest
financial events of her adult life, and both of them are houses she
bought or held in his orbit.

**337 Saratoga and the move to 463** are covered where they belong — in
the money section's bankruptcy account, and in §2025–2026 — because what
the record shows about them is less a transaction than a relocation of
the shared household: the house Dan grew up in was sold out from under
both of them, and the staying simply changed address.

## Money, in its place

Everything in this section is documented and none of it is in dispute as
fact. It is one section of eleven because that is its proportion in the
relationship: money was the medium emergencies travelled in, and this
relationship had a great many emergencies.

**Capacity.** Her own accounting, given to a homeowners' association in
June 2020: *"My property taxes are $10,000/yr. I have only earned between
$11,000–$14,000/yr in the last decade. I understand that's not their
problem."* Almost every other financial detail in the thread is
consistent with that figure: $20 needed for gas (June 2017), a car she
feared would be repossessed (February 2018), $642 needed or the gas would
be shut off (April 2018), two years' work on one client returning $562.50
in commission — *"I'm a fool"* (April 2019). The clearest single datapoint
is the lockbox message of 8 January 2020: a licensed agent with a showing
in two hours, unable to open the door because the electronic key needs
$14.37 on an account holding $7 — *"Would it be possible to PayPal me $8?
… I have $7 in there now."* The sources she names most often for keeping
the phone, lights and cable on are not commissions: eBay, the flea market
at Braddock's, and Poshmark.

**The transfers.** Money moved both directions for a decade and was
disputed the whole time. The clearest artifact is her message of 3
October 2018, which is not prose but an account statement — *"$14,000+ /
Sum of transactions on app… If you can give me $1450, that erases the
above and adds $1000 to what I borrowed from you."* The $14,000 moved
**from Dan to Suz** in 2018, two years before the estate distributed; by
June 2019 it had become the thing they fought about (*"I LENT YOU 14,000
… which i'm supposed to be paid back when the inheritance comes right"*,
20 June), and on 3 July 2019 — one of the eight highest-volume days in
the thread — both directions became legal threats. Her framing that day
is the one she returned to for the rest of the decade: *"$14,000 is a
drop in the bucket compared to what you have cost me since 2004."* Dan
says he recovered $4,000 of it; no source records any further repayment,
and neither claim was ever adjudicated or withdrawn. The honest summary
is the one the old version of this page eventually reached: irregular
transfers of $20 to $3,000 running both ways, entered as debts by both
parties, never reconciled — a standing feature of a household in which
neither side ever had enough, rather than evidence about what the
household was.

**The bankruptcy, and why 337 Saratoga was sold.** On 30 March 2026 Dan
pasted a case audit into the thread. The document is model-generated and
must be read as **AI-secondary**, with the caveat that its inputs were
evidently things she had told him — but it is specific in a way that is
checkable from outside the corpus, and no other source in `raw/`
contradicts it: **Case #24-22285-GLT**, Chapter 13 voluntary petition,
**filed October 2024**; scheduled liabilities **~$157,000**; primary
creditors **IRS priority claims for tax years 2018–2021** plus PA
Department of Revenue arrears; plan payments of **$300/month** depending
on liquidating 337; 337 Saratoga **owned free and clear** — the sole
mechanism for resolving the debt; asking price **$615,000 (May 2025)**,
adjusted down; a court **"drop dead" provision in August 2025** —
without a binding sales agreement, automatic conversion to Chapter 7 or
dismissal. Her own messages bracket it: September 2024, before filing —
*"I don't even think bankruptcy is going to work for me"*; 8 November
2024, after — *"I need insurance that doesn't bankrupt me. Oh! Wait! I
already AM bankrupt!🤭"* The sale that followed was therefore not a
decision she made about Dan's housing; it was the remedy in her own
bankruptcy, executed under a court deadline by a woman eight months off
a double ankle fracture that had stopped her working. The buyers closed
at **$465,000 on 23 June 2026** with a seven-day vacate; Dan executed the
move alone on 8 July 2026, a week past the window
([[wiki/legal/463-morgantown]]). What happened next is not a financial
story, and it is told in §2025–2026.

## Gambling, drinking, and the Uniontown supply economy

These three are one economy in Fayette County and she is documented in
all of it — as a participant in her own right, not only as her son's
infrastructure.

**Gambling.** Lady Luck at Nemacolin is the venue, and the mechanism
visible in the record is free play — comped credit that makes a trip
rational even with no money: *"I just need $20 so I can use my $80 free
play"* (October 2019). The pattern runs continuously from the start of
the corpus through 2020 and then **stops**: a taper is visible in 2020
(*"I also haven't been drinking as much"*; *"Didn't even look when I
passed the casino"*), and on election night 2024, offered a hypothetical
bet by Dan, she states it flatly in the third person, like a fact about
someone she used to be: *"Suzanne does not gamble anymore."* Pressed:
*"That was a very easy addiction to leave behind… Seriously. I don't
even think about it anymore"* (5 November 2024). That arc — a decade of
documented play ending in a documented stop — is one of the few complete
behaviour-change arcs in the corpus, and it is entirely hers.

**Alcohol.** Wine is a logistics category in this thread the way gas and
cigarettes are. She asks for it to be collected (*"Can you stop and get
me a bottle of wine?"*), tracks the supply (*"I seem to be out of wine
unless I left some at grams. I need to maintain an even keel"*), and in
July 2025 ranks it: **"#1 Cigarettes #2 wine #3 food."** She also
self-reports the other side of it — *"I always do better sober and
during the day when no one is here"* (December 2017); *"I also haven't
been drinking as much and when I do it's kicking my ass"* (January
2020). On 1 January 2016 she cut off a friend over his drinking in a
message that is, structurally, the same letter she writes Dan: *"YOU
have a problem with alcohol and I will no longer be party to it."*
`CONTEXT_CORE_EXPANDED.md` lists her among four generations of
"functional alcoholics" in Dan's family, and records his own zero-alcohol
position as formed against it.

**Supply.** Her role as Cash App intermediary for the Tom pipeline is
real but is the late, narrow version of something much wider, and the
rail itself was Dan's idea: *"Do me a favor, download 'cash app' from
the App Store"* (31 August 2018). The wider role is as an ordinary
participant in the local market. She buys weed and vape product from
[[wiki/people/jim-vrabel|Jim Vrabel]] for a decade, and in 2015–16 she
is a routine cocaine source for Dan and
[[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] — the
[[wiki/timeline/annie-record|two-sided December 2015 record]] has him
going to her house for it several times a week. Her own comment on the
world she had ended up inside, sent at 01:18 on 12 December 2015, is
worth keeping whole:

> Wow!!! This is not what I was brought up to handle. I'm not supposed
> to know these people. I should write a book.

## Politics

`CONTEXT_CORE_EXPANDED.md` renders her politically as a **"Blue MAGA
wine mom."** The label is Dan's, and the primary record supports the
observation underneath it while dating the transition precisely. She was
raised in and married into a Republican household, and was still
forwarding chain-mail conservative content in December 2018. The break
is dated. On **10 March 2020** she posted publicly against Trump for the
first time — *"I couldn't stay quiet any longer."* On the night of **2
June 2020** she wrote to Dan: *"I went to vote. I wrote in Biden's name
and submitted without voting for anyone else on the republican ticket. I
don't trust any of them… time to switch parties."* From there the record
is consistent: an MSNBC diet (Morning Joe, Nicole Wallace, Lawrence
O'Donnell quoted by name) and close tracking of the Biden-to-Harris
transition. Politics is also the thread's single largest shared
entertainment — the 224-message debate night of 27 June 2024 is its peak
day across sixteen years — and it postdates her switch: the political
companionship is a product of the 2020 turn, not a precondition of it.
It is the one subject on which the two of them are uncomplicatedly on
the same side, and the thread's happiest bulk sits there.

## Terminal-phase spillover

Suz is the only member of Dan's family the
[[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] relationship reached directly, and the
record holds five distinct episodes — four of them hostile, the earliest
(the September 2020 warning above) predating the terminal phase by four
years, and the last a repair rather than an attack.

**January 2026 — the "Suzy" call.** During the post-Annie forensic period
a speakerphone call reached Suz (~4 January, 19:00): a drunk Annie asked
"I was just at your house, right?" — Suz confirmed; a male voice said
"Suzy"; the call cut in under two seconds. The `Gemini-_07` analysis —
AI-secondary — treats the diminutive as a high-entropy social-access key
and the instant hangup as a non-verbal signal. Her own thread that night
carries only *"Is Annie still here?"* at 03:23.

**April 2026 — the impersonation campaign.** A recurring blocked-caller
campaign targeted Suz directly, personally insulting her and threatening
retaliation ([[wiki/timeline/events/april-may-2026-final-weeks]]). Her
one message on the situation is procedural rather than distressed: *"I
don't know what is going on with Annie, but I have way more important
issues to deal with right now, and whatever is going on is a huge,
unnecessary thorn in my side. I need some communication and a resolution
to this whole situation!!"* (1 April).

**~26 May 2026 — the three-hour call.**
[[wiki/people/tuquick-17248123683|Tuquick]] — the man Annie had left Dan
for — is reported to have spent three hours calling Suz with the same
script he later directed at Dan, naming Annie several times. Five days
later came the [[wiki/timeline/events/end-fight|May 31 End Fight]]. The
source is a secondary AI podcast dramatisation of the underlying logs,
not independently re-verified against a call record; treat as
plausible-and-specific pending confirmation. The caller is identified as
[[wiki/people/jerel-coles|Jerel Wayne Coles]] — provenance: commercial
background-check aggregator, not docket-verified.

**August 2026 — the apology.** On 2 August 2026 Annie walked to the house
and apologised to Suz in person for the harassment campaign
([[wiki/timeline/events/july-august-2026-reentanglement]]) — the only
repair either party made to anyone outside the relationship in the entire
terminal record. Two days later, Suz's comment on Annie coming to cut
Dan's hair on the porch: *"How did I know she would be there?"*

The terminal-phase dossiers name Suz as the target of a deliberate
image-management effort: Annie maintained a warm, attentive persona with
her — wine, help around the house — which in the dossiers' reading
converted Dan's most natural ally into an asset. The wiki presents that
as the dossiers' reading rather than a settled finding, and the primary
record complicates it in one direction worth stating: the warmth between
Suz and Annie is documented from December 2015, ten years before the
terminal phase, and it is mutual and specific — the hair chair, the
thank-you note, the haircuts, *"She is like / The biggest #dannie
supporter."* Whatever the terminal-phase persona was, it was built on a
decade of real relationship, which is what made it usable.

## 2025–2026: the return, the shared house and the August rupture

Dan returned to 337 Saratoga on 22 February 2025 after six years in
Manhattan, into a house already inside the bankruptcy's logic — listed
once at $615,000 that spring, sold the following June. By February 2026
she was at [[wiki/legal/463-morgantown|463 Morgantown St]] several times
a week — *"I'll get cigarettes and drop some off on my way to Morgantown
st"* (10 February), *"Going to Morgantown st"* (14 February) — and
through the last month at 337 the June 2026 stretch is also the warmest
sustained material in the late corpus: she brings him pepperoni rolls
and asks which dessert he wants, gets him a day's paid work moving
furniture for a friend, and asks — twice in one week — whether he has
applied at Dollar General. On 21 June, after a neighbour knocked for
money: *"she called me to ask me for money, so I walked over and got you
a black and mild and loaned her a $20."*

Then the sale closed (23 June), Dan moved out alone on 8 July, and the
thread — the most reliable daily instrument in the corpus — nearly
stopped: **14 messages in July 2026 and 28 in the first eleven days of
August, against an average of 296 a month over the preceding six** — a
collapse specific to her, since the corpus overall runs normally in the
same window. It is not an export artifact. What the record holds at the
end of the silence, on **11 August 2026**, is two messages from her:

> It's time for you to go.
>
> I'm so tired of you stealing from me. You have no respect for me and
> you don't care one single bit about me. I'm so disappointed.

That was the old version of this page's live edge, and the old reading
treated it as terminal. The record since complicates it, and the
complication is the point of this rewrite. Five days after the eviction
sentence, on the night of 2026-08-16, Coles resumed calling and texting
her from Annie's phone, and named her in a written threat the next day.
**She received none of it.** Dan revealed why on August 17: *"I blocked
your number from my moms phone just in case that happened. So she never
actually got the calls or texts"* — he had predicted a specific harm to
his mother, taken a specific pre-emptive step against it days in
advance, and been right ([[wiki/timeline/events/august-2026-morgantown-call]]).
And the thread itself resumes: the living iMessage master carries the
two of them, in September 2026, running a shared house by text — her
nightly closing checklist on **21 September 2026** at 00:17 (*"Before we
call it quits for the night: Clean out fridge before we… Take out
garbage / Check windows and lock car / Bring in orchids / Work on more
laundry / Put dishes…"*), and at 02:19 her asking him to go out in the
dark and reconnect the corrugated pipe to the downspout *"because it's
gonna b[e]…"* — his whole reply: *"Yep."* Hers: *"Thank you."*

Where exactly Dan is living, and whether the 11 August sentence was ever
formally acted on, the corpus still does not say. What it says is that
six weeks after she told him to go, she was sending him the household's
closing checklist at midnight and he was going out to fix the downspout.
The vow outlasted the sentence. That, and not any balance between them,
is the relationship's current state in the record.

## Assessment

The record supports one judgment above the others: this is a
relationship organised by a refusal. She watched her parents lose their
children — by rejection on one side and estrangement on the other — and
built her own parenting as the negation of it, then kept the negation
running as daily labour for sixteen years: the rides, the houses, the
interventions, the memorial administered alone, the thread written
mostly by her, the debate nights and the downspout. The costs of the
arrangement are also in the record and are also real: availability
without an exit becomes its own kind of leverage, her grievances are
genuine and itemised when they surface, and the same woman who would
never lose contact told her son, in August 2026, that it was time for
him to go. The wiki's reading is that the sentence and the checklist six
weeks later are not a contradiction to be resolved but the relationship
itself, stated at both of its poles: the contact is unconditional; the
terms never have been.

What would change this reading: a documented period in which she, not
circumstance or an export gap, withdrew contact and sustained the
withdrawal; or her own account of the principle in terms that displace
it — she is the one living person who could rewrite this page in a
paragraph, and the standing gaps are the ones only she can close: her
account of the 2005 rupture (still entirely Dan-side; the contemporaneous
record begins in 2015, ten years after); what became of the teaching
plan of August 2024; the Chapter 13's outcome on the 337 proceeds; and
where, exactly, the household at 463 stands after August 2026.
