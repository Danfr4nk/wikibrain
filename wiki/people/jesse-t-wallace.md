# Jesse T. Wallace

Superintendent of the Laurel Highlands school district (Uniontown, PA) —
the center of the district's 2026 crisis, the subject of a two-night
investigation Dan ran with Sammy on 2026-09-15, reading the district's own
evaluation documents against a year of scandal and board politics.

The short version: fourteen months ago, one board president's solo review
called Wallace Distinguished at everything. Now a different president's
full-board compilation calls him a dismissive micromanager the community
doesn't trust — signed September 1, 2026, four weeks after the board killed
his contract extension 6-3. He signed it under written protest.

## The two evaluations

**2024-25:** a single-evaluator form, board president Joe D'Andrea alone —
all six standards Distinguished, comments praising Wallace as "an extremely
effective communicator." One ally's opinion, no tabulation, no other voices
on the page. It was posted publicly on the district site; a search cache
from ~August 31 still showed it. Then it was pulled and replaced.

**2025-26:** compiled by new board president Marcus DiNunno from all nine
board members' scores. Nearly every indicator Needs Improvement or Failing —
6 of 9 rated him Failing at identifying and resolving district problems.
DiNunno's comments accuse Wallace of micromanaging, being dismissive, and
playing politics with board members. Wallace signed under protest, disputing
the accuracy on the record — his exhibit A if it ever goes legal.

The two documents agree on exactly two things, and they're the most
trustworthy parts precisely because they cut against each author's interest:
Wallace can't hold staff accountable (his *ally* named it first, as the one
improvement area in the golden review), and he can handle money (his
*enemies* conceded fiscal responsibility as his top strength). Everything
else flipped 180°. Communication was his superpower last year and his worst
category this year. Skills don't invert like that in a year; relationships
do. The board isn't rating a changed Wallace. It's rating a broken one.

## The kill move: August 5, 2026

That night the board voted **6-3 to rescind Wallace's three-year contract
extension** — approved in April 2025, would have run through June 2030; now
he's done summer 2027. Same night, the board accepted the demolition
evaluation by majority vote. The no votes on the rescission: Tom Landman,
Ken Meadows, and Joe D'Andrea — the man who wrote the golden eval. D'Andrea
called it "a complete ambush" and "politically motivated," and told the
Daily Courier he'd been in contact with the Pennsylvania School Boards
Association. DiNunno cited executive-session legal analysis, refused
details, and insisted the rescission was "completely separate" from the
scandals.

The sequence matters: the eval isn't the opening move. Signed September 1,
four weeks after the extension was killed, it's the paperwork *after* the
decision. And the ratings are a curious instrument for a firing squad — five
of six standards landed "Needs Improvement," the vocabulary of "fix it or
else," not "you're done." The board already got what it wanted: a lame duck
with ten months left, no buyout, no hearing, no lawsuit. The eval is the
narrative cover for that. The only sentence in twelve pages that flirts with
cause language is the one about student safety — if the attorney general's
review or the district's outside counsel turns up something on mandatory
reporting, that line becomes the hinge. Short of that, the smart money is a
managed exit, not a firing.

## What the town went through

Underneath the board politics is real damage. March 2026 is the year that
broke the district: teacher Martin Gatti arrested over sexual comments
toward a student, accused of trying to kiss her — fired at the March 24
special meeting. JROTC instructor Daniel Cervone arrested over an alleged
sexual relationship with a 15-year-old dating to 2020. Parents packed the
library, demanded Wallace's resignation, alleged he knew for years — never
conceded, never charged. A Change.org petition for his resignation went up.
DA Mike Aubele warned that failure to report child abuse is itself a crime.
State Rep. Charity Grimm Krupa asked the Pennsylvania attorney general to
review the district. The board hired Pittsburgh firm MBM Law for an outside
review. A third employee went on leave in April over a 2009 allegation and
was fired unanimously August 5 — the same night as the rescission. Earlier:
math teacher Ashley Thurby-Kolesar, 6,000+ texts with a 17-year-old in 2023,
felony wiretapping plea, teaching certificate suspended effective September
2025.

March was the anger spike. Since August: silence. The evaluation — the
actual document — has sat on the district website for two weeks and no
reporter in seven outlets has touched it. The press covered the rescission
only as a sidebar to the scandal reporting. Next regular board meeting:
September 16, 6pm, high school library. No agenda published.

As of 2026-09-15 the district website still lists Wallace as superintendent,
photo up, new eval posted, old one gone.
