# The Libby wage dispute: shifts, rates, and the open estate question

This is the ledger page for [[wiki/people/libby|Libby Titus]] — the
shift-by-shift payment reconstruction the main page does not attempt, and the
estate-claim question the main page leaves as its first gap. Everything below is
drawn from the Dan↔Annie iMessage thread (`imessages_2124702449_last6months`,
timestamps UTC; local dates are EDT, UTC−4 — see the method appendix), which
holds 107 messages containing the string "Libby" between 2024-02-09 and
2025-03-21. The main page's 116-message count likely includes variant
spellings and adjacent threads; the 107 is the audited figure from this
thread **[DERIVED]**.

> **Sensitivity note, carried from the main page.** This concerns a named,
> identifiable, now-deceased person's household finances and a wage dispute,
> recorded from one side of a private conversation she was not party to. She
> has surviving family. It is kept because the unpaid balance is the only
> documented income event on Annie's side of the relationship with a dollar
> figure attached, and written to that standard and no further.

## The rate's biography: $50 to $75 to $500-in-cash

The demand letters state **$75/hour**, *"our normal, agreed upon rate"*, *"set
by Libby Fagen"* (2024-08-14). That is the end of the rate's story, not the
beginning. The thread records three distinct pay regimes, and the famous
contradiction only looks like one if the first two are missing.

**February — the $50/hour opening.** On **2024-02-08**, the first payday,
Annie reports: *"Dan. She paid me $350."* Twenty minutes later she quotes the
negotiation verbatim:

> *"She goes 'how much can I pay you?' I said that's up to you! However much*
> *you feel comfortable paying me. She goes 'let's start at $50/hour how many*
> *were you here?' I said well 3 1/2. So that's 175."*

$50/hour was Libby's opening offer **[ATTESTED]**, and $175 for 3.5 hours was
the arithmetic of that day. The $350 total against a $175 day's arithmetic
most plausibly covers two days — Annie had already been asked to *"come again
tomorrow"* the same morning, implying a prior unrecorded visit — but the
thread does not say so, and the $350 is not itemized **[INFERRED, seam
showing: two-day reading is the arithmetic fit, not a statement]**.

**March — the triple-pay day.** On **2024-03-17**: *"She says she's triple my
pay today lol."* No amount stated, no hours stated. The rate was already
being overridden ad hoc within five weeks of the opening offer.

**By August — $75/hour, "set by Libby Fagen."** The letters' figure. Annie's
2024-08-14 addition — *"I also added in that draft that the hourly rate was
set by Libby Fagen"* — attributes it to Libby directly. Somewhere between
February and August the contracted rate rose from $50 to $75. The thread does
not record the raise itself **[UNKNOWN — the raise is inferred from the two
endpoints, not attested]**.

**May — the cash largesse, a third regime.** And this is the one that
matters, because it is what the contradiction is made of:

- **2024-05-17:** *"I was literally with Libby for just over 3 hours today..
  she paid me 500."* — roughly **$145–167/hour** against the $75 contracted
  rate.
- **2024-05-23:** *"Please guess what Libby paid me today for 4 hours of
  helping her."* Dan guesses **"700"** at 19:53:55 UTC. Annie, within the
  minute: *"YES / exactly / How you know"* — then the gloss that reframes the
  whole contradiction: *"But she paid me an insane amount for today.. I said
  it was only four hours. She goes but today was a nightmare."*

**$175/hour for four hours, confirmed by Annie, and Libby's own stated
rationale was hazard pay for a bad day** [ATTESTED]. This is not a
contradiction between two rates. It is two payment modes: a contracted
$75/hour rate and ad hoc cash/check overpayments framed by the payer herself
as compensation for nightmare shifts. The May 23 payment was also a
**check** — Annie spent that evening failing to deposit it (*"I have tried to
deposit this check ten fucking times / It keeps automatically picking up the
wrong amount / $10. $11. $1"*), which matters because the July arrears she
later chased were also framed around checks, not cash.

## Shift-by-shift: the payment chronology, February to July

The thread is a conversation, not a timesheet, so "shift-by-shift" means
every shift the thread evidences, in order, with what the thread says about
pay. Days with no mention are not days with no work — the register is set in
the first week (*"I gotta gotta gotta get up early tomorrow and help Libby.
I need money"*, 2024-02-16) and the work is near-daily through the spring.

**February — the trial.** First visit implied before 2024-02-08. Paid $350 on
02-08 at the $50/hour opening. *"I am taking Libby food from Antonuccis. So
hopefully she will give me something for bringing her food"* (02-11) —
payment informal, per-visit. *"I may try to see Libby tomorrow to get some
money"* (02-14). Hospital trip 02-20. The month's shape is piecework: show
up, get paid something, need money.

**March — the job turns medical, and the money keeps coming.** Brain cancer
disclosed 03-05; right-lung recurrence 03-06. Annie is driving to
appointments, sitting through them, spending half-hours on the phone with
doctors (03-19), receiving **cemetery-plot emails** (03-20). Pay mentions are
sparse but the direction is up: the triple-pay day (03-17). *"Well. Libby
just texted me. 'I can leave it at the front desk' meaning the check"*
(03-29) — checks now, left at a desk, collected between shifts.

**April — check logistics.** *"I asked Libby if I could pick up a check and
she said yes she has to get more checks from her apartment.. so hopefully by
tomorrow"* (04-05). *"Cause libby texted me again around 7 just saying 'I
need to go get checks'"* (04-06). *"Well Libby got checks"* (04-06). The
friction is already check-shaped: a dying woman with no checks on hand.

**May — the peak and the largesse.** Twenty-five mentions, the highest
monthly count. Annie is working both jobs — *"Now I'll be here 6 days a week
and then Libby in mornings…"* (05-08) — and the Libby money is earmarked
for phones and phone service. The two documented cash shifts: $500 for ~3+

Donald emails Annie a **breakdown of every single household bank account**
and she *"felt wrong looking at it"* — the household's finances are now
visible to her in a way that will matter in July, when the trust breaks.

**June — weekly pay discussed, then normal service.** *"I told her she could
pay be tomorrow or we talked about just paying weekly which would be good"*
(06-05). *"Got paid for this week finally"* (06-07) — the "finally" doing
quiet work; payment is already slipping by early June. *"She's gonna pay me
tomorrow"* recurs 06-18, 06-21 (twice), 06-25. *"I just want paid for today
and Monday"* (06-26); *"Getting paid thankfully"* (06-26, 18:50). The
pattern of June is chase-and-collect: every payment arrives a day late and
thanked-for, which is the sound of a pay relationship degrading before the
dispute formally opens.

**July, first half — still collecting.** *"I just went paid"* (07-01, in the
apartment while the daughter is there — *"I feel awkward"*). *"I dont know
if I will get paid or not today"* (07-01, 18:53) — indecision at the
household level now governs whether the day's work pays. *"She paid me

comfortable in May is not: *"Sooo basically I have under a hundred left…

07-18 the messages are all waiting: *"Do you want to start walking toward
the mark bc she gonna pay me soon"*, *"Bc I still need paid"*, *"I would
have been there for her regardless if she wanted to pay me"*, *"But I need
to get paid"*.

**July 19 — the last paid-adjacent shift, and the one that wasn't paid.**
Annie is *"in the car literally in between Donald and Libby"* (07-19). The

xoxo"* — sent to a woman who will be dead in eleven weeks, who does not pay.

## What the arrears cost: the household footprint

The dispute's numbers are small — hundreds, not thousands — and the thread
shows exactly what hundreds meant in that household in the summer of 2024.

**The rent checks.** *"I may try to see if John will hold this weeks rent

Libby"* (2024-08-01). Two weeks later the ask is more desperate: *"Did you

a receivable from a dying woman's household, and Annie is asking Dan to tell

**The mother, twice.** On 2024-07-22: *"My mom will help what needs to be

receivable is being borrowed against from Ellen before it is even collected,

**The phones.** The May largesse was earmarked before it arrived:
*"hopefully she will give me something for bringing her food"* becomes, by
May, *"I think I will order us phoned and get our service switched"* (05-18)
— the $500 and $700 shifts converted directly into new phones and Metro
service. When the money stops in July, the phone bill becomes the pressure
point (*"So phone bill payment came out today.. I need to pay loan by

and the landlord.

**The second job absorbs the shock.** *"So now I have no choice but to find

Annie's documented working life — are being replaced, in real time, by the
prospect of morning serving shifts. The wage dispute's material outcome is
not a settlement; it is a downgrade.

None of this is moralized here. It is the reason the $675 matters: it was
rent-week money, borrowed-against money, phone-bill money. The estate
question is not abstract because the arrears never were.

## The contradiction, narrowed

The main page carries the contradiction as unresolved: $75/hour in the
letters vs. $500 for ~3 hours on 2024-05-17. The fuller record narrows it
without closing it:

1. The $75/hour was the **contracted** rate — "our normal, agreed upon
   rate," "set by Libby Fagen" — and the $675 arrears figure is arithmetically

2. The $500 and $700 were **ad hoc overpayments**, and Libby's own framing
   survives for the $700: *"today was a nightmare."* Hazard pay, in her
   words, not a rate change.
3. The rate itself moved once: $50/hour (Feb, Libby's opening offer) → $75
   (by Aug). The raise is endpoint-inferred, not attested.

What remains genuinely unresolved is not the rate but the **total**: every
draft from Aug 1 to Aug 14 carries placeholders, and the one hard number —
$675 — covers only "last week" as of July 25. Whether shifts between July 26
and the Aug 6 break added to the arrears is not in the thread. The
contradiction was never a math problem; it was a documentation problem, and
it is still one.

## After the death: who a claim would even run against

This is the part the record never reaches, so it is stated here as
structure, not as finding. Libby Titus died 13 October 2024. Three parties
survive her in the thread's account, and a wage claim would have had to
choose among them:

1. **The estate.** The wages were owed by Libby as the employer — *"the
   hourly rate was set by Libby Fagen"* — so the claim, post-death, is
   against her estate. Filing a creditor's claim in Surrogate's Court
   requires knowing the estate is in probate, having a docket to attach to,
   and stating a sum certain. The letters never stated a sum certain.
2. **Donald Fagen.** He survives her, he was cc'd on the household finances
   (the May 30 bank-account breakdown came from his email), and Annie's
   own strategy was *"Let me know what to sign and when I can get my
   check"* addressed to both of them. But nothing in the thread establishes
   he was the employer; the rate was set by Libby, the checks were Libby's
   to write until she claimed she couldn't.
3. **The business manager.** The unnamed figure Libby claimed the wages had
   been *"turn[ed] over to"* — *"she's not allowed to write checks anymore,
   and that the business manager has to"* (Annie, 2024-07-28, calling it
   *"a total fucking lie"*). If true, the manager was the payor of last
   resort; if a lie, a stalling fiction. The thread never names them.

Against all three stands Dan's pre-death assessment — *"totally off the
books and totally non-contracted"* — and the absence of any written total.
A DOL complaint names an employer; an estate claim names a sum. The dispute
had neither firmly in hand when the employer died. That is not a legal
conclusion; it is the shape of the hole the record leaves.
