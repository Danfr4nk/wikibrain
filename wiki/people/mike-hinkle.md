# Mike Hinkle

Mike Hinkle is a professional caddie — in Dan's words, the realest "looper" he knows. He is a year-round working looper who spent the summer season at Nemacolin's Mystic Rock and the rest of the year in Tampa, looping at Old Memorial. At one point he lived with Dan for about a month. He belongs to the Nemacolin caddie-yard world that runs through Dan's 2016–2019 working life, the same yard documented from the inside on [[wiki/people/davey-fitzpatrick|Davey Fitzpatrick's]] page — but where Davey was the yard's management layer and the page's other named caddies were its paycheck-to-paycheck edge, Hinkle was the thing Dan actually admired: the man who had made the loop a life.

The record of the friendship is small and two-sided in the literal sense: an 85-message iMessage thread running March 2018 to July 2019, and three unanswered Facebook messages Dan sent on a single afternoon in July 2017. Around those correspondences sit the golf-circle mentions — a caddie-yard joke, a gratuity note, a standing invitation — that place him in Dan's working orbit from 2016 on. The thread's surviving center is a message Hinkle sent in the week Fran Coldren died: *"What? Are you a pimp now or something? Hope Fran is ok."* He knew Fran by her first name. That is not a distant contact's message; it is someone who knew the household.

This page matters to the wiki for two reasons beyond Hinkle himself. First, the friendship is the corpus's cleanest peer-insider caddy bond — not a boss, not a transaction, not a romance, just a working looper Dan held up as the standard. Second, the page's history is the wiki's cleanest identity-resolution case: for months the corpus carried two Hinkle pages with an explicit instruction forbidding their merger, and Dan's direct testimony of 2026-09-17 closed it in one move.

## The looper

"Looper" is the yard's own word for a caddie who carries the bag — the working professional as distinct from the forecaddie, the honorary, the double-bag trainee. Dan's phrase, "the realest looper" he knows, is doing two jobs at once: it names Hinkle's profession and it places him at the top of the caddie yard's private status ladder. That ladder is documented across the [[wiki/work/nemacolin-caddying|caddying record]]: the "real loopers" are the ones who get called up for off-property day trips to prestige tracks, the ones whose loop fees become the yardstick everything else is measured against. Dan's own May 2018 call-up to Laurel Valley — *"first time getting called up with the real loopers"* — was the milestone of his caddying life. Hinkle, in Dan's telling, lived at that altitude permanently.

The circuit is the tell. Mystic Rock summers, Old Memorial winters: that is the professional caddie's migration pattern, following the golf calendar the way farmhands follow the harvest — Pennsylvania mountain resort in the season, Tampa in the off-months, year-round income from a trade most of the yard treated as a stopgap. Dan's own caddying ran April 2016 to November 2019 and never left Nemacolin; the Scottsdale transfer he raised with Carl in May 2018 was the closest he came to Hinkle's life, and it never happened. The admiration in "the realest looper" reads, against that record, as admiration for the version of the job Dan never got to live: not a seasonal gig at one resort, but the loop as a career, taken on the road.

Nothing in the held corpus establishes Hinkle's age, hometown, or surname beyond "Hinkle" — the testimony gives the trade, the circuit, and the character judgment, and the corpus gives the rest.

## In the caddie yard

The held corpus names a Hinkle as a golf-circle contact from Dan's first full caddie season on. A case-insensitive sweep (`kb/data/0978`) finds the hits spread across 2016–2018:

- **2016-08-09** — *"invited to golf with hinkle,"* four months into Dan's first season at Nemacolin, inside the summer the hire was still new.
- **2017-10-01** — *"hinkle wrote in our grat for yesterday."* "Grat" is caddie gratuity — Hinkle filling in the tip sheet, a working-yard chore, at the tail of the 2017 season (the season's bonfire invitations are dated October 18 and 27).
- **May 2018** — three more hits (May 15, 17, 20), inside the professional-peak season of the Laurel Valley call-up and the two-bag milestone.
- **2018-04-30** — *"17 yr old Hinkle tripping,"* a row inside the iMessage thread itself. Its referent is unestablished: it may be a joke, a nickname, or another person entirely. The row is recorded as found and left unresolved rather than smoothed into a reading.

The yard's own verdict on Hinkle survives in the Davey Fitzpatrick thread. Dan's line to Davey: *"I'd pay Hinkle $100 to work our bags lolz"* — Davey's laugh. The joke's shape is the tribute: the fantasy of hiring the yard's gold standard to carry *your* bag, the looper's looper turned, for a punchline, into hired help. It is affectionate mockery of exactly the kind the yard reserved for its fixtures, and it is consistent with everything else: in this record, golf-adjacent is nearly an identity, and Hinkle is firmly inside it. The Davey page's link for that joke now resolves to this article.

## The month under one roof

At some point Hinkle lived with Dan for about a month. That is Dan's testimony, not the corpus's — no lease, no dated message, no address pins it down. It sits in the same window as everything else: the caddie seasons, the iMessage thread, the Facebook messages.

A month under the same roof is the kind of fact that explains the casual register of everything that follows. The Fran message is not written like a first condolence; it is written like someone who already knows the household's names and feels entitled to joke on the way in. The July 2017 Facebook messages are not written to a stranger either — *"hey dude"* is not how Dan opens a cold contact. The testimony supplies the missing backstory the correspondences assume: these two men had lived together, briefly, and the thread is the after-echo of that month. Stated as his account, not as a corpus finding — but the corpus reads differently with it than without it.

## The iMessage thread

Eighty-five messages, March 2018 to July 2019 — 64 sent by Dan, 21 received from Hinkle (2018-03-08 to 2019-07-03 Eastern; the corpus timestamps run 2018-03-08 20:21 UTC to 2019-07-04 01:58 UTC). The direction split is worth stating plainly because it is the relationship's shape in miniature: Dan doing most of the talking, Hinkle answering short. The held sample is one received message, and it is the one that placed Hinkle in the record's human center:

> *"What? Are you a pimp now or something? Hope Fran is ok"*

Received 2018-03-09 00:08 UTC — the evening of March 8 Eastern — two weeks before Fran Coldren's death on April 4, 2018. The message's anatomy is the whole friendship in two clauses. The opener (*"What?"*) is responding to something Dan said that the record does not keep — a reminder of what the restored half actually is: the 64 recovered messages are Dan's sent half, so what the recount restored is the *context* for Hinkle's replies, not Hinkle's missing words. The jim-shaffer case was the inverse (the page lacked the counterparty's half); here the page had Hinkle's half all along and lacked Dan's. The joke (*"are you a pimp now or something?"*) is the yard's register, teasing and crude; and the pivot (*"Hope Fran is ok"*) is real — a check on a 98-year-old woman's health, by first name, in the middle of her final decline. It is a short message doing three things at once, and the third is the one that matters. Davey Fitzpatrick's unprompted condolence the day after she died is the yard's other same-week marker; Hinkle's is the earlier one, the check-in while she was still alive.

The thread's timing has its own story. It opens March 8, 2018 — the 2018 season's paperwork week (the caddying record has Dan and Jack Rusko going up to Mystic Rock for their paperwork on March 9) — and it is still going in July 2019, eight months after the November 2018 New York move that ended the Davey Fitzpatrick thread cold. Davey's last conversation is Dan telling him about the city; Hinkle's thread just keeps going. That makes it the one Nemacolin-era channel in the held record that outlasted the era itself — the friendship was not purely yard-proximity, or it would have stopped when the yard did.

The thread ends July 3, 2019, inside the wind-down of Dan's caddying era — the resume dates the job to November 2019, and the year's corpus texture is institutional decay rather than personal arc. There is no farewell, no departure scene, no last-day message. That is consistent with how the corpus usually ends things: not with an event, but with the messages about it stopping. Whether the friendship survived the end of the Nemacolin years in any channel is not in the record.

## The three Facebook messages

On Friday, July 14, 2017, Dan sent three messages to Michael Hinkle on Facebook Messenger. No reply ever arrived in that thread:

> *"hey dude"*

> *"let me know if you see this"*

> *"annie will be home soon, so i'll call u if not. i found us something"*

Three messages is the exact length of the escalation — greeting, acknowledgement request, then the fallback (*"i'll call u if not"*) with a deadline (*"annie will be home soon"*) and finally the thing he opened the app to say. He did not send a fourth. He said what he had, named a way around the silence, and stopped.

The third message's clock is the most informative clause: it places Dan at home, alone, with Annie out and expected back — in July 2017, that residence is 155 Virginia Ave in Uniontown, the house he had moved into with Annie after the Scerba's place. And the month is the [[wiki/timeline/periods/2017-poverty-floor|poverty floor]] at its floorest: water shut off, the account overdrawn, gold and clothes sold, Fran waking with nosebleeds at 3am, the calendar logging the year as a single sustained emergency. What *"i found us something"* referred to is not in the record, and this page declines to guess — though *"us"* implies a standing joint interest of some kind, which now reads naturally as a caddie's *"us"*: a loop, a game, a piece of the trade. The old page treated the unanswered thread as a mystery about a stranger. It was never that. It was a caddy texting a fellow looper.

Whether the July 2017 phone call happened is not in the record. *"i'll call u if not"* is the last thing either man says in any held source — the iMessage thread does not begin for another eight months, and when it does, there is no backward reference to the call. The prior wiki filed the three messages in its own timeline (whole.txt:106387) as well as quoting them verbatim (whole.txt:58082); both survivals are through the old-wiki export, since the underlying Facebook thread is not held in this repository — `raw/facebook-threads/` holds only the manifest, and the manifest's fingerprint search narrowed the July 2017 thread to two candidate files, neither yet pulled.

## The 904 number and the Tampa winters

The iMessage handle is a 904-number handle, and the old page read that as Jacksonville, Florida. Dan places Hinkle's winters in Tampa. Area codes are not addresses — people keep numbers when they move, and a working looper who migrates seasonally is exactly the kind of person who would — so this page drops the Jacksonville inference and keeps the handle. The number is retained in the infobox as the corpus's identifier for the thread, nothing more. (The underlying kb datum, `kb/data/0978`, redacts the handle per the standing redaction rule; it is kept here only because the original page already carried it live, verbatim, as the thread's identifier.)

One detail stays open rather than smoothed over: if a contacts export ever ties the 904 number to a Tampa address, that closes it. Until then the discrepancy is a reminder, not a problem — the testimony says Tampa, the handle says 904, and both can be true of one man.

## Conflicts in the record

- **The 21-message count (2026-09-09, superseded).** This page previously reported the thread as 21 messages, "all received (export artifact)." The held corpus holds 85 — 64 sent, 21 received. The 21-received figure was never wrong as a count; it was wrong as a claim about the relationship's shape. The recount is documented in `kb/data/0978`, and the correction pattern is collected in `kb/interpretations/received-half-count-pattern.md`, which names the Hinkle thread the flagship instance of fourteen identical one-direction errors across the people pages: "a one-sided instrument's output was relabeled as a two-sided finding." The missing half — Dan's 64 sent messages — leaves no trace in the page's confidence, which is what made the error durable.
- **The do-not-conflate instruction (2026-09-09, superseded 2026-09-17).** `kb/data/0977` carried an explicit evidence-layer instruction: *"Do not conflate the two Hinkles without evidence"* — 'Michael Hinkle' the unanswered Facebook contact and 'Mike Hinkle' the 904-number iMessage contact were separate pages, and the datum's extraction section stated flatly that the golf-circle Hinkles were "all about a *different* Hinkle." Dan's direct testimony of 2026-09-17 is the discriminator the evidence layer asked for: one person. The instruction served its purpose — preventing exactly the kind of sloppy merge this page now performs with evidence in hand — and is retired.
- **Two pages, one man (resolved 2026-09-17).** The corpus carried two Hinkle pages that did not obviously describe the same man: a Facebook contact (3 sent, 0 received, one afternoon in July 2017) and an iMessage contact (a 904 number, 85 messages across sixteen months, casual familiarity, Fran condolences). The michael-hinkle page maintained three live readings — one man, two men with one surname, two unrelated men — and selected none, while both pages were quietly claiming the same man anyway: the Davey Fitzpatrick page linked its caddie-yard Hinkle joke to the *Michael* page, while the golf-circle sweep was filed as narrowing the *Mike* page's open question. Dan's testimony closed it. The second page is now a pointer to this one, and the Davey joke's link resolves here.
- **The Jacksonville inference (dropped).** The old page read the 904 handle as Jacksonville, Florida. Dan places Hinkle's winters in Tampa. The inference is dropped; the handle is kept as an identifier only. See the section above.
- **The "stranger" reading of the July 2017 thread (retired).** The old page treated three unanswered messages as a mystery about an unknown contact, with no known relationship and no referent for "i found us something." The identity resolution retires the mystery: the relationship was the caddie yard, the referent was presumably the trade, and the silence is just an unanswered afternoon.

## Assessment

What the record supports is a narrow, legible thing: a peer friendship inside the caddie yard between Dan and a working looper he admired, running from at least August 2016 (the first golf-circle hit) through July 2019 (the thread's end), with a month of living under one roof somewhere in the middle that the corpus never dates. Hinkle is the only figure in the Nemacolin record who occupies exactly this slot — not management (Davey), not the paycheck-to-paycheck edge (Vaughn), not a near-member who never made it in (Steve Kezmarsky), but a fellow looper Dan regarded as the real article, who knew his household by first name and checked on his dying grandmother unprompted.

The shape of the surviving evidence says something about the friendship's direction of energy. In the one correspondence the corpus keeps whole, Dan sends three messages for every one he receives — the July 2017 Facebook afternoon is Dan reaching out into silence, and the 2018–19 thread is Dan doing most of the talking. That is consistent with the admiration running one way: Hinkle was the standard, Dan the junior. It is also consistent with the simpler reading that Dan was just the chattier texter. The record does not adjudicate between those; it does establish that when Dan wanted a caddie's company, Hinkle was who he texted.

For the wiki, this page is the cleanest demonstration of the testimony-as-discriminator rule: an evidence layer that correctly refused to merge without proof, a direct witness who supplied the proof, and a merge performed with the receipt in hand. It is also the flagship instance of the received-half count pattern — the reminder that the wiki's governance rule, "documented counts override generated metaphor," was for a time being enforced with denominators that were systematically half.
