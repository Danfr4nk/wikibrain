# Fran Coldren, Caregiver Years (2017–18)

For the last year and a half of her life, [[wiki/people/fran-coldren|Fran Coldren]] — Dan's maternal great-grandmother, the person he names as the single largest influence on his life — was kept alive at home by a small rotation of people who loved her, including, for the last six months, a grandson paid fifteen dollars an hour to play video games with her. She died on April 4, 2018, at 97, in the Uniontown Hospital pediatric wing, with Dan and Annie the only two people in the room. The estate machinery — an eviction notice, a trespass letter, a trustee, a daughter who never visited — was running in parallel the whole way down. This page is the caregiver years as the corpus actually records them: the job, the year, the falls, the exclusion, the vigil, and the ending — which, by the standards of Dan's entire life record, went impossibly well.

> **CONFIDENCE AND SCOPE.** Everything dated here rests on contemporaneous messages unless labeled otherwise. The 2017 portion is thinner than the 2018 portion — the vigil is the most documented four weeks of Dan's pre-corpus life, while the pre-fall caregiving year is carried mostly by photographs, a few dated messages, and his later testimony. Where the record is thin, this page says so rather than smoothing it. [[wiki/people/fran-coldren]] holds the full life; [[wiki/timeline/events/the-fall-of-fran]] and [[wiki/timeline/events/fran-death-vigil]] hold the event-level sequences this period summarizes.

## The arrangement

Dan's own contemporaneous account is the cleanest version: *"i spent the past 6 months looking after her full time ($15 an hour to play video games with gram is a good deal)"* — written April 6, 2018, two days after her death, which puts the paid arrangement at roughly October 2017 onward. This is narrower than the wiki's earlier, looser framing of him and Annie as paid caregivers across "~2015–2018." The earlier years were informal — the grandchild and the girlfriend stopping by, the handyman work at the house — not employment. The employment was six months, and it was a job Dan named precisely, with the wage, on the record, in the same breath as the eulogy.

The money came through the estate, and the authorization chain is on the record too. In March 2018 Dan reasons that *"fred would have put the kaibosh on me being paid to watch gram if there was an ongoing issue"* — [[wiki/people/fred-adams|Judge Fred Adams]], Ira Coldren's old law partner, trustee of the estate accounts. So the caregiver arrangement was, formally, the estate paying Dan to do what the estate would otherwise have paid a stranger to do — and the difference mattered, because a stranger would have done it in a facility and Dan was doing it at home, where Fran wanted it done.

The standing directive governing all of it was categorical, and it was given to both Dan and [[wiki/people/suzanne-frank|Suz]] in the same terms: **keep me at home no matter what, do everything you can to not let me get taken from this house.** The rationale was not obscure to anyone who knew her. A woman born in a West Virginia coal town in 1920, who had gone dirt-poor to coal money in one generation and kept a golf handicap into her nineties, understood institutions exactly as well as her great-grandson does — as places you don't come back from. She turned out to be right about that, which is why the directive governs the reading of every decision made in her last year, including some that are hard to defend. One night in March 2018 would put the directive to its worst possible test, and Dan would honor it over the alternative. (See [[wiki/timeline/events/the-fall-of-fran]].)

The division of labor inside the arrangement was clean, and Dan does not flatter himself about it in the record. [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] was, in his own assessment, *"10,000× more responsible"* — the changing, the day-to-day survival, the actual nursing work. Dan's contribution was the opposite pole of the same job: he refused to sanitize her existence and treated a 97-year-old with dementia as a peer. That is not a soft claim. The record holds the footage. They watched *Pretty Woman* together and analyzed it — Fran ruling the prostitute narrative, in her considered judgment, *"way classier than some Cary Grant movie."* She was already prone to talking to people on the television when nobody was watching; Dan handed her something she could not parse at all — *GTA V* in stage mode, several hundred cars detonated on a photorealistic Los Angeles bridge — and harvested the line the family kept forever: **"WHAT. THE FUCK. IS HAPPENING IN THIS WORLD ANYMORE."**

She hit his vape. There is the photograph of her posing delightedly with his glass bong, which she had complimented as *"a lovely vase"* — grey sweatshirt behind her in the frame, accidentally composing itself into rising smoke, which is how a decade-old family optical illusion was born. (Dan re-sent those December 2015 frames byte-identical in September 2026 and solved the smoke question himself: the white sweatshirt on the table behind, the bong at the perfect angle, the two-dimensional projection. No smoke. His correction, on his own visual read — the same move he would make with the death itself two Aprils earlier.) A frame from November 6, 2017, shows her in teal polka-dot pajamas and a purple chenille jacket gripping a PlayStation DualShock 4 with both hands, the light bar glowing, mouth open mid-exclamation — she is not being entertained, she is *playing*. On February 20, 2018 — two and a half weeks before the fall that ended everything — a frame shows her in a white turtleneck and dark purple fleece, tissue in hand, a hand extending a lime-green box mod toward her.

He filmed most of that year, which is why any of it survives at all. On September 22, 2026, Dan corrected the standing assumption that the family hadn't kept the record: the media registry holds 34 Fran items, 19 of them videos — home footage, the nursing home, the wheelchair, the hospital bed with the oxygen mask, the night vigil with his hand entering frame. His framing of what he was documenting is the right one and survives in the record: a wild spirit resisting institutional capture, in the months before the institution got her.

The caregiving rotation was bigger than two people, and the page should name it properly. [[wiki/people/vicki|Vicki]] was the longest-serving non-family caregiver on the rotation (2016–2018) — her thread carries the vigil's best primary testimony from outside the family, and on the morning of April 2 she wrote what may be the vigil's truest sentence: *"I wouldn't want to be anywhere else, this lady is like my grandmother I never had."* [[wiki/people/marla|Marla]] carried the night shift through winter 2017–18; her first message to Dan is December 8, 2017, which dates the formal rotation. Marla's April 4 messages — *"she made it thru the nite,"* sent 7:23 that morning — are what fix the death date on the record. And Suz, the keno morning notwithstanding (below), showed up at the end: on April 2 she visited the hospital and, per Dan's footage, *"really held herself together and made it very light spirited and happy."*

The caregiving year overlapped Dan's Nemacolin caddie seasons ([[wiki/work/nemacolin-caddying]]) — 2016 through 2019, the hardest financial years of his adult life — and the pairing is worth naming plainly: by day, two loops in the freezing cold for a shrinking purse; by night, the 24-hour rotation at the pediatric wing. The corpus tags April 2018's opening day as caddie-season opening day, and Dan's account of April 1 is exactly that — worked two loops, got the call. This page is set against that backdrop because it was the backdrop: the caregiver and the caddie were the same person in the same week, and both jobs were about showing up on schedule in the cold.

## 2017: the pressure year

The caregiver year did not begin as a vigil. It began as a holding action, and the record shows the siege was already visible in the summer of 2017 — not in Fran's health, but in her money.

An August 2017 letter (surfaced in the September 2026 ingestion) shows [[wiki/people/diane-moore|Diane Moore]] — Fran's only child, Dan's maternal grandmother — pressing Fran to sell the Palm City, Florida house. The letter has the texture of a decision already made: the realtor lined up, [[wiki/people/fred-adams|Fred Adams]] already nodding along. Palm City had never appeared in the record before; it was possibly the Florida condo the old wiki had been carrying undocumented. A second letter, dated November 2017 — ten weeks later — records the posture shifting to a $20,000 loan against Fran's inheritance, Fred Adams formally cc'd, with Fran's shoulder surgery and the canceled Palm City trip in the same letter. In three months the shape of the ask had changed from *sell the house* to *borrow against the estate*. Both letters went through the trustee. The estate was already a moving thing while its owner was still at home, and the person moving it was not the person being cared for.

In November 2017, four months before the fall and five before the exclusion letters, Dan wrote that certain people had to be kept away from Fran — *"especially and most specifically not people directly connected to my grandparents who are looking for any excuse to throw her in a nursing home."* That is not a retrospective read. It is a contemporaneous warning, written while Fran was still lucid at her own table, that the family conflict which the April 2018 letters formalized was already understood as an active campaign. The fear was specific, named, and — as the spring would prove — accurate in its forecast of exactly which couple would act.

The load was already falling on Dan alone, in stretches. An October 2017 episode, reported to an unidentified contact: *"gram is bleeding… i'm only one here."* The solo-caregiver load predated the falls by months — the paid arrangement may have started that month precisely because October was when one person stopped being enough, or it may have started and October simply showed its limits. The record doesn't resolve that; both readings fit the same facts.

**The keno morning (~late 2017).** The filmed crisis the record knows best — Dan waking to a "bump" at 8:00 AM, finding Fran collapsed on the carpet wrapped in a white faux-fur blanket, screaming in agony, unable to stand — was not the fall that ended her residency. She survived this one. His own bedside manner on the tape is panic behind laughter: *"I didn't know what to do... move her..."* He admits reading the first signals wrong, taking her "all right" at face value until specific kitchen sounds forced the correction, then trying a food-based reboot: *"Hungry yet?"* She stayed on the floor most of the day. Suz arrived around 10:00 AM, having spent the entire night into the morning in a video-keno marathon. The two of them tried to lift Fran; she screamed. Dan's response was the correct one, and his mother overruled it in five words: he told her *"we have to just call an ambulance, this is crazy,"* and Suz said **"nope. i'll be back in a little bit"** — and left. At 2:00 PM Fran was still down, *"barely making any sense and screaming whenever she tries to stand,"* while Annie worked a full shift. Suz got back at 4:00 PM and performed the manual lift herself, captured in IMG_0569.MOV.

What kept Dan from overriding his mother for eight hours is on the record and is not squeamishness. He sustained himself through the hours of solo triage, by his own later account, with cocaine bumps — and he was, in his own words, terrified that if the situation went sideways his grandparents — *"already hated"* him — would use it to strip his inheritance *"or worse."* The pair was recorded in the wiki as "Diane and George" for six weeks; on 2026-08-17 Dan identified them as **"Dian and Dave"** — Diane and her second husband, Dave Moore. That correction turns the fear into a forecast rather than a mood: the same couple then served the March 29 eviction notice, took the hospital's calls through Dave, and sold the Florida condo within four months. An eight-hour delay in treating a fallen 97-year-old is thereby explained by an estate, which is the family's whole architecture in one morning.

Fran recovered from the keno morning and *"ma[de] it a few more months after this spill."* The formal shift rotation dates from roughly this period — Marla's first message is December 8, 2017. And on December 30, 2017, Dan told Vicki: *"She's the best I've seen her in years today so I got her talking and she said something interesting referring to Diane. At the end mostly."* What Fran actually said is not in the corpus. It is the single most valuable missing sentence in the whole caregiver-year record — the matriarch, on a lucid day, offering a final read on her own daughter — and it was never written down.

By the turn of 2018, the geometry was fixed. Fran at home, declining, directive intact. Dan and Annie paid to hold the line. Vicki and Marla on the rotation. Diane and Dave outside it, already moving the money. Everyone understood the nursing home was the thing to prevent, because Fran had told them what entering one meant: never getting out.

## The fall: night of March 7–8, 2018

The full night has its own page ([[wiki/timeline/events/the-fall-of-fran]]); what belongs here is what it did to the caregiving arrangement, and to Fran.

Two things Dan had been building toward for the better part of a year arrived in the same house within minutes of each other. The first was the opening encounter of the non-monogamy arrangement he and Annie had been discussing since early 2017 — [[wiki/people/danny-matthews|Danny Matthews]], a local realtor, due at the door. The second was Fran's fall. The first impact came while Danny was still en route — loud enough that Dan's own retelling renders it as a detonation. Dan reached her and knew immediately this one was different from the keno-morning spills. She was hurt. She said, characteristically, *"No, I'm... I'm okay"* — and the doorbell rang on the same beat.

The decision Dan made in that moment is the part of the night he defends rather than apologizes for, and he defends it with her own instruction. The standing directive — keep me at home no matter what — was categorical, and it had been given to both him and Suz jointly. She said she wanted to go back to bed. Calling an ambulance meant overriding the one thing she had asked of them. He put her back to bed, and the evening went ahead.

He does not remember the encounter. His account is explicit about the gap — *"I don't remember any of it. It happened"* — the next thing in memory being the door closing behind Danny and the walk back to the bedroom. The second impact came before the payoff he was staging for. It was louder than the first, and this time Fran was screaming. Dan called 911 with the physical residue of the preceding hour still on him — a detail he keeps in every telling, because the juxtaposition is the point of the story to him. The next fixed image in his memory is Fran being carried out of her own house on a stretcher.

Her hip was broken. She was stabilized and moved to a rehabilitation nursing home — the precise outcome the standing directive existed to prevent, and the one she had predicted would finish her. It did. Her mind did not come back. What remained was a single fixed question she asked without stopping: *when am I leaving?* Footage from the period (IMG_4361.MOV) shows her aphasic in a wheelchair, working at the lap belt, still trying to get free — and Dan marks it as **the last time he actually spoke with her**; she was nonresponsive for everything after.

The stretcher is the hinge of everything on this page. She never re-entered 117 Belmont Circle ([[wiki/places/117-belmont-circle]]). A fifty-year residency — the three-storey house on the edge of the UCC fairway she had held for over five decades, the seat of the golf legend, the scrapbook clippings, the Palmer photographs — ended in one night. Not by sale, not by decision, but because a fall arrived in the middle of a plan that was in motion, and the directive that had been built to prevent institutional capture had just been used to argue against calling the ambulance that might have delayed it. That is the unresolvable knot of the night, and it belongs on the record as a knot rather than a verdict.

The next morning's messages date the ambulance precisely. Dan to Vicki: *"she was screaming so loud when they were putting her on the gurney it was just awful."* Marla asking: *"What time did ambulance take her?"* Vicki noting: *"it's a good thing Marla wasn't there, she would have been really scared."* And Dan's same-morning message naming what the night meant to him: *"so much relief that we didn't drop the ball last night. i was always worried something would happen and i wouldn't hear her and she would lay there suffering."* This time even Suz said *"we don't have a choice."*

## The exclusion

While Fran was in the nursing home deteriorating, the estate's other machinery moved. On **March 29, 2018** — mid-collapse, three days before the final admission — Dan was served an eviction notice terminating his residency at [[wiki/places/155-virginia-ave|155 Virginia Ave]] and demanding the keys (IMG_4647.jpg). The attribution: **"Dian V. Moore."** His grandmother. The estate moving on the property before Fran was even gone.

Then, on **April 3** — the day before Fran died — a letter arrived. Dan's messages that evening record it being read, forwarded, and lawyered in real time: Suz at 17:49, *"I'm going to need that letter"*; Dan at 18:01, *"Annie is really upset about that letter"*; Annie at 18:53, *"We will need a lawyer for Dian too."* The letter set rules for Dan's presence in Fran's house, demanded the keys, and closed with the line that named its stakes: *"any violation of the rules will be considered trespassing."*

**Annie never received a letter.** Dan's own message that evening says it in as many words: *"lol also if they are writing such professional correspondence why wouldn't **Annie get her own letter**"* — she was upset about *that* letter, the one addressed to him. The earlier version of this story, carried from a 2026 capture, had said Dan and Annie *each* received a letter and that it had been sent *"well before"* the final admission. The contemporaneous messages correct both counts: one letter, to him, being read on the terminal day. The error had a consequence worth naming — it doubled the apparent scope of the exclusion and made it look like a decision about the household, when the document was addressed to one person.

**Dan's read of the letter, that same night, was that Dave wrote it.** His message at 21:32: *"i think **dave** wrote it — read the last paragraph. 'any violation of the rules will be considered trespassing' ....so if i violated the rules by not leaving the key i would be trespassing?"* Dave Moore — Diane's second husband, the man who on April 1 was *"speaking on her behalf"* to the hospital's social workers while Diane herself *"hasn't been able to be reached by anyone."* That is a first-person read of the prose, not proof of authorship, and it is the only evidence the corpus offers on the question: it came out under Diane's name and, on the reading of the man who held it, in her husband's voice.

The operative consequence was concrete. When Fred Adams mandated that someone be with Fran twenty-four hours a day in the pediatric wing, shifts were assigned — and the two people who had kept Fran at home for three years were given none. Dan's own note on this is characteristically flat: *"I wasn't really complaining."* They went anyway, on their own, off the roster. They were the two people in the room when she died.

Dan's objection to the letter was never that it inconvenienced him. It was, in his words, that *"nothing would have made Fran more upset and furious than this."* That sentence is the whole of the exclusion dispute as Dan understood it: not a fight about access, but a fight about what Fran would have wanted — with the people who had spent a year honoring her directive being formally excluded from the building where her daughter was managing her assets.

And Diane herself, during the vigil? She was calling Suz, irate, demanding her mother be released and sent home with 24-hour care. Suz's fullest account, March 14: *"Dian...no 'e' is irate and irrational and thinks she's going to demand that her mother be released and sent home with 24 hr care and that's that. The problem is, it isn't that simple. Even if she could go home right now, someone, not Vicki, has to be taking care of all of the things it takes to have her at home. **My mother doesn't know what she's talking about and she needs to shut the fuck up.**"* Six days later Suz reported having *"put a muzzle on Dian,"* and Dan's reply is the most politically alert thing anyone says during the whole vigil — a warning to his own mother not to let a temporary alignment become a bloc: *"probably doesn't need to be said but just be careful that you don't foster the idea that there's a new 'diane and suzanne' faction."* He had already logged the same person as *"a useful idiot right now"* and noted, with visible relief, *"at least diane isn't trying to take charge."* The demand Diane was making — send the 97-year-old home with round-the-clock care — is, read cold, the same outcome Dan and Annie had spent the full caregiving year producing. The disagreement was never about the goal. It was about who would do it and who would be permitted near the house while they did.

## The vigil (April 1–4, 2018)

The full four weeks have their own page ([[wiki/timeline/events/fran-death-vigil]]); the terminal four days are the vigil proper, and they belong here because this is where the caregiving year discharges itself.

On April 1 — the first day of caddie season — Dan worked two loops at Nemacolin in the freezing cold, then got the call. Fran was moved to a wing of the Uniontown Hospital pediatric section to die; she and Ira had been major donors, and the room was secured by their donations. Fred Adams mandated that someone be with her twenty-four hours a day. The rotation held it in shifts: Vicki arriving at 7 AM, Marla continuing nights — Dan, April 1: *"you've been doing an amazing job for gram, especially since she's been in the hospital"* — and Dan and Annie taking their turns, off the roster, present anyway.

The hospital cut off her water as comfort care — Dan: *"they cut WATER which i thought was weird"* — and she fought through roughly four days of dehydration, nonresponsive, at times seizing, *"calling for Ira last week"* per Vicki. Suz visited on April 2 and, per Dan's footage, held herself together and made it light-spirited and happy. Dan took his minutes alone with her: to tell her it was all okay, that she could let go and finally rest. By the night of April 2 he could write: *"i have said my goodbyes to her like 3 or 4 times now... i've got all the closure i need with her."*

On day four of the water shutdown, Dan stood in the room blowing enormous clouds from a 110-watt SMOK box mod — his refusal to sanitize the room's reality — while Suz puffed a *"dainty"* 10W vape in the hospital bathroom. Then Dan stepped into the room's own bathroom, took one pull from the sub-ohm device, set off the smoke detector, and triggered an alarm that chain-spread through the entire hospital. Four fire trucks arrived within minutes. A security guard escorted him off the property and wrote up charging documents while Fran lay dying feet away. **The charging documents were real**: a Fayette County summary non-traffic case, docket MJ-14101-NT-0000082-2018, filed **April 5, 2018 — the day after Fran died** — before Magisterial District Judge Michael M. Metros, on a single local-ordinance count of smoking in a hospital. It was printed at 2:51 that afternoon and mailed to 337 Saratoga Drive, arriving during the week of the funeral. The full account is at [[wiki/timeline/events/uniontown-hospital-vape-alarm]].

The wider family, in Dan's telling — *"these fucking shitheads"* — *"did not come to visit her ONE. TIME."* The bedside belonged to the rotation.

## The death: April 4, 2018

Fran died with Dan and Annie present at the moment itself. Dan's testimony is direct: *"fran was happy when she died SPECIFICALLY because annie and i were there with her."* A same-night Facebook message to [[wiki/people/lucie-dobbin|Lucie Dobbin]] — a longtime friend of Fran's from her Florida winters, chosen, in Dan's words, because she *"already knew just how magical that woman was"* — gives the fullest first-person account of the moment on record anywhere in the corpus.

Annie had fallen asleep at the bedside. Dan, alone and awake, silently tried *"communicating with gram"* using only his mind — *"it was a silly idea, and something i never expected to produce a result...but right when i asked her if she could hear me: she woke up."* He woke Annie; the two of them told Fran they loved her; *"she smiled. then we sat there in stunned silence for a couple seconds and she smiled again...and then she was gone."* He recorded the moment on his phone. The hospital pronounced her immediately after — *"you can even hear us talking to the nurse who walked a second later"* — closing a three-day catatonic stretch the doctors had not expected to last six hours.

**He filmed it, reviewed the footage the same night, and took the supernatural part back.** At 23:26 on April 5 — roughly three hours after telling the story to Lucie — he messaged his sister Vanessa: *"upon further review of the video, it looks like a monitor went off alerting us that her pulse was dropping. neither of us remembered it but the video helps correct the timeline. def isn't as 'supernatural' knowing that fact. but doesn't take away from the cool moment we had with gram."*

This is the single most characteristic thing in the record of him, and this page — not a forensics page — holds it as a human fact: inside twenty-four hours of the most emotionally loaded event of his life, holding a story that had visibly moved everyone he told it to, he went to the primary source, found the mundane cue that explained the timing — an alarm neither witness consciously registered — and corrected himself unprompted, to a family member, at no benefit to himself. Both halves belong on the record. The retraction does not delete the moment. His own final clause refuses that, and so does this page.

Three minutes after she died, heavy snow began falling outside Uniontown Hospital (1080p.mov) — an April snowstorm the record keeps as the closing image. His verdict, to a friend two days later: *"it really was about as quick, painless and easy of a death as one could wish for. if anyone deserved it, it was her. she grew up DIRT fucking poor and went on to live an absolute batshit crazy life."*

A memorial celebration followed at the Uniontown Country Club on May 6, 2018, 12–4 PM — deliberately framed, per Dan's invitation to Lucie, as *"a happy occasion and something that gram would have approved of,"* rather than a conventional funeral. The woman who went dirt-poor to coal money in one generation got sent off at the club where she'd won the Women's Golf Crown.

And the four statements of good outcome, dated, inside six days — three of them to people outside the household — are the corpus's single worked example of an ending going right. A sweep of all 106,629 sent messages finds no second instance of Dan reporting a good outcome from something he *did* rather than from someone he *had*:

> **2018-04-01 19:28** — *"i've grown from seeing this from such a close perspective and i got to make sure she had fun during her last days."*
> **2018-04-02 09:15** — *"she spent the last year of her life with people who cared about her and legitimately had fun."*
> **2018-04-02 22:58** — *"i have said my goodbyes to her like 3 or 4 times now… i've got all the closure i need with her."*
> **2018-04-06 17:55** — *"i spent the past 6 months looking after her full time ($15 an hour to play video games with gram is a good deal) and we had a lot of fun. got good closure and finally did something in my life that wasn't completely selfish haha"*

The mechanism, per [[wiki/mind/synthesis/closing-the-set]]: self-set, bounded by a death, finished. The one death he attended produced closure within forty-eight hours, stated four times unprompted; the one long relationship's ending in the same corpus produced none and required an external severance signal after ten years. The contrast is the corpus's cleanest.

## The aftermath

The grief and the calculation ran concurrently, and the page keeps both rather than choosing. In **January 2018**, with Fran alive and declining, Dan wrote to Annie about what comes *"after gram goes and we have a chance to get above water with the inheritance... i want to seriously get out of here."* On **April 4, 2018**, the day she died, he texted an acquaintance: *"inheritance will help with the mourning proce$$."* Two days later: *"now that i'm not saddled here with gram anymore....and that inheritance money coming...ya boy is getting out of PA."* A first cheque arrived **April 27, 2018**. The mourning was not performed — the video, the vigil, the six months, the closure are all real. The calculation was also not performed. They simply ran concurrently, which is a more honest description of how inheritance works than the reverent version. The 2020 court-ordered distribution of **$144,069.31** — reduced by a $10,000 advance to a final $134,069.31, released September 1, 2020 and routed through Suz — would become the maternal source node of [[wiki/mind/synthesis/estate-money-spine]], the largest documented single capital event of the biography and the funding floor of the years that followed. See [[wiki/self/2018-spending-analysis]] for the ledger that sits on top of this loss: 329 Amazon receipts from July 18 to December 29, 2018, $22,931.99, plus his own accounting of roughly $450 a day in cocaine through the era — the spending-impulsivity year beginning three and a half months after her death. The timestamps on the receipts, as he said the night he read them: the money tells you what he wanted, the timestamps tell you when he couldn't sleep.

Diane's side of the aftermath is on the record in one sentence, three weeks after the death: she *"didn't come in to see gram at all before she died, but she went to florida to take over her condo down there and sell it and keep the profits herself."* The Florida condo is the one asset in the whole estate sequence that appears to have moved outside the distribution — and it moved to the one heir who did not attend. In March 2018, mid-collapse, Dan had written Suz *"diane deserves the house in florida for sure"*; Suz's entire reply: *"Fuck Dian."* A year later, setting the sale against her own labor on Fran's remaining estate, Suz: *"fucking Dian, if she wants a cut, she will have to give me a cut of what she got for selling Florida."*

In August 2020, negotiating his estate distribution, Dan asked the estate attorney what the odds were that his grandmother would contest it. The grandmother was Diane; the read came from Suz — *"She isn't going to do that. Rod talks to her"* — and it held: no objection appears anywhere in the corpus through its end in August 2025, and the distribution completed. The last trace of her is a March 28, 2020 message from Dan to a friend, at the opening of the pandemic: *"i hope diane and dave both drown in their own vomit after simultaneously contracting coronavirus."* Twenty-three months after the funeral. Still a pair. Still present tense.

And the Fran-family pole's opposite, the warm end of the maternal line, holds its own date in this record's future: **Eddie and Brenda Cogar's surprise 50th anniversary party — Saturday, November 7, 2026, 3–6 PM at The Silo, 1135 Main Street, Granville, WV** — the same extended family that contains Diane, containing also the people for whom Dan is expected with love rather than excluded by letter. The RSVP deadline is October 31 (to Erica, 304-282-6579). The contrast is the point: the same family tree holds both rooms.

## Why she matters to the architecture

She is the counterweight to the vertical-authority template that governs the rest of the family record ([[wiki/mind/synthesis/vertical-authority-skepticism]]). Structurally the relationship was **lateral**: peer humour, patronage without control, mutual delight, and an explicit trade she named herself — she did anything he asked, so he showed up for her. His own summary, two days after her death: *"gram did aaaaaanything i asked her to, so the least i could do was be there for her."*

She is also the counter-case to the [[wiki/mind/synthesis/november-2005-hinge]] — the Nov 2005 rupture (his father's rehab admission, his mother's affair with the family dealer) that the wiki treats as Dan's life-hinge, the event the whole attachment model is built from. Fran was the constant through it: the generation-skipping attachment, the person who raised him alongside Suz, whose loss eight years later is the most documented grief in the corpus. The hinge's axioms have to absorb her — the one relationship the pattern cannot explain — and this page is the evidence they absorb: a bond that held before the rupture, through the rupture, and out the far side, terminated only by her death.

On the altruism question, the wiki's position is revised and Dan's own words settle it: *"finally did something in my life that wasn't completely selfish."* The caregiving is not counter-evidence to Altruism at the 1st percentile — it is evidence that altruism was never the operative variable. Fran's care was a goal he generated himself and was never assigned, which is the only kind of goal the completion drive can serve ([[wiki/mind/concepts/acquisition-drive]]), and it therefore got the full engine. Six months, held to the end, at the worst of it, under the eye of people who were actively trying to remove him — the corpus's only documented case of the drive working end to end.

And she is half the reason the Annie relationship carried its mythic charge. The deathwatch the two of them kept, three years into their decade, is the emotional high-water mark of the entire record of them as a couple — the one extended episode where Dan and Annie function as a unit under real weight.

## Gaps and limits

The nursing home is still unnamed. The keno morning is dated only to "~late 2017" by inference (a few more months plus the rotation's December 2017 start). Whether the March 29 eviction notice, the April 3 letter, and the visiting-rules letter Dan recalls from 2026 are one document, two, or three remains open — the operator held all of them, and one sentence from him distinguishing them would settle it. What Fran said about Diane on December 30, 2017, was recorded as having happened and not as what it was — the single most valuable missing sentence in the record. The April 27, 2018 cheque is unquantified and its relationship to the 2020 distribution unestablished. And the media ledger remains outstanding: IMG_0569.MOV (the keno-morning lift), IMG_4361.MOV (the last conversation), IMG_4637.mov (Suz's April 2 visit), 1080p.mov (the post-death snow), IMG_4647.jpg (the eviction notice), and the death video itself — the one that produced the retraction, and therefore the only primary document that could settle what actually happened in that room — exist only as uploads inside AI sessions. Locating the originals is the highest-value collection task attached to this page.

This page's limits, stated plainly: it is written from the corpus's message record plus 2026 testimony, not from a neutral observer. The people who wrote the messages that carry it are the people it is about. The vigil's density of documentation is real — four weeks at the center of a family's attention, filmed, messaged in real time — but the year before it is thinner, carried by photographs and a handful of dated lines. Nothing here is inferred beyond what those sources support; where the record is silent, the page says silent rather than inventing.
