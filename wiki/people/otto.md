# Otto Gates

Otto Gates is [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]]'s nephew — the younger child of
[[wiki/people/claire-ulmer|Claire Ulmer-Gates]] and [[wiki/people/garrett|Garrett
Gates]], little brother to [[wiki/people/alice|Alice Gates]], and the *"unnamed
nephew"* that Claire's page had recorded as a gap since it was written. He is
mentioned roughly half as often as his sister, and the record of him is thinner
and younger: diapers in the summer of 2024, a fixed afternoon nap by the spring
of 2025, sent home from school for saying "fuck" by the spring of 2026, and
picked up from school by Annie in September 2026.

If Alice is the child Annie helped raise, Otto is the child the record
watches grow up. The two arcs overlap — same household, same aunt, same years —
but Otto's mentions cluster around the small, physical facts of a toddler
becoming a little boy: haircuts, naps, lunches, meltdowns, a playground request
with a pleading emoji. And where Alice's arc is almost entirely tender, Otto's
carries the one place the childcare visibly damaged the relationship at the
center of this wiki.

One fact of geography governs the whole record. Annie lived in New York
City until February 2025; Alice and Otto grew up in Pennsylvania. Every sighting
before 2025-02-22 is a visit home — a Halloween party, a February school pickup,
a July meltdown held through — not daily life. The intensive childcare of 2025
is what her return made possible: once she is back in Pennsylvania, the
children enter the record at a pace visits never produced.

## The younger sibling

The first record is a birthday party. On **2023-10-29** Annie is going to visit
her grandmother's new apartment and then *"ottos party"* — Claire has cancelled
the children's half of the guest list because *"Otto isn't feeling well. So
it's just family now."* Ten days after Alice's party, late October, family
only: the sibling birthday pair that dates them both.

Through 2024 he is a baby in the record's background. Annie picks him up from
school and buys him shoes on the same February afternoon (2024-02-23). In July
2024, holding Alice through a meltdown: *"She wouldn't let me put her down and
Otto didn't want up lol win win."* And then the most intimate single line in his
file, **2024-07-11**: *"I successfully changed Otto's diaper without him
screaming and crying lol I was terrified bc when Claire does he screams bloody
murder."* He is still in diapers in the summer of 2024, he screams when his
mother changes him, and his aunt is terrified-and-proud of getting through one
change quietly — the childcare at close range.

By March 2025 — Annie back in Pennsylvania — the household runs on his schedule: *"Nap time is at 1:00 until
3:00 for Otto.. while Alice has quiet time"* (2025-03-07) — a toddler's sleep
against a school-age sister's supervised rest. *"Otto sleeping."* *"Otto told
me to go away lol."* The same spring they are handled as a pair: soccer practice
together (2025-03-19), Sweet Frog (2025-05-08), a sleepover with pedicures
*"from aunt Annie"* (2025-05-17), church the next morning at their own request.

2026 is the year he becomes a little boy in the record. Annie cuts his hair in
January (*"I was cutting Otto's hair Dan"*) and again in March (*"MAYBE I am
going to cut Otto's hair again bc he didn't let me finish the last time
lolololol"*). In May he gets sent home from school: *"Well Otto had to leave
school because he said fuck hahahahah"* (2026-05-21) — Annie taking him to
lunch after, *"then I'll cash the check."* In August he has a doctor's
appointment for his asthma in Morgantown (2026-08-02) — the file's only health
detail, and a small one that says he is a real child with a real chart
somewhere. And Annie's own characterization, **2026-07-24**: *"He's a sour
patch kid. First sour then sweet. Just like Otto."*

Three places the held export sharpens the picture. The
March haircut line arrives mid-message about her own grooming — self-tanning,
coloring her hair, then *"MAYBE I am going to cut Otto's hair again"* — the
child folded into her own Sunday routine. On 2026-03-27 the handoff names a
"Greg" — *"I will come over after Greg gets Otto"* — the only pickup in the
log attributed to anyone besides Claire or Annie (speculation: likely
Garrett/Geeg; unestablished). On the evening
of 2026-04-05 (timestamped 2026-04-06 01:08 UTC in the held export — see
Conflicts in the record),
at Claire's with Geeg's sister and her three older boys: *"Anytime we have a
holiday at their house they are there."* The cousins are a standing fixture —
Alice and Otto's generation arrives in a pack.

The through-line is the school pickup. Claire calls and Annie goes — sick in
January 2026 (*"He is sick again 😖 so I am going to be with him today"*),
"being bad" in February, three separate pickups in a single day in May, and then
**2026-09-17**: *"Got a call to please pick up Otto from school lol."* The
recovery memo flagged this Otto as a new unidentified name. It isn't. Same
aunt, same school-pickup pattern, school-age by 2026 exactly as the birth
inference predicts. The nap-schedule toddler of March 2025 is the school pickup
of September 2026.

## The household economy

The household economy complicates the "unpaid" story on Alice's page.
On **2025-10-31**, after watching Otto all morning on Halloween: *"My dad is
supposed to pay me for watching Otto."* **Bill Ulmer** — not Claire — pays, or
is supposed to pay, for at least some of the Otto-watching. So the family
childcare economy has at least two tracks: Claire's, which Annie names as
unpaid on the record (*"which of course I wasn't paid for"*), and her father's,
which carries an expectation of payment. *"I am taking Otto to lunch then I'll
cash the check"* (2026-05-21) reads the same way — a check exists, for Otto.
Whether it was ever cashed, and whether Claire paid for either child, the
record does not say.

The October 31 sequence gives the payment line its context: Dan warns at 17:06 — *"alright you must be good for today - i'm
not waiting if this is how you're gonna treat me"* — her apology lands six
minutes later (*"Dan i have been watching otto all morning. I have been busy i
am sorry"*), the economic claim ten minutes after that (*"My dad is supposed
to pay me for watching Otto"*). The payment wasn't why she missed him; it was
the receipt she produced afterward. Whether Bill ever paid is unestablished —
the sequence suggests the claim did the money's work.

That October 31 instance is also the only Otto entry in the 12-instance
family-excuse corpus of 2026-09-22 — filed as a *probable* plan-break. The
corpus's broader finding is that family was far more often a *scheduling
constraint* than a plan-breaker: the standing "have to watch Otto at 3:30ish"
logistics shaped evenings without breaking them. Then the adjacent note: on
**2026-01-19** she jokes, about someone else, *"I'll just tell her I need to
go pick up the kids lol"* — the ready-made excuse was conscious, not
just circumstantial.

## What it cost

On **2026-02-20** Annie takes Otto to Ohiopyle, where
there is no cell service, for five hours, without telling Dan. His reply is one
of the angriest messages in the corpus:

> *"you won't do anything FOR ME AND FOR US? annie you did not take Otto for 5
> fucking hours and lose service thew whole time and not think to tell me and
> then not tell me after you got back either"*

This is the one place the childcare becomes active damage
— a fight about Otto, in February 2026. It is the sharpest evidence that the
two halves of her life — the children and Dan — were pulling against each
other well before August.

The held export corroborates the date under the page's UTC convention — her
message at 2026-02-21 00:04 UTC, his reply at 00:50 UTC: the evening of
February 20 Eastern.

And yet the pattern outlived the relationship anyway. Five weeks after the
supposed terminal end, she is still the one Claire calls.

## Age

Late-October birthday, established by the 2023-10-29 party (confidence: high).
The year is inference from the developmental trail: in diapers in July 2024,
fixed 1:00–3:00 nap in March 2025, sent home from school in May 2026, routine
school pickups by September 2026. That trail fits a **late-October 2021**
birth best — three and a half at the nap-schedule stage, almost five and
kindergarten-age by September 2026 — with late-October **2022** as the live
alternative (confidence: medium on 2021, low-medium overall; no dated birth
record exists). Either way he is two to three years younger than Alice, which
is the relationship the record actually needs.

The limit on the whole inference is who narrates it. In the held export, 27 of
28 word-boundary "otto" hits are inbound from Annie; the one sent-side message
is Dan's angry February 20 reply. Suz's 372,687-row 2013–2026 archive has
zero nephew hits — the single "otto" match is her own 2022-11-11 *"the guy
from Otto brick,"* a property reference. No other voice — not Claire, not
Garrett, not Bill, no school or medical record held here — describes Otto. The
figure on this page is a one-witness reconstruction.

The birth-year inference rests on exactly that one voice. Its anchors — diapers
in July 2024, the fixed nap in March 2025 — are the material the held corpus
does *not* reproduce (see Conflicts in the record): every pre-October-2025
Otto mention rests on the unheld full dump. The evidence that makes 2021 the
best fit is the evidence the repo doesn't hold; confidence stays medium-low
for that reason.

## The Waylon footnote

A third child, Waylon, appears exactly twice — both on **2024-07-11**:
*"Otto Alice and waylon"*, and Dan's reply, *"Waylon Jennings?"* Two mentions
in the corpus is not enough to establish whether Waylon is a third sibling, a
cousin or a friend, so no page is created and the fact is recorded here and on
[[wiki/people/claire-ulmer]]. The Gates surname now resolves
Alice and Otto; Waylon's surname, if he is a sibling, would presumably follow —
but presuming is exactly what the record does not do. The 2026-09-09 check
found zero "Waylon" hits corpus-wide. Answerable by the operator in a word.

## Assessment

This page has one structural finding and everything else is chronicle. The
finding is the two-track childcare economy: the grandfather pays (or is
supposed to pay) for Otto's care; the mother pays for nothing; a check is
named for Otto but never confirmed cashed. That asymmetry is the only place in
the aunt-years record where money is priced at all: one lane paid by the
grandfather, the other by no one.

The developmental trail is the wiki's clock on the aunt-years. Diapers in
summer 2024, a fixed 1:00–3:00 nap in spring 2025, school pickup by autumn
2026 — the arc maps exactly onto Annie's return to Pennsylvania and gives the
intensive childcare a dated shape that the visits of 2023–2024 never produced.

Otto holds three roles without contradiction: the most physically intimate
care on the record (the diaper line), the most readily deployed
scheduling cover (October 31; the January 19 joke), and the one instance where
the childcare became active damage (February 20, Ohiopyle). Same afternoons,
same aunt, same child: the genuineness of the labor is what makes it usable as
cover and what makes its cost legible when it breaks.
