# Claire Ulmer-Gates

Claire Ulmer-Gates is [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie Ulmer]]'s sister. She enters Dan's documented life twice, eleven years apart, and both entries turn out to be load-bearing. The first is a dog. Claire found a starving stray Chihuahua in her neighbourhood, could not keep him because her own dog would not tolerate the newcomer, and routed him to Dan and Annie for a single night's foster. That night the dog chose Dan permanently. [[wiki/people/milo|Milo]] became the most durable presence in the record that followed — and, in July 2026, the channel through which a relationship that had been closed for fifty-two days reopened.

The second entry is slower and larger. Claire is married to [[wiki/people/garrett|Garrett Gates]] and is the mother of [[wiki/people/alice|Alice Gates]] and [[wiki/people/otto|Otto Gates]]. From 2023 onward, and at full intensity after Annie's return to Pennsylvania in February 2025, Annie's weeks organised themselves around Claire's children: haircuts, school runs, dance classes, sick days, bedtimes. It is the largest sustained unpaid commitment anywhere in Annie's documented life, Annie named it as unpaid on the record herself, and it ran without interruption through the collapse of the relationship at the centre of this wiki and past its supposed end. Claire herself rarely speaks in the corpus. She is present the way a household's centre usually is in someone else's messages — as the person who calls, asks, drops off, picks up, and decides — while the narration belongs to her sister.

That indirectness is the first fact to hold about this page. Claire has no confirmed direct message thread with Dan. The held corpus traces her through Milo, through her husband's nine verified mentions, through her parents' threads, and above all through Annie's real-time narration of the childcare years. The portrait that follows is therefore a portrait of a role — sister, mother, household anchor — assembled from the records of the people who orbited it, with the limits of that assembly stated where they bite rather than gathered at the end.

## Before the children: the sister in the record

Claire's earliest documented function in Dan's record is as a conduit. In November 2015, Annie left her partner [[wiki/people/emilio|Emilio]] within days of meeting Dan. On the night of December 2, 2015, the new relationship met its first family crisis, and Annie's own account, in the moment, of why her sister had turned on her was that Emilio had reached the family through Claire: "because fucking Emilio texted her about me" (18:45, 2015-12-02; `wiki/people/emilio`). The objection that carried the evening, on the record, was Dan's reputation rather than Emilio's claim — but the channel matters for this page. Before Claire was the reason for a dog or the mother of the childcare years, she was the sister through whom a displaced partner could still address the family, and the first Ulmer sibling Dan's record shows taking a position on the new relationship at all.

The marriage that organises Claire's adult record is established in Dan's own words, and early. On January 19, 2017, Dan wrote "Hahah idk um garretts wife" — the marriage-establishing line, and the earliest held mention of Garrett (`dat:0628`). On October 3, 2017, again in Dan's words: "garrett works for jim shorkey right" — the Jim Shorkey dealership employment that Alice is still recorded as visiting, as "Garrett's work," in March 2025, an eight-year span for a single employers' name in a corpus that rarely keeps one that long. The one sideways glimpse of the marriage's interior comes from Annie's handle on April 29, 2018: "So Claire said she didn't mind. Then she said that Garrett is being weird about it because it's literally like three of his friends and their girlfrie…" (`dat:0628`) — Claire reporting, Annie relaying, the sentence cut off by the export. It is the closest the held record comes to Claire's own voice on her own household, and it is third-hand and truncated. That is characteristic. The corpus knows what Claire's household did; it almost never hears her describe it.

Claire attended a family funeral with Annie and their mother in April 2017, consistent with an otherwise ordinary, close sibling relationship in the years before the children dominate the record. Her parents are [[wiki/people/bill-ulmer|Bill Ulmer]] and [[wiki/people/ellen-ulmer|Ellen Ulmer]]; Bill's thread, the warmest adult channel Dan had outside his own family, runs from April 2017 to June 2020 and then stops, while Ellen's runs on to 2025 and beyond. Claire's record runs on a different clock entirely. It barely registers until the children do, and then it never stops.

## Milo: the one-night foster

The origin story is Dan's, given directly on July 14, 2026, and preserved on [[wiki/people/milo]]. Milo was found as a stray by Claire — spotted around her neighbourhood by several people, clearly not equipped to survive on his own. He turned out to be the runt of his litter, with only one developed testicle; a vet later told Dan this was likely why his pack had rejected him. Claire's own dog would not tolerate the tiny, starving newcomer in her house, so she and Annie arranged the transfer: Dan and Annie would take him for one night.

Dan was pointedly against adopting another dog and made Annie write "we are not getting a new dog" on her own hand in Sharpie before they went. That night, while Annie made the dog scrambled eggs, Milo walked over and sat on Dan's lap — the origin point of an attachment that stayed exclusive and intense ever since. Per Dan, Milo trusts no one else the way he trusts him. Surgery records place the dog in Dan's life by at least September 2018, which dates the rescue to the Uniontown-era relationship, before the household's move to New York; he made that move with them and remained a fixture of the New York apartment for years.

It would be possible to file this as a charming footnote — the sister who found the dog — and the record forbids it. When the relationship physically split in early 2025, the dogs split with the household: Milo stayed with Dan, while Annie kept Betty. After the [[wiki/timeline/events/group-chat-closure|June 1, 2026 closure]], contact stopped for fifty-two days, the longest severance in a relationship holding 127 declared exits. Three approaches from Annie in June went unanswered. What ended the silence was an email she sent over the July 4 weekend asking whether Milo had been frightened by the fireworks; Dan answered on July 23, and the relationship reopened. Across the eight days that followed, every transition from hostility back to warmth ran through the dog. On the night of August 18, 2026, with her phone compromised, Annie attempted to authenticate herself to Dan by naming the two dogs: "Betty. Milo. Whatever sos words." The stray Claire could not keep became, in sequence, a pet, a residual channel, a carrier of a reopened relationship, and a password. No other act by any Ulmer sibling carries that much of the record's weight, and Claire performed it, so far as the corpus shows, without ever learning what it had set in motion. Whether Dan has any independent relationship with Claire beyond the Milo story and shared family events is, on this record, unestablished — the Milo transfer itself appears to have run through Annie.

## The children

Claire's children are [[wiki/people/alice|Alice Gates]], the elder, and [[wiki/people/otto|Otto Gates]], the younger. A third name, Waylon, appears exactly twice in the larger record, both times on July 11, 2024 — "Otto Alice and waylon," and Dan's reply, "Waylon Jennings?" — too thin to establish whether he is a third sibling, a cousin, or a friend; he is left without a page rather than guessed at, and the held corpus returns zero hits for the name at all (see Conflicts in the record).

Alice's birth is inferred as October 2019, from Annie's June 19, 2025 line — "I missed the first four years of Alice's fucking life," a reference to the New York years — together with a birthday party on October 19, 2025, ten days before Otto's late-October birthday, and a new school year beginning in August 2026 that fits a fall-2019 birth starting first grade. Otto is younger: in diapers in the summer of 2024, on a fixed 1:00–3:00 nap in March 2025, sent home from school for saying "fuck" in May 2026, and picked up from school by Annie as late as September 17, 2026 — "Got a call to please pick up Otto from school lol." The sibling birthday pair, ten days apart in late October, dates them both as a set.

Claire's household geography governs the shape of the whole record. Annie lived in New York City until February 2025; Alice and Otto grew up in Pennsylvania. Every sighting before February 22, 2025 is therefore a visit home — a Halloween party, a February school pickup, a July meltdown held through — not daily life. The intensive childcare of 2025 is what the return made possible, and the velocity of the children's mentions after it is the proof: once Annie is back in Pennsylvania, they enter the record at a pace visits never produced. Claire, throughout, is in Pennsylvania — the fixed point the visits travelled to and the return travelled back to.

## The childcare years, 2023–2026

The record opens at a kids' Halloween party on October 28, 2023: "I am at a kids Halloween party with Claire and Alice. I got Alice an Elsa outfit. She LOVES it." The next day Annie reports kid overload — up since nine, Alice woke her, trick-or-treating in the rain, "I'm literally ready for bed." Otto's first record is the next day, October 29: a birthday party Claire had cut back to family only because "Otto isn't feeling well. So it's just family now." The template for everything after is already present in that first weekend: Claire's household sets the occasion, Annie absorbs the children into her days, and Annie narrates it to Dan, usually tired, usually fond.

Through 2024 the texture fills in around Claire's requests and Claire's schedule. Annie gets Alice early from school (February 23, 2024), takes her to an art show, photographs her chanting at a CVS receipt. The same February afternoon she picks Otto up from school and buys him shoes. In May 2024, when Garrett's father died, Dan asked whether Claire would explain it to Alice; Annie answered, "Well Alice handled the other death well" — a line that records both a five-year-old's history with bereavement and Claire cast, by her sister, as the parent who would do the explaining. In July 2024 Annie naps with Alice, reads to her, holds her through a meltdown — "She wouldn't let me put her down and Otto didn't want up lol win win" — and changes Otto's diaper, recording with terrified pride that she got through one change quietly where, when Claire does it, "he screams bloody murder." That last detail is worth pausing on. It is the record's most intimate glimpse of Claire as a hands-on parent, and it arrives as the baseline against which an aunt measures herself: the mother is the standard, screaming included.

2025 is the intensive year, and its shape is Claire asking and Annie going. The dated log on [[wiki/people/alice]] carries the full sequence; the Claire-centred beats are these. February 23, 2025: Claire drops Otto off while taking Alice to a party — the household's logistics already running through the returned aunt within days of the move. March 6: Annie retrieves her own childhood American Girl dolls and takes Alice to the American Girl event at the Heinz History Center — the aunt handing the niece her own childhood, literally. March 13: lunch taken to Claire and Alice at the school. March 17: the possibility of watching Alice tomorrow held open against a school building still not fixed, with the one place Annie says the quiet part out loud: "if I do end up watching Alice tomorrow I could make some money. Hopefully." March 20: up at 7 a.m. to learn whether she is watching Alice or Claire is staying home. March 31: the bad-day message, treated in full below. April 4: Alice's "medicine" — Miralax, "poop juice" — picked up at Claire's. May 2: "Well we have Alice with us," and Dan's sharpest line on the separation of the two families: "alice wouldn't be so unfamiliar with my name that she literally can't understand what you're saying when you use it." June 14: Annie lies with Alice for over an hour and ends up staying the night because "she will not fall asleep unless I am in bed with her," an apology to Dan folded into the same message. June 16: watching both children at 1:00 while Claire is at a doctor's appointment. June 29: Claire in North Carolina with Alice. July 10: teaching Alice to ride a bike, then the library. August 20: Claire, not feeling well, asks Annie to pick Alice up from school; Annie's father covers until Claire is done around four. October 19: Alice's party. December 11: Claire invites Annie to the Nutcracker at the state theatre with her and Alice — an invitation, notably, rather than a request for labour.

The calls keep coming through 2026, past every rupture the corpus records elsewhere. January 22, 2026: "Once my sister comes to pick up Alice I'll come over." August 24, 2026 — inside the window the wiki once treated as terminal: "Bc I gotta watch Alice. I gotta hurry"; "I gotta cut Alice's hair now." August 25: Annie heading to Claire's "to hear about Alice's first day." April 5, 2026 places Annie at Claire's for food, with extended family present — "it's cute to see Alice and Otto interact with their cousins" — and April 21 has her at Claire's at 3:30 doing Alice's hair for a dance recital that night. Otto's parallel log (on [[wiki/people/otto]]) runs the same pattern a year younger: the 1:00–3:00 nap honoured in March 2025, haircuts in January and March 2026, the May 2026 school expulsion-for-swearing followed by lunch with his aunt, an asthma appointment in Morgantown in August 2026, and the September 17, 2026 pickup call, five weeks after the relationship's supposed end, with Annie still the one Claire calls.

Two features of this record deserve explicit statement. The first is its asymmetry, and Annie named it herself. On March 31, 2025, listing everything that had gone wrong in one day — a mouth still hurting a week on, Dan angry, an unemployment denial letter — she arrives at: "Then the kids came over. And Claire wanted me to cut Alice's hair. (Which of course I wasn't paid for) she even jokingly said 'should we tip annie?' And then laughed." The same message contains the only record of Annie applying for unemployment, dating her income collapse to March 2025. Read with the [[wiki/people/libby]] record — paid caring work for a stranger through 2024 — Annie's 2024–25 economics take a shape: paid care for a stranger, unpaid care for family, nothing else in between. The Otto record complicates the picture in a way that reflects on Claire specifically: on October 31, 2025, after watching Otto all morning, Annie wrote, "My dad is supposed to pay me for watching Otto." Bill — not Claire — pays, or is supposed to pay, for at least some of the Otto-watching, and a check exists to be cashed after the May 2026 lunch. So the family childcare economy has at least two tracks: Claire's, which Annie names as unpaid, and her father's, which carries an expectation of payment. Whether either was ever paid, and whether Claire's track ever paid for either child, the record does not say. The asymmetry as documented is the grandfather paying for the grandson's care while the mother does not pay for either child's — stated here as the record shows it, without a judgment about Claire's means or intentions, neither of which the corpus documents at all.

The second feature is the cost on the other side. On February 20, 2026, Annie took Otto to Ohiopyle, where there is no cell service, for five hours, without telling Dan. His reply is among the angriest messages in the corpus: "you won't do anything FOR ME AND FOR US? annie you did not take Otto for 5 fucking hours and lose service thew whole time and not think to tell me." The childcare is the one place it appears not as tender background but as active damage — the children and Dan pulling against each other well before August 2026 — and it belongs in any honest accounting of what Claire's household, innocently or otherwise, required of her sister. Dan, on the evidence of his own May 2025 line, had never met Alice. The children were the centre of Annie's weeks and strangers to his life, both true at once. Claire's family and Dan's relationship ran in parallel for three years, touching mainly at the points where Annie's exhaustion or absence carried news of one into the other.

Dinner at the parents' house on August 2, 2025 — "Okay… well Claire Garrett and the kids are here for dinner," seventeen days before the August severance — and four post-closure Garrett mentions running from December 2025 to August 2026 (a work Christmas party, Monday late work, a dance pickup, a kids drop-off) show the family graph continuing on its own schedule, indifferent to the rupture at its edge (`dat:0628`). That indifference is itself the finding. Claire's household is the one structure in the Annie record that never reorganises itself around Dan at all.

## Conflicts in the record

**The Wednesday-alibi reading (carried by this page until 2026-08-23; falsified 2026-08-23).** This page previously carried an AI-authored dossier's speculation that Wednesday visits to help with Claire's kids functioned as a recurring alibi pattern during the relationship's suspicious period. Tested against the full 217,573-record dump by day of week, the reading fails: Alice mentions fall on Wednesday at 6.1% against a 15.1% corpus baseline — Wednesday is the least likely day for Alice — and Claire mentions at 9.7%, well below baseline, concentrating instead on Friday and Saturday (100 of 257, 39%). Otto, at 16.1%, sits near baseline. Current standing: rejected, not merely unverified. The test rules out one specific mechanism on one specific axis and says nothing else about the [[wiki/timeline/events/eli-incident|Eli]] period, which is untouched by it. The falsification is preserved here, low on the page, because a wiki that deletes its own dead readings cannot be trusted on its live ones.

**The mention counts behind that test (page figures vs held corpus; flagged 2026-09-09).** The figures above — Alice 66, Otto 31, Waylon 2, Claire 257 — come from the 217,573-record dump the original analysis cited. A full-text pass over the held 192,140-row corpus returns zero hits for each of the three child names and 210 hits for Claire (`dat:0394`). Current standing: unreproducible from held data, not falsified. The difference is consistent with the missing records lying in the unheld slice, and the standing caution applies: counts derived from different slices of the corpus will not match, and re-deriving from the currently held file is the fix, not adjudicating between snapshots. No downstream claim on this page depends on the exact counts; the childcare chronology rests on the dated messages quoted from [[wiki/people/alice]] and [[wiki/people/otto]], not on frequencies.

**Claire's surname and the children's (gap carried until 2026-09-22; resolved by operator testimony).** This page long recorded Claire's surname as unresolved — "presumably Ulmer, unconfirmed; she may be married" — and the children as an "unnamed niece and nephew." The corpus never contained the married name. On September 22, 2026, Dan supplied it: Gates. Claire is Claire Ulmer-Gates; the children are Alice Gates and Otto Gates; Garrett is Garrett Gates. Current standing: resolved by Dan's direct testimony, the one fact about this household the message record never held. Garrett's surname had been the reason four pages in this wiki were filed under single names.

**Garrett's mention count (page claim vs held corpus; reconciled 2026-09-09).** The Garrett page claims ten mentions in eleven years. A scan of the held corpus returns nine, all consistent with the page, spanning January 19, 2017 to August 13, 2026 (`dat:0628`). Four dated specifics from his page — "Claire and Garrett are here" (October 21, 2018), "Garrett's dad died" (around May 9, 2024), the March 17, 2025 weather-delay school run, and the July 5, 2025 "show" — are absent from the held corpus and rest on unheld dumps, as does a tenth mention; a 2021 "my uncle Garrett" message from an unrelated correspondent is a different person and is kept separate. Current standing: the marriage and the Jim Shorkey employment are verified verbatim in Dan's own words; the remaining specifics are page-attributed. Garrett's marriage date, the nature of the 2025 "show," and whether Dan has ever had a direct exchange with him remain open — on this record, all contact appears mediated through Annie's family channel.

**Alice's relation to Otto in early drafts (minor; corrected).** Early versions of the children's pages described the siblings loosely; the current record states Alice as the elder and Otto as the younger, fixed by the diapers/nap/school sequence above and by the late-October birthday pair. No substantive claim turned on the error.

## Assessment

Claire matters to this wiki out of proportion to her word count in it — she may be the clearest example of a person whose importance is structural rather than vocal. She performed the single act, the Milo transfer, from which the record's longest-running channel grew, and she anchors the household that absorbed the largest sustained commitment of Annie's documented life. Both facts are visible only from the side: the dog is documented on Dan's pages, the childcare on Annie's, and Claire herself appears mainly as the grammatical subject of other people's logistics — the one who found, asked, dropped off, and called.

The record also supports a narrower judgment about what her household reveals. For three years, Annie's life had a centre that Dan never entered and apparently never met, that made demands on her time which repeatedly damaged the relationship, that she named as unpaid exactly once and otherwise absorbed, and that continued unchanged through every phase of the relationship's collapse, including after its end. Whether that reads as a family supporting itself in the ordinary way or as an asymmetry Annie should not have carried is a judgment the corpus does not make: it documents Claire's requests and Annie's compliance in detail, and Claire's means, intentions, and side of the arrangement not at all. This page leaves the asymmetry stated and the motive unstated, because that is where the evidence stops. Her age relative to Annie, any direct relationship with Dan beyond the Milo story, and her own account of any of it remain undocumented — the standing gaps of a page built, necessarily, from other people's messages.
