# John Paci Jr

John Paci Jr was the landlord and owner of [[wiki/places/307-e-76th-st|307
E 76th St]] for the entire six-year tenancy Dan and
[[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] held there (February 2019 – February
2025), and the one person outside the household who knew that the
February 2025 eviction was not real. In late January 2025, two or three
weeks after the [[wiki/timeline/events/eli-incident|Eli discovery]], Dan
telephoned him with a partly-false story and asked him to *pretend* to be
filing an eviction if Annie called. Paci agreed. Annie called. Her
parents called separately. He told both of them it was done — and Annie
never found out. He is therefore not, as this page's first version had
him, simply a patient creditor who got stiffed for $10,000; he is a
co-performer in the single most consequential piece of stage management
in the corpus, and the debt he was left holding was run up on purpose
while he was performing it.

## The building and the owner

Public records now corroborate what the corpus always assumed. Per
Augrented's building report (updated ~2025-12-30, crawled 2026-06-21):
*"307 East 76th Street is a 5-story, 20-unit Old Law Tenement building
located in Manhattan's Upper East Side, built in 1910 and **currently
owned by John Paci Jr.**"* PropertyShark confirms the building facts
(Old Law Tenement C4, built 1910, 20 residential units, 11,135 sq ft;
2025/26 tax $153,496, market value $3,002,000) but masks the owner name.
This resolves the article's old UNRESOLVED note: **"John Paci Jr" is his
actual name**, from a public-records source, not just Dan's contact
card.

Augrented's HPD data adds texture consistent with the message record:
recurring heat/hot water complaints 2020–2022, a February 2023
fire-escape safety concern (resolved, no violations), a mid-2023
unsanitary-conditions/pests complaint (investigated, no violations),
bedbug annual reports 2020–2025 all zero, and Housing Maintenance Code
violations 2019–2020 (notice-posting, mostly addressed). A small
walk-up with the usual small-walk-up complaint history — and with Jim
Blanchard handling the exterminator, per the threads.

The operator's own characterization, offered unprompted in a 2026-08-10
capture — "elderly i-talian owner of the building and probably NYC's
sweetest and nicest landlord" [OPERATOR] — is consistent with the
corpus's own record of him. Annie's first description of him, September
2020, calls him "a GREAT guy," and Dan's gloss the same day — "he's the
one who stuck his neck out for us and held the apartment when we found
it" — describes someone who took a chance on them rather than a purely
transactional owner [LOST SOURCE — dox-scan dump]. He ran the building
himself: leasing, exterminator scheduling, rent and debt collection all
went through him directly rather than through a management company, and
building-wide notices went out over his name and signature
("Exterminator will be at 307 on Monday morning. John PACI,"
2024-12-08 15:19 ET, from `+16312588085`) [LOST SOURCE — dox-scan dump].

Jim Blanchard's endorsement is the only third-party character reference
in the corpus, and it comes from the man who worked the building under
him for years. Jim to Suz, 2020-09-17 16:19 ET: "Hey Suzanne,
everything is fine at the apt. Problem solved and just have Dan call
John Paci the landlord force quick chat. I'm in this insane business
for 28 yrs and I have a very, very eclectic resume' and life
experiences as a civilian. **John is one of the most outstanding
people/ landlords I have come across in all these years.** I love Dan
and Anne, they are a great young couple." ("force" is Jim's
speech-to-text for "for a.")

gap and fixes the tenancy's original money in John's own payee name.

and **$11,500** — six months' rent minus the check Dan had already
brought Jim — both as cashier's checks **payable to "John Paci Jr"**,

months' deposit and six months' rent up front. Move-in was set for

## The arrangement, January–February 2025

The plan, as the operator sets it out in the 2026-08-13 decode, had
seven steps; Paci appears in the first two and is load-bearing for all
of them [OPERATOR — decode doc, full text re-archived at
`raw/self/captures/2026-08-13_john-paci-staged-eviction-operator-decode.md`]:

1. Dan called Paci and told him the apartment had to be vacated because
   his father had suffered a TBI and he would need to travel back
   constantly. **The TBI was true; the need to travel constantly was a
   lie.**
2. Dan asked Paci to help by *pretending to be in the process of filing
   an eviction* if he got a call from Annie, on the stated logic that it
   would speed everyone's exit.
3. Dan then told Annie that Paci was evicting them because she had
   vomited in the hallway the night Eli brought her home. (The vomiting
   was true; the causal link to an eviction was invented.)
4. Dan stopped paying new rent for the final two months and banked the
   money as moving capital, knowing the balance would never be paid.

Paci agreed to step 2 and executed it. Per the decode: "not only did
annie call but she had her PARENTS call to see what could be done. He
was so over us that he said it was cooked." Dan's own retrospective
judgment: "thank god i actually called him instead of just winging it
because not only did annie call but she had her PARENTS call to see
what could be done" [LOST SOURCE — dox-scan dump]. One tension to keep
on the record: the decode says Annie's parents called Paci; Dan's
2025-02-06 text to Paci says *"I had to call her parents"* — both can
be true in sequence, but the agency differs, and the record doesn't
settle it.

An important qualification the record forces, which the decode does not
make: **the vacancy itself was real and Paci had money riding on the
date.** On 2025-02-03 20:39 ET he wrote "Give me a call please. Are we
still good for this 8th. I I want to bring my guys in on 9th" —
contractors booked against a February 8 move-out. What was performed
was the *eviction threat*; the departure was genuinely agreed and
genuinely scheduled, and when it slipped he was genuinely annoyed ("Ok
, but that will put my plans on hold. Let me know as soon as
possible," 2025-02-04 20:38 ET). His 2025-02-04 09:53 ET line — "I guess
if I don't hear from you I will have to start the eviction. Does it have
to come to this ?" — is best read as both things at once: a real
landlord's real frustration at a slipping date, written in the register
he had already agreed to write in. That dual character is exactly why it
survived as evidence: it reads perfectly as a genuine threat, which was
the point.

The plan executed completely and failed anyway. Annie refused to stay
in Pennsylvania [OPERATOR], and Dan said so in writing four days before
the move: "We are most likely going to be back here to stay again
before too long. Annie doesn't want to leave" (2025-02-19 20:23 ET)
[LOST SOURCE — dox-scan dump].

## What this predicts

If the decode is right, three things should hold. First, **no message
Dan ever sent Annie contains the word "eviction"** — a search of the
current corpus returns zero "evict" hits in Annie's live 2124702449
thread (31,461 rows, 2023-01 → 2026-09) and, in the gapfill export, only
the three Ally rows quoted above. **Caveat:** the Jan–Feb 2025 window is
missing from every current export (see below), so the claim cannot be

around it. Second, **the money should be visibly short rather than
visibly saved** — the final week shows the electricity cut off (Annie,
2025-02-04 15:57 ET: "Please can you call coned. See what the minimum is
to get it back on") [LOST SOURCE] and no functioning driver's licence
or credit card between them to rent a truck, which is what banked cash
looks like when it is being spent on daily supply rather than
logistics. Third, **Paci's own later messages should show no awareness
of having been used**, and they do not: the March 5 accounting is
businesslike and the July 25 follow-up is mild. The decode would be
falsified by any recovered message in which Paci refers to the eviction
as fictitious, by an Annie-side message showing she knew, or by a rent
receipt dated between 2025-01-05 and the move-out.

## Method note

Current message CSVs store `date_sent` in UTC with non-zero-padded
hours, so lexicographic sorting misorders rows (2025-02-03 20:39 ET
sorts after 2025-02-04 09:53 ET; 2024-08-17 22:27 ET after 2024-08-18
07:47 ET). Every time above is converted to America/New_York (EST
Nov–Mar, EDT Mar–Nov). The article's previously cited ET times were
