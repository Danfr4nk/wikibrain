# Marty Martin

[[wiki/people/index|people]] · [[wiki/work/bfs-foods]]

Marty Martin worked the gas station at BFS Foods alongside Dan in the
spring of 2026 — roughly six months into the job by May, to Dan's
account — and in a two-month tenure that ended in a fight over $50, he
was the coworker who told Dan how the store actually worked.

That is the human shape of the record, and it is worth stating before
the dispute mechanics: when the drawer-shortage demand came down, Marty
was the person inside the building Dan trusted to explain what was
happening. When Marty's wife was sick, Dan covered his shift until 11
on a night he wasn't even scheduled. And after Dan was fired, it was
Marty — through Dan's mother, because that is how the channel ran —
who sent the $13-an-hour job lead. A coworker, in the old sense. The
rest of this page is the documentation.

## The spring of 2026 at BFS Foods

Dan worked the BFS Foods gas station as a cashier in the spring of
2026; the dated log on this page places his tenure across April and
May, roughly two months, ending in the drawer-shortage fight. Marty was
the established hand of the two: about six months into the job by May,
per Dan's account in the bootloader documents. He worked the same gas
station side of the building — the register-and-drawer side, where the
$50 shortage would later be manufactured — rather than the pizza
counter or the back office.

The store's management structure, for context, ran through
[[wiki/people/anita|Anita]], the general manager, and
[[wiki/people/brandon-hill|Brandon Hill]], the assistant manager. The
BFS article treats Anita as the locus of agency in the dispute and
Brandon as its instrument; Marty belonged to neither pole. He was not
management. He was the person who had been there long enough to know
how things worked and who chose to tell the new guy. The record does
not show how they became close — no first meeting, no first
conversation survives. What survives is the role: when Dan needed
someone inside the building to interpret what was happening to him,
that someone was Marty.

## "Make the store whole to keep your job"

Marty's most consequential contribution to the record is a single piece
of framing, and this page states its evidence layer honestly up front:
the exact phrase *"make the store whole to keep your job"* has **zero
hits in the held message corpus**. It comes from the BFS bootloader and
strategy documents — Dan's contemporaneous case files — not from a
message thread this wiki can re-read. It is page-attributed testimony,
not a held quotation. ([`dat:0936`](../../kb/data/0936-marty-martin-bfs-employment-verified-and-unverified.md)
audits the whole page on exactly this axis.)

What the framing says is what matters, and it is corroborated in
substance by everything around it: the store ran an off-books culture
around cash shortages, and the established practice was to quietly make
the store whole — eat the loss yourself — so corporate never heard
about it. Not a one-off shakedown. The way things were done. The BFS
article treats this as the primary evidence for reading the $50 demand
on Dan as structural rather than targeted ([[wiki/work/bfs-foods]]):
the coercion was in the structure, and it ran on people who meant no
harm.

The audit behind the evidence-layer caveat is worth describing, because
it is the reason this page can be this honest. Per
[`dat:0936`](../../kb/data/0936-marty-martin-bfs-employment-verified-and-unverified.md),
the verification was done by exact-phrase and handle-scoped search over
192,140 held records: "make the store whole" searched across all
handles returned zero hits, and the May 24 message was located by its
chat identifier in the Suz thread. That is the whole asymmetry of the
page in one procedure. The held-corpus work is real and reproducible;
the unheld documents are Dan's contemporaneous case files, and they are
treated here as testimony with the seams showing, not as held
quotations. The audit's own summary line is the one this page keeps
repeating because it is true: Marty Martin was a real workplace contact
with real post-termination contact — the page's skeleton is plausible —
and the "make the store whole" quote, the page's central evidentiary
claim for the off-books framing, is exactly the part with no held hit.

The logic has a second half, and the bootloader records it as Marty's
own survival wisdom "from inside the cage": eat the loss to protect
the job. That bargain makes sense exactly as long as there is a job to
protect. The week of the dispute, Dan's hours were cut to nothing. At
that point the question answers itself: *what am I paying to prevent,
then?* The demand arrived as a test of the culture Marty had described,
and Dan's refusal was the moment the bargain stopped being legible to
him.

The BFS article goes further, treating Marty as an intelligence asset:
the coworker whose framing named the off-books culture, whose logic
the page quotes, and whose collapse-point — hours cut to nothing, the
bargain void — is the fulcrum of the structural reading. The coercion
being in the structure rather than in any one person is the claim, and
Marty is the evidence for it.

## The group text

Marty appears as a participant in a group text alongside
[[wiki/people/anita|Anita]], [[wiki/people/brandon-hill|Brandon Hill]],
and an unidentified fifth number in the days before the incident. The
group text is one of only two channels in which Marty appears at all
in the record; the other is the Suz relay.

On May 16 he pushed back on overtime and clocked hours — *"Time is
tight and going over allotted hours and corporate is not liking the
over time"* — the same corporate pressure the off-books culture
existed to keep at bay. (Like the "make the store whole" phrase, this
quotation is page-attributed from the bootloader documents; no held
exact hit.) The complaint is legible as its own small piece of
corroboration: the pressure Marty was naming in the group text is the
same pressure the off-books practice was built to deflect. Make the
store whole so corporate never hears; keep hours tight so corporate
doesn't ask questions. Two faces of the same avoidance system, voiced
by the same person in the same week.

The fifth number has never been identified. And the bootloader treats
a check-in with Marty on the group text as the practical way to tell
whether Dan has been quietly removed from it — a detail that reads
differently in hindsight than it did in May. Marty was the person
whose presence on the thread was assumed stable; whether Dan's own
presence was stable was something to be tested against Marty's. In
other words, even the dispute's own case files treated Marty as the
constant: the fixed point in the store's social graph, the one whose
continued presence could be used to measure whether Dan's was slipping.
That is an odd and telling position for a coworker to occupy in
someone else's legal strategy document — not a witness being
cultivated, not a name to keep at arm's length, but a reference point.

## May 19: a name in someone else's pressure

He is invoked, but not present, the day after the $50 demand starts.
On **May 19** Brandon tells Dan *"and I heard you owe Marty money"*
while pressing the debt claim — held in the corpus, verbatim, inbound
to Dan, at 2026-05-19 03:44:43 UTC. Marty is a name being used in
someone else's pressure conversation, not a participant in it. This is
the only held-corpus utterance that names Marty at all, and it does
not come from him or to him. It is a rumor of a debt, deployed as
leverage, arriving in the middle of a separate debt claim.

Dan, correcting the record later, said the back-room exchange that day
ran: clarifying questions (*"was it the gas thing?"*), irritation at
an unrelated dig about owing Marty $20, and leaving. He did not refuse
to pay it — a correction to the page's earlier telling that matters
for what the dispute was. The $20 dig was unrelated to the $50 demand,
and it was not the substance of the day; it was background noise in a
pressure conversation. Whether any money was actually owed to Marty,
and for what, is undocumented — the record holds the invocation, not
the substance.

He was not present for the May 21 sidewalk recording where
[[wiki/people/maddox|Maddox]] gave the fuller account of the period's
blame-pivot pattern. The pattern of Marty's placement in the dispute
record is consistent throughout: named by others, trusted by Dan,
never himself a party to a confrontation.

## May 23: the shift

The most human entry in the record, and the one Dan himself kept
returning to.

On May 23 Marty's wife was sick, and he asked Dan to cover — stay
until 11. Dan did it, on a day he wasn't even scheduled. Dan's account
of it, written May 24 at 06:30:49 UTC, is the evidence he offered that
he *"didn't walk out and didn't quit"* the job: when the store needed
someone, he showed up; when he left, it was not a walkout.

The message sits in the **Suz thread** — family-thread testimony,
addressing Anita inside the quoted text — not in a partner thread. The
page previously attributed it to a message to Annie, and the
correction is recorded in Conflicts below; the substance stands
regardless of the thread. In the middle of a dispute about whether Dan
was the kind of employee who walked out, he reached for the night he
stayed late for a coworker's sick wife as the counterexample. The
counterexample was offered to his mother, with Anita named inside it
as the audience who needed to hear it.

The shift cover is also the clearest window into the relationship
itself. The "trusted source" framing has no documented origin — no
first meeting, no first conversation — but on May 23 the trust was
operational: Marty asked Dan to hold the store until 11, and Dan did.
That is what the trust looked like from the outside. The record never
shows what it looked like from Marty's side.

## The Suz relay channel

Nearly all of Marty's contact with Dan after the incident runs through
Dan's mother, [[wiki/people/suzanne-frank|Suz]], rather than direct —
and the relays are the warmest part of the file. Everything
Suz-sourced on this page is hearsay testimony: Suz reporting what
Marty said or asked, not Marty's own voice, and the page keeps that
layer visible.

On **May 23**, the same day as the shift cover, Suz asks Dan what
Marty called the food he'd sent home — *"chili"* or *"Goulash"* (held
2026-05-23 20:16:37 UTC). A coworker sending food home with Dan's
mother. The detail is small and domestic and it matters precisely
because of that: the relationship between Marty and Dan's family had
reached the point where food moved through it, and where the name of
the dish was a question worth relaying.

On **June 7** she passes on a message from Marty about a job lead
paying **$13+/hour** (held 2026-06-07 20:46:49 UTC) — adding,
unprompted, that Anita had hired a friend who then failed to show up
for her first shift. The man who explained the store's off-books
culture to Dan spent his post-firing contact trying to get Dan hired
somewhere else. The June 7 relay is the last documented contact
between Marty and the Frank side of the record.

The juxtaposition inside that one relay is worth sitting with. On the
same relay, Suz reports two hiring facts: Marty passing Dan a job lead
at $13-plus an hour, and Anita's own new hire failing to show up for
her first shift. The store that fired Dan over $50 could not keep its
own replacement for a single shift — a fact the record holds through
Suz, not through Dan's editorializing. And Marty, the coworker, was the
one trying to place the fired man somewhere else. The relational
geometry of the whole dispute is in that message: the person with
power couldn't staff her store; the person with no power was doing the
work of getting Dan employed.

There is no primary Facebook or standalone iMessage thread between
Marty and Dan in the held corpus. The group text and Suz are the whole
of the channel. That absence is itself information about the
relationship's shape: it ran through the building and through the
family, not through a direct line.

## August and after

On **August 11** the BFS article notes a rehire offer to Dan —
unconfirmed. It sits in the dated log with that caveat and no further
detail; whether it reached Dan, what terms it carried, and whether
Marty had any hand in it are all undocumented. The Marty record ends,
for now, with the June 7 job lead.

## Assessment

Marty Martin matters to the record in two ways, and they pull in
opposite directions — which is what makes him worth a long page.

The first is the trust. In a dispute where the general manager was the
locus of agency and the assistant manager was its instrument, Marty
was the one person inside the building Dan treated as a source of
truth about the building. The BFS article's structural reading of the
$50 demand — off-books culture, not targeted shakedown — stands on
Marty's framing. Without him, the demand looks like a personal
extortion; with him, it looks like the store's ordinary machinery
applied to the wrong person at the wrong time. That is a large
evidentiary load for one coworker to carry, and the page has always
treated him as carrying it.

The second is the asymmetry, and it runs the other way: the
load-bearing claims about Marty are exactly the claims the held corpus
cannot verify. Four messages, a few sentences, all held — and the
phrase the whole structural reading hangs on is page-attributed from
documents no reader of the corpus can re-open. This page does not
resolve that tension by picking a side. It states the testimony, shows
the seams, and lets the asymmetry be the article's central fact about
itself. If Marty's own account ever enters the record — the first
conversation, the debt substance, the current standing — the page
supersedes with a dated note.

There is a third thing, quieter than either, and it is the reason the
page opens the way it does. The dispute record is a record of
pressure: demands, pivots, blame, backward narratives. In the middle
of it sits a coworker who explained the machinery to the new guy,
asked him to cover a shift when his wife was sick, sent food home with
his mother, and passed him a job lead after he was fired. None of that
is disputed testimony. None of it is load-bearing for the structural
reading. It is simply the warmest part of the file, and it is held.
The page's assessment of Marty Martin, insofar as this record supports
one, is that he was a coworker in the old sense — and that the dispute
would be a thinner, colder document without him in it.

## What the record does not show

- **How they became close.** The page's "trusted source" framing has no
  documented origin — no first meeting, no first conversation. A
  coworker became the person Dan trusted inside the store, and the
  record does not show how.
- **Whether Dan followed up on the job lead.** The $13+/hour relay is
  the last documented contact.
- **The "owe Marty money" substance.** Brandon invoked a debt to Marty
  as pressure; Dan was irritated by a $20 dig. Whether any money was
  actually owed, and for what, is undocumented.
- **The group-text fifth number.** Unidentified.
- **Marty's own account of any of this.** Everything here is Dan-side
  or Suz-relayed. He has not been interviewed for this page.
- **Current status.** Whether Marty still works at BFS, and where the
  relationship stands now, are undocumented.
