# Morley Jay Frank

Every day, according to the one account of him written by someone who knew
him, Morley Frank stopped at the Eat'n Park near his house for a coffee,
looked at the shelves of the chain's famous smiley-face cookies, and asked
the cashier the same thing: *"I'm having a shit day, can't you be fair and
make me a frownie face cookie?"* They laughed. They never made him one. When
he died in December 1998, his wife had boxes of frownie-face cookies made up
by the local Eat'n Park for the funeral party. The people who got the joke,
the relative who wrote it down said, were the ones you wanted there anyway.

He was Dan's paternal grandfather — *"Pap pap"* in the file name Dan gave
the essay — born in Brownsville, Fayette County, on 20 August 1927 to the
immigrant couple [[wiki/people/david-j-frank|David J. Frank]] and
[[wiki/people/sadie-harris|Sadie Harris]], and dead in Hopwood, in the same
county he was born in, on 13 December 1998, when Dan was ten. Dan did not
have long with him. What survives of that time is not an anecdote but a
rule, which Dan was still quoting twenty years later: *"if there's one
lesson morley taught me it was / don't be an asshole, be a sonofabitch."*

This page leads with the person because the record, for once, has one. The
coordinates — censuses, yearbooks, a Seattle directory, a Florida flight
manifest, a grave — follow, complete, below.

## The joke that took years to land

The essay is by [[wiki/people/alex-frank|Alex Frank]], a music and culture
journalist on the paternal side of the family, for whom Morley was *"Uncle
Morley"*. It reached Dan as a forwarded email — *"Fwd: Alex Frank Blog
Piece"* — sent to him by his father, [[wiki/people/rick-frank|Rick]], on
2 June 2016; Dan clipped it to Evernote under the header *"SAVE!!!!"* and
still had the file nine years later, when he uploaded it to ChatGPT as
*"Pap pap frown cookies.pdf"* on 22 July 2025 ([[wiki/people/alex-frank]],
which reproduces the essay in full; `raw/chatgpt/dfrank88-2025-08-05/dfrank88 5 AUG 2025/chat.html`,
attachment on the conversation *"Family Mythos Unleashed"*).

What the essay says about him, in its own words:

> *"He was a real pisser, you know? You might have an Uncle Morley in your
> family, but this guy made the mold. His humor was a mix of
> self-deprecation, sexual harassment, and insult, but his endearing, crooked
> half-smile (my Grandpa had the exact same grin) kept the smart,
> well-humored ones from being too hurt by his jokes. I was too young to
> quite get it, but I did get a sense of no-bullshit from him that children
> always respond to."*

And about the funeral:

> *"When Uncle Morley died, his wife Sis had boxes of frownie face cookies
> made up from the local Eat'n Park for his funeral party. When we all
> walked in and saw the inverted symbol strewn amongst the more generic
> catered items, it expressed a certain kind of familial understanding that
> comes from generations of genetic personality traits, like a fucked up
> sense of humor and melancholy lense on life. The more earnest funeral goers
> didn't get the joke, I suspect. But the smart, well-humored ones did. And
> they're the only ones you want at the party anyway."*

The structure of the joke is the thing worth seeing. Morley's line was a
bit — the same request, every day, to the same kind of counter, with the
refusal built in. It was self-deprecating in form and a small complaint about
the world in substance: the cookies are always smiling and he was having a
shit day. It only paid off once he could no longer deliver it, when his wife
finished it for him, in public, at the one gathering where it would sort the
room. The essay's final sentence — the funeral as a test that separates the
people worth having from the rest — is the part Dan kept.

## What Dan took from him

The record holds three dated moments in which Dan reaches for Morley, and in
all three he is doing the same thing: explaining that his abrasiveness is
inherited and that it is a form of regard.

**28 April 2018.** Dan was caddying at Nemacolin and in a small standoff
with his boss there, who had told him to *"keep it professional"*. His
fellow caddy [[wiki/people/jack-rusko|Jack Rusko]] advised him to drop it —
*"I would let it go"*. Dan, 19:13 EDT:
*"you're right but i've got a lot of morley in me"*, then *"if there's one
lesson morley taught me it was / don't be an asshole, be a sonofabitch"*,
then *"also 'you only break balls with the people you care about'"*
(`raw/imessage/messages-part1-2011-2019.csv`, 2018-04-28 23:13:21–23:15:17
UTC; [`dat:1115`](../../kb/data/1115-morley-frank-residence-record-and-personal-contact.md)).
These are the only two messages in the entire 198,354-row held message
record that contain the name *morley* — searched for this revision across
all three CSVs. The distinction he draws is precise: an asshole is cruel to
people; a sonofabitch is difficult on purpose, as a style, and — by the
third line — only with people he likes. He took Jack's advice (*"trust me i
will / like my job too much"*). The context around it matters: four days
earlier he had learned of a $50,000 distribution from his great-grandmother
[[wiki/people/fran-coldren|Fran]]'s estate, and in the same exchange he
asked Jack to keep the inheritance between them, as *"one of the few
examples of people who didn't just fuck off with their family money."* The
Morley line comes in the middle of a conversation about what family leaves
you.

**22 July 2025.** Dan opened a ChatGPT conversation with *"GPT we're gonna
hit a TOTALLY new area of interest here. Let me share something with you
about my family:"* and attached the essay. When the model read *"Uncle
Morley"* literally, Dan corrected it within half a minute — *"well morley
isn't uncle. morley is grandfather. the father of.....MY FATHER"* — and six
minutes later gave the family in three sentences: *"My great grandfather
started that store in the early 20th century. my grandfather was a real
character and a rare breed. my dad is absolutely insufferable and all of the
bad qualities i have are from him."* (`chat.html`, messages created
2025-07-23 02:45:37 and 02:51:49 UTC). *A real character and a rare breed* is
the only adjective Dan ever puts on Morley in writing. The conversation then
turns to Rick and belongs to [[wiki/people/rick-frank]].

**3 September 2025.** In a late-evening exchange with
[[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]], directly after telling her *"i never joke
around with the people i don't like or am ambivelant about"*, Dan wrote:
*"okay now THIS is a byproduct of growing up in a jewish family / no joke /
eat n' park delivered frownie cookies to my granfather's funeral i swear to
god lmao"*. She: *"Lmfao / Best story I ever heard I'm so sorry RIP"*. He
supplied the mechanism — *"they did it because he went there every day and
used the same line"* — and the year: *"also it was 1998 lol / different
world / fuck im old."* Minutes later she was still ranking things against it:
*"Mmm idk the frownie cookie is a close one ☝🏼"*
(`raw/imessage/messages-part2-2019-2026.csv`, 2025-09-03 23:18–23:36 UTC).

Seven years apart, the two message-record uses say the same thing in two
phrasings: *"you only break balls with the people you care about"* (2018)
and *"i never joke around with the people i don't like"* (2025). The first
is attributed to Morley. The second is Dan's own, and he produces the
Morley story immediately after it as the evidence. The rule has been
absorbed; the grandfather is its citation. The frownie story is also the
one story Dan tells about his paternal family that is warm all the way
through — in the same ChatGPT message that calls Morley *"a rare breed"*,
Rick is *"absolutely insufferable"*.

Two things the record does not show. It does not show a single first-hand
memory of Morley in Dan's own words — a place, a visit, a thing he did with
him — only the lesson and the story. And it does not show what Dan, at nine
or ten, understood of the funeral; his retellings are of the essay's
version, not his own.

## The life, as far as it can be dated

**Brownsville, 1927–1945.** Morley was born on 20 August 1927 and appears
in Brownsville in the 1930 census as a son in his parents' household,
single, and again in the 1940 census (enumerated 1 April 1940), which also
places the household in Brownsville in 1935. He is in the Brownsville High
School yearbooks for 1943, 1944 and 1945 — twelve yearbook citations in the
tree across the school's two name forms — and a 1945 Brownsville record
lists him for the first time as head of his own entry rather than as a son
(the GEDCOM extract, below). His parents had come to Brownsville from New
York City around 1920, after David J. Frank's Manhattan and Bronx years
([[wiki/people/david-j-frank]]); Morley was born into the first generation of
the family to be from Fayette County rather than to have arrived there.

**The Army, 1945.** The tree records a military event on **24 October
1945** at Pittsburgh — two months after his eighteenth birthday and seven
weeks after the Japanese surrender — alongside a Selective Service System
record held at the National Archives in St. Louis and an entry from the
Army Surgeon General's hospital admission card files. What branch, how
long, where, and what the hospital admission was for, the extract does not
carry. The record does not show Dan or anyone else in the message corpus
ever mentioning Morley's service
([[wiki/interests/favorites/books/topics/war]] reaches the same negative).

**Seattle, 1957.** The next dated record is twelve years later and three
thousand miles west: a 1957 Seattle, Washington entry. The held GEDCOM
extract shows something the prior page could not: **Gay Gillingham is at
the same Seattle entry in the same year**, and the two records carry
consecutive Ancestry record identifiers in one collection — the signature
of one document listing both of them together. Gay was born in 1939 in
North Union Township, Fayette County, the daughter of the Uniontown dentist
Dr. George Dale Gillingham and Frances (Lewellen) Gillingham
([[wiki/self/ancestry]]); her mother died on 7 January 1956
([[wiki/self/lineage/family-tree]]). By 1957 Gay was seventeen or eighteen and
Morley twenty-nine or thirty, and they were in Seattle together. Morley's record also
cites the Western States Marriage Record Index — a regional index of
marriages in the western states — but the extract carries only the citation,
not the record, so whether they married in Washington, and when, is not
shown. The *why* of Seattle is not in anything held.

**Back, 1959–1960.** Their first child, **Richard Harrison Frank** —
[[wiki/people/rick-frank|Rick]], Dan's father — was born on **22 May 1959**
(Uniontown, per Rick's page). On **6 February 1960** Morley and Gay both
appear on a passenger manifest of an aircraft arriving at Port Everglades,
Florida — the INS series for flights arriving there, which records arrivals
from outside the United States; again the two entries are consecutive. It is
the only record in the extract of either of them outside Pennsylvania after
Seattle. Where the flight came from is not carried.

**The family, 1959–1971.** The extract gives Morley and Gay four children:
Richard Harrison (1959), David Dale (1961), Gregg S. (1966, died 2015 in
Pittsburgh), and Wendy L. (1971). Rick's sister Wendy appears elsewhere in
the wiki as the aunt whose independent read of the 2005 family rupture
corroborates Dan's ([[wiki/people/rick-frank]]). Morley's record also
carries a **second family link** (`FAMS @F56@`) whose members are not in the
extract: the tree knows of another marriage or partnership for him, and the
held material does not say with whom, or when.

**Fayette County, 1959–1998.** From 1959 on, every dated record puts him in
the county he came from: Uniontown (1984, 1990), Hopwood (1989, 1993, 1996,
1997, 1998) and, from 1996, Champion. A Pittsburgh Post-Gazette item of
30 November 1997 is attached to him; its content is not carried, but it ran
three days after his mother died, and an obituary for her naming him as a
survivor would fit — an inference from the date, not a reading of the item. He died in
Hopwood on 13 December 1998. The Post-Gazette of 15 December 1998 — two days
later — is attached as a source for his death, residence and burial, and is
presumably the obituary; it is not held. He is buried in Hopwood (Find a
Grave memorial 201542137), in the same cemetery as his mother, who had died
a year earlier, on 27 November 1997, and his father, who died in 1960
([[wiki/people/sadie-harris]], [[wiki/people/david-j-frank]]).

## What his record does inside the wiki

**The Fayette rule.** [[wiki/mind/synthesis/fayette-return]] uses Morley as
the one member of the line before Dan who is documented leaving Fayette
County and coming back, and [[wiki/mind/synthesis/ancestral-dialectic]]
carries a Gemini report's claim that his Seattle arc is *"the template"* for
Dan's own Full Sail → New York → Uniontown returns. This page does not
repeat that argument; it supplies two refinements to it from the held
extract. First, the departure was a couple's, not a man's: Gay is in the
same 1957 Seattle record. Second, the return date is softer than stated —
the tree's *"1959, Uniontown"* residence line cites no source, so what
actually dates the return is Rick's 22 May 1959 birth and the 1960 Florida
arrival of two people who then never appear outside Pennsylvania again. The
turnaround is still at most two years. It is dated by a birth, not a
directory.

**Champion and Seven Springs.** The prior page recorded that Morley's
1996–1999 Champion, Pennsylvania listings — Champion being the town at the
Seven Springs resort — overlap the childhood years in which Dan skied there
every weekend, and declined to infer a link. Two things have moved since. The
[[wiki/timeline/events/bald-eagle-cummings]] capture records that the
family *"rented at Seven Springs for years before buying unit K2"*
([`dat:0209`](../../kb/data/0209-bald-eagle-cummings.md)), which turns the
listings into a specific question: that rental, the K2 unit, or a separate
property ([[wiki/places/seven-springs-childhood]]). And the held extract
shows the Champion listing running **1996, 1997, 1998, 1999** — the last
year after his death — and the Hopwood listing likewise running into 1999.
Entries that outlive the person are address-index records, not evidence of
where anyone slept; the Champion line is better read as an address
associated with him, consistent with a family property at the resort, than
as a second residence. The record still does not connect it to Dan.

**Ashkenazi descent.** The genomics pages treat Morley as Dan's one
fully-Jewish grandparent and read Dan's 21.4% Ashkenazi result against the
~25% that implies ([[wiki/self/lineage/23andme-genomics]],
[[wiki/self/lineage/hybrid-analysis]];
[`dat:1359`](../../kb/data/1359-hybrid-analysis-genomic-claims-filed.md)).
Dan's own 2025 framing of the frownie story — *"a byproduct of growing up in
a jewish family"* — is the one place he connects that line to something he
recognizes in himself, and it is humor, not ancestry, that he points to.

**The family business.** Frank's Auto Supermarket was founded in Uniontown
in 1938, when Morley was eleven, and no held source names the founder
([`dat:1407`](../../kb/data/1407-franks-auto-supermarket-family-connection.md);
[`ent:franks-auto-supermarket`](../../kb/entities/franks-auto-supermarket.md)).
Dan's July 2025 line — *"My great grandfather started that store in the
early 20th century"* — is testimony that points at David J. Frank, which is
where the arithmetic already pointed. Whether Morley ran it, worked in it,
or had nothing to do with it is not in the record; Rick runs it in the
wiki's present tense.

## Coverage limits

- **The GEDCOM extract is partial and second-hand.** It is 25 people of 515,
  embedded as text in a ChatGPT conversation (*"𝙈𝙀𝙈𝙊𝙍𝙔 𖤍"*, created
  2025-08-17) captured in the January 2026 Google Takeout. The full tree zip
  the prior page cited is still not held. Every GEDCOM row is a citation to
  a record, not the record; none of the censuses, yearbooks, manifests,
  directories or newspaper items is on disk.
- **Nothing first-hand from Morley exists.** No letter, recording, photo or
  message. The GEDCOM references a primary photo (`OBJE @O120@`) that is not
  in the extract and not in `media/registry.json`.
- **The only personal account is Alex's**, written years later, at one
  remove (*"I was too young to quite get it"*), and published somewhere the
  record does not identify.
- **Dan's contact with him is attested only by Dan's retrospective lines.**
  Dan was ten when Morley died. *"morley taught me"* is consistent with
  childhood contact and with nothing stronger.
- **"Sis" is not identified.** The essay's widow is *"his wife Sis"*. The
  tree's wife is Gay Gillingham. Whether "Sis" is Gay's nickname or the
  second family link's spouse is not shown.
- **Occupation is absent.** No record held says what Morley did for a
  living.
- **1946–1956 is still blank** except for the undated military and marriage
  citations; the 1945 military event and the 1957 Seattle entry are twelve
  years apart with nothing dated between them.

## Appendix — forensic notes

**A1. The 1959 residence is unsourced.** The GEDCOM's *"1959, Uniontown"*
line has no `SOUR` beneath it; every other residence row does. The prior
page's residence table and [[wiki/mind/synthesis/fayette-return]]'s
"Uniontown by 1959" both rest on it. Rick's birth (22 May 1959) carries the
date instead.

**A2. Seattle was two people.** Morley's and Gay's 1957 Seattle rows cite
collection 2469 records 759167987 and 759167988; their Port Everglades rows
cite collection 8842 records 9019934 and 9019935. Adjacent identifiers in
the same collection indicate one document listing both. The prior page's
Seattle gap (*"a brief posting rather than a relocation"*) should now be
read as: a young couple's stay, of unknown length, ending by 1959.

**A3. Two family links.** `FAMS @F56@` and `FAMS @F153@`. F153 is Gay and
the four children. F56 has no members in the extract.

**A4. The kinship chain.** [`dat:0199`](../../kb/data/0199-alex-frank-cousin-and-frownie-cookie.md)
calls Morley *"the shared great-uncle"* of Dan and Alex and Alex Dan's
*"first cousin once removed"*. Morley is Dan's grandfather and, on the
essay's own evidence, Alex's great-uncle; the reckoning is corrected on
[[wiki/people/alex-frank]] (second cousins on the stated chain) and is not
repeated here.

**A5. "Great-grandfather."** [[wiki/interests/favorites/books/topics/war]]
calls Morley Dan's *"great-grandfather"*. He is Dan's grandfather.

**A6. The 1942 draft registration.** [[wiki/timeline/master-timeline]]
