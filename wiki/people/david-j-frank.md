# David J. Frank

A man born in Russia in August 1892 was a child in Manhattan by 1900, and the
census takers found him at his father's address there in 1900, again in 1905,
and again in 1910. In June 1915 he was in the Bronx, still with his father. He
was twenty-two. Somewhere in the next five years — nobody knows when, nobody
knows why — he left the only city his family had lived in for three generations
and went to a coal town in southwestern Pennsylvania, where the 1920 census
found him and where he stayed for the remaining forty years of his life. He
died in Brownsville, Fayette County, on 6 April 1960, four months short of
sixty-eight. He is buried in Hopwood, a village of a few hundred people about
sixteen miles south of where he died.

That is the whole of him as a person in this archive: eleven dated coordinates,
a wife, a son, and a grave. No letter, no photograph, no anecdote, no recorded
word. His great-grandson's wiki runs to hundreds of pages of quoted speech and
timestamped argument, and the man the paternal line's entire geography starts
from is present in it only as a sequence of addresses
([`dat:0355`](../../kb/data/0355-david-j-frank-great-grandfather-nyc-chapter.md)).

What those addresses turn out to say is the reason this page exists at more than
stub length. The founding move of Dan Frank's paternal line was not Russia to
Fayette County. It was **New York City to Fayette County**, made by choice
fifteen years after the immigration had already ended somewhere else — and it is
the identical vector his great-grandson runs twice, in
[[wiki/timeline/periods/uniontown-return-2013-2015|2013]] and again in
[[wiki/timeline/periods/feb-apr-2025-return-and-rupture|February 2025]].

**Row 16 closes a gap this page declared open, and the wiki's own backlog is

to this day. It is not live. A naturalisation record is in the export, on a
sibling page, and has been since at least 2026-06-23. **GAP CLOSED [2026-09-20]** —
though what it closes it also complicates, because see below.

**Rows 14 and 15 are the undated Fayette entries**, which matter only for

family-tree page appears never to have received it. Two transcriptions of the

**Row 17 is the one to throw away, and it is kept visible on purpose.** A
city-directory residence dated 1993–1997 sits in David's record, thirty-three to
thirty-seven years after his death. It is a mis-attached listing — a namesake, or
a surviving-household entry carrying his name — and it is printed here rather
than deleted so that no later reader finds it in the export and treats it as
evidence of anything. The 515-individual tree contains enough automated
source-matching that entries of this kind should be expected. The wiki's own
evidence layer singles this out as *"a clean example of the wiki's own data
hygiene: the page both uses the GEDCOM extraction and warns against trusting it,
naming the specific bad row"*
([`dat:0355`](../../kb/data/0355-david-j-frank-great-grandfather-nyc-chapter.md)).

### Chicago, 1929

The naturalisation entry is a genuine anomaly and nothing in the corpus explains
it. By 1929 David had been recorded in Fayette County for nine years — Brownsville
in 1920, Uniontown directories in 1921, 1923 and 1925 — and he would be recorded
there again in 1930. A naturalisation in Illinois sits inside a residence record
that never leaves Pennsylvania.

Three readings are consistent with what is on disk and the record chooses between
none of them: that he travelled to Chicago for the proceeding; that the entry is
another automated mis-match of the row-17 kind; or that there was an
undocumented Chicago interval inside the 1925–1930 window, which is the only
gap in the Fayette sequence wide enough to hold one. The third is the
interesting one and the least supported. It is flagged here as a checkable
question, not as a finding: US naturalisation petitions are public records
indexed by court and year, and a single lookup would settle it.

What the entry does establish, whichever reading holds, is that his
naturalisation came **at least twenty-nine years after he was first recorded in
the United States** — Manhattan 1900 to Chicago 1929, at age thirty-six or
thirty-seven. The immigration itself remains undated. "Born Russia 1892, in
Manhattan by 1900" is still the whole of the arrival.

## The household he left: Max Frank, and a contradiction nobody had caught

The most consequential thing in the GEDCOM about David J. Frank is not in his own
record. It is in his father's.

[[wiki/self/lineage/family-tree]] gives the Frank patrilineal line as five
generations: Morley (b. 1927) ← **David J. Frank (b. 1892, Russia)** ← **Max
Frank (b. abt 1860, New York; d. 5 Oct 1941, NYC)** ← Henry Frank (b. abt 1815,
Germany; d. 15 Apr 1901, Manhattan) ← Dorothea Christiana Charlotta Francken
(b. 1 Juli 1781, the German month spelling preserved in the export). Max's own
residence record runs to twelve entries, and the page summarises it in four
words: *"Lifelong New Yorker. Never left the city."*

Set the two residence tables side by side for the years they overlap:

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1870 | New York Ward 11 District 15, NY | *(not born)* | — |
| 1880 | New York City, NY | *(not born)* | — |
| 1900 | Manhattan, NY | Manhattan, New York, NY | ✅ |
| 1905 | Manhattan, NY | Manhattan, New York, NY | ✅ |
| 1910 | Manhattan Ward 12, NY | Manhattan Ward 12, New York, NY | ✅ |
| 1915-06-01 | Bronx, NY | Bronx, New York, NY | ✅ |
| 1920 | Bronx Assembly District 4, NY | **Brownsville, Fayette Co., PA** | ❌ — the split |
| 1925-06-01 | New York, NY | *(Uniontown, PA — 1925 directory)* | ❌ |
| 1930 | Manhattan, NY | Brownsville, PA | ❌ |
| 1935 | New York, NY | Brownsville, PA | ❌ |
| 1940-04-01 | New York, NY | Brownsville, PA | ❌ |
| 1941-10-05 | died, NYC | *(Brownsville, PA)* | — |

**Four consecutive enumerations, four exact matches, and then a permanent
split at 1920.** Two readings are available and both are worth stating. The
straightforward one is that David was enumerated in his father's household
through age twenty-two, which is what the rows literally say, and that the
1920 divergence is the moment he moved out of it — the departure from the
family is the same event as the departure from the city. The sceptical one is
that a genealogy service attached the same household census records to both
men, in which case the four matches are one match repeated. Either way the
1920 split is real, because it is the point at which the two records stop
agreeing.

And either way the consequence holds: **David's move to Fayette County breaks a
Frank presence in New York City running from at least 1843.** Henry Frank is
documented in the city from 1843 until his death in 1901 — fifty-eight years —
and [[wiki/self/ancestry]] reads that as placing the family in the pre-Civil War
Ashkenazi immigrant wave rather than the 1880–1920 pogrom-era influx. Max then
runs 1870 to 1941 without a single non-New-York row. From Henry's first
recorded year to Max's death is ninety-eight years of continuous Frank residency
in one city. David ends it, alone, in his twenties, and dies in a coal town
his father never saw.

> **CONTRADICTION [2026-09-20]:** the patrilineal table cannot be right as it
> stands. Max Frank is given as born in New York about 1860 and as a man who
> *"never left the city"* across twelve documented residences — and his son
> David is given as born in **Russia** in 1892. A lifelong New Yorker does not
> have a Russian-born son. At least one of three things is wrong: Max's
> birthplace, David's birthplace, or the parentage link between them. The
> cleanest resolution on the available evidence is that the Frank patrilineal
> line above **conflates two families** — an established German-Jewish New York
> Frank line (Henry → Max) and a separate Russian-Jewish immigrant named David
> J. Frank who shares the surname — which would be an ordinary and very common
> failure mode for automated surname matching, and which the mis-attached 1993–1997
> row already demonstrates this tree is capable of. Nothing on disk settles it.
> **This is the single most load-bearing unchecked claim on the page**, because
> if the Max link fails then the four matching census rows above fail with it,
> the "ninety-eight years in New York" figure is not this family's, and
> [[wiki/self/ancestry]]'s pre-Civil-War-wave reading of the Frank arrival loses
> its basis. The 21.4% Ashkenazi measurement is untouched either way; it comes
> from the genome, not the tree.

The falsifier is cheap and specific: David's 1900 or 1910 Manhattan census
schedule names his parents and their birthplaces in the same row it names his.
One lookup separates "Max's son" from "another Frank in the same ward."

## What the New York years do to the Fayette rule

[[wiki/mind/synthesis/fayette-return]] states a rule over four generations:
*no member of the paternal Frank line, from G2 onward, has both left Fayette
County and ended elsewhere.* David's place in it was originally "the anchor
rather than an instance" — an immigrant arriving is not a person returning.
That reasoning was correct as far as it went and it was built on an incomplete
record: it did not know the arrival had a fifteen-year New York City stop in
the middle of it.

The correction, made on that page on 2026-08-02, does not convert David into an
instance of leaving-and-returning. He had nowhere in Fayette to return *to*.
What it establishes is that the county was **chosen over New York City at the
very first opportunity the family had to choose anything** — and that the
specific migration Dan performs in
[[wiki/timeline/periods/feb-apr-2025-return-and-rupture]], Manhattan to Fayette
County, is the same vector the line was founded on. Dan's returns are recorded
across the timeline as retreats and collapses. The founder's identical move is
not available to be read that way, because he had never lived in Fayette County
before making it.

That finding propagated further than most. [[wiki/mind/synthesis/the-2025-collapse]]
now closes its geography section with it — *"the hinge runs a vector the family
has run for 125 years"* — and [[wiki/mind/synthesis/instrument-is-subject]] uses
the same correction as its worked example of residue beating testimony:
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/ancestral-dialectic]] had the paternal line fleeing the
pogroms *directly into* the Appalachian coal patch, which is the moment its two
incompatible inherited "operating systems" are supposed to collide, and the
census record says the immigration terminated in New York and reached Fayette
fifteen years later *"as a second and separate decision."* Testimony and residue
disagreed about a dated fact and the residue won
([`dat:1161`](../../kb/data/1161-ancestral-dialectic-self-contradicting-frame.md)).

One caution on how much weight that carries. The parsimony argument
`fayette-return` built on David — that the return pattern is documented inside
the paternal line alone, so a two-line psychological dialectic is not needed to
explain it — **did not survive the 2026-08-18 re-check of that page.** Once the
maternal line was assessed and turned out to be Fayette-anchored as well (the
majority of all ninety direct ancestors die within twenty miles of Uniontown),
"the paternal line reproduces the pattern by itself" stayed true and stopped
being distinctive. David's fifteen New York years are unaffected by that
reversal — they are a dated fact about one man, not an argument about two lines —
and they are the part of the case that still stands.

## The burial, and what it does and does not prove

David died in Brownsville and was buried in Hopwood. [[wiki/people/sadie-harris|Sadie]]
died on 27 November 1997, with **no death place recorded anywhere in the export**,
and was buried in Hopwood. [[wiki/people/morley-frank|Morley]] died in Hopwood on
13 December 1998 and was buried there. Three burials spanning thirty-eight years
and two generations, in one village that none of the three was born in and only
one of them died in. That convergence is what sharpened `fayette-return`'s rule
from a county to a cemetery, and it is a stronger form of the finding than the
residence record alone supports.

Two qualifications belong on it, and neither has been stated before.

**"Buried beside him" is not established.** This page's own `connections:` block
has said since creation that Sadie *"is buried beside him in Hopwood."* The
GEDCOM records a burial *place* — a village — for each of them. It does not
record a cemetery, a section, a plot, or an adjacency. Two people buried in the
same small town thirty-seven years apart is what the record says; two people in
adjoining graves is an inference that reads naturally and is not evidenced. The
frontmatter claim is preserved unchanged, because these connection strings are
consumed by tooling elsewhere in the repository, and it is flagged here instead.
A Find A Grave lookup or a single cemetery-office query would settle it.

**The fourth burial is uncorroborated.** [[wiki/self/ancestry]] states that
Sadie's burial in Hopwood is *"the same cemetery as David J. Frank, Morley Jay
Frank, and Fran Coldren."* [[wiki/people/fran-coldren]] — a 76,000-byte page
about the maternal great-grandmother Dan names as the largest influence on his
life, covering her death on 4 April 2018 in Uniontown in considerable detail —
**records no burial place at all.** So the fourth name in that list is supported
by one sentence on one page and contradicted by nobody, which is not the same as
being supported. It matters more than a stray detail, because Fran is *maternal*.
A maternal-line burial in the same Hopwood ground would be evidence that the
terminus is **regional** rather than **lineal** — which is the live open
question `fayette-return` says it cannot currently answer and should stop
implying it can. A checkable fact that would move a standing question has been
sitting in a cross-reference for months.

## The one thing his record does not say, and the candidate for it

The previous version of this page closed with three gaps, and one of them has
since acquired a named suspect from a direction nobody was looking.

**Occupation unrecorded.** The GEDCOM extraction pulled no trade, employer or
industry for David, which leaves the family's economic footing in its first
Fayette decades entirely blank. He arrives in a coal county at twenty-seven in
1920, is in the Uniontown directories for 1921, 1923 and 1925, and is back in
Brownsville for the 1930, 1935 and 1940 censuses. Nothing says what he did.

Meanwhile: **Frank's Auto Supermarket was founded in Uniontown in 1938.** It is
the Frank family business — five stores across western Pennsylvania, run in Dan's
lifetime by his father [[wiki/people/rick-frank|Rick]], eventually acquired by
O'Reilly Automotive, with a *"Thanks for 78 great years"* marquee that puts the
name's retirement around 2016 by arithmetic. The founder is **unidentified in
every held source**. The wiki's own evidence layer does the subtraction: Morley
was born in 1927 and was therefore eleven years old in 1938, so *"the founder is
one generation up, plausibly David J. Frank ... but no held source names the
1938 founder"*
([`dat:1407`](../../kb/data/1407-franks-auto-supermarket-family-connection.md);
[`ent:franks-auto-supermarket`](../../kb/entities/franks-auto-supermarket.md)).

David was forty-five or forty-six in 1938, eighteen years into Fayette County,
with three Uniontown directory listings already behind him, and he is the only
adult male Frank the tree places in the county at that date. The inference is
clean and it is still an inference. It is recorded here because it is the first
candidate answer this gap has ever had, because it would connect the line's
founder to the capital line [[wiki/mind/synthesis/estate-money-spine]] calls the
paternal source — the *"anti-union 'kushy lives' engine"* Dan indicts in his
August 2020 family-thread message — and because the check that would settle it
is a Pennsylvania business-registration or Fayette County fictitious-name filing
from 1938, not a re-reading of anything already on disk.

**Do not cite this as established.** No source in this repository names David
J. Frank in connection with Frank's Auto Supermarket. The arithmetic excludes
Morley; it does not elect David.

## Coverage limits

Stated plainly, because the length of this page is misleading about how much is
actually known.

- **No primary document is held for any claim on this page.** The GEDCOM zip,
  the extracted tree text and the DNA report PDFs are all under
  `raw/self/ancestry/`, which does not exist in this repository. Every date,
  place and source-type above is the prior wiki's transcription of a file
  nobody can currently open. This is *unverified*, not false — a recoverable
  state, and the README's standing instruction applies: take the argument,
  verify the quote.
- **The census rows themselves have never been checked against the census.**
  US federal census schedules for 1900, 1910, 1920, 1930 and 1940 and the New
  York state censuses of 1905 and 1915 are public. A future pass could look up
  David J. Frank, b. 12 August 1892, Russia, directly, and that would promote
  this entire page from derived-from-unheld to independently corroborated
  ([`dat:0355`](../../kb/data/0355-david-j-frank-great-grandfather-nyc-chapter.md)).
  Nobody has run it.
- **The immigration is undated and unevidenced.** No passenger manifest,
  arrival date or port appears anywhere. "Born Russia 1892, in Manhattan by
  1900" brackets an eight-year window and narrows nothing inside it. The 1929
  Chicago naturalisation is the only immigration-adjacent document in the
  record and it postdates the arrival by at least twenty-nine years.
- **The reason for the 1915–1920 move is unknown and unattested.** Work, kin,
  marriage or a business opportunity are all plausible; none is supported.
  Sadie's own first Fayette record is the 1920 census, so whether she preceded
  him, followed him, or arrived with him is also open — the couple's move is
  bracketed only by his June 1915 Bronx row and the 1920 Brownsville schedule,

  ([`dat:0986`](../../kb/data/0986-sadie-harris-residence-record.md)).
- **His occupation is blank for forty Fayette years.** See above; the 1938
  Frank's Auto candidate is arithmetic, not attestation.
- **Nothing personal survives, and that is itself the finding.** No letters, no

- **The parentage is disputed as of this revision.** See the CONTRADICTION

## Appendix — forensic notes

Compact, and kept out of the body on purpose. None of this is about David; all
of it is about the instruments that describe him.

**A1. Fifteen years, or twenty?** Both [[wiki/self/ancestry]] and
[[wiki/self/lineage/family-tree]] state, in the same words, that *"the family
lived in Manhattan for at least 20 years before moving to Pennsylvania."* David's
own documented New York span is 1900 to June 1915 — **fifteen years**, which is
the figure this page and `fayette-return` use. The twenty-year sentence is wrong
under either available reading: too long if it means David, and far too short if
it means the family, since Max's record runs 1870 to 1941 and Henry's from 1843.
Not corrected on those pages; recorded here.

**A2. The European-born table mislabels him by a generation.**
[[wiki/self/ancestry]]'s "European-Born Ancestors" table gives David's line as
*"Paternal GF"* and Sadie's as *"Paternal GM"* — grandfather and grandmother.
They are **great**-grandparents; the grandfather is Morley. The same page's
"Jewish-Line Ancestry" table has it right (*"Great-grandfather (gen 3)"*), so
the page contradicts itself two sections apart. This is cosmetic for a human
reader and not for a parser.

**A3. The count cluster that page's table cannot support.** The same table is
the subject of [`dat:1358`](../../kb/data/1358-family-tree-european-born-count-tension.md):
the body claims sixteen European-born forebears, the heading says fourteen
corrected, the table prints thirteen rows, and two of those thirteen give West
Virginian birthplaces — so the displayed evidence supports eleven. David is one
of the three the page identifies as genuinely European-born **and** on a direct
parental line, which is the part of that cluster that is internally consistent
and the part worth keeping. Do not cite "14" as a measured figure.

**A4. A correction that reached two pages and stopped.** Sadie's death place was
corrected in 2026-08-02: the GEDCOM records only a date, and Hopwood is the
**burial** place. [[wiki/people/sadie-harris]] carries the correction and
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/fayette-return]] carries it. [[wiki/self/ancestry]] still
prints *"Sadie Harris (b. 1900, Austria; d. 1997, Hopwood, PA)"* and
[[wiki/self/lineage/family-tree]]'s immediate-family table still prints
*"Austria – Hopwood, PA"* in a birth-place-to-death-place column. The cascade
was run upward from the entity page to the synthesis and never sideways to the
two source pages. Flagged, not fixed — fixing it is an edit to pages outside
this rewrite's scope.

**A5. Two transcriptions, two different tables.** As noted above,
[[wiki/self/lineage/family-tree]]'s fourteen-row table omits the 1993–1997
Uniontown row that this page has always printed, and includes two undated
"Fayette, PA" rows this page never carried. Neither table is a superset of the
other. Anyone re-deriving from the GEDCOM should expect to reconcile them rather
than pick one.
