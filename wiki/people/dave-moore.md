# Dave Moore

Dave Moore is the second husband of [[wiki/people/diane-moore|Diane]] — Dan's
maternal grandmother, the only child of [[wiki/people/fran-coldren|Fran
Coldren]] — and therefore Dan's step-grandfather by marriage. He is one of
the quietest people the wiki holds: no quoted words, no sent or received
messages in the corpus, nine identifiable mentions across five years. He is
on the wrong side of every one of them.

He matters for one specific reason. In the spring of 2018, while Fran was
dying, Dave Moore was the person the hospital talked to. Diane — the heir,
the daughter, the only person with standing — could not be reached by anyone,
and Dan reported twice on the night of April 1 that her husband was "speaking
on her behalf" to the social workers about the day-to-day decisions. Two
days later, holding the document that demanded the keys to 155 Virginia and
closed with a trespassing clause, Dan's first conclusion was about authorship:
he thought Dave had written it. And in August 2026 the operator answered the
wiki's standing question about the "grandparents who already hated you" —
the pair Dan had feared during the earlier fall, when an eight-hour delay
in calling an ambulance for a fallen 97-year-old was explained by estate
anxiety — with two words: **"Dian and Dave."** The couple Dan feared was the
couple that then served the notice, took the hospital's calls, and sold the
Florida condo.

That is why a man who never speaks gets a page. Dave is the only person in
the family architecture who exercised authority over Fran's death without
holding any relationship to her at all.

## Placement: what the record can and cannot say

The biographical file on Dave Moore is nearly empty, and the emptiness is
itself worth stating precisely. Diane's first marriage was to George Dixon
Shrum Jr., Suz's father; Dave is the second husband, and nothing in the
corpus gives the date of that marriage, its length, or whether it is still
current. No age, no occupation, no location. The GEDCOM record set that
settles Diane's descent from Fran through Emmet Graden Van Voorhis has
nothing on Dave — the diane page's descent table carries him as "not in the
record." His surname is attested in exactly two messages, both Dan's, both
on 2018-04-01: *"dave is 'speaking on her behalf'"* and *"the social workers
are talking to dave moore about the day-to-day stuff."* His first name
appears five years earlier, in 2016, always as half of a pair. He has no
photograph in the corpus, no message history, no quoted speech. Everything
the wiki knows about him is somebody else's sentence — most of it Dan's,
one datum Annie's, one datum Suz's at a remove.

What he is to Suz — her stepfather by her mother's second marriage — is a
standing fact the corpus never once shows in operation. No word between
them is recorded anywhere. The one post-funeral contact between the two
halves of the family is Suz running into both of them together, and even
that arrives at second hand, through Annie's reaction to hearing about it.

## The nine records, in order

The corpus holds nine identifiable mentions of Dave Moore between 2016 and
2020. Each is dated, each is quoted or paraphrased below, and each is labeled
for whose words it is. Nothing is smoothed.

**1. 2016-08-13 — the birthday gift.** *"Diane and Dave went ALL out this
year on a very creative and not the same thing they got last year birthday
gift for gram"* — Dan, sarcastic. This is the first corpus appearance of
the pair and the only record of either of them marking Fran's birthday in
any year. Read with the rest of the record it lands as a joke at his
expense about presence: the one birthday on the books, and it is being
mocked. Testimony: Dan's, in a message.

**2. 2018-03-29 — the eviction notice.** Served mid-vigil, three days before
the final admission, signed **"Dian V. Moore"**: it terminated Dan's
residency and demanded the keys, and it was the instrument whose final
paragraph — *"any violation of the rules will be considered trespassing"*
— turned a rule violation into legal trespass. Dave's name is nowhere on
the document as the corpus records it. What connects him to it is the
next record but one: Dan's read of the prose, made while holding it, that
Dave wrote it. The connection is the recipient's stylistic judgment, not
a signature. Testimony: the notice is a document; the authorship link is
Dan's.

**3. 2018-04-01, 21:32 — the unreachable heir.** *"meanwhile diane moore
hasn't been able to be reached by anyone. dave is 'speaking on her behalf'
and it's very unusual stuff."* The quotation marks around *speaking on her
behalf* are Dan's, and they carry the forensic read he puts on everything
vertical: this is not a husband relaying a message, it is a substitution
he is being asked to accept. The heir is unreachable; the husband is
available; the hospital treats availability as standing. Testimony: Dan's.

**4. 2018-04-01, 21:50 — the social workers.** Eighteen minutes later, the
detail that makes the substitution concrete: *"no comment required. it's
just wild, the social workers are talking to dave moore about the
day-to-day stuff lol."* The day-to-day stuff is end-of-life care in a
pediatric wing — comfort measures, water cutoff as comfort care, who is
permitted in the room — discussed with a man who is not the patient's
relative, while the two people who had kept her out of a nursing home for
three years had been formally excluded from the house. The hospital's
institutional need is legible: someone has to answer. The choice of whom
is the whole story. Testimony: Dan's.

**5. 2018-04-01 — the roasting.** *"i don't know if anyone wants a 45 minute
roasting of diane and dave"* — Dan, on whether to speak at the service.
What matters here is the unit: by the night Fran entered the pediatric
wing, Dan no longer treats them as a person and her husband. The adversary
is the pair, spoken in one breath, one target. Testimony: Dan's.

**6. 2018-04-03 — the day before she died.** *"just glad that diane and
dave aren't super upset, they're probably busy working through the tragedy
of her death and that's why they haven't checked in on her"* — written
while Fran lay dying, about the people who had not visited once. The
sarcasm documents the absence rather than the presence: the heir and her
husband are not in the building. Twenty-four hours later Dan and Annie
would be the two in the room. Testimony: Dan's.

**7. 2018-04-03, 21:32 — the authorship read.** *"i think dave wrote it —
read the last paragraph. 'any violation of the rules will be considered
trespassing' ....so if i violated the rules by not leaving the key i would
be trespassing?"* This is Dan doing stylistic attribution on a legal
instrument in the hour he receives it, and the logic is visible: the
trespassing clause is doing the instrument's real work — it converts a
grandmother's house rules into an evictable offense — and Dan reads that
work as a man's drafting, not his grandmother's voice. The same message
hour also carries the question that locates the letter's scope: *"lol also
if they are writing such professional correspondence why wouldn't Annie get
her own letter"* — one letter, addressed to him. Testimony: Dan's, held
throughout this page as a read of prose style, not a finding of fact.

**8. 2018-08-01 — the encounter.** Annie, four months after the funeral:
*"Wait wtf. Your mom ran into Dian and Dave today?!"* The exclamation is
the measure of how unexpected the contact was. This is the only documented
post-funeral contact between the two halves of the family, and it is
hearsay twice over: Suz's encounter, reported to Annie, reported by
Annie. It establishes only that Suz and the pair were in the same place
on the same day, and that nobody expected it. What Suz said to them, or
they to her, is unrecorded. Testimony: Annie's, reporting Suz's.

**9. 2020-03-28 — the last trace.** *"i hope diane and dave both drown in
their own vomit after simultaneously contracting coronavirus"* — Dan to a
friend, at the opening of the pandemic. Twenty-three months after the
funeral the pair is still present tense, still spoken as one. Nothing in
the corpus after this date mentions Dave again. Whether he is living is
unknown; whether the marriage is current is unknown. Testimony: Dan's.

A note on the count. "Nine records" is the count of identifiable mentions
in the held corpus, 2016 through 2020 — not a claim that the file on Dave
Moore is closed. New captures could add more, and the corpus's own end in
2025 means five years of his life after the last trace are simply blank.
The number is held, not settled.

## The terminal week: how a non-relative takes the hospital's calls

The sequence of records 2 through 7, read in order, is the whole of Dave
Moore's consequential life on the record, and it is worth reconstructing
straight. On March 29 an instrument signed by his wife terminates the
residency of the paid full-time caregiver and demands the keys under a
trespassing clause. On April 1 Fran is moved to the Uniontown Hospital
pediatric wing to die, and Judge Fred Adams — Ira's old law partner,
trustee of the estate accounts — mandates that someone be with her twenty-
four hours a day. The rotation is assigned: Vicki at 7:00 AM, Marla on
nights, Dan and Annie on their turns. But the instrument decides who is
eligible for the rotation, and the two people who had held the house
together for three years are given no shifts. Dan's note on this is four
words: "I wasn't really complaining." They went anyway, off the roster,
and were the two present when she died.

Meanwhile, on the night of April 1, the one person the hospital is supposed
to be talking to — the only child, the heir — cannot be reached by anyone.
So the social workers talk to Dave about the day-to-day stuff. Dan's two
messages that night are eighteen minutes apart, and the compression is the
point: the arrangement was reported, and then the detail that made it
strange was reported, while it was happening. The institution needed an
answerer; Dave was the available husband; availability became standing.
On Dan's contemporaneous account, nothing in the arrangement was ever
routed through Diane herself — the proxy did not report back to a
principal who then decided. The proxy decided.

On April 3 Dan holds the letter and does the attribution. The instrument
came in Diane's name; the voice, on his reading, is Dave's. The trespassing
sentence is the tell for him: *"any violation of the rules will be
considered trespassing"* — the move that converts house rules into an
evictable, chargeable offense — reads to its recipient like a man
drafting, not like his 79-year-old grandmother writing. This page does not
resolve authorship. It records the read, the reasoning behind it, and the
consistency between the read and the social-worker arrangement two days
earlier: the same substitution, the instrument issued in the heir's name
and produced in the husband's voice.

What the week cost is concrete and is recorded on the vigil and diane
pages: the exclusion's operative effect was the roster, and the roster is
why the record shows Dan and Annie attending the death of the woman they
had cared for as unassigned visitors. The institution that excluded them
was not the hospital — it was the family, acting through paperwork, with
Dave as its point of contact on both the institutional side and, on Dan's
read, the drafting side.

## The pair as a single actor

Six of the nine records speak of "Diane and Dave" as one breath, and the
operator's 2026 identification explains why. The wiki had carried the
keno-morning fear — the "grandparents who already hated you" whose
presumed inheritance hostility kept Dan from overriding Suz and calling an
ambulance for eight hours — as Diane and George, the biological
grandfather. The operator's answer, "Dian and Dave," repositions the
whole fear: the couple Dan was afraid of in the fall of 2017 was the
couple that served the notice, took the hospital's calls, and sold the
Florida condo in the spring of 2018. The earlier reading made the fear a
mood — dread of family disapproval, diffused across the grandparent
generation. The corrected reading makes it a forecast: one specific
adversary, correctly identified before it moved, moving exactly as feared.

This is why the pair-as-unit is not a stylistic habit but the page's
central finding about how Dan processes them. The 45-minute roasting is
of both. The sarcasm about them not being super upset is about both. The
coronavirus line drowns both. And the 2018-08-01 encounter is of both —
Suz did not run into Dian; she ran into Dian and Dave, and Annie's shock
is about the pair arriving together, four months after the funeral, in
the only post-death contact on the record.

There is a second, quieter implication. A marriage treated as a single
adversarial actor is also a marriage whose internal division of labor is
invisible to the record — and the record's one glimpse of that division
is the terminal week itself. Diane: unreachable, the name on the
instrument, the heir with standing. Dave: available, the voice on the
phone, the hand on the drafting — on Dan's read — with no standing at
all. The unit faces outward as one; inward, the work splits exactly
along the standing line. The one who had the standing did not act; the
one who acted did not have the standing.

## Authority by proxy: the template's purest instance

[[wiki/mind/synthesis/vertical-authority-skepticism]] names this case as
the family-scale template in its purest form: *"a man with no blood tie to
the dying woman and no role in her care, speaking for the heir to hospital
social workers and drafting the correspondence."* The expansion of that
claim is the reason this page exists at major tier, and it has three
parts.

**First, the purity is comparative.** Diane at least had standing — she
was the daughter and the heir, the one person in the family whose
authority over the ending no one could dispute on paper. Fred Adams had a
trustee's seat, an office with a mandate. Dave had neither blood nor
office nor caregiving history. His authority entered through the one
channel that requires no standing at all: proximity to the principal.
When the principal could not be reached, the proxy became the office —
not by appointment, not by document, but by being the person who picked
up. That is authority by proxy in its cleanest definition: standing
borrowed from adjacency, exercised in the principal's absence, with no
mechanism anywhere in the sequence for the principal to have countermanded
it, because the principal was unreachable.

**Second, the shape is the one Dan's skepticism is built to detect.**
The synthesis page documents the same architecture at every scale: the
workplace drawer dispute (an unverified claim whose missing paper trail
was the point), the Creative License notarized retaliation, the
institutions of the J6 analysis. In each case the move is authority
exercised at a distance, through paperwork and institutional conduits, by
people who are not present and do not do the work. The terminal week is
that move inside a family: the exclusion runs through a letter and a
trespassing clause rather than through anyone telling Dan to his face to
leave; the hospital's decisions run through social workers talking to the
available husband rather than through the heir. Dan's scare quotes around
*"speaking on her behalf"* are the skepticism's forensic read made
visible — the exact punctuation of a man who has spent his life refusing
unverified vertical claims, applied to his grandmother's husband. The
corpus's most ironized vertical-actor phrases all take this form; this
one is aimed at a step-grandfather.

**Third, the proxy structure explains the silence.** Dave Moore is never
quoted because a proxy does not need a public voice — the instrument
carries Diane's name, the hospital's notes would carry Diane's name, and
the man doing the talking is structurally invisible to every record
except the recipient's. The nine mentions are nine sightings of a role
rather than a person: the husband who is there when the heir is not.
That is why the page's biographical section is empty and why the
emptiness is not a gap to be filled but the finding. The authority was
designed — whether by intention or by institutional convenience — to
leave no trace of its actual holder.

## The Suz encounter, and what corroboration would look like

The task for this expansion named the 2018-08-01 encounter as the record
to dig on, and the digging turned up the honesty rather than the
corroboration. The datum exists in exactly one place: Annie's message,
*"Wait wtf. Your mom ran into Dian and Dave today?!"* No staged page in
the current engine pass carries any other version of it — the people
directory was searched, and the encounter appears nowhere else. Suz
herself never messages about it in the held corpus; Dan never follows up
on it; no location, no time of day, no account of what passed between
them.

What the single line does establish is narrow but real: four months after
the funeral, the two halves of the family — Suz on one side, the pair on
the other — were in the same place on the same day, and the person
closest to Dan found that shocking. The shock is itself a datum about the
state of the split: whatever the vigil had broken, it had not mended by
August. What it does not establish is anything about the encounter's
content, its tone, or whether it was even a conversation — "ran into"
covers a wave across a parking lot and a confrontation alike. The page
holds it as Suz-sourced hearsay, reported through Annie, and notes that
it remains the only documented post-funeral contact between the two
sides through the corpus's end.

## Limits and what is not on the record

The limits are load-bearing for a page built almost entirely on one
person's contemporaneous messages, and they belong in the body rather
than as an apologetic tail. The authorship read is Dan's and only Dan's;
the document itself is not in `raw/`, and no second witness to the
drafting exists. The social-worker arrangement is Dan's report of what
he was told or observed on April 1; the hospital's own notes are not in
the corpus. The eviction notice is signed by Diane alone — Dave's part
in it rests entirely on the April 3 read. The Florida condo's sale is
asserted by Dan and Suz with no document, price, or date in the corpus,
and Dave's role in it, if any, is unrecorded beyond the pair-as-unit
framing. Whether Dave attended the May 2018 memorial service, whether he
is alive, whether the marriage continues — none of it is on the record,
and the last mention of him anywhere is Dan's 2020 message. The page
does not fill these in.

## Assessment

The record supports three findings, stated flatly. **First**, Dave Moore
is a standing proxy-actor rather than a bystander: the hospital took
day-to-day direction from him during the terminal week, and the exclusion
instrument reads to its recipient as his drafting. Neither act required
him to hold any relationship to Fran, and neither left a trace in his own
voice — which is the proxy structure working as designed. **Second**, Dan
treats the pair as one adversary, and the 2026 identification of the
feared grandparents shows that read was already correct a season before
the terminal week; the fear was a forecast, and it was right. **Third**,
the synthesis page's claim stands on the evidence: this is the purest
family-scale instance of authority by proxy in the corpus — a man with no
blood tie and no caregiving role speaking for the heir and drafting the
correspondence, with the principal unreachable and no mechanism for
countermand. What the record does not support is any claim about Dave
Moore the person — his motives, his biography, his marriage, his life
after 2020. The page's subject is the role, because the role is all the
record ever shows.
