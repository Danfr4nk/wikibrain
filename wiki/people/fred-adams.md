# Judge Fred Adams

Fred Adams is the institutional node of the Coldren family's money. He was
[[wiki/people/fran-coldren|Fran Coldren's]] third husband Ira Burdette Coldren
Jr.'s law partner — the firm was **Coldren Adams** — and after Ira's death he
became, in Dan's own description, "basically the trustee of her accounts,"
the overseer of everything to do with Fran's money while she was alive and
the executor of the estate afterward. He later became a judge, and the corpus
refers to him from that point as Judge Adams.

The capital he administered has two stages, both hers by marriage: Fran's
second husband, Thomas Whyel, was a coal-and-coke man; Ira Coldren was the
lawyer she married within months of Whyel's death. So the money originates
in a Fayette County coal fortune and was administered through a legal one —
with Adams, Ira's old law partner, as the institutional valve
([[wiki/mind/synthesis/estate-money-spine]]). He is the reason the family's
largest asset never behaved like an inheritance in waiting: for decades it
sat under one man's discretion.

He appears in the record at two moments, and they are the two moments that
matter most in the family's history.

The first is the vigil. When Fran was moved to a private room in the
Uniontown Hospital pediatric wing on April 1, 2018 — a room she got because
she and Ira had been major donors to the hospital — it was Adams who mandated
that someone be with her twenty-four hours a day so that she would not be
alone when she died. That instruction is the reason a formal shift rotation
existed at all ([[wiki/timeline/events/fran-death-vigil]]), and it is
therefore also the reason that Dan and [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]]
being excluded from it — by the family letters described on
[[wiki/people/diane-moore]] — was an exclusion from something rather than
merely an absence of invitation. The mandate is a humane instruction issued
by the person furthest from the bedside, and it is the clearest single
illustration of how the family's authority and the family's presence sat in
different hands.

The second is the money. The Ira-estate capital Adams administered is the
upstream source of the **$144,069.31** court-ordered distribution Dan
received in 2020 — a $10,000 advance in June, the Orphans' Court order signed
August 21 (**not by Adams**; see below), and a final check of $134,069.31
released September 1, routed
through [[wiki/people/suzanne-frank|Suz]]. That arrival is the largest single
capital event in Dan's documented life
([[wiki/mind/synthesis/estate-money-spine]]), and the fact that it took two
and a half years to arrive after Fran's death is a function of the structure
Adams sat at the centre of.

## Who he was: the valve on the coal money

Ira Coldren Jr. is the whole origin of Adams's authority: law partner first,
then trustee of the accounts Ira's estate left Fran. Dan's "basically the
trustee of her accounts" is the operative description; whether he was the
executor in the formal legal sense or the trustee of a separate instrument is
not distinguished anywhere — the two roles are used interchangeably in every
telling.

**He was "Judge Adams" by April 2017, and "Fred Adams" for years after.** The
earliest *judge* usage is **2017-04-25**, in a message to
[[wiki/people/rod-banks|Rod Banks]] about Fran's property — so the
appointment is on or before that date, twenty-two months after the 2016-06-03
message that calls him plain *Fred Adams*. The titles then run in parallel:
Suz writes *"Fred Adams"* on 2018-09-04 and *"judge Adams"* on 2018-11-05.
**The corpus's usage is not a clean before-and-after and should not be used
to date anything on its own.**

## He was already in the family's money disputes while she was alive

Adams was not only the estate's valve after Fran's death. While she was
alive, the family treated his agreement as worth reporting — and looped him
formally on demands against the inheritance.

**2017-08-25 — Diane Moore to Fran (photographed at Fran's house 2017-08-31).**
Diane presses her mother to cooperate in selling the Palm City vacation
house — Fran uses it less each year, the vacant house is expensive to keep —
and wants permission to bring in a realtor to price and list it. The
credential she offers is his: *"I have spoken with Fred about this and he
agrees this would be a smart move"* (dat:1410). The trustee's verbal
agreement is presented as what makes the ask reasonable.

**2017-11-01 — Diane Moore to Fran (photographed at Fran's house 2017-11-06,
cc: Fred Adams).** Ten weeks later the November trip is canceled — Diane is
scheduled for shoulder replacement surgery on November 13, "an intense
operation with 3 months of recovery" — and the ask shifts from asset
disposition to a cash advance: a $20,000 loan against her inheritance,
covering what insurance will not, including in-home nursing and therapy
(dat:1411; the figure is carried by the master timeline — see Conflicts in
the record). This time he is not consulted in advance; he is **formally cc'd
on the request itself**.

Both letters predate the winter-2017–18 paid-caregiver rotation and the April
2018 vigil conflict. The valve was already in use — as a decision-maker and
as an audience — before the estate ever opened.

## The caregiving wage he authorised

For roughly the last six months of her life, Fran employed Dan at **$15 an
hour** to keep her out of a nursing home — a wage authorised through the
estate by Adams himself ([[wiki/meta/journeys/the-supply-line]]). In March
2018 Dan reasons, in his own words, that *"fred would have put the kaibosh
on me being paid to watch gram if there was an ongoing issue"* — which puts
Adams directly in the payroll chain. The trustee's non-objection is the proof
Dan offers that the arrangement was legitimate: the veto power worked in
reverse, as certification. This is the only place in the record where Adams's
authority touches money flowing *to* Dan.

## The two decisions at the end of her life

**The decision that Fran would not go home is dated, and it is his.** Sent
2018-03-14: *"fred adams made the decision yesterday that she won't be going
home"* — **13 March 2018**, eighteen days before the move to the private room
this page opens on. The vigil mandate is the second decision he made about
her last weeks, not the first, and the first is the larger one. On the day of
the move itself: *"they're trying to get fred adams up here, which doesn't
sound super promising"* (2018-04-01). This page argues from narration that
the family's authority and the family's presence sat in different hands;
that sentence is the argument happening in real time, written by somebody in
the room about a man who was not.

## He was dead before the money moved

**Fred Adams died in the third week of May 2020.** Suz's message is sent
2020-05-24 at 07:24 and says she learned of it *"last night"* — 23 May — and
that it had happened *"several days ago"*, which places the death around
**18–21 May 2020**. It is second-hand and it is the only record of it here;
Fayette County records would settle the day.

Set that against this page's own second section:

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| ~2020-05-18 to 05-21 | **Fred Adams dies** |
| June 2020 | $10,000 advance reaches Dan |
| 2020-08-06 | Dan: *"i talked to jasón adams today and am going on saturday morning to sign for the inheritance in advance"* |
| 2020-08-21 | Court signs the distribution order, $144,069.31 |
| 2020-09-01 | Final check, $134,069.31 |

**The man this page describes as the valve on the money was dead two to three
weeks before any of it moved, and three months before the order.**

> **What that does and does not license.** It is a sequence, not a cause. The
> estate had been in process since Fran's death in April 2018 and the corpus
> shows Dan chasing calls to Adams through 2019 with no result; a distribution
> can be signed by an Orphans' Court judge for reasons that have nothing to do
> with who died in May. But this page's claim that *"the fact that it took two*
> *and a half years to arrive after Fran's death is a function of the structure*
> *Adams sat at the centre of"* now has a testable form it did not have — the
> structure lost its centre, and the money moved within weeks of it. **The**
> **corpus cannot settle that**, and settling it means the estate docket, which
> is outside this repository.

> **One phrase on this page must not be read the way it now reads.** The
> second section says *"the judge's order signed August 21"*. That is the
> Orphans' Court order, as [[wiki/mind/synthesis/estate-money-spine]] states it
> — **not Judge Adams**, who could not have signed anything in August 2020. The
> ambiguity was harmless while nobody knew when he died.

## The estate attorney has a name, and it is not his

Three messages establish the office and a second Adams:

- **2018-07-15**, from Suz: *"I told Jason Adams I would get them to him
  Monday"* — invoices.
- **2019-08-01**, sent: *"i just left a message with jason adams"*.
- **2020-08-06**, sent: *"i talked to jasón adams today and am going on
  saturday morning to sign for the inheritance in advance"* — fifteen days
  before the order, and it is **Jason**, not Fred, who handles the signing.
  (The accented *jasón* is why the earlier corpus search missed this row.)

And the firm's later name is in the record too. Suz, 2020-08-24: *"I have not
taken any money for what I've done. I did not receive it directly or through
**Adams & Adams**."* The firm this page knows as **Coldren Adams** — Ira's
partnership, and the name Dan uses in 2018 — is **Adams & Adams** by 2020.
Whether that is a rename, a successor practice or two firms is not established.

**Jason Adams has no page and is owed one.** He is the person Dan actually
transacted the largest capital event of his life with, and until this pass the
wiki did not contain his name.

### The working hand had already moved

The estate's disbursement machinery runs on a two-step chain: **Jason Adams
authorises, Rod Banks writes the checks** (dat:0985). Five advances between
**2019-10-12 and 2020-04-17**, totalling **$32,500** ($5,000 / $10,000 /
$7,000 / $3,000 / $7,500), each one authorised by Jason and written by Banks
— and Dan writes to Banks the way you write to somebody who controls a
budget. The finding is old-wiki testimony from a dox-scan thread the held
corpus does not contain (moderate confidence); the advance schedule itself
was independently resolved from held threads (dat:0051).

**The operational hand on the family's money had already moved to the younger
Adams in the last half-year of Fred's life.** Dan spent 2019 trying to get
Fred on the phone and getting nothing; meanwhile Jason was authorising
five-figure flows. The valve was, by the end, a name on the structure more
than a hand on it.

  held corpus: 2017-04-25, 2019-04-15 (sent and inbound *"Gotta call judge

  the held corpus demonstrably lacks — the held Suz thread has zero rows in
  2016-06 and zero in 2020-05 — so the absence is structural, not suspicious;
  the rows were read 2026-09-04 from the richer export the old wiki
  preserves. The death report (Suz, 2020-05-24) is the most consequential
  unverified row; a Fayette County obituary would be an independent check —
  flagged, not performed.
- **[2026-10-05] The November letter's authorship.** The master timeline's
  "second letter, dated November 2017" is **Diane Moore's** letter to Fran
  (typed, photographed at Fran's house, cc: Fred Adams), not Dan's
  (dat:1411). The $20,000 figure is carried by the timeline; the kb node's
  full-text extraction renders it as "a 0,000 loan" — the leading digit
  redacted in that node's convention — so the form differs while the
  amount's identity does not.
- **Standing open questions.** His full name, the years he practised, when
  and to what bench he was appointed, **the exact date of his death in May
  2020**, the relationship between Coldren Adams and Adams & Adams, and who
  Jason Adams is to him are all unverified and answerable from Fayette
  County bar, court and probate records outside the corpus. **The estate
  docket is the single most valuable missing document for this page**: it
  would date the death, name the August 21 signing judge, and say whether
  the distribution's timing had anything to do with the valve losing its
  centre.

## Assessment

The record supports a judgment about what Adams was. He was the family's
institutional conscience about money — the person whose agreement Diane cited
to make a house sale sound reasonable, whose formal cc made an
inheritance-advance demand official, whose non-objection Dan invoked to
certify his own caregiving wage, and whose mandate created the rotation the
family then used to exclude Dan. His authority was exercised at a distance —
from the office, from the firm name, from the cc line — and it was most
visible in the moments other people borrowed it. Rick's 2018-01-06 line —
*"I'm tired of u treating me or thinking about me like Rod Banks or Fred
Adams"* — names him and Banks as the men Dan takes counsel from instead of
his father: the only place in the corpus that says what Adams was to the
family rather than to the estate, and it dates the resentment to three
months before Fran's death.

Against that, his operational presence fades across the record's own
timeline: by 2019 Dan cannot get him on the phone while Jason authorises
five advances totalling $32,500; by May 2020 he is dead and the distribution
moves within weeks. The corpus cannot say the death caused the movement.
What it can say is that the man the family treated as the valve — the name
Rick resented, the name Dan applied to like a process, the name Diane cc'd
— had already become a name on the structure before the structure released
the money: decisive from a distance, borrowed by everyone, absent at the
arrival.
