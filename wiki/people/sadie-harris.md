# Sadie Harris

A girl born in Austria on 14 December 1900 was living in Brownsville, Fayette
County, Pennsylvania, by the 1920 census, when she was about nineteen. She
married a Russian-born man eight years older than her who had spent his
childhood in Manhattan, lived with him for a few years in Uniontown and then
back in Brownsville, and in August 1927, at twenty-six, gave birth to their
son. At fifty-nine she was widowed. Two years later, at sixty-one, she was
issued a Social Security number. She was still listed in Brownsville at
ninety-two. She died on 27 November 1997, seventeen days short of her
ninety-seventh birthday, and was buried in Hopwood. Her son died a year and
sixteen days after her. Her great-grandson Daniel was nine years old and
living in Uniontown
([`dat:0986`](../../kb/data/0986-sadie-harris-residence-record.md);
[[wiki/people/morley-frank]]; [[wiki/self/location-history]]).

That is everything. It is not a summary of a longer record; it is the
record. No letter, photograph, recipe, anecdote, quoted sentence or family
story about Sadie Harris survives anywhere in this repository. Her
great-grandson's own message archive — 198,354 rows across fifteen years
(`raw/imessage/summary.json`) — does not contain her name once. When Dan
writes about "my great grandma" in 2016 and 2018 he means
[[wiki/people/fran-coldren|Fran Coldren]], on the maternal side, the woman
whose April 2018 death draws condolence texts in the same archive. Sadie is
the matriarch of the paternal line and she is absent from the family's own
words. She survives as eleven dated coordinates in a genealogy export that
this repository does not hold.

This page exists at length for three reasons. First, those coordinates carry
more weight in the wiki than any other eleven rows about a single person:
her burial is one leg of the finding that tightened
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/fayette-return]] from a county to one village, and
her birthplace is half of the Ashkenazi identity claim that
[[wiki/self/ancestry]] treats as load-bearing. Second, several things the
wiki says about her are stronger than the rows support, and a few are
wrong. Third, what was said *about* her after her death — by a genealogy
parser, by a psychogenealogy report written by an AI in July 2025, and by
this wiki — now outweighs, by a wide margin, anything she is recorded as
having done. The page tries to keep those three layers apart.

## The life, as far as it can be dated

What follows is the human outline, row by row, with ages computed from the
birth date. Every date is the prior wiki's transcription of the Ancestry.com
GEDCOM inside `raw/self/ancestry/23andme-ancestry-family-tree-20260623.zip`,
which is **not held in this repository**
([`dat:0986`](../../kb/data/0986-sadie-harris-residence-record.md), confidence
`low`). Nothing below has been re-read against the source.

**Austria, 1900.** She is born on 14 December 1900. The GEDCOM place string
is "Austria"; [[wiki/self/ancestry]] glosses the span of her life as running
"from the Austro-Hungarian Empire to the 21st-century United States." Her
father appears in the tree as a single entry — surname "Harris," no given
name, no dates, birthplace "Czechoslovakia" — and nothing else is known of
him ([[wiki/self/ancestry]], "The Harris Paternal Line Gap";
[[wiki/self/lineage/family-tree]]). Her mother is not named anywhere on disk.
Her emigration is undated: no passenger manifest, port or arrival year
appears in any page or node.

**Brownsville, 1920.** The first American record of her is the 1920 federal
census, in Brownsville. She is about nineteen. Whether she came to Fayette
County directly, came via New York, came with a family or alone, or met
David J. Frank before or after arriving, the record does not say. Her
husband's own rows put him in the Bronx on 1 June 1915 and in Brownsville by
1920, so the couple's arrival in the county falls somewhere in those five
years and nothing narrows it ([`dat:0355`](../../kb/data/0355-david-j-frank-great-grandfather-nyc-chapter.md)).
No marriage record is transcribed on any page.

**Uniontown, 1923 and 1925.** City directories put her in Uniontown in 1923
and 1925. Her husband's directory rows are Uniontown in 1921, 1923 and 1925
([[wiki/people/david-j-frank]], rows 7–9). This is the "one brief Uniontown
interval" of her record, and it is almost certainly the same household move
seen twice.

**The son, 1927.** Morley Jay Frank is born in Brownsville on 20 August 1927
([[wiki/people/morley-frank]]). Sadie is twenty-six. The record shows one
child. Whether there were others is not stated — the 515-individual GEDCOM's
collaterals have never been enumerated ([[wiki/mind/synthesis/fayette-return]],
Gaps) — so "one child" is what was transcribed, not what is known.

**Brownsville, 1930–1940.** Census rows for 1930, 1935 and 1 April 1940 put
her in Brownsville. Her husband's rows match every one. Her son's census
rows for 1930, 1935 and 1940 are also Brownsville ([[wiki/people/morley-frank]]).
One household, enumerated three times.

**Widowhood, 1960.** David J. Frank dies in Brownsville on 6 April 1960
([[wiki/people/david-j-frank]]). Sadie is fifty-nine. Their son has by then
been to Seattle (1957) and come back to Uniontown (1959)
([[wiki/people/morley-frank]]); Dan's father Rick is born in Uniontown the same
year ([[wiki/people/rick-frank]]). Sadie becomes a grandmother and a widow
within roughly a year.

**The Social Security number, 1962.** A number is issued to her in
Pennsylvania in 1962 — at sixty-one or sixty-two, depending on the month.
The earlier version of this page said sixty-one; the birthday is in
December, so the year alone does not settle it. An SSN at that age is
consistent with entering recorded employment as a widow or with a survivor's
claim, and the export does not say which
([`dat:0986`](../../kb/data/0986-sadie-harris-residence-record.md)). It is the
only document in her record she generates on her own account rather than as
a member of a household.

**Brownsville, 1993.** A directory lists her in Brownsville. She is
ninety-two. It is the last residence row.

**1997.** She dies on 27 November 1997, aged ninety-six. **The GEDCOM records
no place of death.** She is buried in Hopwood
([`dat:0986`](../../kb/data/0986-sadie-harris-residence-record.md)).

**1998.** Morley dies in Hopwood on 13 December 1998 and is buried there
([`dat:1115`](../../kb/data/1115-morley-frank-residence-record-and-personal-contact.md)).
Mother and son die a year and sixteen days apart. (Morley's page says
"fourteen months"; the dates give twelve and a half.)

Two things fall out of printing it whole.

**Eleven own rows, and seven of them are residences.** 1920, 1923, 1925,
1930, 1935, 1940, 1993. Six of the seven fall in the twenty years
1920–1940 and match her husband's rows exactly, year for year. On the
rows' own evidence, she is enumerated as a member of David's household for
the whole of the period when most of her residence record was made. Her
independent paper trail — the part of the record that is about her rather
than about the house she lived in — is three items: an SSN in 1962, a
directory listing in 1993, and a death date.

**Fifty-three blank years.** Between the 1940 census and the 1993 directory
there is no residence row for her at all. The 1950 census has been public
since 2022 (a general fact about US records, not a repository claim) and
nothing on disk shows it was checked. Every statement that she "never left"
Fayette County, that she "stayed in Brownsville the entire time," or that
she held "seventy-seven years in Fayette County with no attested departure"
rests on reading a fifty-three-year gap as continuity. That is a reasonable
inference for a widow in her sixties to nineties, and it is an inference.
"No attested departure" is exactly right; "never left" is one step further
than the rows go. The frontmatter connection strings making the stronger
claim are preserved unchanged, because other tooling consumes them, and
flagged here instead.

## What her record does inside the wiki

### The Fayette rule

[[wiki/mind/synthesis/fayette-return]] states that no member of the
paternal Frank line from G2 onward has both left Fayette County and ended
elsewhere, and later restates it in its better-supported form: *the
terminus is always Fayette*. Sadie cannot test the first version — she is
an immigrant, not a returner; she had no Fayette County to come back to.
She satisfies the second version more completely than anyone else in the
tree, because she never leaves on the record. [[wiki/people/rick-frank]]
plays the same part three generations later: the anchor form of the
pattern, not the journey form.

She also supplies the geographic sharpening. David died in Brownsville
and was buried in Hopwood. Sadie's death place is unrecorded and she was
buried in Hopwood. Morley died and was buried in Hopwood. Three burials
across thirty-eight years in one village that none of the three was born
in and only one is recorded as dying in ([[wiki/people/david-j-frank]], "The
burial"). That convergence is what moved the rule from a county to a
village, and it is the strongest single fact the rule has.

Two qualifications, both raised first on David's page and both apply here
with equal force. **The GEDCOM records a burial place, not a cemetery.**
"Buried beside him" — the phrasing of this page's own connection to David
since creation — is not established; two burials in the same small town
thirty-seven years apart is what the rows say. **And the fourth name is
uncorroborated.** [[wiki/self/ancestry]] says her Hopwood burial is "the same
cemetery as David J. Frank, Morley Jay Frank, and Fran Coldren."
[[wiki/people/fran-coldren]] records no burial place. If Fran — maternal, not
a Frank — were in the same ground, that would be evidence the terminus is
regional rather than lineal, which is the question `fayette-return` says it
cannot yet answer. One sentence on one page carries it.

### The parsimony argument, and its reversal

Sadie is half of the argument `fayette-return` made against
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/ancestral-dialectic]]: both G1 immigrants — Sadie from
Austria, David from Russia — are on the Ashkenazi side, so if the return
pattern reproduces inside that one line, a two-line psychological dialectic
(Ashkenazi hypervigilance against Appalachian numbness) is not needed to
explain it. The previous version of this page repeated that argument as
settled.

It is not settled. The 2026-08-18 re-check on `fayette-return` withdrew the
parsimony leg once the maternal line was assessed: the majority of all
ninety direct ancestors are born, live and die within twenty miles of
Uniontown, on both sides, and Fran Coldren supplies a maternal
leave-and-return with a Fayette terminus. "The paternal line reproduces the
pattern by itself" stayed true and stopped being distinctive. Sadie's
record is unaffected by that reversal — it is a set of dated rows, not an
argument — but the use this page used to make of it is withdrawn.

### The Ashkenazi measurement

[[wiki/self/ancestry]] and [[wiki/self/lineage/hybrid-analysis]] match Dan's
21.4% Ashkenazi Jewish composition to "the known paternal Jewish immigrant
line (David J. Frank from Russia, Sadie Harris from Austria)," and
[[wiki/self/lineage/23andme-genomics]] notes the 23andMe estimate of that
ancestry at one to three generations back is consistent with two
great-grandparents
([`dat:0897`](../../kb/data/0897-ancestry-23andme-figures-and-anomalies.md)).
Sadie is one of the two people through whom that percentage is supposed to
arrive. The record supports her being born in Austria and marrying a
Russian-born Frank; that she was Jewish is the wiki's inference from the
line and the genome together, and no row on disk records her religion,
congregation or community. The inference is reasonable. It is not a
document.

## What was said about her after she died

Sadie has no recorded words. She has, however, been talked about at length
since June 2026, and two of those accounts deserve to be read side by side
with the rows.

### The genealogy parser

The European-born table on [[wiki/self/ancestry]] and
[[wiki/self/lineage/family-tree]] lists her as *"Paternal GM"* —
grandmother. She is a great-grandmother; the grandmother on that side is
Gay Gillingham. The same pages' "Jewish-Line Ancestry" table has the
generation right (*"Great-grandmother (gen 3)"*), so the pages contradict
themselves two sections apart
([[wiki/people/david-j-frank]], Appendix A2). The same tables are the
subject of [`dat:1358`](../../kb/data/1358-family-tree-european-born-count-tension.md):
a European-born count given as sixteen, then fourteen, over a table of
thirteen rows of which two are West Virginian. Sadie is one of the three
the pages identify as European-born **and** on a direct line, which is the
internally consistent part of that cluster.

The sentence "her father 'Harris' contributed ~12.5% of Dan's DNA" appears
on [[wiki/self/ancestry]], [[wiki/self/lineage/family-tree]] and
[[wiki/self/lineage/hybrid-analysis]], and in the open-questions digest it
is harvested into. The arithmetic is off by a generation. Dan's expected
autosomal share from each great-grandparent is one-eighth, 12.5%; Sadie is
the great-grandparent. Her father is a great-great-grandparent, with an
expected share of one-sixteenth, about 6.25%. The gap named in those pages
is real — he is the least documented direct ancestor in the tree — but it
is half the size the pages say.

### The Gemini report, 24 July 2025

The one extended account of Sadie as a person-with-a-psychology is held in
this repository as primary material. On 24 July 2025 at 8:35:05 PM (as
logged), a Gemini deep-research run was started; its completed report,
*The Dan Frank OS: A Psychogenealogical Architecture of Collapse and
Recursion*, is preserved in the Takeout activity log at
`raw/takeout/takeout-20260103T040931Z-3-001/Takeout/My Activity/Gemini Apps/MyActivity.html`.
Its Section 1.1, "The Syntax of Suffering: The Ashkenazi Jewish (Frank/Harris)
Inheritance," names her directly: *"The genealogical record traces this line
directly to David J. Frank, born in Russia in 1892, and Sadie Harris, born in
Austria in 1900."* It then places the family's inheritance in "the pogroms of
the Russian Empire, particularly the waves from 1881-1882 and 1903-1906,"
naming Odessa and Kishinev.

Read against her rows, that frame has nothing to attach to on her side.
She was born in Austria, not in the Russian Empire; the first pogrom wave the
report cites ended eighteen years before she was born; nothing on disk puts
her anywhere near Odessa, Kishinev or the Pale. The trauma inheritance the
report assigns to "the Frank/Harris line" is, on the record, an account of
David's birthplace applied to both of them.

There is a second detail worth recording because it cuts the other way.
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/ancestral-dialectic]] summarised the report as having
the paternal line "fleeing the 1880s–1900s Russian pogroms into the Fayette
County coal patch," and its 2026-08-02 REVISED block corrected that image
against the GEDCOM's New York rows ([`dat:1161`](../../kb/data/1161-ancestral-dialectic-self-contradicting-frame.md)).
The report's own text is more careful than the summary: *"Their journey did
not follow the more common path of settling in a major American Jewish
cultural hub like New York City, though records indicate an initial period in
Manhattan."* The report knew about Manhattan. It framed it as a way station
rather than as fifteen years, which is the part the census rows correct —
but the wiki's paraphrase dropped the caveat the report had, and then
corrected the report for lacking it. That is recorded here as a small,
specific instance of the pattern [[wiki/mind/synthesis/instrument-is-subject]]
describes: the summary of a source drifting further from the residue than
the source did.

## The Harris side of the tree

Her ancestry is the thinnest branch of the tree, and the tree's own
presentation makes it look thicker than it is.

[[wiki/self/lineage/family-tree]] prints a section headed **"The Harris
Matrilineal Line (Sadie's mother's line — 5 generations)."** Its rows are
Gay Gillingham (b. 1939), Francis Gillingham (b. abt 1903, d. 1956), Lillie
Diwins (b. abt 1871), Susannah Elizabeth Emerick (b. 1848, Allegheny Co.)
and Nancy "Annie" Wikert (b. 1828). None of them is a Harris. The first row
is Dan's paternal grandmother, Morley's wife
([`dat:0898`](../../kb/data/0898-ancestry-gedcom-scale-and-lines.md)); the
table is a Gillingham-side line printed under Sadie's heading. It is not
her mother's line. The mislabel propagated: the master timeline files
Nancy Wikert's 1828 birth and Susannah Emerick's 1848 birth under "The
Harris Matrilineal Line (Sadie's mother's line)." (Separately, `dat:0898`
names Gay's father as Dr. George Dale Gillingham, DDS, while this table's
generation-3 row is Francis Gillingham; that is a Gillingham question and
is not reconciled here.)

With the mislabel removed, Sadie's documented ancestry is one entry: a
father surnamed Harris, born in a place the GEDCOM calls Czechoslovakia.
The migration-corridor paragraph on [[wiki/self/ancestry]] reads that as
*"suggesting the family may have been displaced within Eastern Europe
before immigration."* A caution belongs on that inference. Czechoslovakia
did not exist as a state until 1918 (general historical fact, not a
repository source), and genealogy services routinely normalise old place
names to modern states. A father born in Bohemia, Moravia or Slovakia and a
daughter born in "Austria" in 1900 could both have been born inside the
same empire. The two labels do not, by themselves, show a displacement.

## The great-grandson's side

The human story here is partly an absence on Dan's end, and it is worth
stating precisely because the record lets it be stated precisely.

Dan was born on 1 November 1988 in Uniontown
([[wiki/self/location-history]]). Sadie was then eighty-seven and, on the
next row in her record, still in Brownsville. They overlapped for nine years
and twenty-six days. Nothing on disk says whether they met, how often,
or what either made of the other. [`dat:1115`](../../kb/data/1115-morley-frank-residence-record-and-personal-contact.md)
found Dan's two messages about her son — *"you're right but i've got a lot of
morley in me"* and *"if there's one lesson morley taught me it was / don't be
an asshole, be a sonofabitch"* (2018-04-28) — which establish Morley as a
live figure in Dan's childhood. No equivalent exists for Sadie.

For this rewrite the held message parts were searched directly:
`raw/imessage/messages-part1-2011-2019.csv`,
`raw/imessage/messages-part2-2019-2026.csv` and
`raw/imessage/messages-master.csv`. The string "sadie" occurs zero times.
The string "great grandm" occurs a handful of times, and every hit postdates
Sadie's death by at least fourteen years: a 2016 text about taking "my great
grandma to the hospital," a 2018 line about "my great grandma's house" next
door, and the April 2018 condolences — *"Your great grandmother was a special
lady"* — that arrive in the days after Fran Coldren's death on 4 April 2018
([[wiki/people/fran-coldren]]). Where a hit identifies anyone, it is Fran. In
Dan's own vocabulary "great-grandmother" is, on this evidence, a maternal
word. Sadie is the paternal line's anchor in the genealogy and a
blank in the correspondence.

This is not evidence that she did not matter to him. The corpus begins in
2011, fourteen years after her death, and an adult's texts are a poor
instrument for a great-grandparent who died when he was nine. It is
evidence that everything the wiki says about her comes from the paper, and

## Appendix — forensic notes

Compact, and about the instruments rather than the woman.

**A1. The tenure figure.** "Seventy-seven years" is 1920 → 1997, the page's
own arithmetic, and
[`dat:0986`](../../kb/data/0986-sadie-harris-residence-record.md) already
warns against reusing it as a living count. Attested residence spans
1920–1993 (seventy-three years), with a fifty-three-year gap inside it.

**A2. "Stayed in Brownsville the entire time."** The frontmatter connection
to David says so. Her own record has Uniontown in 1923 and 1925, inside
the marriage. The Uniontown rows predate his death, so the claim may be
meant to cover only the widowhood; as written it covers everything.

**A3. Morley's "fourteen months."** [[wiki/people/morley-frank]]'s
connection to this page says he was buried "fourteen months after her."
27 November 1997 to 13 December 1998 is twelve months and sixteen days.

**A4. The DNA share.** Her father's expected contribution to Dan is about
6.25%, not 12.5%. Three pages and the open-questions digest carry the
larger figure.

**A5. The mislabelled matrilineal table** — see "The Harris side of the
tree." A Gillingham line printed under Sadie's heading, propagated into
the master timeline.

**A6. Generation label.** "Paternal GM" in two European-born tables; she is
a great-grandmother.

**A7. SSN age.** Issued in 1962; her birthday is 14 December, so she was
sixty-one or sixty-two. The page previously said sixty-one.
