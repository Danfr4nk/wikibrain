# Shane Brannan

On **25 March 2020**, in the middle of a run of self-incriminating stories
told to his oldest friend, Dan stopped to note who else knew one of them:

> *"i think shane brannan is the only person i ever told about that.*
>
> *i don't even know why i told him of all  people but i knew that it would
> be a crime to tell someone that wasn't in the brannan family"*

The story itself is a sexual mishap from his early twenties, recounted
immediately above in the same thread; it is not reproduced here because
nothing in this page's argument needs it. What matters is the sentence about
Shane. It is the only place in the entire archive where Dan names a person as
the *sole* recipient of something — and the reason he gives is not intimacy
with Shane but membership: the story belonged to a **family**, and Shane was
in it.

That single line overturns how this page read for the first sixteen days of
its existence. Until now it described Shane as "the person Dan talked to about
video games, and almost only about video games" — a co-op partner, legible
through one subject, present in the tweet archive and nowhere else. The tweets
are real and the gaming is real. The rest was an artifact of having looked in
exactly one place.

## The Brannan family

Seven weeks before the disclosure line, on **3 February 2020**, the same
thread carries an exchange that explains what Dan means by "the brannan
family." It starts as a throwaway political guess:

> **21:53:26** Dan: *"apropos of nothing: i bet shane brannan is supporting
> tulsi"*
> **21:54:08** the other party: *"Hahahaha that's a high probability"*

Then, three minutes later, it stops being throwaway:

> **21:56:43** Dan: *"is grandma brannan still around?"* · **21:57:23** *"🤞🏼"*
> **21:57:27** reply: *"Sadly no... she passed away 3-4 years ago, and pap
> this past year"* · *"Seriously the two sweetest people I've ever met"* ·
> *"Wholesome ass people"*
> **22:01:14** Dan: *"ah fuck bummer. dude me too, i only had like 3 or 4
> interactions with her but they were super memorable and explained a lot
> about why that whole family is the most hilariously interesting and
> intensely like-able group of people"*
> **22:02:52** reply: *"That's the best description of the Brannan family
> I've heard haha"*

Three things follow, and they are the human content of this page.

**Dan was in that house.** Three or four remembered interactions with a
friend's grandmother is the texture of someone who was around, not someone
who played Halo with a screen name. The record does not date those visits or
place them, and that is a gap — but they happened, and Dan's account of them
is that they explained the family.

**He was still asking after them in 2020**, four years after the last tweet
and with no evident prompt — *"apropos of nothing."* The hopeful emoji before
the answer arrives is the tell that he did not know.

**The unit is the family, not the individual.** Dan's own formulation on
25 March — *"a crime to tell someone that wasn't in the brannan family"* —
treats Brannan-ness as a qualification. The counterparty's reply on 3 February
confirms the unit is recognised by a third party too: "the Brannan family" is
a thing people in this circle say.

The counterparty in both exchanges is the handle `+19165013615`, which is
almost certainly [[wiki/people/jerad-friedline|Jerad Friedline]]: the same
handle carries the `josh brannan is innocent.wav` attachment of 30 May 2025
that the wiki independently places in the Jerad thread
([[wiki/people/jerad-friedline]],
[`dat:0678`](../../kb/data/0678-josh-brannan-wav-artifact-verified.md)), and
the held Jerad thread's first message is dated 2020-02-04, one day after the
grandmother exchange. The identification is reasoned, not asserted.

## How big a channel this was

The measurement is new and it is the strongest quantitative claim this page
has. Counted over all 2,741 tweets in `raw/twitter/archive.jsonl`:

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Total tweets in the archive | 2,741 |
| Distinct handles mentioned anywhere | 254 |
| Tweets that **open** with a handle | 363 (13.2%) |
| Distinct handles ever led with | 110 |
| Shane — any mention | 19 → **4th of 254** |
| Shane — lead address | 18 → **3rd of 110** |

The three handles above him are `@alexisarmel` (56 lead addresses,
2009-12-14 → 2014-06-17 — the seven-year partner), `@ericjester` (22,
2009-02-26 → 2013-08-11) and, level with him, `@danpolyak` (16). Josh
Brannan is 6.

Two readings follow. The archive is **overwhelmingly a broadcast medium** —
seven of every eight tweets are addressed to nobody — which makes the
363-tweet conversational subset the place to look for relationships, and
inside that subset Shane is third. And the friendship is **the only
non-romantic, non-Full-Sail, non-media entry in the top five**: Alexis is the
partner, Jester and Polyak are the Orlando cohort, `@opieradio` at 15 is a
radio show. Shane is the Uniontown channel, and it ran on games.

**The 2017 line is worth its own sentence.** Dan opened that Facebook thread
with *"I miss the fuck out of u. Call me when u get the chance sometime"*,
posted his phone number thirteen seconds later, and 127 seconds after that
made Shane the first third party named in the reconnection — as the friend
who *does* call. The joke ("loser of the year 2016") is affectionate and it
is also evidence: **in early 2017 Shane was the one in contact, and Josh was
not.** The friendship the page could not see was, at that moment, the more
active of the two.

**And the close-friends entry is the end-state.** Instagram's close-friends
list is a curated allowlist — 73 of 386. Josh was added 9 August 2022; Shane
five days later. Whatever the 2016 silence on Twitter meant, it did not mean
the account stopped being someone Dan chose to show things to, ten years after
the Halo tweets.

## What the friendship was about, re-read

The original reading — that this is a relationship "legible entirely through
one subject" — survives for **Twitter** and fails for the relationship.

**On Twitter it was a gaming channel, and a real one.** The Halo 4 sequence of
6–13 November 2012 runs across eight days and eight tweets: the IGN review
consulted before buying, the purchase, the *"open tray"* hardware failure, the
taunting dashboard, the diagnosis (*"it runs a LOT. But it's also 6 years
old"* — dating the console to roughly 2006), the impatience, the replacement
console bought inside 48 hours, the request for a gamertag, and finally the
invitation to play. The Slender exchange of 19–20 July 2012 is craft
criticism, not reaction: *"the character model is so bad…but it's still scary
as fuck when you turn and it's there"* is an observation about horror design
surviving bad assets. And the June 2011 StarCraft II opener uses *gosu* — the
Korean competitive term — while offering a spare trial code, which is the
vocabulary of someone inside the scene ([[wiki/self/twitter/2011]] documents
the ladder placements, named build orders and pros-by-handle around it).

**Off Twitter it was a family and a confidence.** The three items that were
not visible when this page was written — the grandmother, the family
description, the sole-recipient disclosure — are all about closeness of a kind
the games never register. They are also all retrospective: 2017 and 2020,
years after the tweets stopped, in threads with other people. Dan talks
*about* Shane where he used to talk *to* him.

The honest formulation is narrower than a reversal and sharper than the
original: **the tweet archive recorded the channel, not the friendship.** One
platform captured one register of a relationship that ran in at least four,
and the page mistook the register for the whole.

## What this does to the video-games page

[[wiki/interests/video-games]] carries these tweets as its densest gaming
evidence and already qualified its thesis twice on the strength of them. One
of its own limits now hardens: *"this window is visible only because Shane was
on Twitter. Gaming in 2013–2015 could have continued at the same intensity
with nobody to tweet at about it, and the archive would look exactly as it
does."* That is now demonstrated rather than hypothesised — the **friendship**
demonstrably continued past 2016 in channels that record no gaming at all, so
the 2016 tweet-silence is silence about a medium, not about a hobby or about
a friend. Nothing on that page needs withdrawing; its caution needs promoting
from a caveat to a finding.

## Limits of record

- **Nothing here is Shane's voice.** Every item in the log is Dan's outbound
  text, Dan's contact list, or Dan's platform metadata. Not one of Shane's
  nineteen replies is held, even where the archive shows they exist
  (`replies: 1` and `2` on nine tweets). There is no inbound message from him
  anywhere in the archive.
- **The tweet count is a floor, not a total.** `wiki/self/twitter` rates
  2011 and 2012 as *"in progress"* — assembled from a live scrape capped at
  ten results per month-bounded query — and states plainly that *"no claim
  about cadence, volume, or a subject going quiet is supportable for those
  years."* 2016 is documented as demonstrably incomplete (the 7 February 2016
  gig tweet was live the whole time and missing from both the spreadsheet and
  the scrape). Nineteen is what survives, not what was sent.
- **No iMessage or SMS thread with Shane exists in the record**, and this
  page does not infer from that. Per `CORPUS_POLICY.md`, an absence in a
  partial view supports nothing.
- **The 2020 quotations are tier-flagged.** They were located in the held
  `raw/takeout/…/all_imessages_part_*` dump — a full-corpus dump rather than a
  per-contact fragment, so the specific frame error `CORPUS_POLICY.md` was
  written against does not apply. The policy's tier rule still does: they
  require confirmation against `corpus/messages.csv`, which is gitignored and
  not present in this repository. Treat them as **located, not yet
  corpus-confirmed**, and re-verify with `bin/corpus-query --text "shane
  brannan" --context 3` when the corpus is mounted.
- **Who Shane is to Josh is still undocumented.** Shared surname, joint
  address on 6 November 2012, one recognised "Brannan family," two separate
  Instagram accounts, two separate contact cards. Brothers, cousins and
  in-laws are all consistent with the record and none is established.
- **The grandmother's visits are undated and unplaced.** "3 or 4
  interactions" is the whole of it.
- **The 2013-07-15 Facebook line may not be him.** Recorded above and left
  open.
- **No photograph, recording or document of Shane exists in this archive.**
  Nothing further to embed.

## Appendix: forensic residue

Four small observations that do not belong above.

**A. The handle changes case mid-archive.** Tweets 1–3 write
`@shane_brannan`; tweets 4–19 write `@Shane_Brannan`. The switch falls on
2012-07-19, between the June "RUUUUuuuude" and the Slender opener. Autocomplete
behaviour or client change, not a fact about Shane — but any case-sensitive
search over the archive splits this relationship in two, and a naive count
returns 3 or 16 rather than 19.

**B. Two tweets carry a double space after the handle.** Rows 18 and 19 —
the only two whose `source` field reads `operator-spreadsheet` rather than
`live-x-scrape`. The whitespace is a provenance fingerprint of the
spreadsheet path, not of anything Dan typed, and it is a reusable test for
which retrieval a row came from.

**C. Two wikilinks point here that are not about him.**
[[wiki/interests/music/aliases/sloppp]] renders `[[wiki/people/shane-brannan|other]]`
and `[[wiki/people/shane-brannan|undated]]` — piped links whose display text
is "other" and "undated," in sentences about DJ collaborations and gigs that
do not concern Shane. They appear to be crosslinker artifacts. Any backlink
count for this page is inflated by two, and a reader following them lands on
prose that never mentions him.

**D. The shortlinks are dead.** Rows 3 and 13 carry `t.co` URLs whose targets
are not preserved in the archive. Whatever *"RUUUUuuuude!!"* was rude about,
and whatever the taunting dashboard looked like, are gone. Two of nineteen
tweets are, in content terms, empty.
