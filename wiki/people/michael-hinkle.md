# Michael Hinkle

On a Friday in the middle of July 2017, Dan Frank sent three
messages to a man named Michael Hinkle. Nobody in this wiki knows who Michael
Hinkle is, and nobody knows what the third message means, and there was never
a reply. That is the entire relationship as the record holds it — a single
short burst of friendliness with a plan attached, sent into silence.

The three messages, in order:

> *"hey dude"*

> *"let me know if you see this"*

> *"annie will be home soon, so i'll call u if not. i found us something"*

He is one of the 181 people with a page under [[wiki/people/index]], and one of
the shortest. It is a small thing and it is oddly complete. Each message does a different
job. The first is a greeting with no content — the opener you send when you
expect the conversation to start on its own. The second is an
acknowledgement request, sent because the first one did not land. The third
gives up on the channel and moves to a fallback (*"i'll call u if not"*),
attaches a deadline (*"annie will be home soon"*), and finally says the thing
he opened the app to say: *"i found us something."*

Three messages is also the exact length of that escalation. There is no fourth.
He did not send *"?"* an hour later, or *"you around?"* the next day. He said
what he had, named a way around the silence, and stopped. Whether the phone
call happened is not in the record.

## The clock in the third message

*"annie will be home soon"* is the most informative clause in the thread, and
it is informative for a reason that has nothing to do with Michael Hinkle.

It places Dan at home, alone, with [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] out and
expected back — that is, it is a sentence about a shared residence and a
closing window. In July 2017 that residence is
[[wiki/places/155-virginia-ave|155 Virginia Ave]] in Uniontown, which the
record has Dan occupying from January 2015 to February 2019 and Annie moving
into after Thanksgiving 2015. The clause also tells you the errand was one he
wanted to run before she got back, which is a small fact about the errand and
not one this page will build anything on.

The year is the hardest year in the corpus.
[[wiki/timeline/periods/2017-poverty-floor]] is unsparing about it: water shut
off, the account overdrawn by $600 in January, gold and clothes sold, four
hours of DJing unpaid, a $120 drug debt treated as a materially significant
liability, his mother's *"you've played your cards out."* The calendar logs 85
events for the year, weighted to the financial and relational categories. That
is the room the three messages were sent from.

## What "i found us something" is not

The record does not say what he found, and it does not say who "us" is.

This page declines to guess, and the reason is worth stating because the
temptation is real: July 2017 is a dense month in the corpus and it would be
easy to reach into it for an answer. The month contains, at minimum, a supply
economy in constant motion, an active caddie season, a green-mapping side
project, and at least one person asking Dan for a job. Any of those could host
a *"something."* None of them is attached to this thread by anything except
proximity, and proximity is not evidence.

What can honestly be said is the shape of the sentence: *"us"* implies a
standing joint interest of some kind — the two of them already had a thing they
were both looking for. That is more than the page previously carried and it is
much less than an answer.

## A man named Hinkle in the caddie yard

The one substantive thing this page can add to the 2026-06-23 version is that
Dan and Michael Hinkle were, on the wiki's own showing, not strangers — and the
evidence for it was sitting on a different page the whole time.

[[wiki/people/davey-fitzpatrick]] documents the Nemacolin caddie yard from
inside: 382 messages from May 2017 to November 2018 with a fellow caddie who
became assistant caddie master. Its section on the 2017 season lists the
running jokes about the yard's cast, and the first name in that list is
**Hinkle** — *"I'd pay Hinkle $100 to work our bags lolz,"* Dan's line, Davey's
laugh. That page links the name directly to this one.

So in the same summer as the three unanswered messages, a Hinkle is a
recurring, affectionately-mocked fixture of the caddie yard Dan was working in,
familiar enough to be a punchline between two caddies.

The held message corpus carries the same figure across a longer window.
`kb/data/0978-mike-hinkle-thread-dimensions.md` records a case-insensitive
sweep for "hinkle" across all 192,140 messages and reports golf-circle hits on
**2016-08-09** (*"invited to golf with hinkle"*), **2017-10-01** (*"hinkle wrote
in our grat for yesterday"* — "grat" being caddie gratuity), **2018-04-30**
(*"17 yr old Hinkle tripping"*), and three more in May 2018. The node's own
conclusion: *"he is not an unknown; he is golf-adjacent, which in this record is
nearly an identity."*

The 14 July 2017 messages sit between the August 2016 golf invitation and the
October 2017 gratuity note, inside the caddie season, in the year the yard
jokes are dated to.

**This narrows the gap. It does not close it** — see the next section for why,
and for the instruction in the record that says not to close it.

The same node records that the corpus's twelve "hinkle" hits include **no

And yet [[wiki/people/davey-fitzpatrick]] has already made the identification
in the other direction — it links the caddie-yard Hinkle to *this* page, not to
the 904 thread — while `kb/data/0978` reads the same golf references as
narrowing the *other* page's open question. **Both pages are currently claiming
the same man, and neither says so.** That is the live contradiction here and it
is stated rather than resolved.

Three readings fit the evidence and the record selects none of them:

1. **One man.** A Uniontown golf-circle contact Dan messaged on Facebook in
   July 2017 and who later reached him by phone from a 904 number. Under this
   reading the "no reply" is a channel change, not a snub, and the eight-month
   gap is the gap between one platform and another.
2. **Two men, one surname, one circle.** A Fayette County golf world can
   easily contain two Hinkles; the caddie-yard references include a
   *"17 yr old Hinkle"* in 2018, who is plainly not the same person as an
   adult contact with a Florida number.
3. **Two unrelated men.** The Facebook contact is somebody else entirely and
   the golf Hinkle belongs only to the other page.

**What would settle it**, in order of cost: pulling the Facebook thread itself
(the counterparty's profile name and ID sit in the file); checking whether the
904 number appears in any Facebook or contacts export alongside a "Michael";
or reading the full Davey thread for a first name attached to the yard's
Hinkle. All three are cheap. None has been done.

## Appendix A — where the thread actually is

The page has cited `raw/self/facebook/facebook-ihatedanfrank/messages/inbox/michaelhinkle_kybmlrlgtq/message_1.html`
since 2026-06-23. **That path does not exist in this repository**, and neither
does any `messages/inbox/` tree: the two 2026-09-08 Facebook exports under
`raw/facebook/` hold posts and Docs-converted text, not the message inbox, and
the only loose message file on disk belongs to a different counterparty
entirely.

The thread's *metadata*, however, is held — and running it down produces a
better result than expected.

`raw/facebook-threads/MANIFEST.json` indexes the June 2026 Facebook export at
thread level: **396 threads, 15,923 messages, 403 files on disk, years 2007 to
2022**, contents gitignored for the same reason `corpus/messages.csv` is. Of
those 396 threads, **135 hold a single message and 223 hold three or fewer** —
the Michael Hinkle thread is an entirely ordinary object in that distribution.

Searching the manifest's counterparty list for "Hinkle" returns nothing, and
the only name beginning "Mich" across all 232 resolved counterparties is
Michelle Peters. **That absence is not evidence, and the manifest explains why
by its own construction.** Counterparties are extracted from message blocks'
speaker names, so a thread in which the counterparty never speaks has no name
to extract. Checked directly: of the **31 threads in which every message is
Dan's, exactly zero have a resolved counterparty name.** A one-sided thread is
necessarily anonymous in this index. Michael Hinkle could not appear in that
list even if his thread is sitting in it.

And it very likely is. Filtering the manifest to the thread's exact fingerprint
— three messages, three from Dan, no reply — returns **five threads in the
whole export**, of which **two are dated 2017 on both ends**:

The first two are the candidates. **This page does not choose between them** —
the manifest records years, not months, and the three messages' combined text
is about a hundred characters, which neither byte count decisively excludes.
But the open item is now a two-file pull rather than an open question, and that
is the single most actionable thing on this page.

One caution carries over from `kb/sources/facebook-export-2026-06-23.md` and it
is the reason the identification was worth doing carefully: in this export
*"Nothing in the text carries the speaker."* Each message is a block of speaker
name, rule, text, timestamp, and extracting a line without its block loses the
attribution entirely. That is precisely how the prior wiki once recorded
another person's DUI as a fact about Dan. **The claim that all three messages
here are Dan's is inherited from the prior wiki, not re-verified**, and it is
only the content — *"annie will be home soon, so i'll call u"* — that makes it
obviously coherent from his side and incoherent from anyone else's.

## Appendix B — the count on the other Hinkle was the received half

Filed here compactly because it bears on how much either Hinkle page can be
trusted about the *shape* of a relationship.

[[wiki/people/mike-hinkle]] carried "21 messages, all received (export
artifact)" until `kb/data/0978-mike-hinkle-thread-dimensions.md` re-derived it
against the held corpus and returned **85 messages — 64 sent, 21 received**.
The 21 was never a wrong count. It was the received half of a two-sided thread,
presented as a thread total.

That is not a one-off. `kb/interpretations/received-half-count-pattern.md`
collects fourteen instances of the identical error across the people pages —
Mohammed bin Salman 281 against 643, Jim Shaffer 894 against 1,845, RJ Ritchey
518 against 1,103 — and states the lesson in one line: *"The 21-received figure
was never wrong as a count; it was wrong as a claim about the relationship's
shape."*

**The inverse applies to this page.** Where Mike Hinkle's page understated a
two-sided thread, this page reports a genuinely one-sided one — and a
one-sided reading is exactly what that pattern says to distrust. The
difference is that the 21 had a recoverable other half in the same export,
and three messages into silence do not: there is no half to recover, only a
thread that was never pulled.

## Appendix C — what an unanswered message is evidence of

`CORPUS_POLICY.md` states the governing rule and states it against exactly this
shape of claim:

> *"In a fragment, absence of evidence looks exactly like evidence of absence.*
> *… Read one thread in isolation and you will reliably infer things that the*
> *complete record contradicts: that contact stopped, that a subject was never*
> *discussed, that a period was quiet, that someone was not around."*

And the specific failure it names is the one hanging over this page: a
single-handle extract *"shows a relationship stopping dead where it only
changed channel."* Reading number one above — one man, two platforms — is that
failure mode in its positive form. It is a live possibility precisely because
the policy says a fragment cannot rule it out.

So the honest statement of what the thread establishes is narrow: **on 14 July
2017 Dan sent three messages and no reply arrived in that thread.** It does not
establish that Michael Hinkle ignored him, that the friendship lapsed, that the
phone call failed, or that this was the only contact between them.

What it does illustrate cleanly is the finding at
[[wiki/mind/concepts/contact-gini]]. That page measures an inbound Gini of
0.9556 across 498 handles — top-1 share 33.6%, top-5 68.0%, only 44 handles
ever crossing 100 messages — and then says something more interesting than the
number: *"The tail is not thin relationships. It is non-events. Hundreds of
handles sent one or two messages and never drew a reply."*

This is that, from the sending side. The Facebook manifest puts a second,
independent number on it: 135 of 396 threads are a single message, and 31 are
one-sided in their entirety — including one thread carrying **421** messages
from Dan with nothing back. The periphery is not a thin social layer. It is a
record of outreach that did not convert, and Michael Hinkle is one line of it.

## Gaps and limits

- **Identity.** Who Michael Hinkle is, and how he and Dan know each other,
  remain unestablished. The caddie-yard evidence narrows it to the golf circle
  on a reading this page marks as inference, not fact.
- **What *"i found us something"* refers to** is unknown, and no candidate is
  attached to it by evidence.
- **Whether the phone call happened** is not in the record. *"i'll call u if
  not"* is the last thing either man says in any held source.
- **Whether Michael Hinkle and [[wiki/people/mike-hinkle|Mike Hinkle]] are the
  same person** is open, with three readings and no discriminator. The corpus
  currently asserts both positions on different pages.
- **The thread itself has never been read in this repository.** The quotations
  survive through the old-wiki export; their attribution to Dan is inherited.
  Confidence on `kb/data/0977` is **low** for exactly this reason.
- **The time of day is not recorded**, only the date. Friday, 14 July 2017.
- **No reply exists anywhere in the corpus**, but the corpus's own documented
  gaps — 2021 nearly void, 3,086 unattributable messages, gitignored Facebook
  contents — mean that is a NOT FOUND rather than a negative finding.
- **No photograph, no profile, no surname verification.** `media/registry.json`
  holds nothing for this person and nothing is embedded here.
- **Inferred versus documented.** Documented: the three messages and their
  date, the absence of a reply in the source the prior wiki read, the Davey
  caddie-yard Hinkle joke and its link to this page, the six golf-circle
  "hinkle" hits and their dates, the Facebook manifest's structure and the
  two-candidate fingerprint, the Mike Hinkle recount. Inferred: that the
  caddie-yard Hinkle and this contact are the same man, that *"us"* implies a
  standing joint interest, and that the messages were sent from 155 Virginia
  Ave.
