# Tattoos

Dan's body art is, with one dated exception, an autobiography written in
other people's iconography — nearly every known piece points at a
specific record, band, or artist rather than a generic symbol. The
inventory below starts with six pieces Dan named directly (2026-07-14);
it is not presented as complete, and further pieces should be added as
they surface. The full tattoo count is unknown — Dan's own framing
("just to start you off") indicates this list is a beginning, not an
inventory.

The exception is also the origin. The earliest piece on Dan's body is not
a cultural reference at all: it is the letter **A**, for
[[wiki/people/alexis-armel|Alexis]], inked the night of Saturday
23 January 2010 — eight years before the next documented piece, eight
weeks after he met her, five weeks before he moved to the city she was
in. The archive records the plan (*getting my "a" tattoo on monday*),
the sixteen-minutes-later reversal (*scratch that going to get it NOW!*),
and the done deal (*my first tattoo! I have her on me forever now <3*)
— the whole pattern of his commitment speed in three tweets.

What the ink means, on his own later testimony: by September 2025 he was
admitting some of it was thinner than it looked — "Getting Radiohead
tattoos when I like... 3, 4 songs total" — and the Kanye piece had been
deliberately hedged from the start, chosen as KAWS's Dropout Bear so it
could be recontextualized ("just a KAWS tattoo") if Kanye's public
standing collapsed further. Body art as identity performance, and as
built-in insurance. The cultural-reference pattern holds for everything
from 2018 on; the first piece stands as the documented counterexample.

## The first one: "A", January 2010

The earliest piece on Dan's body predates the rest of this list by more
than eight years, and the archive dates it to the hour. On the evening of
**Saturday 23 January 2010** — he was still in Florida, five weeks from
the move to Brooklyn — he posted:

> **02:47 UTC 24 Jan** — `getting my "a" tattoo on monday =]`
> **03:03 UTC** — `scratch that going to get it NOW!`
> **04:53 UTC** — `my first tattoo! I have her on me forever now <3`

(The timestamps are UTC; in Eastern local time all three fall on the
evening of the 23rd. The photo the last one links to is on yfrog, which
shut down in 2016 — the image is gone, the caption is not.)

*Provenance note:* the 2010 tweets are carried from the old wiki's
Twitter-archive export, which is not held in the current corpus; the
first-tattoo claim is scored in the old wiki's testimony ledger (t009)
and filed at low confidence until the archive itself is in hand. The
held iMessage corpus begins in 2011 and contains no 2010 traffic at
all — nothing in the held sources contradicts the claim, and nothing
confirms it. (dat:0290)

Three things follow from it, and each cut against what this page used to
say.

**It is not a cultural reference.** Every other documented piece points at
a band, a record or an artist. This one points at a person, and Dan says
so in the same breath: *I have her on me forever now*. The "A" is
[[wiki/people/alexis-armel|Alexis Armel]].

**It is fast.** They met at **Thanksgiving 2009**, on a trip home to
Pennsylvania ([[wiki/people/alexis-armel]], where the meeting is dated
from an operator capture and corroborated by the tweets themselves). The
tattoo is **eight weeks later**. Five weeks after that he is loading a
U-Haul to move to the city she is in
([[wiki/timeline/periods/2010s]]). The tattoo and the relocation are the
same decision at two scales, six weeks apart, and both are executed
within days of being announced — "scratch that going to get it NOW!" is
the whole pattern in six words.

**It is impulsive in a specifically documented way.** The plan is Monday.
Sixteen minutes later the plan is now. Whatever else this page records, it
records that the first permanent mark Dan made on himself was moved
forward two days on an impulse and announced as it happened.

**What the archive does not say:** where on his body it is, who did it,
whether it is still there, and whether it was ever covered or reworked
after the relationship ended in December 2015. None of the later
inventories name an "A", which is consistent with a cover-up, with a
small piece nobody thought to list, or with an inventory that was never
complete. The 2026-07-14 list is six pieces Dan named from memory; it did
not claim to be everything.

## Elliott Smith, intended — January 2018

On 24 January 2018, nine calendar months before the October 2018 Redmond
sequence that Dan framed to the artist as the first of "4 or 5 other
pieces" he wanted done before turning 30, the campaign was already on
his mind: *"Gonna crowdfund an Elliott Smith tattoo for myself. I
deserve it."* The tweet is held-verified verbatim — **2018-01-24
02:23:57 UTC**, tweet id `955989519047766016` (0 likes, 0 replies,
0 reposts), no terminal period — and no later source records it being
done. ("Nine months" is a rounding: 24 January to mid-October is ~8.5
months, not a displacement.) It is worth keeping as an intention rather
than a piece, because it means the pre-30 tattoo campaign predates its
opening move, and the Misfits work was not where the idea started.
(dat:0574)

## Misfits: skull in a pizza slice, and a second Misfits piece

The earliest well-documented piece, and now precisely dated via a
direct Facebook Messenger thread with the artist: Dan first messages
**Chris Redmond** — a Uniontown-area artist (his own messages place him
near Cheat Lake and Hopwood, "coming down the mountain" for
appointments) — on October 12, 2018, explicitly framing it as the
first of "4 or 5 other pieces" he wants done before turning 30 on
November 1, and the pizza-slice piece is inked the next day, October
13, on his forearm at roughly 3" x 1.5". In a November 27, 2018
exchange with [[wiki/people/emaly-minerd|Emaly Minerd]] — who
compliments the tattoo and asks directly why he chose a pizza slice
over the band's standard imagery — Dan explains the design process: he
didn't want the conventional Misfits "Crimson Ghost" variant "where it
looks like it's in a coffin with the arms folded," found a pizza-slice
reinterpretation of the skull online, and had Chris draw it up custom
for him. Elsewhere in the corpus (an August 2018 message and a 2018
exchange with [[wiki/people/tom|Tom]]) Dan self-mockingly frames the
piece as evidence of being "a scumbag... immature" for getting it — "a
literal misfits tattoo" is invoked more than once, by Dan and by
others, as shorthand for arrested-development, still-into-the-
same-things-at-30 self-deprecation, rather than pure enthusiasm.

A **second, previously undocumented Misfits piece** follows quickly:
on October 18, 2018, Dan asks Chris to ink a green-lettering Misfits
band-logo design directly beneath the pizza slice on the same forearm,
explicitly calling it "this most cliché tattoo" — done October 22.
Chris, unprompted, volunteers that he "listen[s] to the misfits, still"
and declines to modify the logo beyond the band's own lettering,
calling it "all unique to the band."

## Radiohead: the bear

Documented by October 27, 2018 — held-verified in the message corpus as
*"There's a pic of my Radiohead tattoo"*, **2018-10-27 05:54:45 UTC**,
outbound (dat:1216) — matching the "Modified Bear," the Stanley
Donwood-designed mascot associated with the *Kid A*/*Amnesiac* era,
which Dan names directly in the same conversation as his favorite
Radiohead album ("I'm a Kid A guy but on a rainy day... you gotta turn
on OK Computer"). The piece recurs as a running bit in Dan's own
self-presentation — in a September 2019 message he jokes that his
favorite Radiohead song is "Karma Police," the one song the band itself
refuses to play live, framing his own taste as slightly contrarian even
within his own fandom.

A September 24, 2025 exchange with [[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]] adds a
genuinely self-aware complication to the whole piece's meaning. In the
middle of a long, unguarded late-night conversation about performing
identity for other people ("Why I am doing the stupid things I am doing.
Painting my nails, asking performative Van Halen questions... Getting
Radiohead tattoos"), Dan admits the fandom behind the tattoo is thinner
than the ink suggests: "Getting Radiohead tattoos when I like... 3, 4
songs total." The line is held-verified in the corpus —
**2025-09-25 03:32:46 UTC** (23:32 EDT on the 24th), outbound (dat:1216).
Read together with the Misfits self-mockery above, this suggests at
least some of the tattoo choices are consciously understood, by Dan
himself, as identity performance rather than pure fan devotion — worth
holding onto as a caveat for reading the whole list, not just this one
piece.

## The sleeve ambition starts in Uniontown, not Brooklyn

The "full sleeve" idea documented below as a Brooklyn-era pursuit
actually originates three months earlier, with Chris Redmond: by
October 16, 2018 Dan is already asking to "start discussing concept for
a sleeve," and by November 25 he's explicit about the aesthetic —
"working it into a full sleeve of Old school/American traditional
motifs" — buying reference books from Amazon by November 27 ("I'm
finding it easier to evolve the vague ideas... into more specific
concepts... wish I would have started getting these tattoos years
ago"). On November 30, 2018 he sends Chris a **traditional rose**
reference image, wanting it placed near the existing forearm work; by
December 20 Chris confirms it's "always do-able," and the thread ends
there without a completed-session message. Whether this Uniontown rose
concept became the "old school traditional rose" Ian Weidrick executed
in Brooklyn roughly ten weeks later (March 2019, below) — i.e. the plan
moved with Dan to NYC before being realized — or whether it is a
separate, unfinished piece is not established.

## The traditional pieces: rose, flapper girl, gravestone

On March 8, 2019, Dan texted [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] with
visible excitement about getting "a real tattoo" at a shop he describes
as specializing in "traditional tattoos" — American traditional flash
work, the genre both the rose and the flapper girl belong to. Annie's
reaction to the finished photo ("Omg that's fu\*king awesome... I love
it... Very very nice") and her comparison question — "Do you know how
long that would've taken **Chris at the edge** to do? LOL" — names a
second tattoo reference point (a different or possibly overlapping artist
named Chris, associated with a shop called "The Edge") against which this
session's speed was being measured; Dan confirms the piece "took an hour
and a half total."

A July 15, 2026 capture resolves the artist and location for the
traditional cluster: Dan names **Ian Weidrick of Allied Tattoo in
Brooklyn** as the artist behind his "Brooklyn tattoos" — the flapper
girl, the rose, and the gravestone. This places all three pieces firmly
in Dan's NYC era and confirms they share a single artist and shop,
though it does not settle whether they came from one session or several.
It also means "Chris at the Edge," the reference point in Annie's March
2019 message, was a comparison shop, not the artist who did the work —
Weidrick/Allied was. Whether this specific session produced the rose, the
flapper girl, or both is still not established in the corpus — traditional
flash sessions commonly bundle multiple smaller pieces, so it's plausible
more than one came from the same visit, but that's inference, not
confirmed fact.

Whether "Chris Redmond" (the Misfits artist) and "Chris at the Edge"
are the same person now reads as unlikely rather than merely
unconfirmed: Chris Redmond's own messages place him working near Cheat
Lake and Hopwood, PA — the Uniontown area, not NYC — making him a
plausible but unconfirmed match for a Uniontown-adjacent "Edge" location
rather than any Brooklyn shop; the March 2019 traditional pieces
themselves were done by Ian Weidrick at Allied Tattoo, not by either
Chris. The gravestone piece has an artist and location but still no date
or corroborating message-corpus trace. (dat:1216 notes the Weidrick/Allied
attribution comes from a 2026-07-15 capture not held in the current
corpus — carried as the page's own stated record.)

## Morrissey

On 24 October 2019: *"basically i'm a horrible shit person but i'm
hoping that having a Morrisey tattoo is helping make up for it"* —
stated as a thing he has, not a thing he wants. It is not on the
2026-07-14 list. Date, placement and artist are all unknown; the upper
bound is the tweet. Like the first tattoo, this one is carried as
old-wiki testimony: the 2019-10-24 piece comes from the tweet archive,
which is not held in this repository (dat:1216).

## Kanye — *808s & Heartbreak*, via KAWS

Disclosed to [[wiki/people/tom|Tom]] on September 22, 2025 — held-verified
in the corpus as two outbound rows, both **2025-09-22 17:05 UTC**
(dat:1216) — with a specific and fairly calculated rationale: "Did I
ever tell you that I specifically got the KAWS version off my Kanye
tattoo. So that when he inevitably crashed out I could say it was just a
KAWS tattoo." The piece is the KAWS-reworked "Dropout Bear," the mascot
artist KAWS redesigned for Kanye's *808s & Heartbreak* era promotional
campaign and toy line — a real, specific, dated collaboration, not a
generic Kanye reference. The reasoning is worth taking seriously as its
own data point: Dan describes deliberately choosing the
art-world-adjacent, one-step-removed version of a tribute to a musician
with a well-documented pattern of public instability, so the tattoo could
be recontextualized ("just a KAWS tattoo") if Kanye's public standing
collapsed further — a hedge built into the ink itself. Tom's reply, "Very
old school Kanye," and Dan's own framing both treat this as Kanye's more
respected creative period, not a current-events endorsement.

## Conflicts in the record

**2026-09-02 — the page's founding correction.** This page previously
read that the body art was "**entirely** cultural reference" and dated
the earliest well-documented piece to October 2018. The tweet archive
broke both halves: the **first** tattoo is eight years earlier, is not a
cultural reference at all (the letter **A**, for Alexis), and the
generalisation was true only of the six pieces the 2026-07-14 list
happened to name. Current standing: the cultural-reference thesis holds
for everything from 2018 on; the "A" survives as its documented
counterexample.

**2026-09-02 — the interval correction.** An earlier version of the
"A" section, written the same day, said the relationship "began in
2009" and left the interval vague at "four months." Eight weeks — from
Thanksgiving 2009 to 23 January 2010 — is the figure, and it makes the
point harder rather than softer: the tattoo and the relocation are the
same decision at two scales, six weeks apart, both executed within days
of being announced.

**2026-09-09 — first-tattoo provenance.** The "A" claim is scored in
the old wiki's testimony ledger (t009) from the tweet archive, which is
not held in the current repository; the held iMessage corpus begins in
2011 and contains no 2010 traffic. Filed at low confidence: nothing in
held sources contradicts it, nothing confirms it. It stays a
testimony-ledger claim until the tweet archive (or a dated photo) is in
hand. (dat:0290)

**Flagged, unresolved — the "last week" discrepancy.** On 7 December
2018 Dan wrote *"I'm 30 years old, got a Misfits tattoo last week."*
The Messenger thread with Chris Redmond dates the pizza slice to
13 October and the logo lettering to 22 October 2018 — six and seven
weeks earlier, and both **before** his 1 November birthday rather than
after it. Either "last week" is loose (he is making a joke, not filing
a report) or there is a third Misfits-related piece from late November
2018 that nothing else records. The Messenger thread is the better-dated
source and the list above follows it; this is flagged rather than
resolved. (dat:1216 keeps the flag standing.)

**2012-01-26 — not a tattoo.** *"Finished the color on my Chris Brown
tattoo today. @chrisbrown UR DA BEZT"*, retracted two hours later —
*"I do NOT have a Chris Brown tattoo rofl. You can all calm down now."*
Recorded here so the first line cannot be found by a future grep and
filed as a piece. It is the earliest evidence of Dan using his own body
art as a bit, seven years before the Morrissey line does the same thing.

**2026-09-22 — duplication note (his words).** Dan flagged that the
first-tattoo event appears on this page "like five different times"
across the wiki (Alexis Armel page, Tattoos page, Dan Polyak thread,
Tattoos list, Twitter archive tags) and gave the standing instruction:
"we can just have the tattoo getting the tattoo, we don't need the rest
of it." This page keeps the event — it is the page's origin claim —
but the instruction stands for the wiki generally: the surrounding
re-narration should not be duplicated wherever the date is cited.

## Assessment

The record supports three readings of the ink as more than decoration.
First, permanence as commitment signal: the first permanent mark Dan
ever made on himself was a lover's initial, executed eight weeks into
the relationship on an impulse announced in real time, five weeks ahead
of a move to her city — the earliest hard evidence of the all-in
commitment speed the relationship history describes, and the "A"
connection claim stands on that frontmatter linkage.

Second, conscious identity performance: the Misfits work is
self-mockingly framed by Dan as "scumbag... immature" evidence, the
Radiohead piece gets the 2025 admission that the fandom behind it is
"3, 4 songs total," and both are his own words. At least some of the
tattoo choices are understood, by the wearer, as performed identity
rather than pure devotion — the caveat governs the whole list, not
just the Radiohead row.

Third, hedging as design principle: the Kanye piece was deliberately
chosen as the art-world-adjacent, one-step-removed KAWS version so it
could survive the subject's public collapse — a recontextualization
option baked into the tattoo itself. Read against the impulse-record of
the first piece, the two poles of the corpus are visible: the ink is
either the fastest decision he ever documented or the most carefully
hedged, and both are his.

What the record cannot support is completeness. Dan's own framing ("just
to start you off") plus the two pieces the list did not have (Morrissey,
Elliott Smith intent) plus the undated gravestone mean any total-count
or final-inventory reading is out of bounds. The page extends as pieces
surface.
