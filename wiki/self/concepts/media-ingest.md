# Media Ingest

The standing protocol for what happens to a photo or video Dan supplies
for the wiki-brain. Two layers: safety handling (unchanged) and the
2026-09-12 correction that redefined what a finished intake is.

## Safety handling (unchanged, 2026-09-12)

Every image is eyeballed at intake — there is no reliable auto-detector,
and borderline cases are flagged rather than guessed. Embedded
derivatives showing nudity or sexual activity are pixelated or blurred
before commit; the uncensored original stays at its canonical location
and is never committed (`dat:1455-media-censorship-directive`). Originals
are never committed at all — compressed derivatives only. Local face
embeddings (the face-tag registry) stay local-only and are never pushed
anywhere, a deliberate carve-out from the public mandate.

## The narrative-spread directive (2026-09-12)

At 2026-09-12T23:42:18Z, Dan corrected the workflow's center of gravity:
intake is not display — it is **narrative spread**. Interpreting the
image means figuring out where it is relevant in the wiki-brain, and
each selected photo must yield narrative substance distributed through
the wiki by at least one of four moves: adding to existing entries,
corroborating facts, correcting missed directions or contradictions, or
opening new directions.

Registry, derivative, and embed are bookkeeping — the completion unit is
prose in `wiki/`. This supersedes the registry/embed-centered framing,
not the safety layer: nothing about censorship, eyeballing, or
local-only originals changed.

## Selection is data

Dan also established that any photo he supplies is deliberately selected
for inclusion in the wiki-brain ("if you picked it, you were pointing at
something"). The choice of image is itself a datum — the curatorial act
is evidence about what he considers worth keeping, not just decoration
to be filed.
