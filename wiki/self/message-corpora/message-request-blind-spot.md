# The Message-Request Blind Spot

For most of 2026 the wiki held a clean claim about [[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin Prentiss]]: no contact after December 10, 2025. Eight months of silence, corroborated off-corpus by Dan himself — which made her severance the corpus's cleanest inbound control case. The claim was true of the pulls. It was false of the world. On August 26, 2026 she sent five messages into Dan's Messenger message-request folder, where they sat unopened for seventeen days — surfaced only by the September 12 pull, live in conversation, at 06:39:57 UTC. Within the hour he had texted her himself. The block was opened from the inside, by the person who had been blocked, and the whole sequence — the knock, the pull, the reactive re-entry — ran through a folder the wiki had never looked at **[ATTESTED, dat:1452]**.

This entry is the instrument lesson, stated as doctrine: **every absence claim in this wiki is bounded by its instrument, and the bound must be named in the claim.** "No contact after Dec 10" sounded like a fact about the world. It was a fact about the pulls — bounded by channel (the blocked iMessage thread, not Messenger), bounded by instrument (exports that never pulled the request folder), and bounded by time (ceilings no one wrote down). The message-request folder is the parable: there is always another folder. The rest of this page is the inventory of what the corpus's pull instruments can and cannot see, the taxonomy of what kind of "no" each can support, the protocol for stating absence claims going forward, and a survey of the standing absence claims the doctrine re-grades.

A note on register, since this is a methods page: the tone is forensic and dry by design. No moral is drawn about anyone's conduct. The subject is the instruments — what they measure, where they are blind, what an honest zero costs.

## The demonstration, compressed

The full case lives at [[wiki/mind/synthesis/kristin-channel]]; this section is the instrument anatomy, kept tight. What happened, as a measurement sequence:

1. **2025-12-09, 18:55.** [[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]] blocks Dan on the phone channel: *"Blocking you now. Don't contact me again or an officer will be reaching out."* **[ATTESTED]** The instrument that later claims the block "held" is the iMessage exports. The instrument's jurisdiction is the phone channel.
2. **2026-08-16.** Dan reports *"no contact after 10 Dec 2025"* **[OPERATOR]**. The wiki records this as off-corpus corroboration — the category of evidence the wiki prizes precisely because it distinguishes a control from an absence of data.
3. **2026-08-26, 17:50–22:23 UTC.** Five messages arrive in the Messenger message-request (PENDING) folder, plus a missed call. No pull surfaces them. Dan does not know they exist. **[ATTESTED, dat:1452]**
4. **2026-09-12, 06:39:57 UTC.** The assistant's Messenger pull tells Dan about the August messages, live in conversation. **[ATTESTED]**
5. **2026-09-12, ~06:53 UTC.** Dan's screenshot shows four outbound iMessages, all Delivered, sent at 2:50 AM. No reply in frame. **[ATTESTED, dat:1453]**

The failure's anatomy, as a checklist of everything that had to be true for the wiki to be wrong while every check it had came back clean. (a) The claim sounded like a presence claim — "no contact" — and was stored as one. (b) The off-corpus corroboration was real and was not exhaustive: Dan's testimony covered what he had seen, and the request folder was a place he had not looked. (c) The block was channel-specific and the claim wasn't: the December instrument covered the phone number, not the platform. (d) The instrument that could have seen it — the Messenger pull — had never been asked to look at the request folder, and the folder's contents arrive only when the pull is pointed at it. (e) Seventeen days elapsed with the evidence sitting in a real location, in a real folder, in a JSONL file that would eventually be imported, while the wiki stated the opposite.

None of these is a mistake in the "someone should have known" sense. It is a design property of the corpus: absence is expensive to establish and the wiki had been quoting it at the price of presence. The protocol below is how the price gets corrected.

## The instrument inventory

Corpus-first, as required: every instrument the corpus actually uses, what it covers, and — the part that goes on the page per [[wiki/meta/instruments/index]]'s third rule — what it structurally cannot see. Numbers below are the documented ones; where the wiki has measured them, the measurement is cited.

### 1. Thread-specific iMessage CSV exports

The workhorses: `imessage_3307038747_both_all_now.csv` (20,009 rows, 2025-09-01 → 2025-12-10 — the [[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]] phone thread), `imessage_2124702449_both_all_now.csv` (23,719 rows, 2022-12-31 → 2026-06-05 — [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]]'s NYC handle), and their siblings. These are the exports the person-level pages are built on.

**Covers:** a single handle or handle set, both directions when the export is `both`, dated, attributable when the handle column exists.

**Cannot see:** anything on another channel (Messenger, Facebook messages, IG, calls, in-person); anything outside the export's window — and the filename's `_all_now` suffix is unreliable as a class, with 18 sources measured as overstating their coverage (the Kristin "all now" file starts 2025-09-01, missing the Aug 29–30 Messenger first contact; the 212 "all now" starts 2022-12-31) **[DERIVED, source-coverage-index]**; direction in some files (the [[wiki/people/casey-bondarenka|casey-bondarenka]] thread reads "Received" throughout — direction as one-way read, not trusted); authorship when the handle is shared (see handle ≠ person, below); and anything deleted or aged out before the export ran.

### 2. The deep export

`imessage_export_deep_20260813.csv`: 186,671 rows, 2011-03-19 → 2026-08-13, 499 handles, 15 columns, timezone UTC validated against a local-time source on 42,895 text-matched pairs — the only source that is both full-range and attributed, and the only one that can settle *who said something* across the whole corpus **[ATTESTED, source-coverage-index]**.

**Cannot see:** anything after 2026-08-13 (a hard ceiling — claims about August–September 2026 made against this file are made against a file that stopped); non-iMessage channels; and — the trap the index did not have — *who was typing*: at least six inbound rows attributed to +12124702449 were produced by [[wiki/people/jerel-coles|Jerel Coles]] on [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]]'s phone across three episodes (2026-07-26, 2026-08-16, 2026-08-18), identifiable from register, occurring exactly during crises — the handle is least reliable where the highest-stakes claims are drawn **[ATTESTED, source-coverage-index]**.

### 3. The direction-reliable dump and its miner

`raw/self/dox-scan/all_imessages_complete_dump.txt`: 223,070 rows, 2011-03-18 → **2025-08-10**, one column, no handle column — the one dump whose direction field is trustworthy and the default for `bin/mine-messages`. And this is the instrument's central trap: it returns **silence, not an error**. A query for a post-August-2025 thread is indistinguishable from a query for someone who never existed **[ATTESTED, source-coverage-index]**.

**Cannot see:** everything after 2025-08-10 — and the [[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]] relationship (22,018 messages, the corpus's densest node) is one of them. Zero of those messages are in this dump. The instrument that measures contact concentration cannot see the most concentrated thing in the corpus **[DERIVED]**. Any absence claim dated after 2025-08-10 that was checked against this instrument was checked against an instrument that had already stopped.

### 4. The master CSV dump

`MASTER_MESSAGES_DB_DUMP.csv`: 175,358 rows, 2011-03-18 → 2026-03-25, no handle column — usable for presence and volume, never for authorship. The [[wiki/people/suzanne-frank|Suz]] correction is this instrument's standing parable: the dump records **2,391** messages for +17243228715 while the authoritative deep export records **33,698** — a fourteenfold undercount that moved Suz from eighth place to second in the entire corpus and sat as the headline statistic for two months **[ATTESTED, master-message-dump]**.

**Cannot see:** attribution (no handle column); post-2026-03-25 content; and — the lesson — its own completeness. Every volume figure sourced to this file is a floor pending recount. A floor is what absence claims get quoted at when they should be quoted at zero-with-a-denominator.

### 5. Messenger pulls

The 2026-09-12 pull (`messenger-all.jsonl`) is the pull that broke the control case. The earlier record is a rendered-UI screen capture from the morning of 2025-09-01 (07:04:39Z–07:53:01Z), recovering 2,009 messages (1,439 from Dan, 570 from her) from Aug 29–30 — the actual first contact, two to three days before the iMessage thread opens **[ATTESTED, kristin-channel]**.

**Covers:** the Messenger inbox of the pulled account, when the pull is run.

**Cannot see:** the message-request folder, until the pull is pointed at it — this is the blind spot the page is named for. The August attempt sat in PENDING for seventeen days while the inbox pulls said nothing, because the pulls were never asked. Also cannot see: threads deleted before the pull; calls that didn't leave a message record (the Aug-26 missed call exists only as a notification row); read state with confidence (Dan's not-having-seen the requests before 06:39Z is his testimony plus the PENDING flag agreeing — no independent read receipt **[OPERATOR, medium]**); and the counterparty's block state (Dan's belief she re-blocked him on Messenger afterward is his inference from being unable to approve the request — could be app state, a deleted thread, or a block; untestable without her device **[OPERATOR, unverified]**).

### 6. The Facebook export

`facebook-ihatedanfrank` (export 2026-06-23, completed and recounted per dat:0054): 403 message threads, posts, groups, events. The corpus's pre- and parallel-to-iMessage surface — 2007–2022 — and the source of the 2025-09-01 UI capture window's context.

**Cannot see:** anything after the export date (the recount completed the archive; it did not extend it); threads excluded from the export; and anything that happened on Messenger-the-app after mid-2026 rather than Facebook-the-archive. A 2026 absence claim checked against this export is checked against 2022's ceiling.

### 7. Gmail

`gmail_bodies.txt`: 22,860 bodies, 2001-09-11 → 2014-12-29, one column. The corpus's only pre-2015 documentary layer of any size.

**Cannot see:** anything after 2014; attribution is coarse (no handle column). Relevant to absence claims only at the biography layer (early-life, Full Sail, pre-[[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]]) — which is exactly where the wiki's thinnest presence claims sit, and where the instrument's blindness is total.

### 8. The Twitter scrape

`bin/mine-tweets`: 2,741 originals, 24 Sep 2008 → 1 Sep 2026, three sources of different fidelity (1,412 spreadsheet · 1,098 live scrape · 231 backend), 125 truncated rows excluded from length figures. The public record, with its coverage share carried on every figure.

**Cannot see:** everything before the Twitter archive's source change on **2013-08-17** — pre-date content is invisible, which is why the SLOPPP start is "mid-2013" rather than dated **[ATTESTED, dance-music-only-phases]**; deleted tweets; DMs (no instrument); anything after the scrape date. Public-record absence ("he never announced X") is this instrument's native claim, and the ten-year silence (908 originals, 2016–2026, zero mentions of making music) is its worked case — with the standing correction that silence in the *announcing* channel is not silence in the *activity* channel (GRIPNOTIC developed in the basement throughout) **[ATTESTED, dance-music-only-phases]**.

### 9. UI captures and screenshots

Operator-supplied: the 2025-09-01 Messenger capture, the 2026-09-12 four-texts screenshot (dat:1453), upload-027's parsed bubbles. Dated to the minute, direction-unambiguous (blue outbound bubbles), reply-absence frame-bounded.

**Cannot see:** anything outside the frame (replies after the capture wouldn't appear); anything not captured. And the capture's own trap: the timestamps on the 2025-09-01 capture are *capture* times, not *set* times — the nicknames were on screen that morning, when they were assigned is unknown; the mathematical-monospace Unicode in the capture defeats naive grep (fold with `unicodedata.normalize('NFKC', …)` before matching) **[ATTESTED, kristin-channel method appendix]**.

### 10. Operator testimony

`bin/wiki-testimony`'s ledger: every first-person claim, what settled it, veracity **57/100** over the settled set, calibration tracked against stated confidence. The category the wiki trusts most for absence claims — nobody else can report what Dan tapped, saw, or didn't see.

**Cannot see:** anything he didn't look at (the request folder — the whole case); anything he misremembers in good faith (the wiki's standing reliability note: stated certainty ≈ 0.25 actual; testimony skews toward good-faith misremembering, date displacement especially, not dishonesty **[OPERATOR-derived, MEMORY]**). And the August-26 [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] block is the ledger's own counterexample: the most trusted absence source — Dan reporting his own block — failed, retracted verbatim sixteen days later: *"there was never a block"* **[ATTESTED, august-26-block-retraction]**. If the prior for operator-reported absence is 57/100 on settled claims, then an absence claim corroborated only by testimony is barely better than a coin flip — and the wiki had been treating it as a control.

### 11. Address books and contact lists

`contacts.csv` and the Facebook address book: both **predate [[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]] entirely** — identity verification for her cannot meet the protocol, and the surname rests on her own first-person testimony, strong but single-source **[ATTESTED, kristin-channel gaps]**.

**Cannot see:** anyone who entered the record after the list was made. An address book is a presence instrument with an expiry date, and every absence claim about a post-expiry person made against it is made against a book that never contained them.

## The blind-spot taxonomy

Seven classes of contact the instruments structurally miss. The [[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]] case is class 1, the worked demonstration; the other six are the survey this page exists to run.

### Class 1: Message requests and unapproved threads

Messenger's message-request (PENDING) folder: messages from non-friends land here, unopened, until the recipient approves the thread — and the pull instruments never looked until 2026-09-12. The August five sat seventeen days in a folder that is *part of the account* but *not part of any pull's default scope*. **[ATTESTED]**

The general form: any platform where contact requires approval (Messenger requests, IG requests — no IG pull instrument exists in the corpus at all, Snapchat — no instrument, Facebook's filtered inbox) creates a second inbox the operator may never open and the pulls never query. An absence claim that doesn't name the request folder's state is an absence claim about half the account. The protocol's standing question for every future Messenger pull: *was the request folder queried, and what was its state?* If the answer isn't recorded, the pull's zeros are about the inbox only.

### Class 2: Voice and the channels with no instrument

No instrument in the corpus pulls call logs, voicemail, or FaceTime. The Aug-26 missed call exists in the record only because the Messenger thread logged a notification row — a voice attempt that left a text-shaped trace. A voice attempt that doesn't is invisible everywhere: no pull, no export, no [[wiki/meta/testimony-veracity|testimony ledger]] entry unless Dan volunteers it. The [[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]] September re-entry's four texts are *"all delivered, no reply in frame"* — but a call placed and unanswered after the screenshot is outside every instrument's jurisdiction **[ATTESTED, kristin-channel]**.

The same class covers disappearing messages, unsent messages, and anything deleted before a pull ran. Deletion is the request folder's mirror image: the folder hides content that exists; deletion removes content that existed. Both produce the same observable — a pull that says nothing — and the wiki has no instrument that distinguishes them. The "did she re-block him or did the request age out" question is unresolvable for exactly this reason: the observable (no approvable thread) is consistent with block, deletion, app state, or expiry, and no instrument adjudicates **[UNKNOWN]**.

### Class 3: Channel-specific instruments vs. channel-general claims

A block is set on a channel; absence claims are stated about the world. The December 2025 [[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]] block was an iMessage/phone instrument. The August attempt was a Messenger event. "The block held" was true per-channel and false of the contact surface — the surface was always wider than the instrument **[ATTESTED, kristin-channel]**.

This generalizes to every block, mute, and severance in the wiki. The [[wiki/mind/synthesis/block-unblock-loop|block-unblock-loop]]'s inbound/outbound table currently carries no channel bounds; this page's standing recommendation is that every row gets one. The December block's honest form: *held on the phone channel Dec 2025 – Aug 2026 as observed via iMessage exports; one inbound attempt on Messenger (unpulled channel) in month eight; broken from the inside in month nine by the blocked party.* Longer. Weaker. True.

### Class 4: Handle ≠ person

The [[wiki/self/message-corpora/source-coverage-index|source-coverage-index]]'s trap: every row answers *which handle* and *what window*; none answers *who was typing*. At least six inbound rows on [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]]'s 212 handle across 2026-07-26, 2026-08-16, and 2026-08-18 were produced by Jerel Coles on her phone — identifiable from register, occurring during crises, including an accusation of sexual exploitation *against Dan* that a naive read files as Annie's own testimony **[ATTESTED, source-coverage-index]**.

For absence claims this cuts both ways. An absence of "Annie said X" in the exports is not an absence of activity on her handle — the handle was active with someone else typing. And a presence of severance-looking language from her handle is not her severance. The practical rule, from the index: the preflight adds a fifth question to *what window, what handles, what columns* — **who else had physical access to the device in this window**. For an iMessage corpus that question has no column and can only be answered from content. Whether there are Coles-class episodes earlier in the record has not been checked.

### Class 5: The ceiling — claims that outrun their instrument's window

Every instrument has a last date, and claims keep getting made past it. The direction-reliable dump ends 2025-08-10; the deep export ends 2026-08-13; the master CSV ends 2026-03-25; the Facebook export is a 2026-06-23 snapshot of a 2007–2022 archive. The [[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]] case is what a claim looks like when it outruns the instrument: "no contact after Dec 10" was checked (to the extent it was checked) against instruments whose windows either ended before the claim's span or never covered the channel **[DERIVED]**.

The standing form: every absence claim carries its ceiling — *as observed via [instrument], window [start] → [end], pull date [date]*. A claim without a ceiling is a claim about all time, and no instrument in the corpus covers all time. The [[wiki/self/message-corpora/source-coverage-index|source-coverage-index]]'s ceiling warning ("the ceiling matters more than the row counts") is the same doctrine; this page is its application to zeros.

### Class 6: Out-of-band — the instruments' hard boundary

In-person contact leaves no pull trace. Dan and [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] talked on 2026-09-10 about her waitressing job at Mom Maruca's and a bad review there — contact, ongoing as of Sep 12, with no message-row correlate required **[ATTESTED, MEMORY]**. Mutual intermediaries are the same class: the [[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]] thread entered the corpus through [[wiki/people/tom|Tom]]'s social circle; anything said through Tom is said outside every instrument. The wiki's message corpora are a *text* record, and the 2026-08-02 axiom test already established the jurisdiction: SMS is a near-zero-introspection medium, the psychological layer sits outside it **[ATTESTED, meta/instruments]**.

An absence claim about "contact" that means *text contact* is admissible with its instrument named. An absence claim about contact that means *contact* is inadmissible on text instruments alone — and the wiki's phrasing has not always kept the two apart.

### Class 7: The un-diffed and the un-filed

The corpus contains exports that have never been reconciled against the canonical ones: `imessage_export_3307038747_20260624.csv` (20,014 rows — *larger* than the 20,009-row canonical Kristin file) has not been diffed against the primary; the 765-message / 72-hour Aug-27–Sep-7 forensic count behind the block retraction comes from an in-conversation pass, not a filed export ("the export covering August 11–September 7 is not yet filed in raw/ — re-export and file it") **[ATTESTED, august-26-block-retraction gaps]**.

An un-diffed export is a Schrödinger source: it may contain rows that would move claims, and until the diff runs, every absence claim in its window is provisional. The protocol treats unfiled forensics as *counted-in-chat, not archived* — citable with the flag, not without it.

## The absence taxonomy

Not all zeros are the same kind of zero. Five grades, in descending strength — the wiki's standing vocabulary for what kind of "no" a claim is:

**Grade A — corroborated absence.** The instrument ran, the window is covered, *and* an independent source attests the negative. The old [[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]] claim was filed here ("no contact after Dec 10" + Dan's 2026-08-16 corroboration) — and still failed, because the corroboration covered what he had seen and the instrument covered the wrong channel. Grade A is the strongest grade and it is not a guarantee; it is the grade whose failure this page documents. Its honest form always names both legs: *no X observed via [instrument], [window]; corroborated by [source], [date]*.

**Grade B — instrument-bounded absence.** The instrument ran over the stated window and returned zero, no corroboration. Admissible, citable, and explicitly bounded: *no contact observed via [pull], [window]*. This is the protocol's canonical phrasing, and it is the grade most of the wiki's absence claims should be carrying.

**Grade C — unobserved absence.** Nobody looked. The channel was never pulled, the window was never exported, the request folder was never queried. This is not a claim at all — it is the absence of a claim, and the wiki's error has been writing it in claim-shaped sentences. The August messages, pre-September-12, were Grade C stated as Grade A.

**Grade D — declaration-shaped absence.** The "absence" is a speech act, not a measurement: the August-26 [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] block ("there was never a block"), the 129 severance declarations with 100% re-engagement. Declarations are *presence* claims about an intention, filed as absence claims about the world. The retraction page is the worked case; the rule here is that declaration-shaped claims are never cited as absence evidence without the enactment check.

**Grade F — true absence.** Exhaustive channel coverage, both directions, both devices — which nobody has and nobody is getting. Listed for completeness and as the asymptote: every honest absence claim is an approximation of this, and the approximation's error bars are the instrument's blind spots.

The Kristin case, graded in retrospect: the pre-September-12 claim was filed as Grade A, was actually Grade C on the Messenger channel (never pulled for requests) and Grade B on the phone channel (exports ran, zero observed two-way contact — which *did* hold, and still holds: the August attempt produced no two-way contact on any channel). The revision doesn't flatten the old claim to nothing; it re-grades it, channel by channel. That re-grading operation — not deletion, not defense — is the protocol's core move.

## The protocol

The standing recommendation. Every absence claim in the wiki, going forward:

**1. Name the instrument.** The canonical phrasing: *no [event] observed via [instrument], window [start] → [end]*. "No contact after Dec 10" becomes "no two-way contact observed via iMessage exports, 2025-12-10 → 2026-08-26." The sentence gets longer. The sentence gets true.

**2. Carry the ceiling.** Every absence claim states the instrument's last date and the pull's date. A claim without a ceiling is a claim about all time; no instrument covers all time.

**3. Scope blocks to channels.** Every block claim reads *on [channel]*. The [[wiki/mind/synthesis/block-unblock-loop|block-unblock-loop]]'s table gets channel bounds on every row; new rows are not added without one.

**4. Ask the request-folder question.** Every future Messenger pull records whether the request folder was queried and what its state was. A pull whose request-folder state is unrecorded has its zeros scoped to the inbox.

**5. Run the five-question preflight.** From the [[wiki/self/message-corpora/source-coverage-index|source-coverage-index]], extended: *what window, what handles, what columns, what ceiling* — and **who else had physical access to the device in this window**. The fifth question has no column; it is answered from content, and unanswered is a valid answer that gets flagged, not a gap that gets skipped.

**6. Price testimony.** Off-corpus corroboration carries its source and its prior: operator testimony on settled claims runs 57/100, and the August-26 block is the on-record case of the most trusted source failing on its home turf. Corroboration upgrades a Grade B to a Grade A; it does not upgrade it to a fact.

**7. Supersede, don't rewrite.** When an absence claim fails, the dated SUPERSEDED annotation goes on the old claim and the re-graded claim is stated alongside — the retraction page's correction protocol, applied to zeros. The wiki keeps the corpse: the history of being wrong about absence is the evidence that the protocol is working.

**8. Treat un-diffed and un-filed as provisional.** Counts from in-conversation forensic passes are cited as *counted-in-chat, not archived* until the export is filed in raw/ and diffed. The Aug-27–Sep-7 765-message count and the undiffed 20260624 [[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]] export are the current instances.

## The preflight, worked

The five-question preflight from the protocol, run end-to-end on the [[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]] absence claim as it stood on September 11, 2026 — the day before it collapsed. This is the worked example future pages copy.

**Claim under test:** "No contact after 10 Dec 2025."

**Q1 — What window?** The claim's window is 2025-12-10 → present (2026-09-11). Unbounded on the right — the claim is about all time since December, which no instrument covers. Flag: the claim needs a ceiling before it can be checked. Honest window: 2025-12-10 → 2026-09-11, stated.

**Q2 — What handles?** The iMessage exports cover +13307038747 (the phone channel). The Messenger pulls cover Dan's account inbox. Neither covers the Messenger request folder — which is not a handle but a *state* of the account, and the preflight's handle question misses it. This is why the protocol adds the request-folder question as a separate rule: the five questions are necessary but not sufficient for platform channels. On September 11 the answer to "what handles" was complete and the claim still failed, because the failure wasn't a handle — it was a folder.

**Q3 — What columns?** The iMessage exports carry direction and handle; the Messenger JSONL carries thread state including PENDING. The column that would have caught it (thread state = PENDING, sender = Kristin, dates = Aug 26) existed in the data model the whole time. The pull just never selected it. An instrument's columns are only as good as the query.

**Q4 — What ceiling?** The direction-reliable dump: 2025-08-10 — predates the claim's window entirely; any check run through it returns Grade-C zeros. The deep export: 2026-08-13 — covers most of the window, misses the last month. The thread exports: various, check each. The Messenger pull: 2026-09-12 hasn't happened yet (on Sep 11). The honest ceiling statement on Sep 11: *no instrument has queried the Messenger request folder at any date; the claim's Messenger leg is Grade C.*

**Q5 — Who else had physical access to the device in this window?** For the Kristin claim: not applicable in the Coles sense (her device, her messages — the question runs the other direction here: did *Dan* see the folder? His testimony + PENDING flag say no). But the question's mirror image matters for absence claims generally: *did the operator look at the surface the claim covers?* For request folders, the answer on Sep 11 was no — and "the operator didn't look" is a valid preflight answer that downgrades the claim, not a gap to be skipped.

**Verdict on Sep 11, had the preflight been run:** the claim grades as B on the phone channel (exports ran, zero two-way contact) and C on Messenger (request folder unqueried) — filed as A. The preflight wouldn't have *found* the August messages. It would have prevented the claim from being filed at a grade it couldn't support, which is the cheaper and more important win: the wiki would have been waiting for the request-folder query instead of being surprised by it. Surprise is the tax on unpriced ignorance. The preflight pays it in advance.

## What the protocol costs

Stated plainly, because a protocol nobody prices is a protocol nobody follows. The tax: every absence claim gets longer, weaker, and more tedious to write. "No contact after Dec 10" (five words, false) becomes "no two-way contact observed via iMessage exports, 2025-12-10 → 2026-08-26; one inbound attempt on Messenger message-requests 2026-08-26, unopened seventeen days; corroborated by operator testimony 2026-08-16 at 57/100 prior" (a paragraph, true). The wiki pays in readability for correctness. The [[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]] case is the receipt showing the old price was wrong.

The asymmetry the protocol prices: **presence is cheap, absence is expensive.** One row proves a message was sent; proving none was sent requires exhaustive channel coverage nobody has. The wiki has been spending its evidentiary budget backwards — stating absences at presence prices. The five grades are the corrected price list.

What would falsify the generalization — stated so the doctrine is killable, per house rules. Not a single case: one unpulled channel systematically checked and found empty doesn't kill the principle, it just makes one absence claim cheaper. What would bound the tax is *repeated* negative results: if the next five request-folder queries across five pulls all return nothing, the prior on "there is always another folder" weakens for this corpus, and the protocol's request-folder question becomes a formality rather than a live risk. The doctrine predicts the opposite — that unpulled channels will keep producing content, because the corpus keeps growing channels faster than it pulls them — and the next Messenger pull with a recorded request-folder state is the test. If it comes back empty, note it. If it comes back with seventeen days of PENDING messages, the doctrine earns its keep again.

A second falsifier, narrower: if the undiffed 20260624 Kristin export, when finally diffed, contains *nothing* the canonical file lacks — no missing December rows, no Suboxone logistics — then the class-7 warning ("un-diffed exports may move claims") gets one clean negative datapoint. The warning stands as a class regardless; the instance would be retired.

And the doctrine's own blind spot, stated because the page demands it of everything else: this protocol prices *text-channel* absence. Class 6 (out-of-band) is unpriceable by design — no pull will ever cover in-person contact, and the protocol's honest move there is the scope restriction (*text contact*, not *contact*), not a measurement. The wiki's claims about the world will always outrun its instruments; the protocol narrows the overhang, it doesn't close it.

## What each instrument's zero is worth

A worked grading of the zeros — because the protocol's first rule ("name the instrument") is only useful if the reader knows what that instrument's zero costs. Each entry: the instrument, what a zero from it rules out, what it doesn't, and the grade its zeros carry by default.

**Thread-specific iMessage exports.** A zero rules out: two-way text on that handle, in that window, as exported. It does not rule out: contact on any other handle or channel, contact outside the window (check the actual start/end, not the filename), contact deleted before export, or authorship disputes (handle ≠ person). Default grade: B, ceiling mandatory. The [[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]] "all now" file's zero on Aug 29–30 is the standing demonstration that the filename is not the window.

**The deep export.** A zero rules out: attributed iMessage/SMS text on any of 499 handles, 2011-03-19 → 2026-08-13. It does not rule out: anything after 2026-08-13 (the ceiling is now over a month behind the present), non-text channels, or shared-device authorship. Default grade: B — the strongest B in the corpus, because it is the only full-range attributed source, and still B, because of the ceiling and the fifth preflight question. Any absence claim dated after 2026-08-13-06:00 UTC that cites this file is citing a file that stopped.

**The direction-reliable dump via bin/mine-messages.** A zero rules out: nothing, for any window after 2025-08-10. The instrument's zeros for post-August-2025 windows are Grade C wearing Grade B's clothes — the tool returns them without an error, and the absence of an error has been read as the presence of a search. This is the single most dangerous zero in the corpus, because it is the default instrument's zero. The worked case is Kristin: a 22,018-message relationship returning silence. Any absence claim in the wiki dated after 2025-08-10 that was checked only through this instrument needs re-grading before it is cited again. That re-grading is queued, not done.

**The master CSV dump.** A zero rules out: presence in the dump's own rows. Given the [[wiki/people/suzanne-frank|Suz]] fourteenfold undercount, the dump's zeros are Grade B with a volume caveat: this instrument has been measured missing the majority of a high-volume handle's traffic, so a zero here is weaker than a zero from the deep export by a known, quantified margin. Use it for presence and volume floors; cite its zeros only with the floor flag.

**Messenger pulls.** A zero rules out: inbox messages in the pulled account, at pull time. It does not rule out: the request folder (unless the pull's log says the folder was queried — and no historical pull's log says that), deleted threads, or anything the counterparty did that left no row (calls, unsends). Default grade: B-inbox / C-requests. The honest citation of any historical Messenger zero: *no messages observed via Messenger inbox pull, [date]; request-folder state unrecorded.*

**The Facebook export.** A zero rules out: content in the archived threads, 2007–2022. Default grade: B with a hard ceiling. A 2026 absence claim against this export is Grade C — the instrument predates the claim's window entirely. The export's value for absence is at the biography layer (pre-2015), where it is the only instrument with any coverage at all.

**Gmail.** A zero rules out: email content, 2001–2014. Same ceiling logic as the Facebook export, one layer earlier. Relevant where the wiki's thinnest presence claims sit — which is exactly where absence claims are most tempting and least supportable.

**The Twitter scrape.** A zero rules out: public originals in the scrape, 2008-09-24 → 2026-09-01. It does not rule out: DMs, deleted tweets, pre-2013-08-17 content (the source-change invisibility), or activity (the ten-year silence correction: silence in announcing ≠ silence in doing). Default grade: B for public-record claims ("he never announced X"), C for everything else. The OSU-gig correction (a real February 2016 live set, fetched 2026-09-02, after the wiki had leaned toward treating it as fabricated) is the standing reminder that this instrument's retrieval is known-imperfect — the 2016 archive gap means public-record absence claims about 2015–2016 carry an extra flag.

**UI captures.** A zero rules out: content outside the frame — nothing else. Captures are presence instruments; their zeros are Grade C by construction. The September-12 screenshot's "no reply in frame" is the honest form: frame-bounded, not world-bounded. Never cite a capture's negative space without the frame bound.

**Operator testimony.** A zero rules out: whatever the operator looked at, remembers looking at, and is reporting honestly — three independent failure points, and the corpus has a documented instance of each (the request folder for the first, good-faith date displacement for the second, and the 57/100 settled-claim veracity for the third). Default grade: A-shaped, B-actual. Testimony upgrades an instrument zero to Grade A; it does not replace the instrument. The August-26 block is the reason this sentence exists.

**Address books.** A zero rules out: nothing about anyone who entered the record after the list was made. Grade C for post-expiry persons, always. The Kristin identity-verification gap is the worked case: two address books, both predating her, neither able to confirm or deny a surname.

## The honest phrasings, worked

The protocol's canonical phrasing applied to real claims, so future pages can copy the shape instead of reinventing it. Each pair: the filed form, then the honest form.

*"No contact after Dec 10, 2025."* → *"No two-way contact observed via iMessage exports, 2025-12-10 → 2026-08-26; one inbound attempt (five messages + missed call) observed via Messenger message-requests, 2026-08-26, unopened seventeen days; operator corroboration of the negative 2026-08-16 covers observed channels only."*

*"The block held eight months."* → *"The December 2025 block held on the phone channel, Dec 2025 → Sep 2026, as observed via iMessage exports; the contact surface was wider than the instrument (Messenger attempt, month eight); broken from the inside 2026-09-12 by the blocked party, reactively, within the hour of the pull."*

*"She never texted after the block."* → *"No inbound rows observed on the blocked handle's exports post-2025-12-10; Messenger request folder unqueried before 2026-09-12."* (This is the sentence that would have prevented the control-case error: the second clause was true the whole time and was never written.)

*"There was never a block."* ([[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]], Aug 26) → *"Operator retraction 2026-09-11: no block enacted; ~25 declarations Aug 15–19, zero executed; daily two-way texting Aug 27 → Sep 7 observed via forensic pass (counted-in-chat, export unfiled)."* Note the shape: a retraction is a positive claim about the record (declarations counted, texting observed), not just a negation. Negations alone don't carry grades; the evidence that replaced them does.

*"Zero severance signals in 41,073 of her messages."* → *"Zero severance signals observed across 41,073 rows attributed to her handles in iMessage exports; attribution caveat: ≥6 rows in crisis windows typed by a third party on her device (handle ≠ person); window ceiling at last export."*

*"The thread went silent / the channel is dead."* → *"No rows observed via [instrument], [handle], [window start] → [pull date]; request-folder/call/in-person channels uninstrumented."* "Dead" is a world-claim; the honest form is an instrument-claim. The wiki keeps using "dead" colloquially and means this.

*"He hasn't replied."* ([[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]], post-Sep-12) → *"No inbound reply observed via iMessage exports and Messenger pulls as of [pull date]; read-state and alternative channels unobserved."* This is a live claim — her silence since September 12 is the observable the control-case annotation waits on — and it is the claim most likely to be misquoted next. Its honest form has a pull date or it has nothing.

## The next pull: a worked procedure

The protocol is only as good as the next pull that follows it. This section is the checklist for the Messenger pull that would close the request-folder gap — written so the operator or the next agent can execute it without reinventing the questions.

1. **Pull the inbox** as before (the 2026-09-12 procedure is the reference implementation).
2. **Query the message-request folder explicitly.** Record: thread count, per-thread state (PENDING / approved / expired), oldest unopened thread, any threads from known handles. This is the step no historical pull took.
3. **Query filtered/spam folders** if the API exposes them — same class as requests, lower prior.
4. **Record the pull's timestamp in UTC** and the request-folder state as of that timestamp. The state is time-bound; a folder queried at 06:39Z is not a folder queried at 18:00Z.
5. **Diff against the 2026-09-12 pull.** New rows since last pull are the only rows that can move absence claims; the diff is the instrument's output, not the full dump.
6. **File the pull in raw/** with the request-folder state in the log. An unfiled pull is a Grade-C observation wearing Grade-B clothes (cf. the Aug-11–Sep-7 export).
7. **Re-grade the affected claims.** The [[wiki/people/ally-lubin|Ally]] post-08-19 silence, the [[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]] post-Sep-12 silence, and any other live absence claim get their pull dates updated or their grades dropped. The re-grading is part of the pull, not a follow-up.

The same procedure generalizes to any new channel the corpus instruments (IG DMs, if a pull is ever built): first pull establishes the baseline *including* the request-equivalent folder; the baseline's folder-state is recorded; subsequent pulls diff. The Kristin case happened because the first Messenger pull to look at requests was also the pull that found content in them. First looks should be empty by procedure, not by luck.

## Cross-channel absence: the general form

The [[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]] case is one instance of a general structure, and the page states the structure so the next instance is recognizable before its pull:

1. **A severance is enacted on channel X.** (December, iMessage.)
2. **Absence is claimed of the world.** ("No contact after Dec 10.")
3. **The claim is corroborated off-instrument.** (Dan, Aug 16.)
4. **Contact is attempted on channel Y.** (August, Messenger requests.)
5. **Channel Y is unpulled or unqueried.** (Seventeen days.)
6. **A pull surfaces Y.** (September 12, live in conversation.)
7. **The absence claim collapses to its instrument bound.** (Phone channel only.)
8. **The collapse itself becomes signal.** (Four texts in an hour — reactivity.)

Steps 1–3 are the setup the wiki keeps building. Steps 4–5 are the blind spot. Step 8 is the part the wiki didn't expect: the collapse of an absence claim is itself a presence event with causal power — seventeen days of unread messages produced nothing, one pull produced four texts. The instrument's blindness wasn't neutral; it was load-bearing. The loop feeds on signal, not on feeling, and the pull *was* the signal.

The general lesson for the survey: any absence claim currently sitting at steps 1–3, with an unpulled channel Y available to the counterparty, is a Kristin case waiting for its September 12. The survey's job was to list them. The protocol's job is to make step 5 impossible — the folder gets queried, the state gets recorded, the zero gets its bound — so that step 8, when it comes, arrives as a scheduled observation rather than a live-conversation ambush.
