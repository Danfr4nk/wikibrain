# Committee to Re-elect the President

*Committee to Re-elect the President* is Dan's Substack — creepy.substack.com, tagline "Live from Political Hellworld," bylined simply *dan frank*. It ran 10 posts from May 2023 to November 2024, totaling 6,961 words, and it is the most sustained piece of public political writing in his record: a leftist, hell-world-framed autopsy of the 2024 election cycle, written in real time, that opened with a founding thesis (Ron DeSantis was a mirage; Donald Trump owned the primary) and closed eighteen months later with the thesis confirmed and the joke dead.

This entry is the publication hub — its history, its shape, the modes he wrote in, why it stopped, and the voice-modeling kit built from it that got the voice wrong. The critical reading of the writing itself lives at [a critical reading](wiki/self/substack/committee-to-re-elect-the-president-critical-reading.md); the major theses each have their own entries. This page is the index.

## 1. What it is

The name is the whole joke and the whole argument at once. The Committee to Re-elect the President — CREEP, Nixon's 1972 reelection machine, the ur-scandal apparatus — resurrected as the title of a leftist blog arguing that Donald Trump was always going to win. It frames the 2024 cycle as a reelection campaign *for* Trump: not as hope, not as threat, but as an established fact being reported from inside [Hell World](wiki/mind/concepts/hell-world-frame.md). The tagline does the rest: "Live from Political Hellworld." The reporter isn't predicting doom; he's filing dispatches from it.

The byline is lowercase *dan frank*, lowercase like his own messages. No author bio, no headshot posture, no institutional affiliation. The whole thing is one man writing like himself, in public, for the first time in the record.

The assumed audience is legible from the vocabulary: politically engaged left-internet readers, the Chapo-to-Bulletin pipeline — people who already distrust cable news, already think the Democratic establishment is cowardly, already use "hell world" unironically, and who pay for Nate Silver but roll their eyes at him. He isn't explaining the basics to anyone. He's writing for readers who know what PredictIt is, who remember Lichtman's 2016 call, who get the Gatsby epigraph without a gloss. The performance is: *I'm one of you, but I do the math.*

The launch context matters. In May 2023, the media consensus he was writing against — documented in the postmortem a year and a half later — had Ron DeSantis "cast as a rising force in GOP" across "Fox to MSNBC," with "Rupert Murdoch push[ing] Fox News to champion the Florida governor, directing anchors to praise DeSantis and ignore (without antagonizing) Trump," and "most pundits expecting him to win the nomination by simply running a halfway competent campaign." Dan's founding move was to plant his flag against that entire consensus on day two of the publication's existence. The Substack wasn't launched into a vacuum; it was launched as an argument with the press, and the press was wrong.

And the math, from the first real post, was a thesis. On May 23, 2023 — the day after launch — he published *The DeSantis Delusion*, arguing that the media's rising-star narrative around Ron DeSantis was fantasy and that Trump's nomination was a *fait accompli*. That post is the founding document of the publication; everything in the next eighteen days is scaffolding around it, and everything after is either application or aftermath. Its own entry lives at [desantis-delusion-thesis](wiki/mind/politics/desantis-delusion-thesis.md); the November 2024 postmortem at [trump-was-always-going-to-win](wiki/mind/politics/trump-was-always-going-to-win.md).

**Total: 10 posts, 6,961 words, 2023-05-22 to 2024-11-12.** One recurring series (POLL WATCH, two installments). No guest posts, no interviews, no monetized tiers evident in the archive — it reads as a straight writing project, not a business.

The opening post deserves a note, because it sets the tone for everything. *538* (2023-05-22) is 18 words: "There are 538 total electors in the Electoral College. There are 538 days left until the 2024 Election." The numerology is the joke — the actual count from May 22, 2023 to Election Day is 533 days, not 538; he wanted the number to match more than he wanted the math to check. That's the publication's thesis in miniature: the numbers are load-bearing, the precision is negotiable, and the performance of confidence is the point. The deep-analysis audit flags it as "a wink, not a crime" — but it's also the first data point in the reliability pattern the corpus keeps confirming: the analysis is where he's reliable; the autobiography of the analysis is where he drifts.

The log itself tells the story before you read a word. Eight posts in nineteen days. Then four months of silence. Then one long post. Then thirteen months of silence. Then the postmortem. A man who writes when he has a thesis and goes quiet when he doesn't.

## 3. The three-phase arc

**Phase 1: The 19-day launch burst (May 22 – June 9, 2023).** Eight posts, and the voice is at its most performative — rapid-fire, joke-dense, visibly showing range. He opens with a numerology joke (*538*: "There are 538 total electors in the Electoral College. There are 538 days left until the 2024 Election." — 18 words, a wink), then drops the founding thesis the next day, then spends two weeks proving he can do every form: the sequel essay, the data roundup, the field guide, the explainer, the news reaction. The confidence is highest here because the thesis is freshest. This is a launch: *watch me do this.*

The burst is also where the publication's two theses crystallize. *The DeSantis Delusion* (2023-05-23) makes the primary call: Trump's 35%-ish floor of the GOP base, plus his proven willingness to sabotage any nominee who bests him, makes the nomination a *fait accompli* — "nothing short of a miracle" prevents it. *How To Ruin Your Political Career* (2023-05-26), published the day after DeSantis's catastrophic Twitter Spaces launch, extends the argument to the man himself: the run forfeits his likely-2028-frontrunner status and makes him "little more than a toy for Trump to bat around for a few months." The pudding-cup detail — DeSantis "claimed to have eaten a pudding cup by using two fingers instead of a spoon" — is the most devastating line in the whole DeSantis coverage, and it isn't analysis. It's vibe as prophecy.

**Phase 2: The silence breaks (October 17, 2023).** Four months after the burst, one post — the longest in the corpus at 1,196 words — and the voice has shifted audibly. *Brain Worms In Hell World* is about Israel/Palestine after October 7, and it is the moral center of the publication: longer paragraphs, fewer jokes, the profanity budget spent once and grimly. The "brain worms" bit — a gag from the launch phase — gets promoted to a diagnostic frame for atrocity. This is the only post where he's writing about the dead rather than the powerful, and the register knows it. It ends not with a punchline but with a flat verdict: "They certainly can't vote their way out of this." The Hell World frame's comedy dies in this post and is never fully revived. (Full treatment at [brain-worms-frame](wiki/mind/concepts/brain-worms-frame.md).)

**Phase 3: The postmortem (November 12, 2024).** Thirteen more months of silence, broken one week after the election by *Trump Was Always Going To Win*. The voice is flatter now — more essayistic, zero profanity, longer sentences, no Poll Watch energy. It's a victory lap by a man who isn't celebrating. The subtitle gives it away: "It's the down-ballot destruction that is so shocking and terrifying." He walks through the 2020 primary mechanics, the lawfare paradox ("Trump's criminal cases, rather than hobbling him, paradoxically strengthened his base"), the Democratic shambles, the popular-vote win, and lands on the flat line that ends the entire corpus: "Brace yourself for… something." Compare that with the burst's energy — "The 2024 election is going to absolutely, unequivocally batshit insane" — and you can hear eighteen months pass. The joke died; the frame survived.

## 4. The modes

He wrote in seven distinct modes across the ten posts, and the launch burst reads like a deliberate demonstration that he could do all of them. What each mode reveals:

**The essay.** The home mode — *The DeSantis Delusion*, *How To Ruin Your Political Career*, *Introduction to the 2024 Dem Primary*, *The Horserace (From Hell)*. Long paragraphs of mechanism, the steelman-detonate argumentative move (build the opponent's best case for three paragraphs, then: "But, here's the thing: none of this matters when you factor in Donald Trump as a variable in this equation"), the ominous one-liner closer. This is where the theses live. The essays are also where the persona is strongest — the insider who refuses the room, fluent in the pundit lexicon and using it to indict the enterprise. ("I became convinced of just how little the press truly understands Trump as a politician — or the movement he commands.")

**The explainer.** One post: *The Keys of Political Predictions* (2023-06-02), a walkthrough of Allan Lichtman's 13-keys forecasting system. The revealing part isn't the system — it's that he platformed a model that pointed the other way. The keys, as of mid-2023, "show that the current climate will favor Biden," relayed neutrally at a moment when his own gut was Trump-leaning (POLL WATCH had Trump +1.4 the same week). He didn't suppress the contrary indicator. That's either intellectual honesty or the analyst's refusal to commit — and the fact that both readings fit is itself the point.

**The data roundup.** POLL WATCH #1 (2023-05-26) and #2 (2023-06-03) — bullet-point aggregate roundups from RCP, FiveThirtyEight, and PredictIt, with screenshots and a one-paragraph kicker. No jokes, no metaphors, no persona beyond the curator. This is a different animal from the essays, and it's the mode that died first. POLL WATCH #2 opens with a Gatsby epigraph — "boats against the current, born back ceaselessly into the past" — over what he calls "more absolutely useless polling results." The series is already eating itself by installment two. (Its own entry: [poll-watch-series](wiki/self/substack/poll-watch-series.md).)

**The field guide.** *Introduction to the 2024 Dem Primary* (2023-05-30) — a tour of Biden, RFK Jr., and Marianne Williamson for readers whose "media diet is restricted to exclusively mainstream/legacy outlets." It's the most generous post in the corpus: he takes Kennedy's platform seriously enough to quote it at length before the verdict ("He's hoping that the stars align… He's going all in on the hopes that he pulls an inside straight"), and he exempts Williamson from scrutiny outright — "Will she win? Uh, no. But I like her and I'm always happy to see the party move left." "Crystal mommy" for Williamson is the nickname kill at its most affectionate.

**The news reaction.** *The Horserace (From Hell)* (2023-06-09), written "less than an hour" after Trump's federal indictment news broke — the fastest turnaround in the corpus. It's the post that most resembles conventional punditry, and the post that shows why he wasn't conventional: the question he asks isn't *what does the indictment mean for the polls* but *will GOP voters rally behind Trump or prefer a candidate who isn't facing time in federal prison?* — and he already knows the answer. "Strap in."

**The moral essay.** *Brain Worms In Hell World* stands alone in mode and in weight. The both-sides-indictment structure (each side gets a full paragraph of condemnation) inoculates him against the charge of tribalism before he lands where he lands: the occupation as "21st century apartheid," the asymmetry named honestly, and a prescription that is really a question — "I am sincerely curious to hear what the options available to Palestine are which would convince Israel to stop the killing." The moral clarity is real; the politics are bleak descriptivism. Nobody organizes in hell; you file dispatches.

**The retrospective.** *Trump Was Always Going To Win* — the postmortem, the scoreboard entry, the one post written knowing the outcome. It's also the post where the audit bites hardest: the "I concluded over two years ago" framing retrofits a certainty that the contemporary posts explicitly hedged (POLL WATCH #1: "it's still too early to look at polls with any reasonable amount of confidence"; the Keys post platforming a Biden-favoring model). The nomination call was stated with total confidence; the general-election certainty was acquired later than remembered. The full audit — 7 hits, 2 hedged, 1 error, 1 retrofitted certainty — lives in the critical reading and on [the-scoreboard](wiki/mind/synthesis/the-scoreboard.md).

## 5. The voice in brief

The full critical treatment — diction, syntax, persona, humor mechanics, the audit — is at [a critical reading](wiki/self/substack/committee-to-re-elect-the-president-critical-reading.md). The compressed version, for this hub:

He writes in three braided vocabularies: political science (*triangulate, fait accompli, lawfare, horserace*), internet poison (*brain worms, hell world, meme wars, crystal mommy*), and profanity used as a scalpel — exactly three instances across all 6,961 words ("clusterfuck," "fucking," "batshit"), each landing because the surrounding prose is clean. His signature paragraph rhythm is medium analytical sentence, medium analytical sentence, then detonation — a standalone fragment isolated as its own paragraph ("This isn't a prediction. This is a precedent…"). His signature argumentative move is steelman-detonate: build the opponent's best case honestly, then remove the load-bearing assumption. Every essay ends with a short, flat, prophetic one-liner: "Strap in." / "Brace yourself for… something."

What the summary can't carry — and what the critical reading does — is the arc: the voice moves audibly across eighteen months, from the burst's performative confidence to Brain Worms' grim register shift to the postmortem's flat dread. Any account of this voice that treats it as static is describing a different writer.

## 6. Why it died

The publication has been silent since November 12, 2024 — over a year and a half as of this writing, with no farewell post. The text supports three hypotheses, in descending order of evidence:

**1. The thesis was spent.** The founding insight — Trump owns the primary — was confirmed by events within months (DeSantis dropped out after Iowa, January 2024). The postmortem then closed the loop on the general. When your model says the outcome is decided and the outcome arrives, there is nothing left to argue. POLL WATCH died first and its death is the tell: the second installment already calls its own subject "absolutely useless polling results." When the horserace is over, the horserace blogger has no job. This is the hypothesis with the most textual support — the corpus reads like a man whose questions all got answered.

**2. He only writes on conviction.** The silences are watching, not absence. "With every development, my confidence grew" — the postmortem's line about the thirteen months before it — means he was tracking the whole cycle and writing nothing, because the view wasn't changing. The pattern of the corpus is write-on-thesis: the burst when the DeSantis thesis was fresh, Brain Worms when October 7 demanded a moral register, the postmortem when the election resolved. No thesis, no post. A publication that only publishes when the author has something to say will look dead most of the time. It isn't; it's dormant.

**3. The joke died.** The hell-world frame works as comedy when the doom is prospective — "the calm, presentable fascism of President DeSantis" is a joke you can tell in May 2023. After October 2023 (real corpses, the Brain Worms register shift) and November 2024 (a real victory, the postmortem's flat dread), the bit can't carry the weight. The last line of the corpus — "Brace yourself for… something," with that ellipsis — is the frame running on fumes. You can hear the comedy giving out. A publication whose engine is doom-as-entertainment can't survive the doom arriving.

All three can be true at once; they probably are. The spent thesis explains the timing, the conviction-writing explains the silences, the dead joke explains why nothing replaced it.

## 7. The danf.rtf voice-modeling kit

Found on his mother Suzanne Frank's computer: a file called danf.rtf containing a rhetorical profile of Dan's writing plus "Implementation Instructions for LLM" — a voice-modeling kit built from these Substack articles, with a 250-word vocabulary list and one AI-generated pastiche sample. Authorship is unconfirmed: it is not known who built it, when, or for what purpose. That is the seam, stated plainly — the kit exists, it is clearly built from this corpus (the device inventory is too specific to be coincidence), and everything else about its provenance is unknown.

The headline audit finding: the kit prescribes roughly **twenty times** the profanity he actually uses. Where the real corpus contains three profane instances in 6,961 words (about one per 2,400), the kit instructs "2-3 profane or slang terms per 300 words." The kit heard the loudness and missed the restraint — and restraint is the actual voice. Its pastiche swears constantly and reads as costume for exactly that reason.

The deeper misses compound it: the kit's sample arguments are all demolition, no construction — it never performs the steelman-detonate that is his actual signature move. It ignores the data-blogger entirely (the POLL WATCH mode doesn't fit the "formula," so the kit pretends it doesn't exist). It treats the voice as static across eighteen months, with no account of the register shift from launch confidence to postmortem dread. And its psychology section (INTP 5w4 stacking, "moderate limbic sensitivity") is horoscope — none of it derivable from ten political posts, flattering and unfalsifiable.

The verdict, stated plainly: the kit is a good mimicry guide for the surface and a poor account of the mind. It captures how he sounds; it misses how he thinks, how he rations profanity, and how the voice moved. Anyone generating "Dan" from the kit would produce a louder, dumber, more static version — which, given the kit's unknown authorship and its "Implementation Instructions for LLM" framing, is worth saying out loud. The full audit is section 8 of the critical reading.
