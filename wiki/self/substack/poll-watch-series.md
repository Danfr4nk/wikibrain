# POLL WATCH: the data-blogger mode

POLL WATCH was the data arm of *Committee to Re-elect the President* (creepy.substack.com) — the mode where Dan Frank stopped writing essays and started curating numbers. Aggregates in, conclusions out, contempt up front. Two installments, one week apart, then the mode was never used again: the first voice in his Substack to die, and the one the danf.rtf voice-modeling kit completely ignores.

This entry is the complete record of the series: every figure from both installments, dated and contextualized, plus the method, the predictive record, the autopsy of why it died, and the kit's blind spot. The publication itself is covered at [Committee to Re-elect the President](wiki/self/substack/committee-to-re-elect-the-president.md); the critical reading of all ten posts — including the scored prediction audit this entry draws on — lives at [Committee to Re-elect the President: a critical reading](wiki/self/substack/committee-to-re-elect-the-president-critical-reading.md).

## 1. The series stated

The corpus divides into three phases: a 19-day launch burst (eight posts, May 22–June 9, 2023), a four-month silence broken by one long essay (October 17, 2023), and a thirteen-month silence broken by the postmortem (November 12, 2024). POLL WATCH occupies two adjacent slots in the launch burst and nowhere else.

Installment #1, "POLL WATCH - #1," was published 2023-05-26 at 03:07 UTC (creepy.substack.com/p/poll-watch-1), ~520 words — the same day as "How To Ruin Your Political Career," making May 26 the only double-publish day in the corpus. Installment #2, "POLL WATCH #2," was published 2023-06-03 at 01:11 UTC (creepy.substack.com/p/poll-watch-2), ~191 words, eight days later. Together they are the only two posts in the entire Substack that lead with data rather than argument.

The format: bullet-point aggregate roundups drawn from RealClearPolitics, FiveThirtyEight, and PredictIt, each bullet anchored by a screenshot of the source chart, closed by a one-paragraph kicker. No jokes in the body. No metaphors in the body. No persona beyond the curator — a straight desk that reads like it was filed from a newsroom nobody else staffed. The voice in these two posts is recognizably his — the diction is clean, the syntax is clipped, the verdicts are delivered with a shrug — but the *moves* are alien to the rest of the corpus. Everywhere else he is the essayist: steelman-detonate structures, ominous one-line closers, the internet-poison slang braided into political science. Here he is the aggregator: here are the numbers, here is what they say, here is why you shouldn't trust them too much, here is the next week's number moving half a point.

And the framing is self-undermining from the first sentence of the first installment. "Election 2024 has OFFICIALLY begun," it opens, and then, immediately: "while it's still too early to look at polls with any reasonable amount of confidence in their individual results…it's worth the effort to start following now as the trends begin to emerge." That is the whole series in microcosm: the data presented with open, stated contempt for the data's predictive value, published anyway because watching the trend lines form is worth the effort. The second installment takes this posture to its endpoint — it opens with a Fitzgerald epigraph draped over a sentence calling the entire enterprise useless — and then the series dies. Two posts. No third week. No farewell. The May–June burst ended, the Substack went quiet until October, and the data-blogger never returned.

The complete-log doctrine applies with unusual force here, because the series is nothing *but* a log: no thesis to extract, no argument to summarize. Sections 2 and 3 are the complete log — every figure from both installments, dated, in the order the posts present them, with nothing removed. The summary above sits above the log; it never replaces it.

## 2. Complete log: POLL WATCH #1 (2023-05-26)

Published 2023-05-26T03:07:39 UTC. Byline: dan frank. Opening caveat, verbatim in substance: it is "still too early to look at polls with any reasonable amount of confidence in their individual results" — the lede of the series is a disclaimer. What follows are two halves: the aggregates ("Getting up to speed"), then the week's new releases.

**The aggregates.**

- **RCP general election (Trump vs. Biden):** RealClearPolitics has tracked 88 general-election polls from reputable agencies, beginning in August 2022. Across the entirety of those results, Trump leads Biden by **+1.4 points**.
- **RCP GOP primary:** RCP has tracked 97 GOP primary polls, beginning in November 2021. Trump leads with **53.9%** support — a **+32.6-point** lead over the second-place finisher, Ron DeSantis, at **21.3%**.
- **FiveThirtyEight aggregate:** sampling around 40 results from pollsters rated C+ or higher. Trump leads by **+33.3 points**, with **54.0%** support to DeSantis at **20.7%**. The post presents this as "a similar spread" — the two aggregates, RCP's all-pollster average and 538's quality-weighted one, agree with each other to within fractions of a point: 53.9/32.6 vs. 54.0/33.3.
- **PredictIt, GOP nomination:** Trump "yes" shares at **58¢**, DeSantis "yes" shares at **32¢** — a **+26-point** (cent) spread. The post's reading: "Betting markets also corroborate this substantial gap."
- **PredictIt, general election:** Biden **45¢** / Trump **32¢** / DeSantis **22¢**, with the mechanics explained in the post itself (see section 4): *"Note: due to the way these markets function, it is difficult to gauge general election results at this stage. After a nominee is chosen, capital from shares of inactive candidates will be reallocated to the nominee, reflecting the state of the race."*

**The week's new releases.**

- **Quinnipiac, fielded 5/16–5/22:** "In a hypothetical general election matchup for president, the race is neck and neck, with President Joe Biden receiving **48 percent** and Trump receiving **46 percent** among all registered voters. These results are unchanged since late March." When Biden is matched against DeSantis: DeSantis **47 percent**, Biden **46 percent** — "essentially unchanged since late March."
- **Quinnipiac, GOP side:** "Former President Donald Trump remains at the top of the list of declared or potential candidates with **56 percent** support among Republican and Republican leaning voters, followed by Florida Governor Ron DeSantis with **25 percent**." A 31-point margin inside a single pollster's sample, consistent with the aggregates above.
- **Marquette, published 5/18:** favored Trump by **+5 points** in both decided and leaning support, a phone survey of **750+ likely voters**.

**The kicker.** The installment closes: "There's no clear picture to be painted from these polls just yet, other than to say that somehow Donald Trump doesn't seem to have shed as much support as many people had thought." The word doing the work is *somehow* — a full syllable of disbelief inside an otherwise flat sentence, the one flicker of the essayist's voice in a post that otherwise refuses it. Then the outlook, two threats named: "With a new challenger in Ron DeSantis and the murmurs of special counsel Jack Smith's investigation into the Mar-A-Lago document scandal…we'll see if Republican voters can hold on through historic amounts of incoming."

Note what the post does not do: it does not convert the Trump lead into *electoral* confidence. The +1.4 is a trend to watch, not a forecast to bank — and that restraint matters for section 5.

## 3. Complete log: POLL WATCH #2 (2023-06-03)

Published 2023-06-03T01:11:37 UTC. Byline: dan frank. ~191 words — the shortest post in the corpus, less than half the length of #1, and the only installment that consists of the aggregate bullets alone, with no "new releases" section. The series is already contracting.

The opener, in full: "Week 2 of this series blesses us with more absolutely useless polling results. But we must beat on — boats against the current, born back ceaselessly into the past."

That is the Gatsby epigraph — the last line of *The Great Gatsby* — laid over a sentence dismissing the entire format as useless. The series is eating itself by installment two: the curator is still filing the numbers, but the voice has stopped pretending the filing matters. The "must" is the joke — there is no must, the series is eight days old, nobody subscribed for this, and yet: beat on.

**The numbers.**

- **RCP general election:** "Trump has grown his lead in the RCP aggregate tracking. He gained almost half of a percentage point this week to widen his lead to **+1.8 points** ahead of Biden." From +1.4 to +1.8 in a week: a ~0.5-point move, small enough to be noise in any individual cycle reading, large enough to note the direction.
- **RCP GOP primary:** "DeSantis got a nice bump in his RCP average, but still trails Trump by **−30 points**." The bump is acknowledged and immediately buried — the gap that read +32.6 a week earlier now reads ~30, and the direction of the *story* is unchanged: DeSantis is thirty points underwater.
- **FiveThirtyEight:** "still has Trump **+33** ahead of DeSantis." The .3 is dropped in the telling; the point stands.

No PredictIt this week. No new-poll digests. Just the three aggregates and the closer.

**The closer.** "It will be interesting to see whether or not DeSantis is able to do anything to begin closing the gap in the next few weeks. If he has ANY chance to stay in this race, he needs to catch up now before the debates — otherwise he's going to be comic fodder for a confident Trump. His best shot is to walk on the debate stage armed with the pitch that his campaign is ascendant and only lagged before his official announcement."

Two things to register. First, the all-caps "ANY" — the voice straining through the curator mask, the essayist's certainty leaking into the data desk. Second, the postmortem knowledge: DeSantis never closed the gap, was comic fodder on the debate stage, and the ascendance pitch never materialized. #2's contribution to the predictive record is the week-over-week confirmation — the numbers said it twice, moving in the same direction.

What happened next: DeSantis's campaign announced its official launch May 24, 2023 — the Twitter Spaces "DeSaster" — between the two installments. POLL WATCH #2 is therefore written *after* the launch that the sibling essay "How To Ruin Your Political Career" (same-day as #1) anatomized as career suicide. The "nice bump" is the launch bump, and the post dismisses it in the same breath it reports it. The series knew.

## 4. The method: aggregates, markets, and stated caveats

The method is visible enough to be audited, which is rarer than it sounds in amateur political writing. Three sources, each with its own epistemic status, and the caveats stated in the text rather than buried:

**RealClearPolitics averages.** The workhorse. RCP's unweighted poll-of-polls, 88 general-election polls (from August 2022) and 97 GOP primary polls (from November 2021) at the time of #1. The advantage the post implicitly claims is sample size — the entirety of the tracked results, not cherry-picked individual surveys. The limitation RCP is famous for (no quality weighting; house-effect-laden pollsters counted the same as gold-standard ones) goes unmentioned, but #1 pairs RCP with 538 precisely to cover that flank.

**FiveThirtyEight's quality-weighted aggregate.** "~40 results from pollsters rated C+ or higher" — the C+ floor stated in the post itself. The check the post is running is visible: do the unweighted average and the quality-weighted average agree? They do, almost exactly (Trump +32.6/53.9 vs. +33.3/54.0). When two independent aggregation philosophies converge to within tenths of a point, the curator treats the spread as real. This is the most data-literate move in the series, and it's never explained as a move — it's just done, in the bullet order.

**PredictIt betting markets.** Cited in #1 for both the nomination (Trump 58¢ / DeSantis 32¢) and the general (Biden 45¢ / Trump 32¢ / DeSantis 22¢), with the mechanics *explained*, not just quoted. The general-election note — that shares of inactive candidates will reallocate once a nominee is chosen — is the post refusing to let the reader misread 45¢ as a probability of Biden winning. Most casual market-citers in 2023 would have written "Biden is the favorite at 45¢" and moved on. POLL WATCH #1 tells you why that number is mechanically misleading at this stage. The full instrument — betting markets as a prediction device, how he reads them, where they sit in his epistemic hierarchy — is documented separately at [prediction markets instrument](wiki/mind/concepts/prediction-markets-instrument.md); this entry covers only what the series itself shows.

**The stated caveats.** Two, both in the lede of #1: early polls can't be read "with any reasonable amount of confidence in their individual results"; the value is in "the trends begin to emerge." The series practices what it preaches — #2's entire content is trend comparison. Individual polls get named (Quinnipiac, Marquette) but never hyped; the Marquette +5 appears as one data point in a paragraph that closes by saying there's no clear picture. The "absolutely useless polling results" line in #2's opener is the caveat curdling into the house style — contempt for early polling's predictive value isn't a footnote to the series — it's the series' stated operating premise.

What the method never includes: polling crosstabs, demographic deep-dives, fundraising numbers, or any qualitative reporting. It's a thin instrument by design — aggregates in, conclusions out — and the thinness is the point. The curator isn't building a model; he's reading the scoreboard. [The Scoreboard](wiki/mind/synthesis/the-scoreboard.md) is the larger frame for how this habit sits inside his political analysis overall.

## 5. The predictive record, scored honestly

The audit method applies: every falsifiable claim, checked against what happened, hits and misses labeled without flattery. Scored on its own terms:

**The primary gap: +32.6 / +33.3 / −30 (May–June 2023). ✅ Hit, and the best call in the corpus.** In May 2023 the media consensus — which the sibling essay "The DeSantis Delusion" documents at length: "From Fox to MSNBC, DeSantis was cast as a rising force" — had DeSantis as a live threat to Trump's renomination. POLL WATCH didn't argue the mechanism — the sibling essays did that (Trump's 35% floor plus his willingness to sabotage defectors); it supplied the evidence ledger: two independent aggregates and the betting markets all reading a thirty-plus-point gap, week after week, unmoved by the challenger's launch. DeSantis dropped out in January 2024 after the Iowa caucuses. The gap was the story, and the gap was right. See [the DeSantis-delusion thesis](wiki/mind/politics/desantis-delusion-thesis.md) for the full mechanism the numbers corroborated.

**The general-election lean: Trump +1.4 (RCP, late May), +1.8 (early June). ✅ Directional hit.** Trump won the 2024 popular vote. The +1.4→+1.8 drift was the right direction, and #1's kicker — "somehow Donald Trump doesn't seem to have shed as much support as many people had thought" — held for the entire cycle: the indictments, the convictions, the document scandal named in the closing paragraph all failed to dent the support the aggregates were showing. The critical reading scores this a hit "directionally," and the direction is what the series claimed — no more.

**The retrofitting problem: what the series does NOT support.** This is the audit's sharpest finding and it belongs on this entry, because POLL WATCH is the evidence the retrofit leans on. The November 2024 postmortem ("Trump Was Always Going To Win") claims: "I concluded over two years ago that a Trump general-election victory was the most likely outcome." The contemporary text says the opposite about certainty. POLL WATCH #1's lede: "it's still too early to look at polls with any reasonable amount of confidence in their individual results." The "Keys of Political Predictions" post (June 2, 2023): the Allan Lichtman framework "will favor Biden." The nomination call was stated with total confidence; the general-election call was, in the May–June text, explicitly hedged. What POLL WATCH actually recorded was a *lean* — Trump ahead by a point and a half in the averages, support not shedding — plus a refusal to convert the lean into a prediction. The November post compresses "I nailed the primary + the polls leaned Trump + my confidence grew all cycle" into "I concluded two years ago." Also note the arithmetic: May 2023 to November 2024 is 18 months, not "over two years" — a six-month date displacement on his own archive, from the man whose standing rule is "trust the corpus over me on years." The numbers on this entry are the corpus; the corpus says: great primary call, directionally-right general lean, certainty acquired later than remembered. Good-faith misremembering that flatters the teller — the reliability note made flesh.

**Net for the series:** two numbers series, both pointing the right way; one explicit hedge honored in the contemporary text; one retrofitted certainty caught by the archive. The honest verdict: POLL WATCH was right about what it claimed and modest about what it didn't, and its modesty is what lets the audit clear it.

## 6. Why it died

POLL WATCH ran twice and never returned. The autopsy has four converging causes, and the first is visible in the text of the second installment itself.

**1. It was euthanized from inside.** The #2 opener — "Week 2 of this series blesses us with more absolutely useless polling results. But we must beat on — boats against the current, born back ceaselessly into the past" — is a joke about the series' own futility, printed as the series' mission statement. This isn't a wobble; it's the format's premise arriving at its conclusion. #1 said early polls can't be trusted individually, only as trends. #2 says the trend is: nothing changed (half a point of drift, still thirty points back), and the numbers remain useless. Once the curator has filed two weeks of "no clear picture," the third week has nothing to file but the same sentence a third time. The Gatsby epigraph is the tell: you don't quote the last line of *The Great Gatsby* — the novel's famous image of futile striving against an irreversible current — over a data series you plan to continue. You quote it when the striving has already been diagnosed as futile. The series knew it was dead in week two; week three just never got the memo typed up.

**2. The thesis was spent.** POLL WATCH existed to answer one question: is DeSantis actually a threat to Trump's renomination? By June 3 the answer was no — not as a thesis to argue, but as a number to read off two aggregates and a betting market, twice. The May 24 launch bump ("a nice bump in his RCP average") arrived and changed nothing: still −30. When the outcome reads as a fait accompli, the horserace bores the analyst first. He writes when he has a thesis and goes quiet when he doesn't — and POLL WATCH is the purest instance: the format consumed its own subject in fourteen days and had no second subject. There was never a "POLL WATCH" for the Democratic primary, for the general, for anything else. The instrument was single-purpose, and the purpose expired.

**3. The burst ended.** The May–June 2023 burst — eight posts in nineteen days — ended June 9 with "The Horserace (From Hell)," a news-reaction essay about the federal indictment. The Substack then went quiet for four months until "Brain Worms In Hell World" (October 17), then thirteen months until the postmortem. POLL WATCH didn't die alone; it died with the burst. The weekly-series premise needed a weekly cadence the publication never sustained again. A "weekly polling roundup" on a Substack that publishes monthly-at-best is a standing commitment to a format the author had already stopped believing in.

**4. The mode that fit him least.** This is the speculative layer, and it should be labeled as such. The curator persona — bullets, screenshots, no jokes — was the mode furthest from his natural register. The essayist's voice leaks through in #1's *somehow* and #2's *ANY* and the Gatsby line; by #2 the leak is the whole post. A man who writes 890-word thesis essays with ominous one-line closers is not going to file "Trump +1.8, DeSantis −30" every Friday for eighteen months. The data-blogger mode demanded he suppress everything that made the writing his; he complied twice, found the suppression intolerable, and went back to essays. The mode that died first was the mode that fit him least.

None of this is a failure. The series did its job — it put the numbers on the record, in public, dated, screenshot-sourced, while the consensus was still casting DeSantis as a rising force — and then it stopped, having nothing left to say. POLL WATCH's honesty includes its own termination.

## 7. The kit's blind spot

The danf.rtf file — a rhetorical profile of Dan's writing plus "Implementation Instructions for LLM," built from these Substack articles, found on his mother Suzanne Frank's computer, authorship unconfirmed — is the voice-modeling kit. The critical reading audits it against the real thing in its ninth section, and the audit's findings belong here too, because POLL WATCH is the kit's largest single omission.

**What the kit describes:** essay-Dan. The braided vocabularies (political science, internet poison, scalpel profanity), the steelman-detonate argumentative moves, the ominous one-line closers, the persona — the critical reading's phrase — of "the clear-eyed bastard." The kit prescribes this register as *the* Dan voice and gives implementation instructions for reproducing it.

**What the kit ignores:** the entire data-blogger mode. POLL WATCH #1 and #2 are bullet-point aggregate roundups with screenshots and a one-paragraph kicker. No jokes in the body, no metaphors in the body, no persona beyond the curator. The kit's implementation instructions would never produce a post that disclaims its own numbers in the lede — the instructions are built for the arguer, and the curator is a different animal wearing the same byline.

This matters for two reasons. First, the mode is not marginal: the two Poll Watch posts are ~700 words combined, nearly a tenth of the entire 7,200-word corpus, and they contain the corpus's best-evidenced claims (the thirty-point primary gap, the early general lean). A voice model that can't write the data-blogger mode can't write a tenth of the corpus. Second, the omission reveals what the kit is *for*: it was built to reproduce the entertaining Dan — the DeSantis-dunking essayist — not the working Dan. The curator is the less quotable half of the writer, and the kit's silence about him is a selection bias with a signature: it heard the loudness and missed the restraint. The critical reading's sharpest datapoint on this: the kit prescribes "2-3 profane or slang terms per 300 words," while the actual corpus contains three profane instances in ~7,200 words — one-twentieth the prescribed rate. The kit doesn't just miss the data-blogger; it mismeasures the essayist, too.

**The corpus contains two Dans; the kit captures one.** The essayist argues; the curator tabulates. Any model of this writer that can't do both is a model of the performance, not the person — and POLL WATCH, all 700 words of it, is the proof that the person is bigger than the performance.
