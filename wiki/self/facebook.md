# Facebook Archive (ihatedanfrank)

On Tuesday, January 9, 2007 — in the pre-smartphone era, when Facebook was still a text box under a profile photo and a status update was the whole medium — an 18-year-old from Uniontown, Pennsylvania opened an account under a handle he would carry for the rest of his documented life: **ihatedanfrank**. Fifteen years later, on September 5, 2022, he downloaded the whole thing as a 79 MB export: the identity record, 403+ message threads, roughly 650 friends, 229–234 liked pages, and some 627 media items across 17+ albums, covering 2007–2022.

This page is the hub: the identity record and the persona synthesis. The corpus detail lives in four data-exhibit sub-pages — messages, posts and media, friends, and interests — each summarized below with its real figures.

| [[wiki/self/facebook/friends]] | ~650-friend network, add/remove history |
| [[wiki/self/facebook/interests]] | Likes, events, groups, ads profile, off-Facebook tracking |

What makes the export load-bearing for the wiki is that it is a bridge specimen: the earliest posts are pre-smartphone text statuses — Harry Potter, Halo, Fall Out Boy at TRL — and the same download contains Facebook's own commercial model of its owner, 339 off-Facebook activity files and a derived ads-interest profile sitting next to his self-presentation. In the terms of [[wiki/mind/synthesis/millennial-digital-witness]], this is the theory instantiated in one person's data: the full arc from pre-smartphone early adoption to repossessing the commodified record.

The handle itself belongs to [[wiki/mind/synthesis/the-name-is-the-instrument]]: ihatedanfrank is the loaded-name instrument pointed inward — proof the pattern is a permanent operating feature rather than a phase.

## Messages: 403 threads and one deep channel

The messages export holds **403 unique threads** containing message_1.html, split into 271 inbox, 128 filtered, 3 requests, plus legacy, your_messages, blocked, and secret-conversation categories. Date range runs from a 2011 legacy sample through 2021–22. Each thread is Facebook's per-thread HTML format: participants, timestamps, content, and system events — video calls, theme changes, nickname sets, attachments. A heavy filtered layer reads as low-engagement or spam-adjacent, which is part of the signal rather than its absence: even the spam is archived.

The primary documented node is the Annie "Eggie" thread (inbox/anneulmer_suopdbnvqg), covering roughly 2017–2021 — intimate, logistical, affectionate, conflict-disclosing, the richest preserved attachment record in the corpus. The system events tell the micro-history of the relationship's digital form: "You set the nickname for Anne Ulmer to Eggie" on August 20, 2018, a 23-second video call in February 2018, chat-theme changes. The text extracts mix baby talk ("i am wonewy," "I am peepy," "Weally weally kute," "Yowr kute," July 2017) with NYC daily logistics ("Getting on Q now," "i'm outside at the hotspot waiting for menore," "my phone is not charging" — October 13, 2021), a Reddit r/nycr4r link (April 2019), and family-conflict disclosure: "Where are you? I want out of here" / "And it really upset me" alongside the Target story with her mother and sister — the sister saying "maybe you should move back home with mom and dad" (July 2017). One thread line, "IP Address: 174.140.71.3," is a reminder of what the export format quietly captures. The thread's 2017–2021 window matches the profile's "since November 28, 2015" relationship timestamp and feeds the attachment record at [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer]] and [[wiki/mind/synthesis/attachment-trauma-bond]].

Below the Annie node, real conversation is short, logistical, and scene-based. **RJ Ritchey** (golf + politics, 2019–22): country-club talk ("We were playing the country club about a month ago...", "Lmao I found all of our slow motion footage"), the caddie-door request ("I’m thinking about coming back for a few months and caddying for the summer. Would you ask your dad if he could get me in the door at Laurel Valley?"), and a politics thread where both sides state the anti-bullshit position — RJ: "I feel like I'm far from falling for that or any other mainstream political bullshit... I'd rather know the truth and find a higher meaning in life than to be ignorant and a willing pawn"; Dan: "yeah dude i just think it's such an important time to make sure people don't fall for this right wing bullshit." **Marc Charles** (golf, 2018): equipment talk and the working-amateur's complaint — "tiger was on some franko customs before he made the jump to taylormade," "nothing worse than an aggressively competitive amateur golfer," "the only reason i turned my 90's into 85's is that i was able to buy new clubs," "it's fucking HARD to level up at our skill level." Shorter real threads: **Sean Teets** ("Mushys and k," a Nintendo Switch Zelda sale for $200, "looking for tax or strpz"), **Charles Davenport** in the 2010 NYC-move era ("how everything n the nyc?", "im here...wats your #", "i'm in need of another run down there"), and a July 5, 2013 Ally Lubin thread of short dark-humor banter (see [[wiki/people/ally-lubin]]).

The noise floor is its own dataset: a 2011 legacy iPhone5 phishing message ("hello, because you are also a close friend of EJ I am going to tell you exactly what I discovered. that website ... is giving away an iPhone5 ... hurry up before they close it."), and Rick Hamborsky message-request spam — repeated suspicious attachments from keqingsong/mxgpujv-style domains plus "Your drivers license is at UCC" across 2018–2021.

The shape of the whole exhibit confirms [[wiki/mind/concepts/contact-gini]] from the public-platform side: roughly 650 friends and 400+ message threads of breadth, with real depth concentrated in a handful of nodes — above all the Annie thread, the deep counter-example to the wide-but-shallow surface. Volume functions as ambiguous tether; closure happens in few channels ([[wiki/people/annie-ulmer]], [[wiki/mind/concepts/attachment-model]]). The comms style is consistent with the core voice — short, staccato, sometimes typo-laden ("wonewy," "kute") — and the ironic armor visibly softens in the private Annie channel.

## Friends: ~659 nodes across three eras

The friends exhibit documents **~650–659 unique friends** spanning roughly April 2009 to September 2022 — from Uniontown roots through the NYC years to the scene years. The export breaks into friends.html (the core list), removed_friends, friends_you_see_less, rejected friend requests, friend_requests_sent and received, people_who_follow_you, who_you_follow, and people_you_may_know. The pruning categories are part of the data: a network that was edited over time, with algorithmic suggestion layers ("people you may know") visible as an artifact of the platform's growth model.

The roster reads as a fossil of contact surface area: family core (Rick Frank, Vanessa, Alan Frank, plus Andy, Becca, Wendy), core people (Jerad Friedline, Tom Maison), the NYC and music-scene years (comedy clubs, EDM, festivals), and the later golf/Pittsburgh re-engagement threads (RJ Ritchey, Marc Charles), with Annie Ulmer as the primary relationship node. Recent activity stamps in the export — Alexandra Lubin in September 2019, Alexis Armel in November 2019, Tom Maison in June 2019 — date the network's late life into the early 2020s.

The standing **Message Thread Rule** was derived in part from this corpus: anyone with documented back-and-forth in a message thread (Facebook or iMessage/texts) receives a dedicated wiki/people/ page. The rule produced or expanded pages for RJ Ritchey, Marc Charles, Sean Teets, Charles Davenport, and Ally Lubin, among others — the network exhibit is what lets the wiki distinguish a name in a friends list from a person with a documented channel.

The network reading mirrors the messages reading: high outward digital social reach across three eras, but the visible exported depth is low and spam-heavy in the samples — the friends list as breadth, the Annie thread as the documented depth exception. That is [[wiki/mind/concepts/contact-gini]] from the roster side, and the attachment-model reading treats the whole graph as a safe/ambiguous tether layer: volume without proportional exported depth, digital connections low-stakes beside the real family and romantic conflict disclosed in the Annie thread.

## Two valves: the Twitter parallel

Facebook and Twitter are two public valves for one person under different platform rules: Facebook registered January 9, 2007, Twitter in September 2008, both running continuously for the documented life. Facebook is the identity valve — profile, relationships, visual life, the curated network. Twitter is the real-time valve — the coverage stream, the practitioner's liveblogging (the Sandy liveblog of October 2012 sits on the Twitter side of the witness record).

Because both run continuously under different rules, a disagreement between them is checkable rather than a matter of which memory to trust. The Brooklyn move-date contradiction is the working example: the two exports disagree, and the disagreement resolves by field type, not by loyalty — documented in [[#Conflicts in the record]]. The parallel runs the other way as mutual corroboration too: where Facebook transcribes something with a real date behind it (Ishlab Studio, March 2010; the Annie timestamp, November 28, 2015), the tweet archive's dated stream independently holds it.

## The ihatedanfrank persona

The handle is a fifteen-year deliberate ironic persona, not a throwaway. Registered at 18 in 2007, the earliest posts already show the two through-lines that survive every later era: genuine music obsession (Fall Out Boy, Say Anything, Radiohead, Lil Wayne — swooning posted without irony) and reflexive scam-detection (calling out a group giveaway as fraud within weeks of joining). The first is the authentic signal that later becomes the electronic/bass production identity ([[wiki/interests/music/overview]]); the second is the earliest documented instance of the forensic disposition ([[wiki/mind/concepts/forensic-method]]).

## Conflicts in the record

### [2026-09-02] `places lived` is not a dated field

The Identity table above once introduced this page with the claim that *every* field cross-checked against [[wiki/self/context-core]]. One does not. `places lived` gives **Brooklyn NYC from January 3, 2010**. The [[wiki/self/twitter|tweet archive]] has Dan in Florida for another eight weeks: *"moving to brooklyn in 9 days"* on February 20, 2010, *"last day in errrlando…hangin in a u-haul store"* on the 27th, *"peace out, florida. it's been real."* on the 28th.

The export contradicts itself, and the other half is right. The `work history` row in the same table starts **Ishlab Studio in March 2010** — and `dan@ishlab.com` first appears in a tweet on March 24, 2010. Where Facebook is transcribing something with a real date behind it (a job, a relationship timestamp, a birthday) it holds. Where it is rendering a value Dan typed into a profile form at an unknown later moment, it is memory, and it drifted by two months.

The distinction is worth carrying beyond this page: **a Facebook profile field is testimony, not a record.** `relationship … since November 28, 2015` is a system-generated timestamp and can anchor a period page. `places lived: from Jan 3, 2010` is free text and cannot. They sit in the same table and look identical. The correct move date is established at [[wiki/timeline/periods/2010s]].

## Assessment

This export is the strongest independent [DOC] confirmation of the biographical spine in the self corpus — birth date, Uniontown → Brooklyn, Full Sail, the work chain, the exact Annie relationship timestamp — and the complete public fossil layer: the self-presentation of 2007–2022 alongside Facebook's own commercial model of the owner, downloaded and repossessed in September 2022.

As an exhibit for [[wiki/mind/synthesis/millennial-digital-witness]], it is the full arc in one object: pre-smartphone text statuses from an 18-year-old through the tracked-commerce era, ending with the owner exporting the platform's surveillance of himself. The witness thesis's psychometric fit — archival memory, low social participation, high pattern recognition — is what makes this archive exist in the first place: the disposition that kept everything is the disposition the theory describes. Limits: visible message traffic begins in 2011 (earlier threads likely truncated), image captions are sparse (the photos carry the data, metadata carries the dates), and the raw source target is unresolved in the current corpus — the figures here come from the parsed exhibits. No contradictions with the existing self corpus beyond the dated `places lived` field above.
