## Theory (Stage 2 — profile-vocabulary statement)

A relationship's binding state is **formalized in a written instrument, and the instrument governs**: the charter opens, closes, and reopens the institution; the certificate executes standing; signed/unsigned is a binary cliff. Unsigned standing is not partial membership — it is non-standing, and the record treats it that way: the honorific withholds, the tier holds, the signatures are still to collect. The document is not a souvenir of a state that lives in behavior; it is the channel the state lives in.

The claim survived Stage 2 translation intact (see Genesis for the one formulation that died here).

## Rivals & discriminators (Stage 3 — run first, per protocol)

**The profile-weighted rival: the souvenir account.** Documents are decorative records of states that live in behavior — the charter follows the relationship, the certificate commemorates standing already earned. The instrument describes; it does not govern.

**Discriminating observation:** whether document state governs subsequent behavior *independently* of behavior — cases where the instrument's state and the behavioral state diverge, and the record sides with the instrument. The souvenir account predicts behavior carrying standing regardless of paperwork; the charter thesis predicts the paperwork deciding.

**Held-data check — the discriminator favors the thesis on all three legs:**
1. **The closure.** Sep 26's closure order stopped the institution cold — no casting, no roster work — for a week, until the Oct 3 reopening order. Behavior tracked the instrument's state, not the other way around; if the charter were a souvenir, the week would have run on momentum.
2. **The unsigned six.** GC-2026-002's six outstanding signatures withhold the GOON honorific while roster-running behavior proceeds. Behavior and document diverge; the record sides with the document.
3. **The entrenchment clause.** Article 1 installs its own unamendability — a rule whose content is about the rule's persistence. A souvenir has no use for an entrenchment clause; a governing instrument does.

No interior term was borrowed to beat the rival: the legs are dated instruments, dated signatures, and dated orders.
