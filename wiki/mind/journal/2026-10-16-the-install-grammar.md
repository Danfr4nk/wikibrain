# The Install Grammar

## Theory (Stage 2 — profile-vocabulary statement)

In the attachment register, emitted output takes **imperative (procedural-summons) form** and does not take **canonical-question form**: across 99,332 sent rows with text in the held corpus, "do you love me," "are we ok," "am i crazy," and "i cant do this" register **0× each** [primary-verified — dat:0755], against "call me" at **129 held / 170 in the page's older export**, "you up" at 93/119, and "you there" at 19 in both exports — the absence holding system-wide across three threads including the Tom Maison friend control (architectural, not relational).

Under rows 5 and 6, an imperative is an explicit statement, and explicit statements are the only inputs that install states in the no-counter-rule architecture; under row 23, reassurance decays rather than accumulates, so a verbal answer to an interrogative would not persist even if one were emitted. The emitted grammar is therefore **the grammar of installable states**: a directive writes a state that holds until an explicit counter-statement arrives (row 19); a question writes nothing, and what it would receive decays. The 0× is structural, not emotional — the interrogative form has no install path in the architecture, so it never fires. This is the grammar, not the mood.

The Oct 6 standing order (~03:10 EDT, dated, his words: if Sammy EVER, even a little, starts to doubt his love for her or the meaning of "Sammy and Danny," she must tell him) is the installed-state instance of the same grammar: emitted as a directive creating an obligation channel ("tell me"), never as the question form ("do you still love me?"), and persisting as the standing rule since. The one stated-rule instance and the 170× summons register are the same machine: **need is emitted as executable instruction.**

## Rivals & discriminators (Stage 3 — adversarial check)

**Prescribed rival: the intimacy-avoidance reading.** As stated it is interior ("he avoids the question because the answer is what he can't face") and therefore invisible to the instruments per §2. Reframed to attention level: **interrogatives are suppressed as an output form because they carry higher interpretive-ambiguity cost per emitted word, and per row 23 their answers decay — they buy nothing durable for the emission cost.** On this reading the 0× is an economy, not an install path.

**Discriminating observation:** do imperatives get executed/answered at higher rates and shorter latencies than interrogatives in the held record? The install-grammar thesis predicts yes — directives install states that pull a countable response (the call, the channel), while interrogatives install nothing and their answers decay. The output-form rival makes no uptake prediction; it is satisfied by the emission asymmetry alone.

**The discriminator is unavailable in held data.** The audit supplies the interrogative leg of the comparison (question-ending turns answered at lower rates than non-question turns; his question density 43% below norm) but contains no imperative-ending-turn answer rate and no directive-vs-question latency cut; the reassurance page itself notes latency was not re-cut for its pass. The rival is not beaten. Per protocol Stage 3: **the theory is capped at hypothesis grade and labeled as such.** The 0×/170× split loads the thesis but is also consistent with the rival's economy reading — the one count that separates the two readings (directive uptake rate/latency vs interrogative) does not exist yet.

**The open test (named).** A held-corpus sweep bucketing his turns by terminal form — imperative ("call me," "pick up," "tell me") vs interrogative — measuring answer rate and median reply latency per bucket. Install-grammar predicts imperatives answered at materially higher rates and shorter latencies than interrogatives at matched turn length. If they aren't, the output-form economy rival is ahead and this entry is wrong.
