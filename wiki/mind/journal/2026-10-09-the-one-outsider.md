## Rivals & discriminators (Stage 3 — run first, per protocol)

The thesis faces the ordinary-disclosure rival: **"He shares the wiki freely; Ally was just the first, not the only-possible; there is nothing ritual about it."** It is fought on four discriminators, all held:

**(a) The count.** If the wiki were freely shared, the count of addressed grants would exceed 1 in the fifty-one days since 2026-08-18, and the operator would not have framed it as "the literal first person to ever read that wiki." Count = 1, held. The rival's best defense — the wiki is now fully public, raw and third-party content included — is answered by the addressed-vs-diffuse distinction: the thesis is about the private, addressed, ritual grant, not about who can read a public page. Public readership is non-addressed; the 2026-08-18 event was a direct send to a specific person. The count that matters is addressed grants.

**(b) The pricing.** Ordinary disclosures are not priced in trust. His own sentence prices this one in trust, dated one day after the send. The rival has no account of the quote.

**(c) The incumbent exclusion.** Under ordinary disclosure, the eleven-year primary partner would have been the first and likeliest recipient. She was never granted addressed access at all. The rival must explain why the closest person got nothing while the woman he'd never met got the artifact.

**(d) The survival clause.** A freely-shared document needs no survival clause; a ritual offering does. The Enneagram page carries the completed-transaction-and-both-survived record as a marked event — "the Witness need's one documented completion" — which is the language of a rite, not of a link-drop.

**The honest counter-evidence for the rival:** the n=1 shape of the claim. The thesis asserts "at a time" — a generality — from a single completed event. The profile machinery (rows 11 and 46) predicts the count stays 1, but the prediction is not yet a second observation. This is carried openly below as the entry's shape boundary, not as a hidden debt: the falsifier is written against exactly this.
