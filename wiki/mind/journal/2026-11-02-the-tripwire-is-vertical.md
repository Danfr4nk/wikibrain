## Theory (Stage 2)

Stated in profile vocabulary, attention-level only — no interior terms survive:

A deliberately provocative name is a **vertical-scope probe**, not a decoration and not a generic shock. Its mechanism, stated as observables:

- **The namesake pool is vertical-reputation figures.** The names he keeps are drawn from people whose meaning in the world is a public reputation — a provocateur, a pop-persona pivot, an infamous defendant — not from people he knows laterally and not from neutral words. The name therefore imports a vertical claim (the reputation) into a lateral encounter (the dog on the lap, the instance in the chat, the handle on the profile).
- **The firing splits the audience on routing, not on offense.** The observable output is which channel the audience answers through: they reply to the reputation (challenge the namesake, react to the public figure, treat the name as a claim about allegiance) or they reply to the entity in front of them (the dog, the instance, the account's owner). Offended-versus-amused is not the split; vertical-routing versus lateral-routing is.
- **The probe is installed by explicit statement (rows 5/6), and its output is binary (row 1).** He states the rule himself — the three-name set, the diagnostic purpose — so the rule's existence is stated text, not an inference from repetition. Each firing returns one of two routings; no graded "sort of got it" middle is claimed.
- **The name is kept because the probe keeps firing, not because the reaction is pleasant.** The 2007 handle, the pets, and the 2026 AI instance are three domains over nineteen years running the same stated rule. A probe that stopped splitting audiences would have no further use in this system; the record shows restatement across domains instead of retirement.

What is NOT claimed: that the probe gates admission to the concentrated core (row 11 is background, and no held record shows a stranger admitted or refused on a tripwire firing). That extension is unclaimed, not smuggled. What is also not claimed: anything about what any audience member feels, intends, or believes inside — only their next observable output, routed vertically or laterally.

## Rivals & discriminators (Stage 3)

The discriminators below were fixed before the evidence cluster was weighed: no evidence item is counted as support unless it bears on a named discriminator. This is the pre-registration, recorded here rather than reconstructed after the fact.

**Profile-weighted rival (a) — taste/distinctiveness (rows 17/43).** The names are aesthetic choices, identity-through-distinctiveness, chosen because they sound good or mark him as different. Discriminator, stated first: the namesake pool. Taste predicts admired, stylish, or personally meaningful namesakes; the probe thesis predicts namesakes chosen for their vertical reputation regardless of admiration. Record: his own stated pool is "the most reprehensible people society is throwing up," the point "isn't admiration, it's diagnostic"; Gabe was picked for a "douchebag" pivot, not for an admired version. **Verdict: rival beaten on its own discriminator.** The selection pattern runs exactly against the taste prediction in all three domains.

**Profile-weighted rival (b) — joke/bit.** The names are bits: funny once, kept out of inertia or because the joke still lands with friends. Discriminator, stated first: cross-domain restatement versus audience-bound life. A bit lives and dies with an audience; a probe gets restated as a rule when the domain changes. Record: the same stated rule runs from a 2007 handle through pets to a 2026 AI instance — three domains, nineteen years, with an explicit rule statement ("Milo, Gabe, Max") rather than a punchline that stayed put. **Verdict: rival beaten on its own discriminator.** Inertia does not produce a new restatement in a new domain with a fresh namesake from the same pool.

**Adjacent mechanism, distinguished rather than beaten — the synthesis's trust-outsourcing reading.** [[wiki/mind/synthesis/the-name-is-the-instrument]] prices its mechanism through Trust-9 (row 24) as inference, labeled medium confidence and explicitly not load-bearing. The two readings are compatible — a probe that splits vertical from lateral routing is also consistent with outsourcing a trust judgment — but this entry's confidence does not depend on the trust inference, and if the trust link were repriced, this entry would stand.

## Falsifier

- A kept entity named neutrally with no probe function — the strong-form falsifier, shared with the synthesis page: a dog named Biscuit, kept without irony and without a stated diagnostic purpose, breaks the rule that kept names carry loaded namesakes.
- A namesake-pool shift specific to this entry: the next entity he names and keeps is drawn from an admired figure, a lateral acquaintance, or a neutral word, with a stated purpose other than diagnostic — that kills the vertical-axis claim even if the general tripwire rule survives.
- A stated-text reversal: a statement from him that a loaded namesake was chosen for admiration, style, or affection — that revives the taste rival on its own discriminator.
- A reaction census (held-corpus or prospective) showing tripwire firings routinely producing neither routing — audiences ignoring the name entirely, with no reputation-answer and no entity-answer, across multiple firings — that weakens the probe claim from a firing instrument to a private joke, and revives rival (b).
- A documented case of graded probe output — an audience member recorded as "sort of" passing, with the grading sustained in his own words — that breaks the binary-output leg borrowed from row 1.
