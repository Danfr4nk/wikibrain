## Theory (Stage 2)

**Thesis, in profile vocabulary:** Dan separates claim ownership from number ownership. The claim under test is his, and it is categorical in row 1's form — Polymarket is the best polling there is right now — with no graded middle attached to it in the record. Every graded number that will adjudicate that claim in the frozen test is issued by an external system (a market price, a model probability, a polling average), frozen as a primary record before results (row 4), under adjudication rules stated explicitly in writing before the event they govern (row 6). His own graded credences (rows 2–3) do not appear in the test at all: no outcome in the snapshot carries a Dan-issued percentage. The observable output is therefore not a prospective log of his forecasts. It is a prospective adjudication apparatus for other systems' forecasts, built so that the verdict on his categorical claim will arrive as a gate-flip under pre-stated rules rather than as a post-results renegotiation of what "best" meant.

**Stage 3 pre-registration (recorded before the evidence verdict, expanded below):** Rival — §5 Shape 6, system-building-as-displacement: the snapshot is an exocortex monument, accumulated analysis whose product is the analysis itself (row 9's diagnosis-to-behavior gap), imposing no downstream constraint on the claim it was built around. Discriminator — closed loop vs. append-only: displacement predicts accumulation with no rule that can declare the builder's own claim failed against his preference; a constraining apparatus predicts a frozen, pre-results rule under which the claim fails on its own terms even in the outcome Dan would most easily declare a win (every market favorite winning, badly priced). If no such rule exists in the file, the rival wins and this thesis fails.

**Rival 1 — §5 Shape 6: system-building-as-displacement.** The exocortex is real (instrument row 10), but the diagnosis-to-behavior gap (row 9, weight 8, supported) prices the alternative: the analysis is the product, and built systems accumulate append-only without changing a downstream decision. On this reading the snapshot is a very thorough monument — boards collected, nothing constrained.

- **Discriminator (pre-registered in Theory above):** closed loop vs. append-only — does the artifact contain a frozen rule that can declare Dan's own claim failed in the outcome he would most easily call a win?
- **Verdict:** favors the thesis. Section 3, point 5 of the snapshot does exactly that: winner calls alone cannot save the theory; a calibration loss fails it "on its own terms even if every favorite wins," and the file forbids editing the frozen numbers after results. A displacement artifact accumulates; this artifact pre-commits its builder to a gate-flip he cannot soften after the fact without visibly breaking his own frozen rule (row 6: the last explicitly stated rule runs until explicitly revoked — and a post-results revocation would itself be a dated, observable event, not a quiet edit).

**Rival 2 — The prospective-log reading (rows 2–3's falsifier shape).** The snapshot is the start of the §7 prospective log: Dan finally aiming his own graded credences at checkable futures, which would falsify row 2's prediction as stated and reprice row 3.

- **Discriminator:** whose percentages the log contains. A Dan-credence log predicts Dan-issued percentages attached to outcomes, scorable against his stated values. The claim/number separation predicts external percentages only, with Dan's contribution categorical (the claim) and procedural (the rules).
- **Verdict:** favors the thesis. The full boards contain market prices, model probabilities, and polling averages, each attributed to its issuer; no outcome carries a Dan-issued percentage. Row 2's prediction therefore survives this cluster untouched — notably, it survives *by construction of the test Dan commissioned*, which is the thesis.

## Falsifier

Any of: (a) a Dan-issued percentage for an election outcome surfacing in the snapshot or its commission record — a graded credence of his own entered into this test; (b) the election-night or post-results scoring redefining the verdict rule after results are known (e.g., declaring the theory vindicated on winner calls alone after a calibration loss) without an explicit, dated revocation of the frozen rule — that would be the displacement rival winning late, and this entry's discriminator reversing; (c) a future prospective test in another domain in which Dan logs his own percentages at checkable futures as the primary graded numbers, which would show the separation was an artifact of this domain rather than the operating pattern this entry claims for it. A Polymarket loss on the frozen rules does NOT falsify this entry: the thesis is about how the test was built, and a theory failing under its own pre-registered rules is the apparatus working as described.
