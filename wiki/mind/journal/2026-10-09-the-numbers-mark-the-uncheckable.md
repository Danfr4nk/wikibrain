# The Numbers Mark the Uncheckable

**Idea Journal — 2026-10-09, pass C (credence clustering).**

Dan's graded numeric credences do not miscalibrate. They *cannot* calibrate, because calibration requires number-outcome pairs and the record contains exactly one — which resolved false and was never revisited. The clustering is not an artifact of what he talks about most; it is a scorability sort: in the private channel, numbers attach to propositions with no scheduled resolution — another person's interior, unwitnessed pasts — at rates that survive a base-rate correction, and every emission is terminal. No graded number is ever re-graded, re-scored, or retracted at a number, on any proposition type. The intensity dial never discharges on unscorable propositions because no outcome ever closes the loop; on checkable propositions the loop closes by outcome, not by numeric revision. Miscalibration is inapplicable, not false.

## Theory (Stage 2 — profile-vocabulary statement)

All terms from rows ≥ 6. All claims at attention level.

Dan's private-channel graded numeric credences cluster on propositions with no scheduled resolution: another person's interior ("98.7% sure you don't believe any of your catholicism," 2026-02-19; "99% sure it was a sedative in that" — an unwitnessed past, 2018-07-20). Of 24 strict graded credences on the old-wiki re-derivation, exactly one is resolvable even in principle; it resolved false and was never revisited at a number. An independent scan of the 203,624-row held corpus run for this entry finds 27 strict outbound credences against 1 inbound, with the non-endpoint graded set sorting 9-to-1 into interiors/unwitnessed pasts against the single checkable case. No proposition that receives a graded number ever receives a second graded number. The 2024-11-07 self-audit of his own past estimate runs entirely in words ("Pretty crazy that even I... was still giving him blue wall states in June") — the audit norm exists, but it never emits a numeric revision.

The verdict: the emission set and the outcome set are disjoint. Numbers are emitted where outcomes never arrive; outcomes arrive where numbers are not re-emitted. There is therefore no population of number-outcome pairs against which "calibrated" or "miscalibrated" could be computed — the n=1 pair is the entire population, and it was never scored a second time. The 0.25 ledger figure (row 3, testimony-grade) prices retrospective assertions sampled years from their events — the [[wiki/mind/journal/2026-10-03-the-narrator-tax|Narrator Tax]] entry already located that sample in the narrator, not the forecaster — not forecasts at emission time.

The thesis survives the translation-kill rule: it was never an interior claim wearing behavioral clothes. No motive is asserted — not "he avoids scoring," not "he needs certainty about the unknowable." The claim is the sort and the record of non-revision.

## Rivals & discriminators (Stage 3 — run first, per protocol)

**The rival: the miscalibration reading.** "He's just badly calibrated, uniformly; the clustering is an artifact of what he talks about most." This is the reading the profile's most alarming number (0.25) has invited for years, and it is the one the thesis must beat rather than assume away.

**Discriminator 1 — the base-rate sort.** If clustering were a topic base-rate artifact, graded numbers would ride the base rate of what he talks about. His private message stream is heavy with checkable propositions — polls, elections, market prices, schedules, logistics — yet the strict graded set sorts 1-of-24 resolvable (page, dat:0664) and 9-to-1 unscorable on the independent held-corpus scan. The checkable half of his talk carries no numbers at private-channel rates. Note the public/private control from the calibrated-confidence page: on Twitter he *does* grade checkable world events ("it's 25% at best for Kamala, Gretch, Newsom," 2024-07-06) — the scorability sort is medium-specific, which is exactly what a base-rate-artifact account cannot produce: the same man, the same years, numbers on checkable things in public and almost none in private. Rival fails discriminator 1.

**Discriminator 2 — the calibration loop.** A uniform miscalibrator emits numbers, outcomes arrive, numbers get revised — the correction loop is what makes "miscalibrated" a coherent charge rather than a slur. The held record shows the loop's complete absence at the numeric layer: zero of 27 outbound strict credences ever re-emitted; the resolved Nemacolin forecast never re-graded or retracted numerically; the 2024-11-07 self-audit revises a past estimate in words with no number attached; he polices others' numbers in words ("That's a level of certainty I'm not sure how you've arrived at," 2022-03-30 — cross-page attested, held-corpus 2022 gap) while never once emitting a numeric revision of his own past number. A forecaster whose numbers are never corrected *as numbers* is not a forecaster. Rival fails discriminator 2.

**The evasion counter-case, folded honestly.** The page already faced it: the 2024-11-07 audit shows the scoring norm exists inwardly, so the habit isn't designed to evade checking — it structurally does. This entry sharpens the point rather than re-litigating it: even when the scoring norm fires, it fires in words. The design that would let him evade — never grading checkable things — coexists with the norm that would let him not evade — auditing himself in public. Both are held data. The thesis integrates both: the numbers mark the uncheckable, and the checkable gets words.

**Restarts: 0.** The starting thesis survived all five stages without patching.

## Falsifier

In held data: any second graded number emitted on a previously graded proposition (a numeric re-grade or retraction), or graded numbers aimed at checkable private-channel propositions at rates comparable to unscorable ones — either would break the disjoint-sets claim. Prospectively: the standing experiment (instrument §7.1) — a forward-dated prediction log with resolution dates — producing dozens of number-outcome pairs that calibrate near stated values, with numeric revisions of past numbers observed in the record. That would make "miscalibrated" applicable, and this entry wrong.
