## Theory (Stage 2)

**Thesis, in profile vocabulary:** Dan parameterizes delegated wiki-engine work on split axes that keep the profile's two number-forms apart. **Quantity** is graded and fenced to a countable domain: floor 10,000 lines/day, goal 20,000 lines/day on wikibrain main — the row 2 shape applied to lines, a count that can be 828 or 920 without becoming a verdict. **Worth** is binary in row 1's form: an addition counts as good work only if it passes a gate — it makes the wiki substantially more insightful, synthesis outranking padding — with no graded middle in which volume partially redeems thinness. **Duration** is the tempo instruction: long, persistent runs that run until done. And **speed is not in the parameter set at all**: latency / hurry is explicitly deleted, by countermand of a hurry-up instruction. The work order is therefore an exocortex specification (row 10) with three live parameters — how many lines, whether they clear the insight gate, how long the run continues — and one conspicuously absent parameter: how fast.

The tension is part of the claim, not hidden from it: a line floor and an anti-padding clause pull against each other. A floor invites filling; the gate forbids filling with padding. The thesis is that the record resolves the tension in the gate's favor — the floor is reported against honestly and left unmet when meeting it would require the wrong kind of lines (see the discriminator below).

**Stage 3 pre-registration (recorded before the evidence verdict, expanded below):** Rival — §5 Shape 6, system-building-as-displacement, in its local form: the floor is a monument metric that produces volume without insight; the exocortex accumulates counts and reports while the insight clause is decorative. Discriminator — closed loop vs. append-only, sharpened for this territory: if the floor were a padding target, sub-floor days would be filled — by padding bursts, by reclassification bulk, by whatever lines are cheapest — until the count cleared. If speed were a live parameter, a hurry-up instruction would stand unremarked. The thesis predicts the opposite on both: sub-floor days are filed as sub-floor and left sub-floor, and the hurry-up is countermanded. If the tracker showed padding bursts chasing the floor, the rival would win and this thesis would fail.

## Rivals & discriminators (Stage 3, run before the evidence verdict)

**Rival 1 — §5 Shape 6: system-building-as-displacement (the live rival).** The exocortex is real (row 10), but the diagnosis-to-behavior gap (row 9, weight 8, supported) prices the alternative: the analysis is the product, behavior is not downstream of it. In local form: the 10,000-line floor is a monument metric — a big number that makes the system look ambitious while producing volume without insight, and the insight clause is the monument's plaque.

- **Discriminator (pre-registered in Theory above):** closed loop vs. append-only, in count form. Displacement-as-monument predicts the count is protected: when the day runs short, the cheapest lines available get added until the floor clears, because the metric is the point. The parameter-split thesis predicts the opposite: the gate outranks the count, so short days stay short and are reported as short.
- **Verdict:** favors the thesis. On Nov 2 the day closed at 828 gross, "missed by 9,172 gross"; on Nov 3 at 920 gross, "missed by 9,080 gross." The noon report on Nov 3 projected "near 900–950, well under the floor" and the final confirms 920 — the projection of a miss was published at noon and the miss was then allowed to happen. The only scale lever named in the tracker, book mining, sat quiet for a tenth and then an eleventh straight day. A monument metric does not get reported at "under one-tenth of the floor" and left there; a gated count does.
- **Honest tension, kept:** the floor and the gate genuinely pull against each other, and this entry does not dissolve that. A different week — one in which a mining batch or a bulk reclassification conveniently landed exactly on a short day — would weaken this verdict. On Nov 2–3, the pull resolved toward the gate. The claim is scoped to the Oct 4 – Nov 3 parameter record, not to every future week.

**Rival 2 — The floor as covert speed pressure.** On this reading, a daily floor of 10,000 lines *is* a speed instruction in disguise: the only way to hit it is to work faster, so "speed" needs no separate mention.

- **Discriminator:** whether speed language, when it appears explicitly, is allowed to stand. Covert-speed predicts an explicit hurry-up would be redundant but harmless — consistent with the floor, never countermanded. The deletion thesis predicts the explicit instruction gets revoked precisely because speed is not a parameter, even though revoking it makes the floor harder to reach.
- **Verdict:** favors the thesis. The hurry-up was countermanded (2026-11-02), and the stated replacement tempo is duration — runs run until done, not fast. A covert speed demand does not survive its explicit form being struck by the same person who set the floor.

**Rival 3 — Duration as incidental scheduling.** On this reading, "run until done" is boilerplate — long runs are just what crawls look like, not a chosen tempo parameter.

- **Discriminator:** incidental features do not get defended. If duration were incidental, the countermand in Rival 2 would have nothing to protect: hurry the run and it still runs until done, only sooner. The deletion thesis predicts the hurry-up is treated as a change to the work order itself, worth an explicit revocation.
- **Verdict:** favors the thesis, at row 16's leans strength. The countermand happened, and the preference was stated comparatively — long persistent runs *over* quick trivial ones (2026-11-02) — which is a choice between tempos, not a description of one crawl. Row 16 (leans) is consistent with a duration-first rhythm over steady-state scheduling, but this verdict rests on the stated preference and the countermand, not on row 16 alone.

**Capped claims:** This entry covers the work-order parameter set for delegated wiki-engine work, Oct 4 – Nov 3, 2026. It does not claim the floor will never be chased in a future week, does not grade whether 10,000/20,000 is a good floor, and does not generalize the parameter set to non-wiki delegation. Row 2 is used for shape only, as scoped in the prior; no credence claim about lines is made or implied.

## Falsifier

Any of: (a) a dated tracker day in this mandate's run in which a sub-floor shortfall is closed by a padding burst — bulk lines the tracker itself or a contemporaneous record characterizes as low-insight, added to reach the floor — which would show the count outranking the gate and the Shape 6 rival winning; (b) a reinstated hurry-up / latency instruction for wiki-engine runs, stated by Dan and left standing, which would show speed re-entering the parameter set rather than deleted from it; (c) a restated mandate in which worth is graded (e.g., partial credit for volume, tiered insight scores feeding the daily verdict) rather than gated, which would break the row 1 / row 2 split this entry claims. Ordinary sub-floor days do NOT falsify this entry: reported, unfilled misses are its central evidence. A day that clears the floor on gated, authored work does not falsify it either: the claim is about the parameter set, not about the floor being unreachable.
