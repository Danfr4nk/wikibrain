## Theory (Stage 2 — profile-vocabulary statement)

The unit of Dan's texting is the **self-contained syntactic package**. In 2026 his sentences run 10.83 words against interlocutors' 5.62 — 1.93×, the single largest lexical deviation in the corpus — while his burst-internal messages carry their own subject and verb at 68.5% against 55.1% for his interlocutors, and conversational filler runs at 0.59× (41% below norm). The words-per-sentence ratio says the sentences are clause-packed; the self-containment rate says the packing completes the thought inside the unit rather than spilling it; the filler deficit says the packaging carries no padding. The density a reader experiences is **compression into complete units**, not sprawl — and nine dated recipient complaints (2018 → 2026-08-08, four distinct people) confirm row 36's prediction verbatim: they cite processing load ("I can't read these texts," "paragraph after paragraph," "I can't read any of that"), never vocabulary. The transactional channel proves the package is deployed by register: eleven years, 3.2–4.3 words per turn, zero 50-word messages — the same operator, a channel where the long package never appears.

Every term in that statement is a count, a rate, a dated quotation, or a channel volume. No interior term survives; "compression" here names a text structure (more clause-content per self-contained unit, less filler), not a motive. The thesis survived translation without losing its claim.

## Evidence cluster (Stage 4 — Layer-A gate)

**D1 — The 1.93× sentence ratio (Layer A).** The [[wiki/mind/profile/texting-deviance-audit|texting deviance audit]]'s 2026 lexical table (computed by `bin/text-metrics`, committed at repo root as the re-runnable instrument, from the 2026-08-13 sender-tagged export of 183,787 rows): words per sentence 10.83 (Dan) vs 5.62 (interlocutors). The audit names it directly: "the single largest lexical deviation in the corpus," and the one a reader feels as density. The clause-packing machinery sits beside it in the same table: logical connectives (because, therefore, whereas) at 2.8×, hedges (essentially, fundamentally, arguably) at 3.1×, meta-discourse (my point is, to be clear) at 2.9× — the grammatical glue of subordinate clauses, each a joint that lets a sentence carry one more clause instead of breaking. Meanwhile words of 3+ syllables run at 6.14% vs 4.95% (1.2×, near parity) and jargon terms at 0.26 per 1,000 words — one every 3,846 words: "he is not burying anyone in terminology." The long sentence is built from clauses, not from big words.

**D2 — The self-containment rate (Layer A).** 68.5% of his 2026 burst-internal messages carry their own subject and verb, against 55.1% for his interlocutors. His fragments are *more complete* than their whole messages. The STACCATO mode (3+ messages, median ≤6 words) runs at 8.5% of his turns against 10.5% of theirs — he staccatos less than they do, and it is his most reliably answered mode (93.8%). The decisive sub-check: his non-first burst messages begin lowercase 33.5% of the time against 2.7% for interlocutors — but his *first* messages start lowercase at 31.8%, statistically identical, so the lowercase opener is a global habit, not a continuation marker. The audit: "This is the check that killed the staccato hypothesis." What reads as fragmented bursts on the surface is a sequence of complete units — paragraph-shaped sentences arriving in burst-shaped packaging.

**D3 — The channel-gated deployment (Layer A).** The NYC delivery contact counterparty table: 2015–19, 4.15 vs 2.68 words/turn; 2025, 4.32 vs 3.05 — eleven years, 3.2–4.3 words per turn, and "never once sent a 50-word message there." The capacity for brief, sparse output is intact and demonstrable. The syntactic package is therefore not a capacity default — not how he *must* write — but a deployed register: it fires on loaded relational channels (Annie 2026: 39.52 vs 10.82, 3.65×) and never on the transactional one. Same operator, opposite shape, channel-gated. This is the audit's own standing prediction #3 ("the transactional-channel immunity holds") used as evidence of deployment rather than capacity.

**D4 — The complaint register confirms row 36's prediction (Layer A, dated).** The audit's complaint table — nine unambiguous complaints from four distinct people, 2019-01-28 → 2026-08-08 — is held evidence for the instrument's prediction that "complaints about his messages cite processing load, not vocabulary":

Two further properties matter: the 2019 complaint sits inside the era where his words-per-turn ratio measured 1.05× — the behavior was legible to recipients six years before it was statistically extreme — and Annie's "I have expressed this to you before" establishes it as chronic, not a single bad night. ## Rivals & discriminators (Stage 3 — run first, per protocol)

**Rival A — Shape 2: the long dense mode is venting/dysregulation.** The profile-weighted rival per §5 is **channel-gated instrumentality**: row 38 proves the alleged dysregulation switches off entirely by channel — a global venting account cannot explain a channel that stays at 3.2–4.3 words per turn for eleven years. The discriminating observation was named before evidence assembly: the self-containment rate. Venting predicts fragments and run-ons (the dysregulated unit is the spill); compression predicts self-contained units (the dense unit is complete). The audit's measurement decides it: 68.5% vs 55.1% subject-verb completeness, staccato below interlocutor frequency, and the lowercase-opener parity check killing the mid-sentence-splitting read. A dysregulated spill does not land 68.5% complete. The rival fails its discriminator.

**Rival B — the vocabulary rival (the headline that died).** On this reading the density readers feel is lexical — post-graduate readability, technical terminology, register theft. Discriminator: jargon density and recipient complaints. The held record answers twice: jargon terms at 0.26 per 1,000 words (one every 3,846 — negligible volume), and nine dated complaints from four people citing processing load, never vocabulary. Row 36's standing prediction — "Complaints about his messages cite processing load, not vocabulary" — is confirmed in the record; the rival's predicted complaint register (about word choice) has zero hits in eleven years of recipient feedback. The rival fails on both legs. Row 35 is cited here only as the corpse, per the brief.

**Rival C — the "it's just more words" collapse reading.** This is really Rival A wearing a coat, but it gets its own check because the turn-level table is loud: STACKED-ESSAY carries 44.3% of his 2026 words from 11.2% of his turns, and his words-per-turn ratio ran to 3.05× in 2026. The discriminator is the same self-containment rate plus the sentence ratio: if the long mode were unstructured sprawl, sentence length and completeness would be at parity with the norm — sprawl adds words, not clauses, and not complete ones. Instead the long mode's *internal structure* is the measured anomaly: 1.93× sentence length, 2.8× connectives, 68.5% completeness. Volume is the loudest number; structure is the real one. The audit says the same thing from the other side: "98.1% of his lifetime messages are a single line" — the deviation lives in turn count × length, and the length itself is clause-packed.
