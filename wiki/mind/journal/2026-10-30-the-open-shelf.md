# The Open Shelf

## Theory (Stage 2 — profile-vocabulary statement)

The exocortex holds an open shelf: a set of items that are neither done nor dropped, recorded once with an explicit completion condition — a date on which a check fires, or an order that has not yet been given — and then left silent. While waiting, a shelf item generates no output of its own: no repetition, no escalation, no workaround, no decay. It does not get chased early, it does not get forced, and it does not quietly expire. It leaves the shelf in exactly two ways: its stated date fires, or an explicit statement orders it moved. Elapsed time alone moves nothing, because the architecture has no counter-rule (rows 6, 19) by which silence could close an item. The shelf is therefore the dormancy structure of the exocortex: the complement of the open set (which repeats because it has no completion condition) and the complement of the gate (which closes a token act the moment it fires). Items wait for gates; the shelf is what waiting looks like when it is working.

Boundary, stated up front so this entry does not re-derive its neighbors: this is a claim about **items waiting for gates**, not about token acts closed by gates — that is 2026-10-29-the-gate-is-the-loop, cited here, not re-derived. And it is not a claim about repetition without completion — that is 2026-10-03-the-open-set, cited here, not re-derived. The discriminator between the shelf and the open set is repetition-vs-silence while waiting: an open-set item repeats; a shelf item, having a stated completion condition, does not need to.

## Falsifier

A shelf item closed with neither a fired date nor an explicit statement (decay-closure — e.g., the Jev pass struck from the inventory without his word, or the bot list acted on without the yeet-or-keep verdict); or a shelf item generating escalating item-driven output while waiting (a forced run, a manufactured order, a workaround built to bypass the missing verdict); or a dated item still standing untouched after its trigger date has fired with no new statement recorded. Any of the three retires the shelf as stated; the first would install decay as a counter-rule, which row 6 forbids.
