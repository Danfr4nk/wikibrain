## Rivals & discriminators (Stage 3 — run first, per protocol)

**The profile-weighted rival: the courtship-gradient account.** Commitment builds through a graded escalation phase — hedged intermediates, provisional framings, a slow load — and the full-strength markers are the *end* of the gradient, not its whole content. The 17-day Alexis arc, in particular, could be read as a courtship.

**Discriminating observation:** whether the dated record between first contact and the full-load marker contains any intermediate *stated* states — his own words at partial load ("taking it slow," "not sure what this is," hedged labels) — or whether the only states present are zero (unnamed, unstated) and one (named, stated, full-strength). The gradient account predicts intermediates; the binary-gate thesis predicts none.

**Held-data check — the discriminator favors the thesis:**
1. **Alexis, Nov 29–Dec 16.** The held record's dated states are: unnamed-public (Nov 29–30 tweets), purchased commitment (ticket, ~week 1–2, per testimony), named-at-full-strength (Dec 14), "girlfriend" (Dec 16). The unnamed fortnight is a lag in naming, not a gradient of commitment — the ticket purchase (the load-bearing act) predates the naming. No hedged intermediate in his words anywhere in the arc.
2. **Annie, Nov 29–Dec 1.** The dated states are: the verdict at ~1 hour ("instantly changed my life," said to her), the stated rule at ~2 days. The gap between them contains no intermediate — the record jumps from verdict to install with nothing between.
3. **The lag is not the gradient.** The two arcs differ in lag (17 days vs 2 days) but agree in shape: zero → one, no middle. A gradient account would predict the longer arc to show *more* intermediate structure; the record shows none in either.
4. **His own framing agrees.** The Oct 17 theme statement — "when he knows, he commits immediately" — is the thesis stated in his own vocabulary, dated, at testimony grade.

The thesis makes no claim about what he felt at onset — only about what the dated record contains. The rival's one plausible leg (the 17-day lag) is integrated as lag, not dodged.

## Falsifier

A dated case breaks this entry if: (a) a future (or newly filed) onset shows an explicit hedged intermediate state in his own words between first contact and the full-load marker — "we're taking it slow," "not sure what this is yet," a provisional label sustained as a stated state; (b) a full-load marker itself arrives graded (a partial naming, a hedged founding rule) rather than at full strength; (c) the lag structure changes the shape — i.e., a long arc that *does* show intermediate stated states, proving the lag was a gradient after all. Any one of these installs the courtship-gradient rival and kills this theory.
