# The Retraction Leaves a Scar

Load-bearing rows, all weighted ≥ 6 with verdict **supported**. No load-bearing row is silent, inverted, or ≤ 4.

- **Row 4 (weight 8, supported as practice) — Forensic method: primary records over narrative, anomaly clusters, procedural tells, lossless retention.** Behaviorally attested across domains. Predicts: records kept whole; conclusions labeled with confidence. This entry tests that prediction turned inward, on his own sent output.
- **Row 5 (weight 9, supported) — Explicit-over-inferred meaning: stated text registers, ambient/implied signal does not.** Predicts: rule-changes require explicit statements; ambiguity stalls rather than improvises. ## Theory (Stage 2)

**Thesis, in profile vocabulary:** In the Annie message file, Dan's retraction of a sent message is an explicit minimal revocation that preserves the event under lossless retention. The prior content is superseded by a stated token — a lone "." — so the record keeps three things and withdraws one: it keeps the turn's existence, its position in the sequence, and its date; it withdraws only the content. The "." is therefore not content, not emphasis, and not agreement. It is the smallest explicit statement the architecture (rows 5–6) can issue to change a prior statement's status, issued in a form the record (row 4) cannot lose.

**Corollary for any corpus reader, including his own forensic method run on himself:** a minimal token in this corpus cannot be priced at face value by length. Length is the wrong meter for this output architecture in both directions — row 37 already shows the long turns carrying a disproportionate share of the words, and the tombstone shows the opposite pole: a one-character turn can carry a complete procedural act (a revocation) while carrying no propositional content at all. Pricing "." as assent, as a beat, or as low-effort noise misreads a scar as a statement.

**Stage 3 pre-registration (recorded before the evidence verdict, expanded below):** Primary shape — Shape 5, verdict/exclusion, applied to his own message: the "." looks like a judgment that excludes the message from the record. Profile-weighted rival — the check-in rung plus the binary gate: declarations in this architecture do not terminate the thing they name; the channel and the prior state keep running, and change arrives as a gate-flip, not a disappearance. Discriminator — (a) whether the retracted item vanishes from the record or leaves a dated placeholder in its slot, and (b) the canonical Shape 5 discriminator, the channel's trajectory after the declaration. Secondary mundane rival — the forced placeholder: an edit interface requiring a non-empty field makes "." the cheapest compliant filler, with no record-keeping intent. Discriminator for the secondary rival — whether the same withdrawal, where no sent record yet exists, also leaves a mark, or leaves none.

## Evidence cluster (Stage 4)

The cluster is small and is stated at its actual size: one dated decode and one dated missing-sentence case. Nothing else is cited as evidence, and no count is invented to make it look larger.

### 1. The decode: a lone "." is a tombstone (2026-11-03, his words)

- **2026-11-03 (Dan's decode, carried in his curated memory as his words):** in Annie's message file, a Dan message that is just "." is a tombstone — a crash-out message he sent, regretted afterward, and edited down to a period. The habit formed because Annie complains about how much he writes. A lone "." from him must never be read as content, emphasis, or agreement.
- What this datum establishes at attention level, without any interior claim: (i) a class of sent messages in this one file was **post-send edited** down to a single surviving token; (ii) the surviving token is uniform — "." — not a shortened version, a summary, or a replacement text; (iii) the convention is **counterparty-scoped in the record as decoded**: it is stated for Annie's file, the channel where the volume complaint exists and where row 37 independently measures the long-turn concentration.
- What it does not establish: how many "." tokens exist, what fraction of his turns they are, what any individual tombstoned message originally said, or what happened in the channel after any individual tombstone. None of that was counted or read in this pass, and none is claimed.
- The word "regretted" above is his testimony-grade self-report (see Profile prior). The thesis needs only the edit event and the surviving token, both of which are observables in the file the decode describes.

### 2. The contrast case: the sentence that left no scar (Nov 1, 2026)

- **2026-11-01 (parents-email sequence, as carried in the same 2026-11-03 memory entry):** his "legally and peacefully" sentence is missing from the sequence — written, reviewed, and edited/unsent away; the phrase exists in no export searched. The same regret-edit pattern presumably explains the absence — "presumably" is the source's own hedge, and it is preserved here: the link between the two cases is his (and the record's) inference, labeled as such, not a verified identity of mechanism.
- The contrast is the evidence, and it cuts in the thesis's direction on the surface observable: the pre-send withdrawal leaves **no token and no slot** — the sentence is recoverable only as a reported absence, discoverable because somebody went looking for a specific phrase. The post-send withdrawal leaves **a token in a slot** — recoverable by anyone reading the file in order, with no prior knowledge of what was removed. Same author, same week, two withdrawal surfaces: where a sent record already existed, a dated scar remains; where none had been sent, nothing remains.
- Limit, stated plainly: the two cases differ in medium (message file vs email sequence) as well as in send status, and this pass did not inspect either underlying export. The contrast is therefore consistent with the thesis and with the forced-placeholder rival (an already-sent message may simply not be erasable the way an unsent draft is). It discriminates against total-deletion readings; it does not, on its own, discriminate intent.

## Rivals & discriminators (Stage 3, run before the evidence verdict)

**Rival 1 — Shape 5, verdict/exclusion (the primary rival).** The "." is an exclusion verdict on his own message: judged, removed, over — the message is out of the record the way a severance declaration purports to take a bond out of force.

- **Discriminator (a), retention:** exclusion predicts the item vanishes, or that its slot carries no information. The scar thesis predicts a dated placeholder remains in the slot. **Verdict on (a): favors the thesis.** Per the decode, the retracted messages do not vanish; a uniform "." remains where each stood, in sequence. That is the binary-gate shape (row 1's family, via rows 5–6): the prior statement's status flips — content in force → content withdrawn — while the record of the statement persists. Nothing is graded, and nothing is erased.
- **Discriminator (b), post-declaration trajectory (the canonical Shape 5 test):** exclusion predicts channel silence and the named thing staying terminated; the check-in/gate reading predicts the channel continuing on its prior terms. **Verdict on (b): unavailable in this cluster.** No post-tombstone window was read in this pass. Under the protocol, an unavailable discriminator caps the theory at hypothesis grade — see Confidence. This cap is applied, not waived.

**Rival 2 — The forced placeholder (mundane/mechanical).** "." is not a chosen scar but the minimum non-empty filler an edit requires: any author retracting inside such an interface produces the same token, and no lossless-retention intent is needed to explain it.

- **Discriminator:** the pre-send case. Pure interface compulsion predicts marks only where the interface forces them (post-send edits) and none where it does not (unsent drafts) — which is exactly what case 2 shows, so the observable record **does not separate** this rival from the thesis. What separates them would be evidence of choice under an available alternative: a dated case in the same file where a retraction used full deletion when deletion was available, or his stated reason for choosing "." over deletion.
- **Verdict:** not beaten. The thesis and this rival agree on every observable in the cluster and differ on intent — and intent here is interior-adjacent (a purpose attributed to the edit), visible only through his testimony. His decode does state the function (a tombstone marking a regretted send, in his words) rather than mentioning interface constraint, which leans the reading toward deliberate convention; testimony leaning is not a discriminator win. Recorded as an open rival, and it is a second, independent reason for the grade cap below.

**Rival 3 — Shape 2, regulation (faced and rejected at Stage 2/3, preserved from the restart).** The edit-down is self-soothing after a crash-out send: the behavior discharges the spike and stops when the author settles.

- **Discriminator:** channel-gating and stopping condition. A global regulation account cannot explain a perfectly scoped distribution; the decode scopes the habit to the one channel with the volume complaint, and the parents-email case scopes the sibling act to a pre-send review — both are counterparty-facing decision points, not free-floating discharge. The regulation account also needs row 30 (Impulsiveness, silent) to do its work.
- **Verdict:** rejected as load-bearing — disqualified twice over (silent row; interior claim under §2), independent of the discriminator. Its observable residue — that withdrawals cluster at counterparty-facing decision points — is absorbed into the thesis as channel scope, with no felt-state vocabulary attached.

**Capped claims:** (1) Scope is one decoded convention in one file plus one contrast case — not a claim about "." tokens in other channels, other counterparties, or other platforms. A "." elsewhere is unpriced by this entry until its channel's convention is established. (2) No frequency, proportion, or trend is claimed for tombstones. (3) The parents-email link is the source's own "presumably," carried as inference, not identity.

## Falsifier

Any one of: (1) a sweep of Annie's message file showing "." turns functioning in context as ordinary minimal replies — answers to questions, acknowledgments in sequence — at rates comparable to their tombstone use, which would break the uniform-token convention the decode asserts; (2) a dated case in the same file where a send Dan identifies as retracted was fully deleted with no token surviving, where the edit path he used for tombstones was available — the scar would then be incidental, not the architecture; (3) post-tombstone windows showing the channel treating the tombstoned content as still in force (answered, quoted, acted on by either party as if unwithdrawn), which would break the revocation half of the thesis even if the scar half survived. A "." later shown to be an accidental send does NOT falsify: the thesis prices a convention, and a convention tolerates noise at the margins — it falsifies only if the noise is the base rate (case 1).
