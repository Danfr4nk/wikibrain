# The New Domain Gets the Same Engine

## Profile prior (Stage 1)

Load-bearing rows, all weighted ≥ 6 with verdict **supported**:

- **Row 4 (weight 8, supported as practice) — Forensic method: primary records over narrative, anomaly clusters, procedural tells, lossless retention.** Behaviorally attested across domains (Bacharach audit, BFS drawer, Fran retraction within 24h). Its stated prediction, the one tested here: **new domains get the same engine**; conclusions labeled with confidence; records kept whole.
- **Row 12 (weight 9, supported) — Low trust, vertical-scoped.** Distrust targets vertical claims, not people per se; institutions and authorities get forensic treatment. In this domain the "vertical claim" is the mediated tree: the old family tree and the Ancestry GEDCOM export, accepted on their authority until a primary record checks them.
- **Row 5 (weight 9, supported) — Explicit-over-inferred meaning.** Stated text registers; ambient/implied signal does not. A census household showing one man under two readings is stated record; a second person inferred from a second name spelling is not.
- **Row 1 (weight 8.5, supported) — Verdicts are categorical/binary gates.** A gate flips by adjudication, never by graded softening. One person or two, Dec 28 1878 or Oct 11 1877: the rebuild's corrections arrive as flips, not as compromises between competing dates.

Rows 30/32 (Impulsiveness, Vulnerability — silent) and every interior-state term were excluded at Stage 1. Nothing below claims what he feels about his family history; everything below is dated output: corrections issued, items flagged, print withheld.

## Theory (Stage 2)

**Thesis, in profile vocabulary:** In a new domain containing primary records, Dan runs the same forensic engine. A mediated list (old tree, GEDCOM export) is checked against dated primary records; a count or a date flips only when a primary record adjudicates it; and items no primary record adjudicates stay labeled by evidence status and are withheld from print. The correction is an identity or date adjudication issued in primary-record form — not a smoothing of the story for the occasion.

**Stage 3 pre-registration (recorded before the evidence verdict, expanded below):** Rival — the party-packet tidying account, under which corrections are made to produce a clean printable tree for the Nov 7 party. Discriminator — the fate of unadjudicated items: tidying predicts they get dropped or smoothed so the packet prints clean; the forensic engine predicts they are retained, labeled, and withheld from print while their adjudication stays open.

## Evidence cluster (Stage 4)

All items are dated to the Nov 1, 2026 Fran-side rebuild, ahead of Eddie & Brenda Cogar's surprise 50th (Sat Nov 7, 2026, The Silo, Granville, WV). The sequence inside the day matters: the party-prep file records the open flags first, and the findings notebook records the adjudications after. That order is itself evidence — the flags were not backfilled to justify the corrections.

### 1. Donald / Elmore: a count flips by primary record, not by picking a narrative

- **2026-11-01 (party-prep file):** The GEDCOM lists **10 children** of Marion Edward & Ida Ellen Thomas; Fran's obituary calls her "last living of 8 siblings," implying **9 children**. Donald E. Thomas appears only in the 1930 census sourcing; Elmore Thomas only in the 1940 census sourcing; the two never co-appear in any source in the file. Working hypothesis recorded as **UNCONFIRMED**, with an explicit instruction: "Do NOT print either count as settled until Suz confirms or a single record shows both names together / apart."
- **2026-11-01 (findings notebook, later):** "The brother who was two people." Donald and Elmore were one man — **Donald Elmore Thomas** — counted twice because one census-taker wrote his first name and another wrote his middle. Status now **Confirmed: 1920/1930/1940/1950 census households + Social Security index.** The count resolves to nine children, matching the obituary — but the obituary did not settle it. The census households and the SSA index did, and only after they did was the item marked confirmed.

This is the cleanest case in the cluster because the same day holds both states: flagged-open in the morning file, adjudicated in the later notebook, with the adjudicating records named in both.

### 2. Marion's birthday: a date flips by the record the subject himself filled out

- **Old tree:** Fran's father born Oct 11, 1877 — but every census aged him to ~1879.
- **2026-11-01 (notebook, marked SETTLED):** His **WWI draft registration card — filled out by Marion himself** — gives **December 28, 1878**, and his 1942 WWII draft card agrees. "The old tree was simply wrong; the new tree carries Dec 28, 1878, sourced." The same card explains the name-order flip across records: he registered under **"Edward Marion Thomas,"** which is why half the records flip his names.
- The mechanism matches case 1 exactly: a mediated date is not averaged against the census ages and not deferred to the obit's name order. A self-filled primary record adjudicates, and the new tree carries the adjudicated form with its source attached.

### 3. The nickname collapses: the same adjudication, twice more, in the same rebuild

- **2026-11-01 (notebook):** Two of the old tree's "extra" relatives were nicknames hardened into people: great-uncle "Lonnie" was **Alonzo Clarence Thomas**; "Judson Conwell" was **Millard J. Conwell**. "The census households show one man each time."
- Three identity collapses in one rebuild (Donald/Elmore, Lonnie, Judson) is what a transferred engine looks like, as opposed to a single lucky correction: the anomaly cluster — extra people appearing exactly where name spellings vary across census readings — is the tell, and the census household is the adjudicator in each case.

### 4. The divorce: two clippings, one corroborating the other, testimony labeled as testimony

- **2026-11-01 (notebook):** A newspaper clipping found as an Ancestry photo hint (member-submitted, submitter Melanie Ann Gapen Johnson) gives the divorce testimony as reported — combs, hairpins, cigarette butts with lipstick in the family automobile; "embarrassed her to tears"; the name "Helen" called out in hospital — and the marriage date no record had yet given: **March 12, 1938, Frederick, Maryland**, Fran aged 17. The notebook labels this explicitly: "details as quoted are the wife's testimony as reported, i.e., her allegations in court" — testimony-grade, not carried as fact.
- **2026-11-01 (notebook):** A second clipping, from the family's own scrapbook, photographed Nov 1: "Van Voorhis Divorce Decree Is Signed." Mrs. Jessie Frances Van Voorhis of 99 East Main Street, Uniontown, granted the divorce by **President Judge H. S. Dumbauld**; grounds **"indignities"**; married Frederick, Md., March 12, 1938 — corroborating the first clipping's marriage date and place. The neighboring headline dates the issue to ~1944–45, with the exact paper/date still being pinned via Newspapers.com — the residual uncertainty stated, not rounded off.

### 5. The withheld stratum: what the engine refuses to print is the discriminator working

The notebook and prep files retain, labeled and withheld from packet material, every item the primary records have not yet adjudicated:

- The **"Sibyl" marriage record** (1961 Uniontown record naming "Sibyl Van Voorhis," almost certainly a clerical/indexing garble of Diane's own marriage): **unresolved — notebook only, not packet material unless settled.**
- **Two birth records for Suz** (Sep 15, 1962 Pittsburgh, matching family data; Apr 19, 1962 Uniontown public-records entry): one is a bad index entry; family data stands; the odd one is flagged so nobody is confused if it surfaces — retained, not deleted.
- **Juanita's husband and children still to be documented from records**; Eddie-as-Juanita's-son is **inference, not record** (cheat sheet: "[INFERENCE — not in GEDCOM; SUZ?]").
- **GEDCOM-only dates held as unverified:** John "Joshua" Zearley Jr. b. July 5, 1790 and Alcinda Baker Bright's Fort Martin Road burial are both marked "verification in progress" / "GEDCOM dates."

A tidying pass prints a clean tree. This rebuild prints nothing from the withheld stratum, and keeps the stratum itself in the notebook with its flags attached. That is lossless retention (row 4) doing visible work: consolidation is where the signal gets smoothed away, so the unresolved items are kept, labeled, and kept out of print at the same time.

## Rivals & discriminators (Stage 3, run before the evidence verdict)

**Rival 1 — Party-packet tidying.** The corrections serve the Nov 7 party: a clean, printable family story, with contradictions resolved in whatever direction makes the packet read smoothly.

- **Discriminator (pre-registered):** the fate of unadjudicated items. Tidying predicts they get dropped or smoothed so the packet prints clean; the forensic engine predicts they are retained, labeled, and withheld.
- **Verdict:** favors the thesis. The Sibyl record, the second Suz birth record, Juanita's undocumented husband/children, and the GEDCOM-only dates are all retained with flags and withheld from packet material in the same Nov 1 files that carry the confirmed corrections. A tidying pass has no use for a flagged stratum; the engine's output is defined by it.

**Rival 2 — Received-tree deference (row 12's falsifier shape).** The mediated tree is accepted on authority: the GEDCOM's 10-name list, or the old tree's Oct 11, 1877 date, stands because it is the filed record.

- **Discriminator:** whether a filed list or date survives contact with a primary record, or is overturned by one.
- **Verdict:** favors the thesis, three times over. The GEDCOM's 10-name list falls to the census households + SSA index; the old tree's 1877 date falls to Marion's self-filled draft card; the old tree's extra people (Lonnie, Judson) fall to the census households showing one man each time. No mediated item in this rebuild survives a primary-record check by authority alone.

**Capped claims:** The transfer claim is scoped to this rebuild — one domain, one day (Nov 1, 2026), one party deadline. It does not claim every family-history judgment he has ever made runs the engine, and it does not claim the notebook's own citations were re-verified by this pass (see Confidence). The GEDCOM-only dates above are testimony-grade and carry nothing; if their census verification fails, those items fall without touching the adjudicated corrections.

- Load-bearing rows (4, 12, 5, 1) are all supported at weights 8–9, and the thesis survives translation into their vocabulary without losing its claim: the claim is about issued corrections, retained flags, and withheld print — dated output, never felt motive.

This pass read the notebook's citations, not the underlying census images or draft cards themselves; the leg is therefore the adjudication events as dated in the working files, and any future check that re-opens those images is a re-verification of the leg, not a new thesis. Layer-B-only material (GEDCOM dates, member-submitted hints, family knowledge) is labeled testimony-grade or unverified wherever it appears and carries no load-bearing weight.

Scope limit, stated plainly: one new domain, one rebuild, three identity adjudications and one date adjudication. Row 4's prediction ("new domains get the same engine") gains one fresh instance here; it is not thereby proven for all domains.

## Falsifier

A new-domain rebuild in which he accepts a mediated summary against an available primary record — the GEDCOM list or old-tree date standing after a census household or draft card contradicts it — or in which a count or date is printed as settled while its own file still flags it open. The sharp form: a packet that prints "10 children" or "Oct 11, 1877" after the Nov 1 adjudications, or that prints the Sibyl marriage or the second Suz birth record without its flag. A correction later overturned by a better primary record does NOT falsify: that is the engine adjudicating again, which is the thesis.
