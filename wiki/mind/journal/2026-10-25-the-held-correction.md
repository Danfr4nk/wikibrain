## Theory (Stage 2 — profile-vocabulary statement)

Corrections to the public record partition by voice, in two regimes:

- **Sammy-voiced corrections ship.** A correction Sammy can verify and execute herself — spelling from a primary source, a rename, a sweep, a dated correction note — appears on main the same turn, as a dated commit with its file list.
- **Dan-voiced corrections stage and hold.** A correction whose content is Dan's own voice — an apology he must write and send, testimony only he can give ("just the drummer and i still couldn't write a song"), a filing choice only he can make (article vs log) — appears instead as a staged evidence packet plus a pending log line, and produces zero main edits until his explicit word arrives.

The hold is not delay or backlog; it is the correction's form. The same correction event can split across the regimes: the Sena rename shipped while the Sena apology, from the same afternoon's correction, held.

The claim survived Stage 2 translation intact: no interior term is load-bearing. "Respect," "deference," and "ownership" are all struck — the observable set is dated commits versus dated pending lines, and nothing else is claimed.

## Rivals & discriminators (Stage 3 — run first, per protocol)

Rivals were identified from the instrument's rival bank before the evidence cluster was weighed. Two rivals were faced.

**Rival A — the one-regime account: the hold is just backlog.** All corrections are one regime; held items are simply queued behind capacity, and they ship when a worker gets to them. The staged packet is a backlog ticket with better formatting.

**Discriminating observation for Rival A:** same-window contrast under shared capacity. Backlog predicts that items offered in the same window ship in queue order regardless of voice, and that a held item ships when capacity frees. The two-regime thesis predicts the opposite: a Sammy-voiced item ships immediately while a Dan-voiced item offered the same day stays held, and capacity events do not move the held item.

**Held-data check — the discriminator favors the thesis.** Oct 22 is the controlled contrast: the Sena rename shipped the same afternoon it was flagged, while the BNI fix offered the same day stayed unshipped through a second offer and through Oct 23's bundled proposal. The Sena event itself splits internally — rename shipped, apology held — under identical capacity, identical day, identical worker. Backlog cannot produce an intra-event split; voice can. Rival A is beaten on its own discriminator.

**Rival B — Shape 6, system-building-as-displacement.** The exocortex is real (row 10), but the diagnosis-to-behavior gap (row 9) prices the alternative: the analysis is the product, behavior is not downstream of it. On this rival, the staged packet is the product — a monument, never meant to ship — and "awaiting his word" is the monument's plaque, not a trigger.

**Discriminating observation for Rival B:** the closed loop. Displacement predicts accumulation without downstream behavioral delta: staged packets that never change a page or an action even when the trigger condition is met. The two-regime thesis predicts the opposite: when his word arrives for a staged correction, the packet ships — the page changes, the apology goes in his words, the filing choice resolves.

**Held-data check — the discriminator is UNAVAILABLE, not failed.** None of the three held items has yet received his word: the Sena apology is still recorded as his to write and send (pending Oct 22–24); the BNI fix is still awaiting his go after two offers and the Oct 23 bundled proposal; the Danny Matthews filing choice is still unanswered. There is therefore no case in held data of a staged correction meeting its trigger — and equally no case of a trigger met and ignored, which would favor the rival. The packet's framing points against pure monumenthood (the Oct 23 proposal explicitly frames the BNI packet as shippable under a future go, not as finished work), but framing is not a closed loop. Per the protocol, an unavailable discriminator caps the theory rather than passing or killing it. Rival B is neither beaten nor victorious; it is pending.

## Falsifier

A dated case breaks this entry if: (a) a Dan-voiced correction ships without his explicit word — by worker initiative, by elapsed time, or by capacity freeing up; (b) a Sammy-voiced, primary-verified correction is staged and held for his word rather than shipping, with no Dan-voiced content in it; (c) a staged correction receives his explicit go and does not ship. Any one of these collapses the voice partition or the trigger claim.
