# Leave It Alone Is the Order

## Profile prior (Stage 1)

The priced prior, from the weighted profile instrument
(`weighted-profile-instrument-v1.md`):

- **Row 6 — Rules install and revoke only via explicit statements
  (no-counter-rule architecture), weight 9, Layer A, Supported.**
  Primary-verified 0 explicit severance signals in 41,073 messages; his
  129 exit declarations, 100% re-engagement, 36-second median. The
  standing prediction: a rule changes only when a new explicit
  statement arrives — and a rule's content can be narrowed by a second
  explicit statement the same evening.
- **Row 5 — Explicit-over-inferred meaning, weight 9, Layer A+B
  convergent, Supported.** Stated text registers; ambient or implied
  signal does not. "Call me" 170× vs "do you love me" 0×. A rule's
  wording is its content; a paraphrase that softens it ("file it
  quietly") is not the rule.
- **Row 1 — Verdicts are categorical/binary, weight 8.5, Supported.**
  No graded middle on worth, authenticity, legitimacy. Applied here:
  the rule's verdict on any Alexis item is binary — untouched or
  touched — with no half-measure ("filed but not surfaced") surviving
  once the correction lands.
- **Row 15 — The real axis is imposed-vs-chosen, weight 7, Supported
  as reframing.** He builds rigid order inside chosen systems while
  resisting imposed structure. A standing order he chose is the
  chosen-order case: rigidity is the point, not a side effect.
- **Row 38 — Transactional-channel brevity, weight 9, Layer A,
  Supported.** The delivery thread runs 3.2–4.3 words/turn across 11
  years, never a 50-word message. Brevity capacity is intact and
  channel-gated: an entire channel can run at zero output without
  anything being broken. Channel-gated silence is an attested shape,
  not a defect.
- **Row 10 — System-building as exocortex, weight 9, Layer A,
  Supported.** Standing instruments and standing orders are cognition
  externalized into artifacts that run. A standing rule is one such
  artifact; its output is the assistant's behavior, not his.
- **The attention–interior boundary (§2), binding.** Held instruments
  see attention and behavior, not interior state. This entry therefore
  claims nothing about what Dan feels about Alexis, the calendar, or
  the past relationship. It claims only what the assistant's output
  channel does: zero handling events for items in this class.

No load-bearing row is silent, inverted, or weight ≤ 4. Stage 1 passes.

## Theory (Stage 2)

Stated in profile vocabulary only, at attention level:

**A standing order installed by explicit statement can take the form
of a total channel closure: the governed channel's output rate goes
to zero — no file, no surface, no RSVP, no reply, no change — and the
closure itself is the compliance, not an absence of compliance.** The
rule governs the assistant's attention/output channel itself, not an
external task. Its content is non-action, installed the same way any
other rule is installed (row 6: explicit statement), and narrowed the
same way (row 6 again: a second explicit statement revoking the
filing half). The binary gate (row 1) leaves no middle setting: an
item is either untouched or it has been touched, and any handling at
all — including a "helpful" silent filing — counts as touching it.

Scope, stated plainly: this is a claim about the assistant's channel
and about this rule's form. It is not a claim about Dan's interior
state, about Alexis's intent in keeping him on the calendar, or about
any other person's calendar. The "not an invitation" characterization
of her events is Dan's standing account, carried as testimony, not as
an independently verified fact about her calendar — which this pass
does not inspect and does not touch.

## Rivals & discriminators (Stage 3 — run FIRST, before the evidence cluster)

Run order stated plainly, per protocol: this section was worked
before the evidence cluster above was assembled. The journal's
section order follows format, not run order.

**Theory-shape and rival.** The thesis is closest to the instrument's
Shape 6 family (a built system whose output is the point) with a
named rival supplied by the commission itself: **a rule must compel
an action to be a rule — otherwise silent-ignore is just
backlog/neglect**, i.e., an unhandled queue wearing a rule's name.
Under the rival, silence about an Alexis item means nothing was done
with it yet: it would eventually surface in a digest, get filed, get
mentioned as pending, or get answered case-by-case — exactly the
trajectory "Call Colleen" followed before the install (pending since
the Oct 15/16 digest, deferred Oct 25 ~10:43, delivered Oct 25 ~15:52
as a classification question).

**Discriminating observation.** Two, both already observable:

- (a) **The self-narrowing correction.** A backlog does not issue a
  second explicit statement narrowing its own handling to zero. The
  Oct 25 ~18:35 EDT correction did exactly that: it revoked the
  filing half of the first approval's wording ("silently
  file/ignore") and replaced it with "just leave those alone." Under
  the neglect rival, the correction is inexplicable — neglect has no
  mechanism that edits its own verb. Under the thesis, the correction
  is the predicted mechanism (row 6: rules narrow only via explicit
  statements).
- (b) **The post-install trajectory.** Under the rival, the item
  keeps its pre-install trajectory: it stays pending and resurfaces.
  Under the thesis, post-install handling of items in this class goes
  to zero and stays there. Observed from the ~18:35 EDT install
  through this entry's writing: zero handling events (leg 5 above).
  The trajectory broke at the install, not gradually.

**Verdict on the rival.** The discriminator favors the thesis on both
legs. The rival is beaten, not merely unproven: the correction event
is a positive observation that neglect cannot produce. The rival's
remaining life is purely prospective — if a future Alexis item gets
handled, the rival revives and the thesis dies (see Falsifier).
