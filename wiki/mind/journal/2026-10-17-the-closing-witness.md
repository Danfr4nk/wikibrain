## Theory (Stage 2 — profile-vocabulary statement)

Interior terms struck before drafting: "appetite," "compulsion," "drive," "craving," "the loop didn't close" (the spending pages' interior phrasing — "the first one didn't close the loop" is restated as "the first order did not terminate the purchase episode"). None survive into the thesis.

**Attention-level statement:** In the 2018–19 Amazon ledgers, short-window exact-duplicate orders — identical item title, identical price, separate order numbers, ≤ ~8 hours apart — terminate purchase attention in that item line. After the duplicate, the line shows no further orders and no support/maintenance orders for the remainder of the ledgers, in records that otherwise show dense follow-on ordering for used categories. Category-expansion purchases show the opposite trajectory: continued, use-attested, escalating follow-on orders (repair parts, accessories, infrastructure, tier upgrades). The duplicate is the closing witness of a binary purchase episode (rows 1, 41) — the second order is the episode's terminating statement — not a second appetite event (rows 30/50, disqualified). Translation did not kill the thesis: every term above is checkable in the receipt logs.

## Rivals & discriminators (Stage 3 — run first, per protocol)

**Theory-shape:** Shape 4 — Acquisition ("he's acquiring/collecting — the drive wants more").
**Profile-weighted rival:** Ti closed-set completion — the duplicate is closure behavior, completing a set to a unique-witness condition, not appetite.
**Discriminating observation (instrument §5):** exact duplicates in short windows (completion) vs novel-category expansion (acquisition); and post-acquisition attention — does the item enter rotation and get attended to, or go silent once the set closes?

**Held-data result:** All three short-window exact-duplicate episodes (watches, wedge, dolls) show post-duplicate silence in the item line — no further orders, no support/maintenance gear, in ledgers where used categories are otherwise followed by dense follow-on ordering. Both control arcs (drones, boards) show the opposite: sustained, use-attested attention (repair parts, belts, wheels, wall rack) and escalation. The acquisition reading predicts duplicates followed by continued category attention and expansion; the ledger sorts the other way: duplicates close lines, expansions open them. **The rival is beaten on its own discriminator.**

**The nominal theory is additionally disqualified at Stage 1:** it borrows rows 30 and 50 (both weight 3, silent/untested) — the instrument itself disqualifies "acquisition drive" as an explanation. The 2019 page's phrasing — "the thing impulsivity is failing to fix" — is interior vocabulary and was struck at Stage 2, not imported.

**Sub-rival adjudicated honestly:** the Boosted Mini X pair could be read as a long-window duplicate. It is distinguished, not dodged: functional-redundancy pairs at eleven-week windows with support infrastructure read as planning (the 2019 page's move-prep framing, his dated prediction about the boards landing twice); identical-item duplicates at ≤8-hour windows with silence afterward read as closure. The two classes separate on window length, functional differentiation, and post-pair attention — three held-data variables, not one.

## Falsifier

A documented short-window exact-duplicate purchase (identical item, identical price, separate orders, ≤ ~8 hours apart) whose item line then shows sustained attended use — repair/support/maintenance orders, accessories, or a tier upgrade in the same line — i.e., a duplicate that *opens* a category instead of closing it. One such case reclassifies the pair as an arc; the mechanism predicts none in held data.
