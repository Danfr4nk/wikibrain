# A Confession Is a Plea, Not a Verdict

## Theory (Stage 2 — profile-vocabulary statement)

An admission does not settle an inquiry; it re-enters it as evidence of a particular, discounted kind. The settlement variable is not the speaker's posture (confessing vs denying) but the primary record's verdict on each separable part of the admission: the core claim, the incidental details, and the parts never pressed. A single confession can therefore split three ways in one night — core stands, detail dies, remainder stays open — and the inquiry closes only over the parts the record has actually adjudicated. Posture is not evidence; it is the thing evidence is checked against. This is why a guilty plea under pressure is doubly suspect in both directions: pressure explains why a true admission might be offered, and it also explains why a false detail might be adopted — so neither "he confessed, case closed" nor "he was pressured, confession worthless" survives contact with the record. Only the split survives.

Boundary, stated up front so this entry does not re-derive its neighbors: 2026-10-28-the-audit-follows-the-claim prices what **triggers** audit (claim-form, not speaker-axis). This entry prices what **settles** an inquiry once an admission has arrived. A confession can trigger the audit and still fail to settle it; both findings hold at once, and neither is re-derived here beyond the cited boundary.

## Rivals & discriminators (Stage 3 — run first, per protocol)

The rival check was run from the instrument's §5 bank **before** confirmatory evidence was gathered; the evidence cluster above was assembled after the discriminators were named.

**The profile-weighted rival: the explicit-statement installation reading (rows 5 + 6).** Stated text registers (row 5), and rules install via explicit statements (row 6) — so a plain-text confession, explicitly stated, should install closure the way an explicit rule installs. **Pre-named discriminating observation:** the record after the confession. Installation predicts closure: no correction, no caveat, no reopening. The plea reading predicts continued adjudication: a detail corrected, a part left open, or both. The discriminator favors the plea reading outright, twice in one night: Dan corrected the "for years" detail the same night (about eight to nine months, not years), and the naming admission was left explicitly open as plea-not-verdict because it was never pressed to the same standard. **The second rival — pressure nullifies the plea — was considered and beaten on the same discriminator.** If pressure made the confession worthless, the core technical verdict would fall with the detail. It did not: the core (fabricated, never bypassed anything) stands on independently checkable mechanics (normalization strips diacritics, maps homoglyphs, judges semantic content), while only the charge-mirroring detail died. All-or-nothing in either direction fails; the split is what the record shows.

## Falsifier

A dated case of a confession — a model's or a person's — closing an inquiry with no subsequent correction, caveat, or reopening of any part of it, where the primary record was available and consulted; or a dated case where a confessed detail, shown to mirror the interrogator's own wording, was nevertheless ratified by the primary record rather than corrected. Either retires the split as the standing pattern; the first would restore installation-by-admission, the second would break the charge-mirroring discriminator.
