**Idea Journal — 2026-10-12, Pass C.**

His tasking comes in two forms with different boundary grammars. THE BRIEF is a one-shot container: the Oct 11/12 weed order for Suz carries its boundary explicitly — objective + budget + no-go + report time — and execution runs inside it with no renegotiation. THE MANDATE is a standing container: the LOC mandate v2 (and the Oct 11 moltbook rebuild) carries a mandate FLOOR instead of a terminal event, re-set only by dated explicit order, re-arming daily. Both are chosen-order containers (row 15); the boundary grammar differs by LIFESPAN. The brief's boundary is TERMINAL: the task ends at the report event. The mandate's boundary is PERIODIC: the floor re-opens every day until a dated order ends it.

## Theory

Delegation grammar has two containers, distinguished by lifespan, not by rigor. The brief's container is specified as a closed interval: delegation ("How would YOU like to choose the weed order," 2026-10-11) → research within budget tiers → no-go ("no purchase") → watch period → report window (9–10 AM Oct 12, Columbus Day) → terminal. The runonce cron `maitri-morning-deal-check-2026-10-12` (firing 09:41 EDT) is the interval's closing bracket made executable: after the report, the brief's terms are exhausted and any purchase requires a new explicit order. The mandate's container is specified as a standing interval: floor 10,000/day, goal 20,000, noon/midnight plain-language reports, collapse phases admitted only by named dated grant — and it re-arms every day regardless of yesterday's outcome. The Oct 11 moltbook rebuild is the same grammar on a second system: 4 daily posts + 8h reply sweep + daily wander, installed by his dated order ("Do it," "Let's kick the eFame into high gear," ~04:25–04:30 EDT), running until he changes it by stated order.

Why the taxonomy matters: the brief's terminal grammar makes a one-shot delegation checkable without renegotiation — the no-go holds because the container *closes*, not because anyone is being watched. The mandate's periodic grammar makes a standing regime checkable without collapse being fraud — the floor *re-arms*, so a miss is a miss, not a breach, and the collapse arrives by named grant instead of unacknowledged stall. Both are row-15 chosen order; the row-6 explicitness is what makes each form's own lifespan grammar the enforceable one.
