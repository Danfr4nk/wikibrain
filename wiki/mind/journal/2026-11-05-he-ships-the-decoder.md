## Theory (Stage 2)

**Thesis, in profile vocabulary:** Dan's rule for incoming signal is row 5: stated text registers, implied signal does not. Applied to Dan as a *sender*, that rule predicts the dated record in this cluster. A sender who holds row 5 cannot rely on his own implied signal registering either — unstated tone, intent, and context are, by his own architecture, not in the message. So he treats his own output as surface text a default decoder will predictably misread in specific, nameable ways — deformed by dictation, stripped of delivery, compressed by typing, extreme in register (row 36). His response is not to change the output. It is to **ship the decoder with the message**: to attach, by explicit statement (row 6's only installation channel), the rule the receiver is to decode that output with.

The system has three observable forms, one system rather than three habits:

1. **Pre-emptive decoders, placed before the risky output.** A disclaimer goes *ahead* of an output whose default decoding is wrong, naming the specific misreading it exists to prevent — that nothing has changed, that caring has not changed — rather than asking generally to be read kindly.
2. **Standing decode rules, installed by explicit statement.** Recurring deformed surfaces get a rule stated once and maintained: dictation garbling is dictation, not a stance; a flat dictated transcript is not seriousness; the blunt register is endearment, not disrespect. The rule, not a per-instance plea, is applied on every later read.
3. **Retroactive annotations of past output.** Where already-sent output mis-signals — a message reduced to "." that reads as content, emphasis, or agreement — Dan supplies the decode after the fact, unprompted, with the instruction that it never be read as what it looks like.

One grammar runs through all three: **an explicit statement (row 6), about a named misreading, attached to output whose implied signal Dan does not trust to register (row 5), then externalized as a standing instrument the receiver consults (row 10).** The last step fixes the system's shape: because the decode lives in the exocortex as a standing rule, per-instance labor is progressively replaced by installed rules. A surface decoded once does not need full re-decoding at every occurrence. New labor appears at *new* surfaces — a new risky output, a new channel deformation, a newly surfaced piece of past output — the signature of installation, not repetition. Attention-level restatement: disclaimers are placed, rules are stated and maintained, annotations are delivered; no felt state is claimed in either direction.

**Stage 3 pre-registration (before the evidence verdict, expanded below):** Live rival: §5 Shape 2, regulation/stim — the annotations are self-soothing, produced to settle the sender. Further rivals: self-consciousness-alone (row 25) and politeness/face-management. Discriminators named in advance: (a) *Shape* — regulation repeats per trigger at undiminished rate; a decoder addresses a named misreading in a receiver, is placed before or attached to a specific output, and once installed replaces per-instance labor for that surface. (b) *Function* — monitoring talk talks about the self; a decoder changes how a receiver decodes later output and is observably applied on later reads. (c) *Channel/target* — face-management concentrates where social costs are highest and targets social misreadings; the decoder thesis predicts full-strength labor in the highest-concentration private channel, targeting factual misreadings (caring, change, what a past message was). Undiminished repetition for an already-decoded surface, annotations naming no misreading, or absence from the private channel would hand the entry to a rival.
