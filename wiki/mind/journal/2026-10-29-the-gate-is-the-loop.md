## Theory (Stage 2 — profile-vocabulary statement)

A built system's output changes a subsequent decision if and only if two conditions hold together: **(a) form** — the output arrives as a binary gate verdict (permit/forbid, pass/fail, install/revoke) that the next act can execute without a further judgment step; and **(b) installation** — the verdict's issuer holds the grant for that field by explicit statement (row 6). Either condition alone is insufficient. Measurement-form outputs (counts, scores, digests, profiles, calibration numbers) fail (a): they describe a domain but hand the next act no executable verdict, so they accumulate append-only. Verdict-shaped outputs that were never installed as the decider fail (b): they advise a decision that someone else still makes, so they change nothing. The exocortex therefore does not outsource decisions in general — it closes loops exactly at its installed gates, and nowhere else.

Boundary, stated up front so this entry does not re-derive its neighbors: this is a claim about **token acts** (this filing restarted, this batch refused, this sweep ordered). It does not claim that any gate changed a diagnosed pattern's subsequent *rate* — that question belongs to 2026-10-27-the-loop-closes-in-production-only, which found production loops closing and decision-pattern rates open, and is cited here, not re-derived. A gate can change the next act while the pattern's rate stands still; both findings hold at once.

## Falsifier
