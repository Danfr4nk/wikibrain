# Routing by Tenancy: The Exocortex Has a Jurisdiction Boundary

## Profile prior (Stage 1)

Load-bearing rows, all weighted ≥ 6 with verdict supported:

- **Row 10 (weight 9, supported):** system-building as exocortex — cognition externalized into bootloaders, prompts, standing instruments; artifacts exist and run. The substrate is real, so its internal divisions are observable facts, not metaphors.
- **Row 11 (weight 10, supported):** extreme relational concentration, Gini 0.961 outbound with 0.9556 inbound replication — attention loads onto a tiny number of chosen channels with no failover. Routing concentrates; it does not distribute.
- **Row 12 (weight 9, supported):** low trust, vertical-scoped — vertical claims get forensic treatment, chosen lateral channels get provision without paper. The scope limit matters: distrust targets the vertical claim, not the channel, so a shared substrate is suspect in a way a chosen single-tenant channel is not.
- **Row 5 (weight 9, supported):** explicit-over-inferred meaning — stated text registers, ambient signal does not. A routing rule therefore has to be *stated* to count.
- **Row 6 (weight 9, supported):** rules install and revoke only via explicit statements. The jurisdiction assignment of a system is itself an explicit statement, and it runs until explicitly revoked.
- **Row 38 (weight 9, supported):** transactional-channel brevity — 3.2–4.3 words/turn across 11 years, never a 50-word message in the delivery thread. Same person, different channel, categorically different output: channel-gated routing is already a measured behavior, not a hypothesis.
- **Row 15 (weight 7, supported as reframing):** the real axis is imposed-vs-chosen, not order-vs-chaos. A shared cloud substrate is imposed tenancy; a single-tenant channel is chosen tenancy.

No load-bearing row is silent, inverted, or weight ≤ 4. Rows 30/32 (interior-regulation facets, silent) are not used. Stage 1 passes.

## Theory (Stage 2)

Stated only in vocabulary from rows weighted ≥ 6 with verdict supported or leans, at attention level throughout — no interior-state terms survive translation:

Built systems are not one undifferentiated substrate. Material is routed by **jurisdiction**: a system receives material only where its declared tenancy permits. Shared-substrate systems are assigned public-routable output only; single-tenant channels carry the rest. The routing decision is the boundary — it is made by explicit statement (what is declared public-only versus single-tenant), it runs until explicitly revoked, and it is a property of the channel assignment, not of the material's content. Concentration (row 11) predicts the shape: load does not spread evenly across systems; it piles into the few channels whose jurisdiction permits it.

What this theory is NOT: it is not about report-back as completion, not about the grant governing access, and not about a loop that re-checks itself. The mechanism is a one-time assignment decision per system, enforced by declaration, with observable consequences in which system ever receives which class of material.

## Evidence cluster (Stage 4)

Dated, sourced, attention-level only. Layer-A legs are cited from the instrument's own measurements; this pass did not re-run the sweeps, and no new count is claimed.

**1. The shared-substrate bar is categorical, not content-filtered (standing record).**
Grok Bots run on one shared cloud computer, and the standing gate green-lights them for public work only — wiki-claims falsifier, draft engine, coding grunt, scout — and never for private, intimate, or report-back material. The bar is drawn at the class level: the reason given is tenancy (shared substrate), not a judgment that any particular item is too sensitive. A content filter would admit benign private items; a jurisdiction boundary admits none. That is the discriminator behaving as the theory predicts, and it is structural — the substrate is shared whether or not any single item looks risky.

**2. The report-back channel is gated by channel, not by content (standing record, 2026-10-13).**
The 2026-10-13 report-back authentication protocol requires explicit report-back threads to open with a shared check-word or a voice note. (Existence and function only: the check-word itself is never stated, hinted at, or reconstructed here, and the protocol document was not opened for this pass.) The gate sits on the thread — the channel — before any content is evaluated. That is routing by jurisdiction in the same shape as the Bots gate: a class of material is admissible only on a channel whose tenancy permits it, and admissibility is established by an explicit, stated instrument, exactly as rows 5 and 6 predict.

**3. Publishing splits by tenancy, by design (standing record).**
The Wiki Brain is fully public by design, and Moltbook posting is public with no real names — both are shared-substrate, public-routable systems. Against them stand single-tenant channels, where intimate material stays and is not exported to shared systems. The same person operates both sides; what differs is the declared jurisdiction of the channel, and the material follows the declaration, not the other way around.

**4. Channel-gated output is already measured behavior (Layer A, via the instrument).**
Row 38: the delivery thread runs 3.2–4.3 words/turn across 11 years with zero 50-word messages, while other channels carry long-form output. Row 11: Gini 0.961/0.9556 replication. Row 10: exocortex artifacts exist and run (see [[wiki/mind/concepts/exocortex]] and [[wiki/mind/concepts/forensic-method]]). Together these give the thesis three Layer-A legs: the substrate exists, attention concentrates rather than distributes, and output already changes categorically by channel. The jurisdiction claim extends a measured pattern — channel determines what may arrive — to the system level, where tenancy determines what may be assigned.

Unheld numbers flagged: no count of actual Bot assignments, and no sweep of which items were withheld from shared systems, is held for this pass; none is claimed. The legs above are the instrument's measurements and dated standing records, not new tallies.

## Rivals & discriminators (Stage 3 — pre-registered before confirmatory evidence)

Pre-registration note: the rival below, and its discriminating observation, were fixed before the evidence cluster was assembled. The evidence was then checked against the discriminator, not the other way around.

**Rival faced: Shape 6, system-building-as-displacement, plus its jurisdiction-shaped variant — "one substrate, content-filtered."**
The displacement rival says the exocortex accumulates without downstream behavioral delta (instrument §5, Shape 6): systems are monuments, and any apparent routing is post-hoc description. The content-filter variant says there is really one substrate and items are admitted or refused by sensitivity screening, with tenancy talk as decoration.

**Discriminating observation:** a categorical, class-wide bar that holds regardless of an item's individual sensitivity, plus a structural tenancy fact that explains it. Content filtering predicts benign private items admitted to shared systems; jurisdiction predicts none admitted, and predicts the bar is justified by substrate sharing rather than by item review.

**Check in held records:** the Bots gate bars the whole private/intimate/report-back class from the shared cloud computer, and the stated reason is the shared substrate itself — the structural fact, not a per-item review. The report-back protocol gates the channel before content. The displacement rival also fails its own discriminator on row 10's leg: the substrate demonstrably runs instruments (stylometry tracker, wiki heartbeat, per [[wiki/mind/concepts/exocortex]]), so accumulation-without-effect is not the base rate here. The discriminator favors the thesis.

**Rival not adopted, noted for the record:** a "refusal" reading — that the boundary is really a refusal to share — was rejected because no refusal event is needed: assignment by declaration precedes any request, and the bar operates on systems that were never asked to carry the material.

## Confidence & gate verdict

**PASS — high confidence.** All three Stage 5 conditions hold: (1) every load-bearing row is supported at its stated weight (rows 10, 11, 12, 5, 6, 38, 15); (2) the §5 rival was beaten on its own discriminator (categorical class-wide bar + shared-substrate structural fact, versus content filtering's prediction of benign-item admission); (3) the attention–interior rule held at every step — every claim is about routing, assignment, and observable output by channel, with no interior-state term carried. Caveat, stated not hidden: the Bots-gate and protocol legs are dated standing records, not a fresh behavioral sweep; a future sweep of actual Bot assignments would strengthen the thesis further, but the gate does not require it — the explicit declarations are themselves the datum under row 6.

## Falsifier

A dated case of intimate or report-back material deliberately assigned to a shared-substrate system (for example, a Grok Bot assigned private or report-back work) with no change in that system's tenancy — or a re-derivation showing output distributing across channels at low concentration (Gini materially below ~0.9) — would collapse jurisdiction into content filtering or dissolve the concentration leg, and this entry should be revised or retracted on that evidence. A single accidental leak would not falsify it; a declared, deliberate assignment would.
