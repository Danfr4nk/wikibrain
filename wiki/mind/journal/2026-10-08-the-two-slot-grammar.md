# The Two-Slot Grammar

**Premise in one line:** His binding grammar has exactly two slots — the rule-declarative and the command-imperative — and the interpretive register is structurally absent: 0× in 106,629 sent messages. Because no feeling-statement has a binding slot, no confirmation can durably settle, and the check-in loop must re-run forever.

## Theory

Read his outbound at the level of sentence-form, not content, and it sorts into two slots and a void:

- **Slot 1 — the rule-declarative.** Positive verbal formulations that install: *"Annie Ulmer from now on its just you and me"* (2015-12-01, primary source, iMessage EXIF), the 129 severance declarations, the AI-bootloader "laws." Declarative in mood, future-directed, universal — rule-shaped. Once installed, no counter-rule from behavior is admitted (row 6).
- **Slot 2 — the command-imperative.** Procedural summons that request an action: "call me" (170× / 129 held), "you up," "pick up," "where are you," "answer me," "you there." Imperative in mood, channel-opening, each with a verifiable completion state — pick up, answer, transmit.
- **The void — the interpretive register.** "do you love me" 0×, "are we ok" 0×, "am i crazy" 0×, "i cant do this" 0× — across 106,629 sent messages (dat:0081), replicated across 99,332 held sent rows (dat:0755), zero in all three measured threads including the Tom friend control. He does not under-use the interpretive question; he does not use it at all. General question density is merely thin (0.75 vs 1.30 per 100 words, 43% below norm) — the interpretive-feeling end of it is a hard zero.

The mechanism this buys: a compliment or a reassurance is an interpretation task — it asks to be believed, and the architecture's form filter (row 5) has no slot for a belief-request. A channel request is a procedure with a completion state. So the only confirmations the system can process are events (channel opens), and events have no durable state — each one expires, so each one must be re-run. **Row 23's decay is the form filter's exhaust.** The check-in loop does not re-fire because memory fails; it re-fires because no sentence in the available register can install a settled state. Eleven years, monthly cadence, zero settle events in the record.

This also explains the inbound side of the same filter: 0 severance signals in 41,073 of her messages (dat:0081) — nothing in the format his system recognizes ever said "closed." The filter is symmetric. Her feeling-declaratives ("I love you," "we're fine") arrive as text; they cannot arrive as binding confirmations, because the grammar has no slot for them to land in.

## Rivals & discriminators

- **R1 — Shape 5's check-in rung (the profile-weighted rival).** "Call me" is reassurance-seeking on the escalation ladder (row 23); the zeros are stylistic, not structural — he just doesn't talk that way. *Discriminator:* if the zeros are style and the summons are reassurance-seeking, the register should track the relationship: new attachments and low-anxiety channels should show different shapes. Observed: the summons register ports to Kristin at near-Annie per-1k rates and the canonical zeros hold in the Tom friend control — the shape is system-wide, not relationship-tuned. The 2026-08-23 correction further weakens the pure check-in reading: Annie answered faster than Dan in every year 2015–2026, so the summons were never latency-driven. R1 is beaten on held data.
- **R2 — The trust-scaled decay (the reassurance page's own mechanism).** Confirmations decay because Trust is 9th percentile — confirmations don't carry as priors. *Discriminator:* trust-scaling predicts the summons rate tracks attachment anxiety/distrust: Annie (11-year trauma bond) ≫ Kristin (10-week new attachment). Observed: 0.79/1k vs 0.97/1k — comparable at comparable intensity. If decay were trust-scaled, a new attachment should run a much lower summons rate; the form filter predicts intensity-tracking instead. R2 loses the discriminator on current evidence. *Caveat:* the Kristin window is ten weeks against eleven years and logistical-vs-check-in function was not disambiguated, so this rival is beaten, not buried — the trust mechanism may still do work the form filter doesn't cover.
- **R3 — The zeros are romantic-vulnerability avoidance.** He won't ask "do you love me" because the answer is scary. *Discriminator:* vulnerability-avoidance predicts the zeros concentrate in romantic channels. Observed: zero in the Tom male-friend control too, and "are you okay" ×20 exists in the held export — procedural check-ins are fine, interpretive ones are absent everywhere. R3 fails the control.

## Falsifier

Any one of: (a) a single "do you love me" (or "are we ok" / "am i crazy" / "i cant do this") in his future sent text — demotes the structural reading to stylistic preference, per the reassurance page's own falsifier; (b) a new high-intensity channel with summons rates collapsing to near-zero at comparable intensity — the trust mechanism beats the form mechanism; (c) a declarative feeling-statement ("I love you," "we're fine") shown to durably settle the loop — asked once, never re-measured — kills the no-slot claim. The standing place to watch for (b) and (c) is any future attachment reaching Kristin-level intensity.
