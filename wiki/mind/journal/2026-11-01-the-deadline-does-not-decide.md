# The Deadline Does Not Decide
_Layer: interpreted/journal — a dated thesis entry. It reasons over self/ exhibits and kb/ evidence, which it cites rather than restating._

## Profile prior (Stage 1)

Load-bearing rows, all weighted ≥ 6 with verdict **supported**:

- **Row 5 (weight 9, supported) — Explicit-over-inferred meaning.** Stated text registers; ambient/implied signal does not. Corpus legs: "call me" 170× vs "do you love me" 0×; lyrics as timbre, not message. A deadline is ambient signal: a date on an invitation, a trial window, a birthday arriving. Under row 5, a date cannot speak; only a statement can.
- **Row 6 (weight 9, supported) — Rules install and revoke only via explicit statements.** Primary-verified 0 explicit severance signals in 41,073 messages; his 129 exit declarations, 100% re-engagement, 36-second median. The architecture has no counter-rule slot for "the date passed."
- **Row 1 (weight 8.5, supported) — Verdicts are categorical/binary gates.** A gate flips by adjudication, never by graded softening. A deadline's passage is graded time, not a gate event; nothing in the architecture converts elapsed time into a flip.
- **Row 19 (weight 9.5, supported) — No-counter-rule attachment.** A bond runs on its last stated rule until an explicit severance statement arrives. Used here only as the architecture parallel: the same gate that keeps a bond open keeps a dated item open.

Boundary row, NOT load-bearing (weight ≤ 4, so barred from carrying the thesis per Stage 1):

- **Row 49 / conflict C13 (weight 4, disconfirmed as worded) — "time = countdown."** Explicit urgency constructions run at 0.35–0.42× of others'; "too late" at 1.86× is the sole exception. The urgency-vocabulary finding is context, not a leg: this entry is about the decision mechanism, not about how often urgency words appear (see Genesis boundary vs the Oct 5 entry).

Rows 30/32 (Impulsiveness, Vulnerability — silent) and every interior-state term were excluded at Stage 1. Nothing below claims what he feels about a deadline; everything below is dated output behavior.

## Theory (Stage 2)

**Thesis, in profile vocabulary:** For Dan, a stated deadline does not produce a stated decision. A decision arrives only as an explicit statement — his yes/no, or another party's explicit statement that changes the item — or it does not arrive at all. When a deadline passes undecided, the channel does not escalate and does not default: it goes flat. The item stays exactly where its last explicit statement left it, neither done nor decided, until a statement moves it.

**Stage 3 pre-registration (recorded before the evidence verdict, expanded below):** Rival — the deadline-as-implicit-verdict account, under which a passed deadline forces a gate-flip or escalates output (the row 37 silence-lengthens pattern generalized to dated items). Discriminator — post-deadline channel behavior: escalation or a default decision favors the rival; flat output with no decision favors the thesis.

## Rivals & discriminators (Stage 3, run before the evidence verdict)

**Rival 1 — Deadline-as-implicit-verdict.** A passed deadline functions as a decision: the item defaults (yes becomes no, open becomes closed) or the pressure forces a verdict. This is the ordinary-calendar reading and the Shape 5-adjacent account: a declaration-like event (here, a date) treated as settling the matter.

- **Discriminator:** the 72 hours after the deadline. Default-or-verdict predicts a recorded decision or a stated closure ("too late, so no"). Flat-output-plus-no-decision predicts the thesis.
- **Verdict:** Cogar favors the thesis. Oct 30 reminder → Oct 31 deadline-day check → deadline passes → no decision, no closure statement, no escalation recorded. The rival's predicted default never appears in the record.

**Rival 2 — Deadline-pressure escalation (row 37 generalized).** Row 37 (weight 9, supported) shows silence lengthening him in a bond channel (2-hour wait → 49.1 words/turn). Generalized, a looming/passed deadline should lengthen or multiply his output about the item.

- **Discriminator:** his own output volume about the item around the deadline vs before it.
- **Verdict:** favors the thesis, with a scope note. Around Cogar's deadline, the dated output is Sammy's reminders, not his escalation; his side stays silent on the RSVP. Row 37 is channel-scoped to an active bond thread under non-response; a dated administrative item is a different channel, and the escalation does not transfer. The scope limit is itself informative: urgency does not generalize across channels.

**Rival 3 — The "too late" exception (C13).** "Too late" runs at 1.86× others' rate — perhaps passed deadlines do register, in that one phrase.

- **Discriminator:** a recorded "too late" (or equivalent) applied by him to one of these items as the reason for a decision.
- **Verdict:** unavailable — no such statement appears for Cogar, LYNY, or the birthday pick in the dated record. The exception stays untriggered here; it caps the thesis's scope rather than defeating it. If he later says "too late, so no" about Cogar, that is the falsifier firing (see below), not a patch.

**Capped claims:** The thesis is scoped to dated administrative/decision items with explicit deadlines or dates. It does not claim deadlines never matter to him anywhere — the "too late" exception and row 37's channel-scoped escalation are live counterweights, held open.

## Confidence & gate verdict (Stage 5)

**PASS — high confidence, scoped.**

- Load-bearing rows (5, 6, 1, 19) are all supported at weights 8.5–9.5, and the thesis survives translation into their vocabulary without losing its claim.
- The Stage 3 rival was faced before the evidence verdict and beaten on its own discriminator: the Cogar deadline passed with flat output and no decision, which is the thesis's predicted outcome and the rival's non-outcome.
- The attention–interior rule held at every step: the claim is about dated output (reminders sent, answers absent, decisions unrecorded), never about felt urgency, caring, or motive.
- Stage 4 gate met: the thesis rests on dated records (Sep 16 invitation, Oct 30 reminder, Oct 31 deadline-day check, Oct 28 shortlist, Oct 31 unanswered birthday question), not on dossier testimony alone. Layer B material (the attachment-model page's testimony figures) is used only as an architecture parallel and flagged as such; the deadline cases themselves are dated-record legs.

Scope limit, stated plainly: three passed-or-arriving dates (Cogar deadline passed; birthday date arrived; LYNY show future) plus one pending test (Wispr Nov 2–4). The mechanism claim is high-confidence for this class of item; it is not a universal law of his decisions.

## Falsifier

A dated case in which he treats a passed deadline **itself** as settling a yes/no — in his own explicit words, with no intervening explicit statement from another party and no new information beyond the date's passage. The sharp form: he says about Cogar, or any comparable item, "the deadline passed, so that's a no" (or "so I'm going") and acts on the date alone. A vendor auto-conversion (e.g., Wispr lapsing or charging without his statement) does NOT falsify: that is someone else's default, not his decision. A decision citing new facts learned after the deadline also does not falsify — only the date-as-verdict does.

Live test: the Wispr Nov 2 check and ~Nov 4 trial end. If he answers the Nov 2 check with a decision, the gate worked as usual (statement, not date). If Nov 4 passes with no statement, the thesis predicts continued flatness — and the item stays open in the record even as the trial ends.
