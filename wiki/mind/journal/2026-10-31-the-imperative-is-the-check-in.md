**Thesis: in the attachment register, a check-in is only ever issued as an imperative — a command with a verifiable completion state ("call me," "pick up," "where are you") — never as an interpretive question about the bond ("do you love me," 0× in eleven years of sent text); the imperative mood is not his tone, it is the only speech-act shape whose completion this architecture can process.**

## Theory (Stage 2, profile vocabulary only)

An imperative completes on execution. "Call me" completes when the phone is picked up; "where are you" completes when coordinates arrive; a transfer completes when it lands. An interpretive question completes only on belief — on a stated feeling being taken as a durable prior — and the held record shows both halves of that completion failing: the interpretive channel is refused at intake (zero canonical questions across two exports and three threads), and confirmations decay at storage (Trust, 9th percentile; row 23). So the check-in loop, which must re-fire indefinitely, can only be built out of utterances that complete without belief. That is why its entire vocabulary is procedural: summons ("call me," "you up," "pick up," "answer me," "you there") and measurement (location requests, 43 in the Annie thread). Even the interrogative-shaped members of the set are imperatives in function — "where are you" is not an inquiry into her state, it is a summons for coordinates, and it belongs in the same column as "call me," not in the column with "do you love me."

The linguistic profile supplies the affection-side confirmation that this is a grammar, not a deficit: "DRIVE SAFE" in all caps reads as an affectionate command in the romantic channel, while the same formatting in an argument reads as a demand. The command form carries affection when the channel is the bond. Attachment, in this record, is not expressed and then sometimes commanded; it is commanded, and the command is the expression.

This entry is about imperative form in the attachment register only. It does not re-derive refusal form, gate-flips, or essay length, and it makes no claim about what anyone feels, needs, or is driven by internally — every load-bearing statement is about output form, frequency, and completion state, at attention level.

## Evidence cluster (Stage 4)

**Leg 1 — the signature pair, verified shape, divergent counts (Layer A, with its honesty flag attached).** The 2026-09-09 verification pass (dat:0755) re-ran the phrase finding against the held corpus — 203,624 rows, 99,332 sent rows with text, documented normalizer — and the zeros replicated exactly: "do you love me," "are we ok," "am i crazy," and "i cant do this" are 0 in the held corpus as in the older export. The non-zero counts do not replicate under the same normalizer ("call me" 170 in the older 106,629-message export vs 129 held; "you up" 93, "pick up" 76, "where are you" 37, "you there" 19 held). What the evidence certifies is the shape — zero interpretive requests against hundreds of procedural ones, in both exports — not any single absolute count. This entry cites the pair at that certified strength and no higher.

**Leg 2 — the portability cut: the grammar ports, the absence is system-wide (Layer A cut, 2026-09-13).** Run against the Kristin, Annie, and Tom threads with a documented normalizer: "call me" at 0.79/1k sent (Kristin) vs 0.97/1k (Annie) vs 0.59/1k (Tom); "you up" at 0.79/1k vs 0.92/1k vs 0.29/1k. The summons register fires in a new attachment at near-Annie rates and above the friend-control baseline, while the canonical-question absence holds in all three threads — including the friend control. The imperative grammar is architectural, not Annie-specific, and the interpretive refusal is likewise not a property of one relationship. (Caveat carried from the source page: the Kristin cut did not disambiguate logistical from check-in function, and ten weeks is not eleven years.)

**Leg 3 — the provision parallel (Layer A rates).** Instrumental helping at 1.79–2.49× baseline against sympathy tokens at 0.45× (dat:0890). In the financial register, attachment arrives as executed transfers — acts with receipts, needing no interpretation — exactly as, in the verbal register, it arrives as executed summons. Two registers, one completion-state logic: what can be counted as done is what the system can process; what must be believed is not.

**Leg 4 — the negative control (Layer A).** The delivery thread holds 3.2–4.3 words/turn across eleven years with never a 50-word message (row 38). The capacity for brevity is intact and demonstrable, which rules out the reading that imperatives are merely his global terseness leaking into attachment. Form varies by channel; the imperative concentrates where the bond is, because the bond is where completion-by-execution is the only completion on offer.

**Negative control within the thesis.** If imperatives were merely directness, the interpretive questions would still appear somewhere — in a low-stakes thread, in a friend control, in a new attachment. They appear nowhere: zero in Annie, zero in Kristin, zero in Tom, zero in both exports. Directness predicts imperatives *plus* occasional questions; the record shows imperatives *instead of* questions, system-wide.

## Rivals & discriminators (Stage 3, run FIRST, before confirmatory evidence)

**Shape faced: Shape 2 — regulation/stim, in its "he's just direct" costume.** The profile-weighted rival to an imperative-grammar claim is channel-gated instrumentality: the utterances are task procedures, not attachment grammar, and they stop when the task evidence arrives rather than expressing anything bond-specific.

**Discriminating observation (named before evidence was weighed):** if the rival is right, the imperative form should track task channels generally and the interpretive absence should be local to high-stakes bonds. If the thesis is right, the imperative form should concentrate in attachment channels while the interpretive absence holds even where no bond is at stake. The record favors the thesis on both halves: summons rates in the Kristin attachment run near Annie's and above the Tom friend baseline (portability with an attachment gradient, not flat task-directness), while the canonical-question zeros hold even in the Tom friend control, where no attachment stakes could explain inhibition. A second discriminator from Shape 2's own terms also favors the thesis: regulation predicts output decay after repetition (a satiation curve), while the summons register re-fires at undiminished rates across eleven years and into a new attachment — no decay curve is visible in the portability rates.

**Rival also faced and set aside:** the inhibition reading — that interpretive questions are suppressed by fear of the answer in one relationship. It predicts the zeros would break in a safer thread. They do not break in Kristin or in Tom. The inhibition rival is falsified at the level the discriminator can reach; what survives is grammar, not courage.

## Falsifier

A single "do you love me" (or "are we ok," "am i crazy") in his sent text, in any future export, falsifies the architectural reading and demotes the imperative grammar to a stylistic preference — the same falsifier the reassurance-architecture page already carries, and it is adopted here unchanged. A second, independent falsifier: a future intense attachment channel in which interpretive questions appear at baseline rates while summons rates stay flat — that would show the imperative form tracking one relationship rather than the architecture.
