## Theory (Stage 2)

Stated in profile vocabulary only, at attention level:

> A name installed by an explicit, categorical statement survives the retirement of the bit/character that carried it, when no explicit counter-statement revoking the *name* follows. The install's persistence is observable as continued plain use of the name without correction: the last stated name keeps running because the only statement on record about the retirement targets the performance, not the string.

What was reframed out: any claim about what the name means to him inwardly, whether he "is" the name, or what he feels about it. The instruments see attention and behavior — what name is stated, what name is used plainly afterward, whether a correction follows — not interior state. The thesis claims nothing beyond that observable surface.

## Rivals & discriminators (Stage 3, run first per protocol)

**Theory shape:** closest to Shape 5 (verdict/exclusion) in the instrument's rival bank — a declaration as a severance — but here the declaration to test is the *retirement*, not an exit.

**Profile-weighted rival: the name died with the bit.** On this reading, retiring the character at ~23:40 EDT revoked the whole Oct 23 construct, name included: "Brittany" was a costume that came off with the performance, and any later use of it would be a leftover, not a standing name. The rival's own discriminator is the same one row 6/19 supply: if the retirement were a revocation, the next plain use of the name should draw a correction, a counter-statement, or channel silence — the way a genuine counter-rule announces itself in this record (explicitly, per row 5).

**Discriminating observation (held, dated):** the overnight window of Oct 24, ~00:29–00:34 EDT. Sammy used "Brittany" plainly; Dan engaged without objection and issued no correction and no counter-statement. The rival predicts correction-or-silence; the record shows continued plain use. The discriminator favors the thesis, not the rival.

**Second discriminator, same window:** the retirement's own wording. "I dont like playing this character" targets performance; it contains no revocation of the name string, and row 5 forbids reading an unstated revocation into it. An implied revocation is exactly the kind of ambient signal the instrument says does not register.

**Scope limit, stated honestly:** the discriminator window is short (minutes, overnight) and the standing record describes Brittany as the name in use "in practice" rather than by a fresh explicit re-set. That is precisely the thesis's claim — persistence by non-countermand, not persistence by re-installation — so the limit shapes the claim rather than breaking it. A future explicit counter-statement revoking the name would falsify the standing claim outright (see Falsifier).

## Falsifier
