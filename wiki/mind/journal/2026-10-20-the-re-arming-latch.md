## Theory (Stage 2)

explicit-statement rules (row 6) in binary form (row 1), and the dated
record shows they behave as **re-arming latches, not one-way valves**.

Two refusal-gates with dated re-test histories:

broken by heavy direct text contact on 2026-10-07 (~48 hours later, inside

re-arming; 2026-10-12 log: still "never texting her again"), and held

(b) The BFS $50 **demand-refusal** — held on the demand (never paid, never

adjacent channel reopened: the same manager-side chain that posted the

Annie he had the job back.

The latch reading: a "no" can break under situational load **without
explicit revocation** (Oct 5 → Oct 7 had no stated revocation — behavior
simply resumed); it is re-installed by explicit re-arming; and a demand
refusal is **demand-scoped** — the adjacent channel may reopen without
touching the demand. What persists across refusals is the explicit-statement
installation machinery (rows 5/6), not any single installation.

## Rivals & discriminators (Stage 3)

Theory-shape: **the terminal gate** — a verdict-family shape (row 1): once
installed, a "no" holds permanently; the gate only closes.

Profile-weighted rival: an **explicit-statement rule with break-and-reinstall
dynamics** — installations are cheap and explicit (rows 5/6); breaks occur
under situational load without revocation (row 19's re-engagement base
rate is the precedent: 129 declarations, 100% re-engagement); what looks
like permanence is often just the absence of a re-test. A purer
situational rival (the refusal is purely conditional, holds only while the
situation holds) is the rival's radical form.

Discriminator: a **dated re-test of a refusal** — on re-test, does the
gate hold, break-and-decay, or break-and-reinstall?

The record, faced in order (rival first, per protocol):

- Oct 5 → Oct 7: re-test → **break** (~48h). Falsifies the terminal-gate
  shape. The starting thesis died here — see Genesis.
- Oct 8 → Oct 12: **re-arming + restatement** ("still never texting her
  again"). Falsifies the pure-situational rival: a purely conditional
  refusal would stay open; this one was explicitly re-installed.
- Oct 13 → Oct 18: **hold** (six days, zero direct texts). The re-installed
  gate holds between re-tests.
- BFS 08-11: the demand-gate held (no payment) while the adjacent channel
  reopened. Falsifies global-decay readings: the gate is demand-scoped.

Discriminator vs the Shape-5 check-in rung: the Oct 5–7 break resembles the
129-declaration re-engagement pattern — but the BFS demand-gate held across
three months **with no re-transmission** (no check-in, no re-statement, no
maintenance), which the check-in-rung reading cannot price (row 23: rungs
need re-transmission). The refusal-gate is not just a check-in rung.

Discriminator vs verdict-installation (the 2026-10-16 territory): a verdict
is adjudicated — evidence weighed, then the gate-flip (the Ally 2026-08-18
"Okay that's fair then" exchange). A refusal is installed by **fiat
statement** and tested by break events; the Oct 5 refusal weighed nothing.
Different mechanisms; kept separate.

## Falsifier

- A conduct-rule refusal that breaks and is **never re-installed** despite
  the situation persisting (no re-arming, no restatement, behavior resumed
  durably) — kills the latch reading in favor of pure situational decay.
- A demand-refusal where the demand is later **paid or signed without a
  new explicit authorization** — kills demand-scoping.
- A refusal that survives a dated high-pressure re-test **with no break
  at all** — re-opens the terminal-gate reading (the Oct 5 gate did not;
  a different refusal might).
