## Theory (Stage 2)

Stated in profile vocabulary, attention-level only: in the 70-track locked canon
("This Shit Is Personal," locked 2026-10-11), output clusters on a harmonic
signature — the I–vi oscillation — and that signature travels with the sound,
not the name. The canon's center of gravity is a two-chord seesaw that shows up
across thirty unrelated artists while nearly skipping his favorite artists
entirely. Canon membership, at the harmonic level, keys to a sonic signature or
to an artist slot — two parallel passes, not one gate. The claim is scoped to
the locked canon only: it says nothing about intake, nothing about the crown,
and nothing about why any individual track was kept.

## Evidence cluster (Stage 4, dated and sourced)

All counts below are from the Taste Constitution §3 and a re-derivation of its
underlying file, `workspace/music-analysis/personal_prog/chord_progressions.json`
(70 tracks, zero missing, chroma frames with triad template match, tonic-relative
Roman numerals). The constitution's numbers and the re-derivation agree.

**The monoculture.** I–vi–I–vi and vi–I–vi–I tie as the top fourgrams at 30 of
70 tracks each (42.9%), with 187 and 184 total occurrences. The next progression,
IV–I–vi–I, reaches only 7 tracks (10.0%) — the seesaw is 4× more common than
anything else. At bigram level, vi–I appears in 38 tracks (54.3%) and I–vi in 36
(51.4%): a majority of the canon touches the oscillation even where it is not
the dominant fourgram. Median distinct chords per track is 4; 22.9% of tracks
use 2 or fewer chords. The canon is harmonically thin by construction, and its
thinness has one shape.

**The signature is artist-independent.** Re-derivation (exact fourgram match for
I–vi–I–vi or vi–I–vi–I, union): 31 tracks carry the seesaw fourgram, spanning
30 distinct artists out of the canon's 67. Fly High (Toby Romeo), Stalking Me
(SWISH), Pull Me In (Medo), Catwalk (Emmit Fenn), Loop (Jerri), Places
(BSEARL;En:vy), 187 (M?STIC), Cosmic Club (AHEE) — the bloc is a cross-section
of the canon's long tail, not a house style. No single artist accounts for more
than one seesaw track except through shared billings.

**The signature nearly skips the orbit.** Exact artist-split matching: only 1
of the 31 seesaw tracks is an orbit track — "Don't Hurt My Heart - Sped Up"
(K3YN0T3;J3RY;T3CHN0T3;Distan). The canon holds 8 orbit tracks total, and the
other 7 sit outside the seesaw bloc: "Me & You," "Your Turn," "Losing The
Fight," "Chemical Reaction," plus the sped-up "Addicted To You" and "Overdose"
and "Red Light." The favorite artists' canon entries run on other progressions
(iv–bVI for the crown track; I–i tonic alternation for "Me & You"; IV–i
oscillation for "Your Turn"). The two passes are nearly disjoint: 30 of 31
seesaw tracks are non-orbit, and 7 of 8 orbit tracks are non-seesaw.

**The boundary case, stated rather than smoothed over.** The crown track
itself — "Addicted To You" (Sped Up), Distan;K3YN0T3 — does not seesaw. Neither
does the most-played track of the Oct 5–11 Spotify week ("Me & You," 25 plays).
The signature describes the canon's center, not its summit: the #1 and the
most-played sit in the orbit pass, and the thesis does not claim otherwise.

## Rivals & discriminators (Stage 3, run first)

Per protocol, the discriminator was named before the per-track re-derivation
was run, and the rival is faced here before the evidence above is treated as
confirming anything.

**Rival — Shape 4 (acquisition / artist loyalty): "he follows artists, and the
canon is the orbit's shadow."** The orbit is real — K3YN0T3 20×, Distan 12×,
T3CHN0T3 9×, J3RY 6× across the 166 resolved liked tracks, ~28% of the liked
list — so the rival has a live base. (The dossier's acquisition-drive 95 is
unpriced per rows 30/50 and lends it no weight; the rival stands on the orbit
counts alone.)

**Discriminating observation (named first):** orbit share of the seesaw bloc.
If artist loyalty drove the canon's harmonic shape, the seesaw bloc would
concentrate on orbit artists. If the signature travels with the sound, the bloc
would spread across unrelated artists and show no orbit concentration.

**Verdict on the discriminator:** favors the thesis decisively. Orbit share of
the seesaw bloc is 1/31 (3.2%), against an orbit share of 8/70 (11.4%) in the
canon overall — and the bloc spans 30 distinct artists. The rival fails its
own discriminator for the seesaw bloc. What the rival keeps, honestly, is the
orbit bloc itself: 7 orbit tracks in the canon that do not seesaw are the
artist pass operating on its own track, parallel to the signature pass, not
absorbed by it. The thesis claims two passes, not one — the discriminator's
split, not a patch.

**Second rival — lyric-meaning selection: "the canon is picked for what the
words say."** Row 18 (weight 9, supported) prices against it before any count:
sung words process as timbre, and the record's one lyric-driven crown
("Addicted To You," whose lyrics mirror the Annie loop) is flagged in the held
record as rare precisely because he normally never cares about lyrics. The
discriminator is the harmonic monoculture itself: a lyric-picked canon would
not show a 42.9% harmonic monoculture across 30 unrelated artists. It shows
exactly that. The rival fails.

## Falsifier

- A re-run of the harmonic pass on the locked canon, with a different tonic
  assignment or template, collapsing the seesaw share to the level of the
  next progression (≤10%) — kills the monoculture half.
- A future canon lock in which the seesaw bloc concentrates on a few artists
  (orbit share rising toward the canon's orbit share or above) — kills the
  travels-with-the-sound half.
- The orbit bloc absorbing the seesaw bloc (orbit tracks becoming the
  seesaw's carriers) — the two-passes split fails and the artist-loyalty
  rival takes the canon.
