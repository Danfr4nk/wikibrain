# Politics — Hub

This cluster opened 2026-08-10 as a door into material that had existed
for weeks with no single point of entry. What it holds, taken together,
is the political life of one mind — and it is a strange and instructive
archive: a man who converted to the left in lockdown in 2020, publicly
audited the very media pipeline that converted him within two years,
wrote the best contrarian call of the 2024 Republican primary eighteen
months early on a mechanism the entire pundit class missed, logged 33
conspiracy verdicts with a discipline most truthers would find
unfamiliar, and ended up with an explicit political axiom whose best
evidence predates the formulation by years. Nothing here is a list of
positions. It is a record of how a forensic instinct learned to run at
civic scale.

The entries carry independent epistemic status — primary-verified
records, testimony-grade dossiers, and interpretations with open source
loops — and the hub states which is which, entry by entry. What unifies
them is the movement the dated record shows: **he converts through the
pipeline and then starts auditing it.** The 2020 conversion, the 2022–23
Hasan disagreements, the 2023 Brooks test, the 2026 verdicts log — read
in sequence they trace one arc, and the arc bends toward increasing
analytical independence from whatever instrument last held his
attention.

The strongest-dated material sits at both ends: the 2020-08-22
conversion is self-narrated in held iMessages, and the 2026-09-22
verdicts were given live in a dedicated session with the corpus
cross-checked the same day. Between them runs the public record —
timestamped tweets, a ten-post Substack corpus scored post by post — and
beneath them the architecture: a vertical-skepticism that makes
concentration of unaccountable power suspect *as such*, wherever it
sits, which is the commitment all of this turns out to be.

## The conversion: a dated origin event

On **2020-08-22** (local; 2020-08-23 02:43–02:45 UTC), Dan narrates the
turn himself in held iMessages: *"i took a HARD turn left recently
thanks to hasan piker, chapo trap house, thoughtslime, etc."* and
*"since i've been stuck inside i've read the communist manifesto, das
kapital, conquest of bread lol"* (dat:0347). The lockdown, the media
pipeline, and the primary texts are all named in the same thread — the
conversion event is the strongest-dated material in this cluster. The
pipeline list carries its own correction history: the 2020-left-turn
page's pipeline credit was corrected (2026-09-14, work-0033) because
ContraPoints appears in no Dan-authored primary record alongside Chapo
and is not in the self-narration's credit line — the standing credit
line is Hasan Piker, Chapo Trap House, ThoughtSlime.

The pre-2016 political identity remains thin — the single stated
exception being a 2021 retrospective via [[wiki/people/joe-oshnack]]
calling him *"an annoying little conservative fuckcunt"*; the corpus
itself is sparse before the Bernie era. One 2021-dated datum anchors
the label in his own voice: the public declaration "democratic
socialist" on 2020-10-03. What matters structurally is not the label
but the asymmetry: the conversion is dated, self-narrated, and
corroborated; everything before it is a stated gap.

Read in sequence — 2020 conversion, 2022 Russia tweet, 2023 Gaza tweets,
2023 Brooks test — these four records trace the through-line the
evidence supports: **the pipeline was an on-ramp, not an allegiance.**
The 2022 Russia tweet and the 2023 Gaza tweets are public, timestamped
disagreements with the very media figure who converted him, on exactly
the foreign-policy axis where his forensic suspicion-of-motive instinct
collides with team loyalty. That is also what the "shades of bad"
formulation forces: where tribal politics demands picking a side, he
does power-analysis instead — which is the axiom in practice before the
axiom had a name.

Whether Brooks shaped the 2020 conversion or was adopted as its

open: the Brooks test is timestamped 2023-10-10, three years after the
conversion, and the record does not establish whether Brooks was a 2020
influence or a later-chosen name for a position already held.

## The prediction record: the Substack as instrument

For ten posts between May 2023 and November 2024, Dan ran a Substack —
*Committee to Re-elect the President* (creepy.substack.com) — whose
tagline, "Live from Political Hellworld," announced the whole project:
American politics as an irredeemable spectacle, narrated in real time.
The hell-world frame (major concept page, 2026-10-01) is the house
epistemology: the worst reading licensed as realism, the doom
prospective enough to be comedy — until November 2024, when the worst
reading came true and the register curdled into dread. Three politics/
pages live on this corpus, and together they are the most audited
prediction record in the wiki.

**The flagship: "The DeSantis Delusion" (2023-05-23).** Published
twenty-four hours before Ron DeSantis would announce his presidential
campaign in a glitching Twitter Spaces call with Elon Musk, the post
argued that the 2024 GOP primary was already decided — not a horserace
but a fait accompli with a campaign season attached. The method was the
steelman-detonate: three paragraphs building DeSantis's *best* case
(more palatable, more competent, fewer scandals, better with suburban
women — "In a political vacuum, DeSantis should be considered AT LEAST
a competitive threat"), then one sentence to destroy it: *"But, here's
the thing: none of this matters when you factor in Donald Trump as a
variable in this equation."* The mechanism had two parts — a loyal
floor (20–30% of Republicans strictly loyal to Trump, so even a
half-demobilized loyal bloc removes any challenger's general-election
path) and the sabotage threat (Trump, documented by the Iowa-2016 Cruz
fraud claims and the 2020 aftermath, will burn the party rather than
lose gracefully), which seals the **donor trap**: McConnell, the
mega-donors, the RNC all know defection loses, so nobody defects, which
confirms the fait accompli. The companion piece three days later, *How
To Ruin Your Political Career* (2023-05-26, after the #DeSaster
launch), argued the run was career suicide — the 2028 frontrunnership
forfeited for "a toy for Trump to bat around for a few months" — and
the 2016 casualty list (Rubio, Cruz, Jeb Bush) was deployed as
precedent: challenging Trump doesn't just lose; it spoils. The audit's
scoring: the nomination call is a strong hit — "specific, contrarian,
mechanistic, correct" — against the full consensus from Fox to MSNBC,
called before the campaign existed. The thesis page generalizes it into
the **floor-plus-sabotage model**, the portable theory of Trump-era
primary dynamics (a standing hypothesis with one strong confirmation,
not a law — its boundary conditions are untested in his corpus).

**The victory lap, audited: "Trump Was Always Going To Win"
(2024-11-12).** The tenth and final post claimed "over two years ago, I
concluded that a Trump victory in 2024 had become the most likely
outcome" — and the audit, post by post and quote by quote against his
own archive, splits the claim. The nomination call survives in full:
total-confidence, mechanistic, correct. The general-election call does
not survive in the form it is remembered in. *POLL WATCH #1*
(2023-05-26) opens with "it's still too early to look at polls with any
reasonable amount of confidence"; *The Keys of Political Predictions*
(2023-06-02) relays Lichtman's framework whose then-current application
favored Biden, straight, no spin. What the archive shows is a
directionally-right lean tracked in real time — Trump +1.4, then +1.8,
with the read that the indictments weren't denting him — hardening as
events confirmed it ("with every development, my confidence grew").
The certainty and the timestamp ("over two years ago" for an
eighteen-month span) were acquired later. The page's thesis is the
reliability pattern made flesh: **analysis reliable, autobiography of
the analysis drifting** — good-faith misremembering that flatters the
teller, exactly as his own veracity ledger documents ("trust the corpus
over me on years"). The post's genuine hits are also scored: the
lawfare read — of four criminal cases, the weakest (the Stormy Daniels
trial) reaching court first, converting legal jeopardy into loyalty
fuel — earned the slow way, as a question posed in June 2023 and
answered by events. And the post is explicit about what surprised him:
the subtitle names it — the down-ballot destruction, a consolidated
party and a popular-vote mandate for a man he holds, "by any reasonable
standard, unqualified." The watchtower never filed another dispatch;
the thirteen-month silence before November 2024 became permanent.

**The explainer stub: "Lichtman's Thirteen Keys" (2023-06-02).**
Incidental tier, complete at its size: Dan's explainer of Alan
Lichtman's fundamentals-based forecasting system — thirteen true/false
statements about the incumbent *party*, six or more false and the
incumbent party loses, no polls, no vibes. He framed Lichtman as the
anti-grifter (10/10 from 1984 to 2016, including the Gore 2000 call
"which, of course, he did" win), tensed against personality-based
punditry "particularly in the age of Trump," and relayed a June-2023
Biden-favoring read without suppressing it. He never adopted the system
as his own — promotion condition on the page is that he ever builds his
own fundamentals model. Public postscript: Lichtman predicted a Harris
win in 2024; Trump won — the system's first public miss.

## The forensic spectator: 33 verdicts

On 2026-09-22, in the 𝖢𝖮𝖭𝖲𝖯𝖨𝖱𝖠𝖢𝖸 side chat, Dan gave live verdicts
on 32 conspiracy theories at his own request, with entry #33 (January
6th) added from the wiki corpus where it was already his most
documented position. Each carries his confidence label verbatim —
**believe**, **reject**, **no position**, and the **gut-vs-official**
splits where he separates the two — plus a corpus status from the
same-day cross-check: settled, evolution, engaged without verdict, or
new-on-record. The tally: 18 believes, 5 rejects, 9 agnostic. Roughly
20 verdicts were first articulations from a single session — provisional
positions, not settled doctrine — and the page states that plainly.

The signature patterns the session produced, recorded on the page:

1. **Standing suspect class: intelligence agencies.** CIA recurs (JFK,
   crack funding, the open 9/11 question), FBI recurs (MLK hit, the
   Tsarnaev asset theory), Mossad recurs (JFK, 9/11). When he smells a
   conspiracy, he names a service, not a cabal.
2. **Rejects the mystical, accepts the structural.** Chemtrails, 5G,
   vax-autism, lizard people are bullshit; Bilderberg and the
   Illuminati are real but demystified — "insular power structures, not
   even hidden." The mechanism has to be boring to be believed.
3. **Tradecraft vocabulary, not tabloid vocabulary.** Limited hangout,
   controlled opposition, exfil, assets gone rogue — including
   operations *about* conspiracy theories themselves (QAnon as
   controlled opposition built to discredit the real phenomenon by
   farce association).
4. **Scrupulous agnosticism where info is thin.** Nine blanks, four
   gut/official splits, and zero cases where he was previously vocal
   about something he is agnostic on today — the agnosticism is the
   native state, not a retreat.
5. **The discriminator is institutional interest.** Lone-shooter stories
   are rejected for the three 1960s system-level victims (JFK, RFK,
   MLK) — where the removal serves an institutional interest — while
   the Sandy Hook official story is accepted and most celebrity deaths
   stay blank.

Two entries deserve their own weight. The **Boston bombing** is the set's
only genuine evolution: 2013 tweets mocking Boston trutherism → a 2026
FBI-asset theory, a 13-year arc whose inflection is queued for the
dissection phase. The **Epstein** entry carries the session's most
important datum — a same-day retraction: the exfil-through-ventilation
claim, sourced to a podcast's cell recreation, he cut himself the day
he made it ("I don't really have the information to confidently claim
that"), leaving only the confident claim that Epstein did not hang
himself. That is the mundanity principle editing a verdict in real
time.

The strongest single finding is visible only when all 33 sit side by
side: the **hybrid template** — real event plus an operation layered on
top, the blur between the layers doing the work — recurs independently
in four verdicts: 9/11 (real hijackers, rigged buildings, Mossad driver,
CIA involvement the open question), J6 (real crowd, inserted lethal
core), Boston (real bombing, FBI-asset nexus), the 2020 election (real
votes, engineered rule changes). Four separate cases, one shape, no
cross-contamination between the verdicts — they were given in the same
session but the shape predates it (J6 alone is ~18 months settled). That
recurrence makes the template a cognitive primitive rather than a
per-case conclusion: **the signature of his judgment is not *what* he
believes but *the shape the beliefs take*.** The synthesis of what the
verdicts reveal about how he thinks is at
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/conspiracy-worldview]].

## A new reading the totality forces

The politics is the **personality profile run at civic scale**, and the
profile's hardest numbers predict it. Trust at the 9th percentile —
corpus-confirmed at 1.96× suspicion-of-motive language
([[wiki/mind/profile/big-five-psychometrics]]) — is the trait-level
reason the first move is always "who inserted what": the J6 hybrid
verdict is suspicion-of-motive applied to history rather than to people,
and the "shades of bad" Gaza formulation is the same facet refusing
team-assignment. The politics is *forensic spectatorship* — power
concentrations treated as failure modes to be audited, never as teams
to join — which is also his stated threat model ("competence correctly
deployed, outcome still catastrophic") in the civic register. This
makes a prediction the record can check: when a future power
concentration arrives wearing his own side's colors, the audit should
fire anyway. The 2022–23 Hasan disagreements are the first instance.

**Epistemic status:** conclusion from dated primary records plus the
corpus-confirmed Trust facet; the prediction is falsifiable.

## Assessment

The politics cluster is the mind domain's newest and, pound for pound,
its most checkable. It holds the wiki's two strongest-dated political
records — the 2020-08-22 conversion and the 2026-09-22 verdicts — and
the most honestly scored prediction work anywhere in the corpus: the
DeSantis call survives its audit with no revision, and the one place
the record catches him (the Trump-redux retrofit) is caught by his own
archive against his own standing rule. That pattern is the cluster's
own verdict on itself: the analysis is reliable, the autobiography of
the analysis drifts, and the drift is itself documented — which is
precisely what makes the analysis trustworthy.

The substantive through-line is stable across every page: a forensic
instinct that treats concentrations of unaccountable power as failure
modes to be audited rather than teams to join. It is there in the 2020
conversion (the pipeline as on-ramp, audited within two years), in the
"shades of bad" refusal of team-assignment, in the hybrid verdicts
logged live in 2026, and in the power axiom stated as an explicit
premise. The falsifiable prediction the totality licenses — that the
audit fires on his own side's concentrations too — has its first
instance in the 2022–23 Hasan disagreements. The rest is future work
for the record.
