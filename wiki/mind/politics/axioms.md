*Part of the [[wiki/mind/politics/index|politics cluster]] — the hub carries the dated primary record this page reasons from.*

Dan Frank's politics is not a list of positions. It is one commitment stated once and then run at every scale — against parties, states, employers, family, and, eventually, the media pipeline that converted him in the first place. That commitment has a sentence: **what is suspect is concentration of unaccountable power as such, wherever it sits** — left or right, state or market, father or manager. It dissolves what used to look like the central paradox of his public profile, the democratic socialist with a Roman-authoritarian fascination: the fascination was never with authority, it was with power analyzed.

Underneath that political commitment sits an older, stranger layer of axioms — four load-bearing sentences about worth, vigilance, love, and time that he first wrote down in his own AI configuration file in May 2026 and that his models then systematized into the canonical four-form set. Those four are his framing, a model's systematization, and his endorsement — not four behaviorally measured laws — and the record grades them that way. This page gives each layer its actual weight: the four axioms with their dated paper trail, the power axiom with its four convergent legs, and the political record in time that both layers postdate.

## The axioms in plain form

**Layer one — the four core axioms**, in the canonical in-wiki form they reached on 2026-06-22:

1. **not exceptional = worthless** — there is no acceptable middle state; ordinary performance reads as failure.
2. **not vigilant = annihilated** — letting guard down is not risky, it is fatal; false dismissal of a real signal is the survival error.
3. **love that does not cost everything is not real** — the criterion of real love is total expenditure; affection that costs nothing is counterfeit.
4. **time = countdown** — time is not a medium to move through but a fuse burning toward irreversibility.

**Layer two — the power axiom:**

**What is suspect is concentration of unaccountable power as such, wherever it sits.** A party, a family, a workplace, a state, a media pipeline: the political valence of the concentration is not the question. The concentration is the question.

The two layers are different kinds of things, and the page treats them as such. The four axioms are a 2026 formulation about operating principles — psychological material. The power axiom is a political premise with legs in his authored persona, his dated reading, his timestamped public statements, and his cognitive architecture. What follows is the evidence for each, axiom by axiom, leg by leg.

## How the two layers cohere — and where they strain

**The inversion stands.** The wiki's 2026-09-13 rebuild inverted the old weighting: the power axiom is the better-evidenced of the two layers — four convergent legs (authored persona relayed, dated reading, verified public statements, stable earned architecture) against one dossier-mediated formulation with a failed corpus audit. Whether the laws govern behavior is a separate measurement — run once, negatively, on the four axioms, and never run on the power axiom at all. The record's prediction: when a future power concentration arrives wearing his own side's colors, the audit should fire anyway. The 2022–23 Hasan disagreements are the first instance.

**The strains are real and they stay in the record.** Axiom 4 drew the audit's clearest negative (the one observable that reaches its content goes the wrong way — less urgency language than controls, not merely absent). Axiom 3 is contradicted by the wiki's own best-measured interpersonal finding (the Fran bond runs its prediction backwards). The four axioms were born in six weeks in 2026 — they cannot be the foundation of the twenty-year architecture; at most they are the 2026 *name* for patterns the record shows running for years. And the CATO bootloader, the power axiom's strongest first-person leg, remains unheld — the specific recovery target that would close the hub's open source loop.
