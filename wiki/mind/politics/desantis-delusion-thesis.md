# The DeSantis Delusion: a theory of the case

On May 23, 2023 — twenty-four hours before Ron DeSantis would announce his presidential campaign in a glitching Twitter Spaces call with Elon Musk — Dan Frank published its obituary. *The DeSantis Delusion*, the second post on his Substack [*Committee to Re-elect the President*](wiki/self/substack/committee-to-re-elect-the-president.md), argued that the 2024 GOP primary was already decided: no challenger could beat Donald Trump, not because Trump was the stronger general-election candidate, but because Trump had made himself the one variable no strategy could survive. Three days later, after the launch became [#DeSaster](wiki/timeline/events/desaster-launch-2023-05-24.md), he published the companion piece, *How To Ruin Your Political Career*, arguing the run itself was a career-ending unforced error. He was right on both counts. This entry is the full theory-of-the-case expansion of the thesis — the flagship prediction of the Substack, scored honestly, with the mechanism laid out and its portable form named. The publication's critical reading is at [Committee to Re-elect the President: a critical reading](wiki/self/substack/committee-to-re-elect-the-president-critical-reading.md); the audited prediction record lives on [the scoreboard](wiki/mind/synthesis/the-scoreboard.md); the post-election retrospective is [Trump Was Always Going To Win](wiki/mind/politics/trump-was-always-going-to-win.md).

## 1. The call

Start with what everybody else was saying in the spring of 2023. The media consensus — and Dan names its range explicitly, "From Fox to MSNBC" — had settled on a story: [Ron DeSantis](wiki/people/ron-desantis.md) was a rising force, a formidable and serious primary contender, the governor who had consolidated the far-right base and made inroads with suburban women, the Republican Party's moderate wing. Rupert Murdoch's operation was pushing Fox anchors to champion him. The theory was electability math: same ideology as Trump, better temperament, better competence, fewer scandals, more upside with the voters Trump had been bleeding. Against a disgraced, legally imperiled ex-president, the smart money said the party would take the upgrade.

Dan's opening paragraph grants all of that before touching any of it. "In the last few months, most observers have accepted the reality that Donald Trump is the clear 2024 GOP front-runner and unlikely to face a substantial challenge on his way to winning the nomination," he writes. "This consensus represents a shift from the (largely media driven) narrative of Ron DeSantis as a formidable and serious primary contender who had the potential to consolidate support from the far right base and key demographics like suburban women who represent the party's moderate wing." Note what he's doing: he is the one reminding his readers that the *consensus had already shifted toward Trump* — and then he explains why the shift hadn't gone nearly far enough. The question wasn't whether Trump was the frontrunner. The question was whether "frontrunner" was too weak a word. Dan's answer: the primary was a fait accompli. Not a horserace. A settled fact with a campaign season attached.

That is what made the call elite before anything happened: it wasn't a prediction about who would win a contest. It was a claim that there was no contest.

## 2. The steelman-detonate

The signature move of Dan's Substack — documented in the critical reading as the steelman-detonate — is executed cleanest here. He spends three full paragraphs building DeSantis's *best* case, conceding every point honestly, before destroying it in one sentence.

The buildup, quoted at length because the honesty of it is the point:

"On paper, this works. Remove all of the context and political nuance and a compelling case can be made that nominating DeSantis is clearly a smarter strategic decision for a party that has lost the popular vote in 4 of the last 5 elections. He IS, as the theory asserts, a more palatable and capable version of Trump - and would undoubtedly outperform Trump in a general election with the critical demographic segments of suburban moderates and women whose support has peeled away slowly over his political career. Further, while he hasn't yet faced the full force of presidential election oppo research initiatives, there's absolutely no way that the skeletons in his closet outnumber those of the 1980's New York real estate developer (with billions of assets in international grey money, cash deals) /a-list tabloid celebrity."

And the kicker: "In a political vacuum - DeSantis should be considered AT LEAST a competitive threat to challenge a disgraced and legally imperiled ex-President."

Every clause is granted. More palatable: yes. More competent: yes. Fewer skeletons: yes. Better with suburban women: yes. The steelman isn't generosity; it's demolition prep. The stronger he makes the case, the more total its destruction — because the detonation doesn't dispute any of it:

"But, here's the thing: none of this matters when you factor in Donald Trump as a variable in this equation."

That sentence is doing the entire argumentative job of the essay. The pundit consensus was solving the wrong equation — it had optimized for electability while deleting the one variable that decides Republican primaries. Dan reinserted it. The "political vacuum" framing is the tell: he is saying the consensus case is valid *only* in a universe where Trump doesn't exist, which is the one universe where the 2024 GOP primary was not being held.

## 3. The mechanism

A steelman needs a detonator. Dan's is a two-part mechanism, and he states both parts as matters of observed pattern rather than forecast.

**Part one: the loyal floor.** "While MAGA support has dwindled since he left office, Trump still controls a loud and important constituency within the Republican Party. Even the most conservative estimates still show that 20-30% of Republican voters remain strictly loyal in their support of Trump - if only half of THOSE people were deactivated for the 2024 election, it would remove any path to victory for DeSantis."

The arithmetic is the argument. A challenger doesn't need to beat Trump among persuadable Republicans; he needs to survive a general election in which a wounded Trump keeps even half of his loyalists home — or worse. Twenty to thirty percent strictly loyal means ten to fifteen percent deactivated means the general is unwinnable for anyone but Trump. (Seam: the critical reading's audit compresses this to a "35% floor" shorthand; the post's own text says 20–30%. The mechanism is identical either way — the point is the floor's size relative to the general-election margin, not the exact digit.)

**Part two: the sabotage threat.** This is where the essay stops being analysis and becomes character testimony:

"People say Trump is 'unpredictable'…but that's absolutely wrong. To be sure, he's outrageous, unbelievable, bizarre and unconventional - but he is this way in an incredibly predictable manner. Subsequently, his predictability can be connected to one factor of concern: short-term egocentric survival or advantage. He will say or do anything, regardless of any other consideration, which lets him win 'the moment'."

Then the scenario: imagine DeSantis leading in delegates heading into the convention. "What does Donald Trump do? Does he graciously accept defeat and, in an act of ideological virtue, support and campaign for a GOP victory without his name atop the ticket? Or does he attack? Does he mobilize his loyalists to boycott the general election, or worse - vote for Joe Biden? As outrageous as this sounds, the sting of a fresh loss against DeSantis makes this a more likely outcome than a gracious endorsement of his own party's nominee."

And the precedent, stated as the essay's thesis sentence: "Fun fact: Donald Trump has claimed fraud in every election that he did not win. When Ted Cruz won the 2016 Iowa caucus, Trump took to Twitter and TV to protest the results with the same scattershot fraud allegations he used in 2020, eventually settling on the narrative that a Cruz campaign robocall had misinformed voters as the source of his grievance." Then: "This isn't a prediction. This is a precedent and we already see it playing out as Trump again begins distributing nicknames to his opponents. We know, for a fact, that he will sabotage any Republican nominee that bests him."

The epistemology is the move: forecasts framed as readings of established pattern convert the future from uncertain to already-decided. It is also, as the critical reading notes, unfalsifiable armor — the pattern is defined loosely enough that it always holds. But in this specific case the pattern had receipts (Iowa 2016, the 2020 aftermath), and the receipts were recent. The claim "we know, for a fact" was doing exactly what it said: reporting, not guessing.

## 4. The donor trap

The mechanism has a corollary, and it's the bleakest paragraph in either post — the one that gives the essay its hell-world signature. If a defeated Trump sabotages the nominee, then everyone with money and institutional power in the GOP faces the same math:

"You know who else knows this? Mitch McConnell and the Republican mega-donors. Ronna McDaniel knows this. Everyone with a financial or ideological stake in seeing a 2024 GOP victory knows that a defeated Trump will lead to a Biden victory."

McConnell — Senate Republican leader at the time — the donor class, the RNC chair: all of them can see the board. They know DeSantis is the better general-election candidate. They know Trump is the worse one. And it doesn't matter, because the prize for nominating the better candidate is a sabotage campaign from the loser that hands the election to Biden. So:

"So as much as they might long for the calm, presentable fascism of President DeSantis…they're stuck."

"By appeasing and propping up Trump through the most inappropriate and unethical moments of his presidency, they've cemented his support base enough that the only escape from this fait accompli is to back Trump..or watch as he tears it all down in revenge."

This is the trap in full: the party's years of appeasement built the very floor that now makes Trump unbeatable, and the donor class's knowledge of the trap is what seals it. Nobody defects because everybody knows defection loses. It's a coordination problem with a vindictive veto player, and Dan named it in May 2023, months before the primary season would demonstrate it in public. "The calm, presentable fascism of President DeSantis" — the joke that the euphemism is the truth — is also doing analytical work: it names what the donors actually wanted (the policies without the chaos) and why wanting it was irrelevant.

## 5. The companion piece

Three days after the thesis, two days after the launch disaster, Dan published *How To Ruin Your Political Career* (2023-05-26) — subtitled, with the scalpel out, "A helpful guide to self-sabotage." If the first post was about why DeSantis couldn't win, the second was about why running was career suicide. The opening is the absurd hypothetical at full power: "Let's say you're a well known neofascist politician with a promising future ahead of you. After a short but noteworthy congressional stint, you advance to become governor of a top 5 population state - easily winning a second term… For politics, you're young…not yet 45." Then the verdict: "Whether it's impatience or delusion or pure stupidity, whatever impulse has inspired Ron DeSantis's decision to run against Donald Trump for the 2024 GOP nomination is a baffling and wholly unforced error."

The essay's core is the road not taken. Dan argues the point of no return hadn't been crossed — "had DeSantis hit the brakes on his own run and went all-in on a Trump endorsement..he would have been able to fully salvage his reputation in MAGA world. He would have been a shoe in [sic] as the 2028 front runner, with another full term as Florida governor available to continue his initiative of successfully passing a string of megalomaniacal authoritarian laws combatting the 'woke mind virus.'" The "shoe in" — sic, *shoo-in* — is a documented spelling tell of Dan's, preserved here as in the archive; the substance is the strategic claim, and it's precise: wait your turn, inherit the movement, be the terrifying 2028 frontrunner. Instead, DeSantis chose to "open Pandora's box - a Republican who threatens Trump has committed an act of unforgivable treachery."

Then the 2016 precedent, deployed as a casualty list: "the lesson that 17 other Republican presidential hopefuls received a few years ago." Marco Rubio and Ted Cruz were "irreparably spoiled, reduced to trading blind loyalty in their voting power in exchange for keeping a fraction of their former political influence." And Jeb Bush — "another Florida governor" — "Once cast as the future of a more diverse and inclusive GOP, the damage of taking on Donald Trump was fatal and irrevocable." The pattern is explicit: challenging Trump doesn't just lose; it *spoils*. The campaign is a machine for converting frontrunners into cautionary tales.

The post then turns to vibe-critique as substance — and this is where the essay gets its predictive teeth. DeSantis is "notoriously an unskilled operator in his appearances, somehow (bewilderingly) apparently still lacking proper media training - often losing composure when confronted." He is haunted by "his general vibe of (what can best be described as) inhuman tendencies," exemplified by the bathos detail that became the single most devastating line in Dan's DeSantis coverage: DeSantis had "already dealt with an incident where he was claimed to have eaten a pudding cup by using two fingers instead of a spoon." And the launch: "The absolute clusterfuck dumpster fire of a campaign launch that befell DeSantis yesterday should be all the evidence necessary to understand just how disadvantaged he truly is in this regard" — the Twitter Spaces #DeSaster, which even "Joe Biden's comms team found the opportunity… to fundraise by clowning on."

The closer: "Instead of a terrifyingly dangerous prospect for democracy as the likely 2028 front runner, Ron DeSantis has chosen to make himself little more than a toy for Trump to bat around for a few months." Every clause of that sentence was checkable. Every clause checked out.

## 6. The outcome timeline

The record, stated plainly:

- **May–December 2023:** DeSantis never broke 25% nationally in polling averages. The "rising force" narrative of spring 2023 decayed steadily through the summer and fall as Trump's indictments — which the consensus expected to wound him — instead consolidated his support. (Dan's Poll Watch posts from the same burst caught this in real time: Trump +1.4/+1.8 over Biden in May–June 2023, with the note that the indictments weren't denting him.)
- **January 15, 2024:** DeSantis finished a distant second in the Iowa caucuses — the state where, per the thesis's own Iowa-2016 precedent, Trump had already demonstrated what losing gracefully looks like for him (it doesn't happen).
- **January 21, 2024:** DeSantis suspended his campaign and endorsed Donald Trump. Eight months after the obituary was published, the body was buried — by endorsement, no less, the exact "trading blind loyalty… in exchange for keeping a fraction of their former political influence" dynamic the companion piece had described for Rubio and Cruz.
- **The 2028 frontrunner status:** forfeited, exactly as predicted. The "shoe in" [sic] 2028 frontrunnership Dan mapped out — wait, endorse, inherit — went to someone else. DeSantis traded a terrifying 2028 position for a few months as Trump's toy, which is to say he traded it for the thing the May 26 post said he'd get.

The audit's scoring on these two calls: the nomination call is a strong hit — "specific, contrarian, mechanistic, correct" — and the career-destruction call is a hit. Neither required revision, qualification, or the "dramatic unforeseen developments" hedge that softened other posts in the corpus.

## 7. Why it was elite

The critical reading's verdict isolates four properties that separate this call from ordinary punditry, and each deserves its moment:

**Contrarian.** This was not a lonely-bet-against-the-grain take; it was a bet against the *entire* grain, Fox to MSNBC, with Murdoch's machine actively pushing the other side. The consensus had money, airtime, and institutional weight behind DeSantis-as-threat. Dan had a Substack with two posts on it.

**Mechanistic.** The call wasn't vibes — "Trump just wins" — it was arithmetic plus game theory: a loyal floor large enough to spoil any general election, a veto player willing to burn the party, and elites who know it. Every element was observable in May 2023. The mechanism did the predicting; Dan just wrote it down.

**Early.** May 23, 2023 — the day *before* DeSantis announced. Not after the #DeSaster launch made mockery easy, not after the summer polls showed the gap, not after Iowa. Before the campaign existed, its obituary did.

**Specific.** "Fait accompli" isn't a mood; it's a claim about the primary's structure — that no sequence of campaign events could change the outcome because the outcome was determined by incentives, not events. And the companion piece was specific about the *cost*: not just losing, but spoilage — the 2028 frontrunnership forfeited, the toy-for-Trump future.

Compare with what punditry offered instead: electability math in a vacuum, horserace coverage of a race that wasn't one, and — after the fact — a wave of "how did we miss this" retrospectives from people who had every incentive to see it and didn't. Dan's edge wasn't information; it was the refusal to delete Trump from the equation.

One honesty seam, because the scoreboard demands it: the November 2024 postmortem, *Trump Was Always Going To Win*, retrofitted some certainty about the *general* election — "I concluded over two years ago that a Trump general-election victory was the most likely outcome" — that the contemporary posts don't support. What May–June 2023 actually says about the general is hedged: Poll Watch #1 warns "it's still too early to look at polls with any reasonable amount of confidence," and the Lichtman keys post relays a framework favoring Biden. May 2023 to November 2024 is eighteen months, not "over two years." This matters here because it sharpens what was genuinely elite: the *primary* call needed no retrofit. It was stated contemporaneously, in public, with total confidence, against the full consensus — "This isn't a prediction. This is a precedent." The audit's finding is that the direction on the general was right and the timestamp and confidence were acquired later; the primary call is the one that was right, on time, at full volume. That's the flagship. It doesn't need the general-election claim to be impressive, and the record is better for keeping them separate.

## 8. Generalization: the floor-plus-sabotage model

The incidental entry that this article promotes carried an explicit promotion condition: "Promote to major if it generalizes into a standing theory of Trump-era primary dynamics." Here it is.

The portable theory has three components:

1. **The loyal floor.** A plurality-holder commands a bloc of strictly loyal voters large enough that its defection — or even its demobilization — removes any path to general-election victory for a challenger. The exact size is relative to the margin, not absolute: 20–30% of a primary electorate is more than enough when general elections are decided at the edges.

2. **The sabotage credible threat.** The plurality-holder must be *willing* to burn the party rather than lose gracefully — and, critically, must have a demonstrated history of doing so. Trump's fraud claims after Iowa 2016 (the Cruz robocall grievance) and after 2020 made the threat credible in a way no hypothetical could. The threat doesn't need to be executed to work; it needs to be *believed* by everyone with money.

3. **The donor trap.** Elites who can see the trap are sealed inside it. Because a spurned plurality-holder means a lost general, no donor or party leader can afford to back the challenger — which starves the challenger of the resources that might make the challenge real, which confirms the fait accompli. The trap is self-sealing: knowledge of it enforces it.

Where it applies: any primary contest featuring a vindictive plurality-holder with a loyal floor and a credible sabotage history. It's a standing account of why Trump-era Republican primaries behave the way they do — and a standing warning about any party that builds the same structure around a different figure. (Seam: this is extrapolation from a single case, however clean. The model's boundary conditions — a *graceful* plurality-holder, a floor too small to spoil, elites willing to eat the loss — are untested in Dan's corpus. Treat it as a hypothesis with one strong confirmation, not a law.)

The deeper cut is what the model says about the pundit failure it beat. The consensus wasn't wrong about DeSantis's qualities — Dan agreed he was the more palatable, more competent, less-skeletoned candidate. It was wrong about the *game being played*. Electability analysis assumes the primary selects the strongest general-election candidate. The floor-plus-sabotage model says the primary selects the candidate the veto player permits. Those are different games with different winners, and in May 2023 exactly one Substack post in the corpus was playing the right one.

That's the thesis, expanded: not just that DeSantis was doomed, but *why* doom was structural — and why the people paid to see it couldn't, because seeing it required putting Trump back in the equation they'd all agreed to solve without him.
