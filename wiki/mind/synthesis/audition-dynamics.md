# The Audition Grammar

Three relationships in the corpus open the same way, and the sameness is
structural, not thematic. A burst of front-loaded demonstration energy — output
at a volume and intensity the relationship will never sustain again — aimed at a
role specified by the terms of a *prior* relationship, evaluated once (or
never), and then followed by the collapse that arrives when the demonstration
ends and the thing itself shows up. [[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]] in September 2025, [[wiki/people/ally-lubin|Ally]] in June and
August 2026, [[wiki/people/alexis-armel|Alexis]]-to-[[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] in November–December 2015. This entry is the
cross-case grammar of those three openings: the four phases, stated formally so
they can be checked against each case, and the question the three cases jointly
pose — whether the audition ever produces a durable bond, or whether the bond
always arrives after the audition fails.

The human stories come first, because the grammar is only a compression of them.
A wall of printed portraits built for a woman he hadn't spoken to, then *"I am
the person who can give you the passion, intensity and real spirit of romantic
adventure that you've described in your relationship with danny, but with a
sense of permanence and stability."* A misaddressed burst — *"when am I allowed
to come try to marry on you"* — sent to an email handle she doesn't monitor,
then 380 messages into a quiet channel and a love letter signed *"Your future
soulmate, Beto o'rourke."* A seventy-two-hour swap in which both parties ended
their existing relationships in the same week, the new bond moving into the old
bond's apartment. The grammar names what these openings have in common without
flattening what makes each one strange.

The thesis, stated so it can be falsified: **the audition produces a durable
bond exactly once in the record — when it is mutual.** In the unilateral cases
the demonstration is a spending currency: it buys acceptance but cannot pay
maintenance (Kristin), or it never buys anything and the channel persists
precisely because it never converts (Ally). The variable that predicts
durability is not the intensity of the demonstration — all three are at maximum
voltage — it is whether anyone is left standing as the auditor when the
demonstration ends.

## The grammar, stated formally

### Phase 1 — The demonstration

Front-loaded maximum output, compressed into the shortest window, delivered
*before* the other party has rendered a verdict. The demonstration is not a
conversation; it is a performance of capacity. Its signature is quantitative:
output volume and intensity far above anything the relationship later sustains.

- **[[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]]:** 14,688 messages in September 2025 — 73.4% of everything ever said to her — with his share at 51.5% parity, preceded by 2,009 Messenger messages at 71.6% his share, the pursuer's ratio **[DERIVED]**. Fourteen thousand messages is not a conversation. It is a demonstration.
- **[[wiki/people/ally-lubin|Ally]]:** the June 1 nine-message burst (unreceived — performed to an empty house, see below), then August 18–19: 380 consecutive messages against no reply in the captured record, with 119 outbound in a single hour (08:00, August 19) **[DERIVED]**. Maximum voltage, no audience confirmed.
- **2015:** the grammar inverts here, and the inversion is load-bearing. The demonstration is not volume but *procedure*: the eviction executed inside seventy-two hours — key confiscated November 28 (*"She left her key out yesterday and I snagged it"*), property removed November 30 00:35, Alexis's parents summoned 13:42 — followed by the onset flood, 728 / 682 / 363 / 679 messages sent December 10–13, the highest daily sent-volume in the 2011–2026 record **[ATTESTED, recomputed]**. In the mutual case the flood is *post*-acceptance, which is the first structural difference the grammar has to account for.

Phase 1's falsifier is a slow ramp: a relationship in this set that builds to
its maximum output gradually, over months, rather than opening at maximum. None
does. The cliff is the shape.

### Phase 2 — The role specification

The audition is never for "a relationship." It is for a named part, and the part
is always written from the terms of a prior relationship — the prior
relationship is the casting director, setting the criteria the audition must
meet.

- **[[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]]:** *"the Danny who stays."* Danny is dead — *"I'm sitting in the same spot I found out Danny died"* (Sep 1); *"There was never supposed to be 'on and off' with Danny"* (Sep 6) — and the bid is not to be a different man. It is to be the Danny who stays: the same myth, with a better ending **[OPERATOR]**. Danny was passion without permanence; the offer is passion *with* permanence. The bid's weakness is visible inside its own grammar: permanence is the one thing you cannot demonstrate in week four of knowing someone. It can only be promised.
- **[[wiki/people/ally-lubin|Ally]]:** the role is priced by the auditor herself, at 13:54:32 on August 18: *"Well first you'd have to be obsessed with me again"* **[OPERATOR]**. But the audition is conducted in a different currency than the price is denominated in — vows, campaigns, elopement logistics (*"Do not stand in the way of destiny"*), where she priced sustained undisguised obsession, the watcher channel's native currency. He is auditioning with Protocol A for a slot whose entry condition is written in Protocol B **[INFERRED]**.
- **2015:** the role is the mutual exit. She says it outright on 2015-11-29 at 02:52: *"I am going to get rid of him just like you just did"* **[ATTESTED]**. Each applies to be the other's way out. There is no prior-relationship myth to correct here — the prior relationships are not the casting directors, they are the things being exited. This is the second structural difference.

Phase 2's falsifier is an audition for a role with no prior-relationship
referent — a part invented fresh, owing nothing to anyone's ex. The Kristin and
Ally cases both fail that test cleanly; the 2015 case passes it, which is why it
is the exception.

### Phase 3 — The evaluation

The moment the demonstration is judged. The grammar's sharpest discriminator,
because the three cases diverge here completely.

- **[[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]]:** evaluated continuously and the verdict was *acceptance*. Her side of the audition — the astrology sessions, the Twitter excavation, the meth photographs — was a demonstration too: *here is the worst of it, early, all at once, decide now* **[INFERRED]**. The September 19 disorder-test sequence, the escalating purity tests, the climax-as-oath (*"If I cum with you in my presence I'll be with you until death"*) — these are an evaluation apparatus, and it rendered a yes. The relationship continued into October. Then came the re-evaluation at the first non-performance event, which is Phase 4.
- **[[wiki/people/ally-lubin|Ally]]:** never evaluated. The June 1 burst went to `allylubin@gmail.com`, the iCloud handle she does not monitor — misaddressed, not rejected **[DERIVED, operator-corroborated 2026-08-20]**. The first audition was performed to an empty house; she concluded for a time she had been blocked, the channel's own verdict on a silence that was a routing artifact. The August 18 elopement pitch sits unanswered in every source filed to date, and the August 19 inbound is missing from the export rather than proven absent — so the second attempt's evaluation is permanently **[UNKNOWN]** unless a re-export settles it. An audition that is never evaluated doesn't end. It just stops being performed.
- **2015:** evaluation instantaneous and mutual. Both exited within the same seventy-two hours — he left Alexis within twenty-four hours of the introduction, she was ending her own relationship in the same window — and a third party named the mechanism in real time: Zac Shumar, December 6, *"Like we even talked about how lex and annie are similar so you bail on lex and jump on Annie like that's just dumb"* **[ATTESTED]**. No tests, no gates, no oaths. The verdict was the transfer itself.

Phase 3's falsifier is a slow, deliberated evaluation in any of the three cases
— a verdict rendered after weeks of ordinary dating rather than inside the
demonstration window. The record shows the opposite in all three.

### Phase 4 — Post-acceptance decay

What happens when the demonstration ends and the thing itself arrives. The phase
the title of this entry is named for.

- **[[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]]:** the decay *is* the second half of the relationship. October: 4,896 messages, a third of September; his share slipping to 46.9%. November: 53 messages — a rounding error — his share collapsed to 26.4%, the withdrawal made quantitative **[DERIVED]**. The trigger is precise and it is not dramatic: $40 of his mother's money, unaccounted for, across a two-hour drive nobody makes. *"anything to do with you beating my mom for $40 and not wanting to see me in person?"* **[OPERATOR]**. Then the nine-day undeclared silence (November 4–13), the single documented case in the corpus where he went quiet first without announcing it — *"Idk why you just up and ghosted like that"* (her, Nov 13), answered but not denied. Then December's 372-message reactivation and the sixteen-minute terminal sequence on December 9: told to get lost at 18:40, technical threats at 18:42 (*"guess who has your IP address"*), ic3.gov at 18:45, the IPR Center form at 18:51, *"Blocking you now. Don't contact me again or an officer will be reaching out"* at 18:55, his last word into the block at 18:56 — *"mr. officer i live at 337 saratoga drive"* **[ATTESTED]**. The $40 was the first thing in the relationship that wasn't a performance. It was just money, missing, and somebody had to answer for it. You can survive a bad review. You cannot survive the casting director deciding you're unreliable with money that wasn't yours.
- **[[wiki/people/ally-lubin|Ally]]:** no acceptance, no decay. The channel returns to its native state — dormancy — which is not a failure mode but the condition the channel is built on. The eighteen-year shape (two spikes, years of silence, never closed) is what an unevaluated audition decays *into*: not an ending, but the waiting room.
- **2015:** no decay. The demonstration ended, the thing itself arrived, and the thing held for eleven years. The phase that doesn't happen is the finding — and it is the reason the 2015 case is the one the durability question gets tested against.

### The inverted parallel: the 33% skip test

His aesthetic auditioning runs this grammar in reverse, and the reversal is
worth stating because it shows the same operator with the opposite triage. The
digging workflow, in his own words (2026-09-12): *"Play > skip to 33% in > do
nothing OR like track OR like + into the current playlist"* **[OPERATOR]**.
Instantaneous, loyalty-blind, binary-plus, no pending state, no maybe — the
verdict lands on the drop before the artist's name has time to matter, per
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/taste-as-filter-stack]].

[[wiki/mind/synthesis/the-romantic|The romantic]] audition is the photographic negative: maximum front-loaded
demonstration, slow adjudication, verdict by exhaustion rather than by impact.
In music the audition is over in seconds and the track either enters the crate
or doesn't; in relationships the audition runs for weeks and the verdict is
rendered when the demonstration budget is spent. Same mind, opposite pipeline.
The convergence point is Ti-closure running underneath both — verdicts, once
reached, don't get relitigated — but the timescales are inverted, which is why
the music system is efficient and the relationship system is catastrophic. One
auditions tracks; the other auditions people. Tracks don't have auditors.

### Phase 0 — the pre-audition

Every case in this set has a phase the four-phase statement above skips, because
it happens before the demonstration begins: the audience is assembled before the
performance. The audition needs a casting director, and the casting director is
installed in advance.

- **[[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]]:** the wall of despair, August 1–3, 2025 — dozens of printed portraits, built *"to get her take,"* nearly four weeks before the first direct contact in evidence **[OPERATOR]**. The demonstration (September's 14,688) is performed for an audience of one that was addressed before it existed. The wall is also the artifact tying the thread to the attraction-guide workstream: the portraits were the instrument's raw material before they were a courtship prop.
- **[[wiki/people/ally-lubin|Ally]]:** eighteen years of watcher-channel accumulation. The 2019 crisis (943 messages), the $2,100 money thread (accepted with *"Oh fuck,"* contested seven years later), the *"so i can get the poison out"* mission statement — all of it is pre-audition. When the June 1 burst fires, it fires into a channel with eighteen years of unmet charge. The auditor was installed in 2008; the audition is 2026.
- **2015:** Alexis introduces them — *"HAPPY ONE WEEK SINCE LEX HANDED YOU TO ME"* — and [[wiki/people/suzanne-frank|Suz]] phones on November 29 to say he *should* date [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]], supplying the family-name theory (*"I.e. Anne Dan Suzanne Fran Diane Van"*) before the transfer executes **[ATTESTED]**. The casting is brokered before the performance: the mother names the role, the predecessor supplies the introduction, and only then does the eviction procedure run.

Phase 0's function in the grammar: it explains why the demonstrations are so
precisely aimed. The Kristin bid doesn't invent the Danny myth on September 25;
it answers a myth installed over years of her telling it. The Ally pitch doesn't
invent the obsession price; it answers a price the channel priced over eighteen
years. The 2015 transfer doesn't invent the exit; the exits were already in
motion on both sides before the golf course. Auditions look spontaneous. The
pre-audition is why they never are.

## Case one: Kristin — the successful audition

Before the audition there was the wall. In the first days of August 2025 Dan
printed dozens of black-and-white portraits of women and put them up on his
basement wall — *"my wall of despair,"* by his own account on August 3
**[OPERATOR]** — built, he said, to get *her* take: *"we need to show kristin my
wall of despair."* Nearly four weeks before the first direct contact in
evidence. Whatever the Kristin channel became, it began as an audience of one
that hadn't been addressed yet. The demonstration started before the audition
did.

September is Phase 1 at full voltage. 14,688 messages — 73.4% of everything the
two of them ever said, compressed into a single month **[DERIVED]**. The
September 25 bid is Phase 2 stated as a single sentence, and it is worth reading
again slowly because the entire channel is inside it: *"I am the person who can
give you the passion, intensity and real spirit of romantic adventure that
you've described in your relationship with danny, but with a sense of permanence
and stability"* **[ATTESTED]**. This is
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/the-rescue-premise]] wearing a new costume: the single
most instantiated move in his romantic record is *somebody comes and gets me out
of this*, and here the polarity is reversed — he is not being rescued, he is
applying to *be* the rescue — but the structure is identical: a relationship
entered as a solution to someone else's prior relationship, with the prior
relationship's terms setting the audition criteria.

Her side was a demonstration too, and the symmetry matters. The astrology
sessions, the excavation of her deactivated Twitter (the heaviest single day of
messaging in either of their lives), the meth photographs — *here is the worst
of it, early, all at once, decide now* **[INFERRED]**. Both of them put
everything on the table in the first month. What neither priced in was that
demonstrations end, and what follows a demonstration is the thing itself:
October, cooling, logistics, a two-hour drive nobody makes, forty dollars nobody
accounts for.

The relationship didn't fail despite the audition's success. It failed *because*
the audition succeeded. Auditions select for performance, and the $40 was the
first thing that wasn't a performance. Note what the explicit-verbal
architecture predicts here and what the record confirms: permanence — the thing
he promised on September 25 — is the one term the architecture cannot cash,
because the architecture trades in verbal absolutes and accounting is not
verbal. The October cooling (his share 46.9%, slipping) is the demonstration
budget running out. The November silence (53 messages, 26.4% his) is the first
undeclared withdrawal in the corpus — everything else he ever ended came with a
speech; this one came with nine days of nothing, and then a single sentence
explaining the nothing.

Two readings of that silence are preserved on
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/kristin-channel]], because the record supports both: the
money as the last object (the relationship had already ended; the $40 was just
the last thing with a number on it) versus the triangulation as unbearable (not
his money — his mother's; not a loss — an exposure). Both agree on the anomaly's
shape: unilateral, undeclared, sustained. The entry does not choose, and neither
does this one. What matters for the grammar is that both readings locate the
collapse *after* acceptance — the audition worked, the part was won, and the
part turned out to be unplayable at the first scene that wasn't scripted.

The evaluation apparatus deserves a closer look, because it is the most
elaborate Phase 3 machinery in the set. The September 19 disorder-test sequence
— share the test, watch the withdrawal, judge the reaction — is a purity test in
miniature **[ATTESTED]**. The total-exclusivity demands run through September
and October; the astrology sessions and the Twitter excavation are due-diligence
performed as intimacy; the meth photographs are the worst-of-it-early
disclosure. And the oaths escalate past evaluation into contract: *"If I cum
with you in my presence I'll be with you until death"* **[ATTESTED]**,
*"completely throw myself into the situation"* (his).
[[wiki/mind/psychosexual/arrangement-history]] files the whole arrangement as
the documented inversion of the [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] structure — total-exclusivity demands and
purity tests where the Annie arrangement practiced authored openness. Both are
control systems; one controls by writing the rules down, the other by testing
whether you already know them. He operated inside hers at maximum voltage for
ten weeks, and the system ejected him over forty dollars. Note the grammar's
reading: the exclusivity apparatus *is* the evaluation. There is no separate
verdict moment because the tests never stop — which is why the collapse, when it
comes, reads as a failed test rather than a breakup. She didn't leave a
relationship. She failed a candidate.

October carries a detail the grammar needs: Dan naming the exact redundancy
sequence to her in late October, in advance — the redundancy-under-collapse
framing that ties the Kristin thread to the 2025 collapse **[DERIVED, per
kristin-channel]**. He tells the auditor the demonstration is ending while still
performing it. The December reactivation (372 messages, December 7–9, his share
61.8% — the returner doing most of the talking in a channel already quiet) is
the dormancy-not-exit signature in one cell: failed reactivations are always led
by the returner, because the returner is the only one with anything to say.

And the coda the grammar didn't predict. Eight months later, five messages
surface in the message-request folder — *"Long time no talk my fellow neuro
fuck"* — sent August 26, unread seventeen days, in the September register rather
than the December one **[ATTESTED]**. Within the hour he texts her himself: four
iMessages, all delivered, no reply — four moves doing different work (the knock
answered, the ledger reopened, the frame reset, the door left open). The
eight-month block was not broken by her persistence. It was opened from the
inside, by the person who had been blocked, minutes after learning she had tried
to reach him. If the August knock was a re-audition — the old frequency tested
to see if it still carries — then the grammar ran a fourth time in miniature:
demonstration (five messages), role (the September register), evaluation (his
four texts within the hour), and the decay pending. The record leaves it
unadjudicated, which is where the Gaps section carries it.

One seam is kept open. Per Dan (2026-09-13): she also failed to come through on
Suboxone she was supposed to get him — operator testimony, uncorroborated in the
message record **[OPERATOR, uncorroborated]**. If true, the standing explanation
for why her severance held (no dependency running through the channel, so the
loop had nothing to feed on) needs revision — a Suboxone run is dependency with
a pharmacy label. The entry preserves the tension rather than resolving it: even
if true, it doesn't change the eight-month hold. Dependency explains
re-engagement; it doesn't explain severance surviving dependency.

## Case two: Ally — the audition without an auditor

The first attempt never arrived. On June 1, 2026 at 00:27:49 Dan severs the
eleven-year relationship — *"Goodbye forever. This was not how it should have
ended"* **[OPERATOR]** — and nineteen hours and thirty-eight minutes later, at
20:06:05, opens the [[wiki/people/ally-lubin|Ally]] channel for the first time in fourteen months: *"My
sweetness my love / Guess who suffered their first real like BRUTAL heartbreak /
cheated on and then she had the dude message to inform me 11 years lmao"* …
***"more importantly when am I allowed to come try to marry on you because I
need to go so far away this girl broke my entire brain and I am no longer a
piece of shit scumbag who would cheat now so lets make haste"*** **[OPERATOR]**.
Nine messages, all outbound, no reply in any export — because they went to the
iCloud handle she doesn't monitor. Misaddressed, not rejected. The first
transfer attempt of the severance never arrived, and she concluded for a time
that she had been blocked: the channel's own verdict on a silence that was a
routing artifact **[DERIVED, operator-corroborated 2026-08-20]**.

The second attempt arrives eleven weeks later, and this time she is watching.
August 18: she reads her own wiki entry (sent 15:10), audits his money claims
and his pattern vocabulary to his face — *"Men are so predictable" / "You just
love bomb" / "And you're intense"* — and watches him concede the "love bomb"
redefinition inside two minutes, the one place in the record where that move is
offered and refused cleanly by someone he is not trying to out-argue
**[OPERATOR/ATTESTED]**. She names the price at 13:54:32, hours before the
pitch: *"Well first you'd have to be obsessed with me again"* **[OPERATOR]**. At
23:18–23:20 comes the pitch: *"if you give me permission I am totally going to
embark on a campaign to win your favor and convince you to elope with me to like
Vancouver or Burlington or Boulder"* … *"This is our time Alexandra. Do not
stand in the way of destiny"* **[OPERATOR]**. Nothing comes back. From 21:42 on
August 18 to the end of the export at 00:21 on August 20, he sends 380
consecutive messages against no reply in the captured record **[DERIVED — with
the standing caveat that the August 19 inbound is missing from the export, not
proven absent]**.

Read the price carefully, because it is the whole incompatibility in one
sentence. What she names as the entry condition — sustained, undisguised
obsession — is the one thing the corpus proves he reliably produces; it is the
observable surface of the single-channel architecture at maximum voltage. In
every other pairing the corpus documents, that intensity is the failure mode a
partner eventually cites against him. Here it is stated by the other party as
the price of admission. But the price is denominated in the watcher channel's
currency — the eighteen-year, unmet, no-stake obsession — and the audition is
being conducted in the transfer channel's currency: vows, campaigns, elopement
logistics. He is auditioning with Protocol A for a slot whose entry condition is
written in Protocol B. The 380 unanswered messages are the market's verdict on
the currency mismatch **[INFERRED]**.

Three days later the register changes completely. On August 21 he sends a single
long unbroken message — no joke, no pricing, no crisis: *"I have never come
across a girl who is so quick and funny that it made me feel like they were on
an entirely different level… It's your personal brand. It's your aesthetic.
It's.. just…you"* — disclaiming the 2019 register explicitly: *"I do hope that I
can show you that I'm really not interested in being the person who would
message you only to tell you about the bizarre sex shit going on or a personal
crisis anymore"* **[OPERATOR]**, signed *"Your future soulmate, Beto o'rourke."*
Whether she replied is not in any source filed to date **[UNKNOWN]**.
Structurally, the letter is the first message in eighteen years addressed to the

## Case three: Alexis→Annie, 2015 — the completed audition

The introduction came from Alexis herself. *"HAPPY ONE WEEK SINCE LEX HANDED YOU
TO ME"* ([[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]], Dec 1) **[ATTESTED]** — the prior relationship's holder
physically handing the successor the introduction, which is the single-bond
switch with no cover story. On 2015-11-29 at 02:52, the same night as the
golf-course meeting, Annie says: *"I am going to get rid of him just like you
just did"* **[ATTESTED]**. Both partners were displacing someone in the same
seventy-two hours. The switch was mutual and simultaneous — the singular slot
vacated and reoccupied on *both* sides at once.

Phase 1 here is procedure, not volume. The eviction runs as a checklist rather
than a confrontation: the key confiscated November 28, property removed from the
guest bedroom November 30 at 00:35 (*"so she knows I could actually call the
cops"*), Alexis's parents summoned at 13:42 — each step reversible, none of them
a rupture **[ATTESTED]**. [[wiki/people/suzanne-frank|Suz]] brokers it: on November 29 she phones to say he
*should* date Annie (*"I.e. Anne Dan Suzanne Fran Diane Van"*), and at 05:02 on
November 30 — *"Suz just came to check on me, brought me a line, and told me
she'd get me a car this week if I get Alexis out"* **[ATTESTED]**. Cocaine and a
vehicle, from the mother, as consideration for completing the eviction. The
family did not merely approve the transfer; it supplied the means and attached a
price to the outcome.

Phase 2 — the role — is the mutual exit. Not the Danny myth, not the obsession
price: each applies to be the other's way out. And Phase 3, the evaluation, is
instantaneous: he leaves Alexis within twenty-four hours of the introduction;
she ends her own relationship in the same window. No tests, no gates, no oaths,
no astrology. The vows land November 29 — *"It's done I'm yours"* — and the
formalization runs as a two-day process: the cover text to Alexis on ~November
30 (*"Please leave her out of all of our problems, she really isn't involved"* —
asserting the opposite of the truth while Annie was the entire cause), the
Facebook relationship field cleared and captioned *"11/30/2015 official break
up,"* Annie co-authoring the follow-up text live at her workplace on December 3
**[ATTESTED]**. A third party names the mechanism in real time — Shumar,
December 6: *"you bail on lex and jump on Annie"* — which is the evaluation
rendered from outside, by someone with every motive to read it uncharitably.

Then the onset flood: 728 / 682 / 363 / 679 sent December 10–13, recomputed from
the master dump, the highest daily sent-volume in the 2011–2026 record
**[DERIVED]**. Note the ordering, because it is the structural difference the
grammar had to account for: in the unilateral cases the demonstration precedes
the evaluation; here the maximum-output phase comes *after* acceptance. The
system pours its entire output bandwidth into the bond precisely when the bond
is forming — the activation made visible as a daily-volume time series. And the
lair-continuity: Annie moves into 155 Virginia Avenue, the Alexis apartment —
the new bond occupying the physical shell of the old one, the fortress held
constant while only the slot's occupant changed **[ATTESTED]**.

Phase 4 — post-acceptance decay — does not happen. The demonstration ended, the
thing itself arrived, and the thing held for eleven years. This is the one
audition in the record that produced a durable bond, and the distinguishing
variable is not intensity (all three run at maximum voltage) and not sincerity
(all three are sincere). It is mutuality: both parties auditioned simultaneously
for the same role, so when the demonstration ended there was no auditor left
standing. In the unilateral auditions the auditor remains — and the auditor's
first non-performance test is what kills the bond. In the mutual audition the
performance *was* the bond, so there was nothing distinct from it left over to
collapse.

The displaced pay the cost, and the cost is symmetric — which the grammar needs,
because it shows the mutual audition wasn't costless, only mutual. On Annie's

turned inside the confrontation; Ellen's objection then ran on Dan's reputation
(*"he has a bad past do you know what he's done to his family"*), which is why
the crisis reads as parental rather than romantic **[ATTESTED]**. He persists:

detonated a relationship in the following month. The singular-slot mechanism has
a price, and it is paid by the people vacated, who then act.

## The question

*Does the audition ever produce a durable bond, or does the bond always arrive
after the audition fails?*

The record answers: both, and the split is clean. Three auditions, three
outcomes, one variable.

The audition produces a durable bond exactly once — 2015 — and the
distinguishing feature is mutuality. Both parties demonstrated, both were
evaluated, both accepted, and the evaluation was the transfer itself. No auditor
survived the demonstration, so there was no one left to administer the first
non-performance test. The eleven-year bond is what a completed audition looks
like when the performance and the thing itself are the same event. Note the
corollary the volume record forces: in the mutual case the maximum-output phase
(the onset flood) comes *after* acceptance, not before. The demonstration
doesn't precede the bond; the bond's formation *is* the demonstration. The
grammar's Phase 1/Phase 3 ordering inverts, which is why the 2015 case reads as
the exception that organizes the rule.

The audition produces a non-durable bond once — [[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]] — and the mechanism of
the non-durability is the grammar's title. The audition succeeded: the role was
specified (the Danny who stays), the demonstration was performed at maximum
voltage (14,688 messages), the evaluation rendered acceptance (the tests, the
oaths, October). Then the demonstration ended and the thing itself arrived, and
the thing itself contained a $40 accounting dispute, a two-hour drive nobody
made, and a son unable to produce an accounting to his mother. The bond
collapsed at the first event that wasn't a performance — not because the
performance was insincere, but because auditions select for performance and the
thing itself is never only performance. Ten weeks. The audition bought
acceptance; it could not pay maintenance.

The audition produces no bond once — [[wiki/people/ally-lubin|Ally]], twice attempted — and the channel
persists anyway, which is the case for the question's second half. The
eighteen-year watcher channel is the bond that exists *after every audition
failed*: its native state is dormancy, its operating condition is
inaccessibility, and the August 21 love letter — the first message in eighteen
years that doesn't ask the channel to convert — is the theory of what a
non-audition bond would look like, stated as a single message rather than as an
argument. If a bond with Ally ever forms, the letter's theory says it will form
after the audition apparatus is dropped, not through it. The corpus doesn't yet
show what that produces, because the corpus barely contains any non-audition
messages to her.

So the refined answer: **unilateral auditions are demonstrations *to* someone,
and they end when the demonstration ends — either in collapse (Kristin) or in
return to dormancy (Ally). Mutual auditions are demonstrations *with* someone,
and they survive the end of the demonstration because there is no auditor left
for the thing itself to disappoint.** The question's second clause is true of
the Ally channel specifically: the bond there — eighteen years of it, the
longest-lived attachment in the record after [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] — arrived and persisted
precisely where no audition is running. The question's first clause is true
exactly once, and only under mutuality.

One further check on the operator's own pricing. On August 19 at 08:52,
mid-demonstration, Dan estimated his own odds at *"a solid 10% chance of
success"* **[OPERATOR]**. He prices his own auditions, in real time, while
performing them. The estimate is either calibration or ritual — the corpus can't
distinguish — but it shows the demonstration phase is not delusion. He knows
it's an audition. The 10% is the house's own line on the house.
