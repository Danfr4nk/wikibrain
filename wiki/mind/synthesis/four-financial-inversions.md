# The Four Financial Inversions

The money in the Dan–[[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] relationship did not flow one way for a decade.

It inverted four times.

Each inversion is real, dated, and documented — which is why every grievance ever built on any single one of them is locally true and historically incomplete.

The financial history only coheres held as one object.

Cut it at any inversion boundary and the piece you are holding will tell you a true story about a false decade.

The four, in order:

1. **~2015–2020: Dan funds.** The household runs on his money, which is substantially his mother's money — [[wiki/people/suzanne-frank|Suz]]'s cash provisioning, the estate advances, the 2020 distribution. Annie moves between jobs.
2. **2023–2025: the landlord-debt years.** Dan working, Annie between jobs; the couple jointly accumulates ~$10,000 owed to [[wiki/people/john-paci|John Paci]] at 307 E 76th St, serviced at $650/week in late 2024 and never retired. Plus a separate $7,000 Con Edison bill.
3. **Mid-2024–Mar 2025: Annie funds.** Dan loses the Au Za'atar job involuntarily (August 2024); Annie becomes the household's sole earner — the Libby income, a second six-day-a-week job, the rent, the phone bill, a PNC loan, the shared drug budget — until the income is cut off by a death and a denied claim.
4. **Feb 2025–: Dan supplies.** The move to Uniontown strips Annie of income, independence, and drug access simultaneously; Dan becomes her supply chain. This is the one inversion attributable to a decision rather than to circumstance, because the move that produced it was engineered by the party it benefited **[OPERATOR]**.

The decade's net, measured once from operator-provided payment-app screenshots (2026-07-15, not in `raw/`, not an audited ledger): Dan sent on the order of **$139K** and received back **$16–20K** — a net outflow of roughly **$119K–$123K**, about **six-to-one** in Annie's favor **[DERIVED — see "The ledger" for the full provenance discipline]**.

The see-saw is real.

So is its tilt.

## Inversion 1: The Suz-backed years (~2015–2020)

### The earliest record: "take people's money"

The first financial statement in the relationship's record is [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]]'s.

It is about whose money the household runs on.

**2015-12-29, 23:25–23:28** — four weeks into the relationship:

> Annie: *"I don't want you having to take people's money. I don't want that. / I want to grow / I want stability / Our own money / Jobs / I don't want to fall into a deeper hole than we already our in"* **[ATTESTED — corpus hand-read, wiki/timeline/annie-record]**

"Take people's money" is doing specific work in that sentence.

It is not "spend too much" — it is a description of the *source*.

The money keeping them afloat arrives from other people, and she is naming that as the problem before she names the amount.

"Our own money" is the counterfactual she wants — earned, not taken.

Four weeks in, the household's funding model is already legible to both of them.

And it is already the thing she wants changed.

The same night, the $200 cocaine spend becomes the decade's first itemized dispute.

Dan: *"Do u know how much I spent on that stuff today? / $200"* (23:46–23:47).

Annie: *"Exactly... / Some people can't even get $200 to pay their rent. / If I had $200 right now I'd pay my damn dentist bill / And that $200 wasn't even your own"* (23:47–23:48).

Dan: *"It was my money"* (23:50) **[ATTESTED]**.

But the structure of the argument is the finding: eleven days before the new year, the question "whose money is this" is already the household's native grammar.

And Annie is already the one asking it.

### The provisioning routine

The "people" in "take people's money" has a name.

The first week of January 2016 shows the routine running in real time.

Annie: *"I have money on me yeah"* (19:44).

Read that slowly, because the whole first inversion is in it.

Suz is the backstop for a $100 drug purchase — "Suz could pay you back tomorrow" is said the way you mention a bank.

As infrastructure, not as a favor.

And "I paid her back for last night" shows the flow is *cyclical*: money moves Suz→Dan→supplier and back, a standing facility, not an emergency loan.

This is not a young man hitting up his mother.

This is a household whose liquidity provider is his mother, operating on a next-day settlement rhythm.

**2016-01-02, 11:23** — Dan: *"Gotta grab some $ from suz before we go. She's getting ready to go to the bank"* (11:23).

Annie: *"That's fine. If we need to meet her we can"* (11:23) **[ATTESTED]**.

Annie's "that's fine" is the sound of a funding model nobody is contesting that week.

So inversion 1's mechanism, stated plainly: **Dan funded the household, and Suz funded Dan.**

The provider direction Dan→Annie is real.

The provider direction Suz→Dan behind it is equally real.

Any grievance that starts "I carried this household" and stops there is telling the truth about the first arrow and omitting the second.

### Withheld rent as a credit facility

Step back from the personalities and look at the mechanism.

In inversion 1, the household's credit facility was a person ([[wiki/people/suzanne-frank|Suz]]) with a next-day settlement rhythm.

In inversion 2, the facility was a landlord's patience — consumed in $650/week increments that never retired the principal.

Both are credit.

Only the first one had a name on it.

Withheld rent is an unusual facility because the lender does not consent to the loan and the borrower does not sign anything.

Paci's patience was not a facility he offered.

It was one the household drew on by not paying, the way you draw on an overdraft by not depositing.

The $650/week payments are best read as *interest on an unacknowledged loan* — enough to keep the facility open, never enough to close it.

Fifteen months, same $10,000.

That is not a repayment schedule.

That is a balance being carried.

And here is the structural point the grievance analysis needs: inversion 2's folk memory ("Dan was working, Dan was paying") mistakes *servicing* for *carrying*.

Servicing is what you do to keep a facility open.

Carrying is what reduces the principal.

The household serviced the Paci facility for over a year without carrying it at all.

The distinction matters because grievance 2's strongest form — *I was sending $650 a week* — is a servicing claim wearing a carrying claim's clothes.

Both halves are real.

Only one of them moved the number.

## What the four rotations add up to

Four findings that only exist at the whole-decade level — invisible inside any single rotation, visible once the object is held as one.

Four inversions, four changes of direction, and the decade still closed at $119K–$123K net, six-to-one.

Finite, borrowable, earnable, controllable.

The inversions get harder to leave as they go.

### Worked example: the November 15 grievance across the boundary

Take *"I WORK 6 FUCKING DAYS A WEEK... You worry about having drugs. That's it"* (2024-11-15) and walk it across each boundary:

- **Across into rotation 1:** inverts. Five years of his family's capital funding the household she lived in — the grievance's implied history (she carries, he doesn't) now runs against the decade's dominant direction.

The grievance does not become false at any crossing.

It becomes *incomplete* — which is the machine's whole point.

Local truth is not a degraded form of truth.

Local truth is not just incomplete across boundaries.
