# Music Taste Report — The Bing Bong Deviation, Week of Oct 15 – 20, 2026
_Layer: interpreted/synthesis — connects two or more concepts. It names the self/ exhibits it reasons over and cites them rather than restating them._

On October 20, 2026, Dan Frank announced that his music taste had been "ALL OVER THE PLACE" for a week, that he was "absolutely devouring new stuff," and that he was "literally on a benny benassi tear." Then he handed over the evidence: a 56-track playlist export named Bing Bong. The record confirms the first two claims, dismantles the third, and — in the dismantling — produces the most interesting taste event since the profile began. For the first time in the measured record, the filter itself moved. The October 4 report's headline was that twenty discovery-feed winners were statistically indistinguishable from the incumbents: the coordinates held, only the personnel changed. Sixteen days later the personnel, the genre center, and the tempo center all changed at once — zero tracks from the K3YN0T3 orbit that supplies his #1 song and roughly 28% of everything he likes, a median tempo eight beats above his home room, and UK garage and bass music where hypertechno used to be. And the Benassi tear, examined closely, turns out to be one track — the exact track a viral tecktonik dancer has been looping on Instagram all year, which Dan had watched, by his own hyperbolic count, five hundred times the night before he added it. His narrative named the song that had been looped into his head. His playlist recorded the week he had actually had. Both are data; they are data about different things. This report keeps them separate, which is what the recursive series is for.

## Dataset and method — and an instrument change, stated plainly

**Subject:** Dan Frank's current taste state, measured against one week of intake (the Bing Bong playlist, 56 tracks, Added At timestamps from 2026-10-15 17:03 ET to 2026-10-20 02:41 ET).

**Dataset:**
- The full Bing Bong export: 56 tracks, 76 distinct credited artists, with Spotify audio features, genre tags, popularity scores, release dates, and add timestamps. Total runtime 166.6 minutes; median track length 170.5 seconds.
- The two standing baselines, used as the comparison versions: the October 4 spectral report (58 tracks, FFT-measured) and the October 11 Taste Constitution (172 liked tracks analyzed; 70-track canon with a full chroma harmonic pass).
- Dan's same-day testimony (quoted in the sources), including his own correction of the Benassi claim and his own identification of the mechanism behind it.

**Instrument change — the recursive caveat.** This version's audio numbers are Spotify's API audio features as delivered in the export, *not* the spectral pipeline of October 4 and *not* the chroma template-matching of October 11. The valid cross-version comparisons are therefore: tempo (both instruments report it), artist personnel, genre tags, release dates, popularity, and intake timing. The invalid comparisons, not made anywhere in this report: spectral centroid, sub-bass share, stereo width (no counterpart in the Spotify feature set), and harmony — Spotify's key/mode fields are a single track-level estimate, and the October 4 report already excluded key estimation from its findings as unreliable on major/minor discrimination for this material. Whether the I–vi seesaw survived the deviation is therefore *unknown from this dataset* and is logged as an open measurement debt in the Assessment, not glossed over. A version series that changes instruments without saying so manufactures drift and stability alike; this one says so.

**Confidence labels** are given per finding, as in the predecessor. Dan's stated testimony outranks the counts where they conflict on *preference*; the counts outrank testimony on *what was added*, because the export is the act itself.

It records what survived a rapid skip-test at 1 a.m., not what a week of considered listening converged on. The purge risk that follows from that is addressed as a falsifiable prediction in the Assessment. **Confidence: high** (timestamps are the export's own).

Two single-add days (Oct 16, Oct 17) sit inside the week like punctuation: one dubstep track, one hip-hop track, nothing else. The "all over the place" week was, in calendar terms, three working sessions (Oct 15, 18, 19–20 overnight) separated by near-silence. Scatter and silence alternate; there is no steady eclectic stream to average away.

The deviation is a civil war inside dance music, not a defection from it. **Confidence: high on tag incidence; moderate on cluster assignment** (Spotify tags are vendor metadata, and 13 tracks carry none).

## Tempo: the triumvirate breaks, the 140 shelf becomes the center

The Constitution's home room is ~125–130 bpm (median 129.9 across the liked list). October 4 formalized a triumvirate: a 128 house shelf, a 140 UK-force shelf, a 174 detonation shelf, with tempo named the hardest gate in the filter.

Bing Bong's distribution: median **138.1**, mean 136.3, range 76.9–187.7.

- 125–130 (home room): **10 of 56 (17.9%)**
- 133–145: 29 of 56 (51.8%) — the former force shelf is now the median habitat
- Above 145: 8 tracks — but these are not the old 174 detonation class; they are dubstep at 145–150 and double-time-tagged drill/jersey material at 162–188, a different mechanism wearing similar numbers
- Below 120: 2 tracks, both instructive (see the tempo-feel conflict below)

In the predecessor's language: the filter did not merely admit off-center personnel, it re-centered. Where October's winners landed "dead-center on his standing coordinates," October 20's intake sits a full shelf up, and the 128 shelf survives mainly as the disco/melodic fringe (125–131: LOVE LANGUAGE, Brunello, Flex (UK), King Julian, Rebūke, the Benassi track at 128.0). Whether this is a durable re-centering or a session artifact — a UKG digging session *is* a 140 session, almost by definition — cannot be settled by one export, and this report does not settle it. What can be said: the tempo gate, previously the hardest in the stack, is the gate whose relaxation is most visible in the numbers, and any next version that finds the median back at ~130 will reclassify this week as genre tourism at a borrowed tempo. **Confidence: high on the distribution; the durability question is explicitly open.**

The pattern: the bridges are the *140-shelf* personnel of the canon (emerge, Hamdi, the mxbeats/DRYFTMODE production axis), not its 128 vocal core and not its K3YN0T3 summit. ## The Benassi event: one track, five hundred videos, and a salience mechanism

The dataset contains exactly one Benny Benassi track: "Love Is Gonna Save Us" — ARTBAT; Benny Benassi, 128.0 bpm, tagged melodic techno / melodic house / techno / progressive house / electro house — added 2026-10-20 at **00:15 ET**, the first add of the overnight UKG session. One track in 56 is not a tear, and Dan corrected the claim himself within the hour: "i was just being hyperbolic about my benassi tear." Under this series' standing rule the correction stands, and the original phrasing is kept beside it, because the *gap* between them is the finding.

The mechanism is documented from three independent sides:

1. **The song is a tecktonik artifact.** "Love Is Gonna Save Us" is a 2003 Benassi track (a single from *Hypnotica*, the electro-house landmark album built around "Satisfaction"). Tecktonik — the arm-heavy electro dance formed at Paris's Metropolis club from 2002 — is the dance of that exact electro-house moment.
2. **The song is currently viral as a dance sound.** The dancer Sasha Cheba (@cheba_man) began posting the old tecktonik moves to 2000s electro hits in February 2026; his May 24, 2026 stage reel — striped shirt, sunglasses, red light, crowd filming — is danced to "Love Is Gonna Save Us," which is the music tag on both of his own key reels; by October the original reel showed roughly 17 million views. Cheba Man is, in the current coverage's phrase, the creator "bringing Tecktonik back."
3. **Dan's own account closes the loop.** The night before the add (Oct 19 into Oct 20), he watched — his words, with the hyperbole flagged as his register, the count uncorroborated and not asserted — "500 cheba_man tecktonik videos last nught [night] on ig." At 00:15 ET, in the first minutes of Oct 20, he added the ARTBAT/Benassi version of the reel's song. (The ARTBAT collaboration is current, not archival: it opens Benassi's 2026 album *Feel The Bass* and reached #1 on the 1001Tracklists overall chart in 2025.)

Read together: this was not a Benassi catalog binge. It was **looped audiovisual exposure to a single song as a dance's soundtrack**, followed within hours by the acquisition of that song. The track entered the playlist at home-room tempo (128.0 — the week's fringe, not its center) wearing melodic-techno tags, as the *first* add of a session that then ran UK garage for 35 minutes. Benassi supplied the doorway; UKG supplied the room.

The generalizable finding, stated as this series' first salience law: **Dan's narrative summary of a listening period names its most-looped single artifact, not its statistical center.** Asked to characterize the week, he named the one track that had been repeated at him hundreds of times by a video feed — a salience maximum — while the week's actual center (UK garage / bass music at ~138–140, zero Benassi beyond the doorway track) went unnamed. This is the same stated-vs-revealed structure the October 4 report documented in miniature (his stated top four vs. play counts; "Meet Me In The Dark" demoted by testimony despite chart persistence), now observed at the level of a whole week's self-description, and — unusually — corrected by the subject himself in real time, with the mechanism volunteered. The law is offered at **moderate confidence**: one clean instance, self-corrected, mechanism independently corroborated; it becomes a finding of the series if the next version's self-summary again names a salience artifact over a center.

A secondary note, because the profile is recursive and the detail is too exact to drop: the exposure object is a *dance* video. The man whose taste system is documented as lyrics-as-timbre, voice-as-texture, and tempo-as-utility acquired the week's most narratively important song through a channel with no lyrical or playlist context at all — bodies, arms, red light, and a 2003 electro riff. The intake stack's Filter 0 has a visual side door, and this week it opened.

## What held: energy, darkness, newness, and the underground

Against the moved parameters, four standing coordinates held almost eerily — and one of them holds to the third decimal.

**Darkness.** Spotify valence across the 56: median **0.322**. The October 4 report, citing the taste-model record rather than a fresh pull, gave his standing valence coordinate as "median valence ≈ 0.322, dark." A genre revolution, a tempo re-centering, a total personnel turnover — and the week's median valence reproduces the standing coordinate exactly. Valence is a crude instrument and the match is partly coincidence at this precision; at the level the claim is made (dark, not bright; 25 of 56 tracks below 0.3), it is the week's clearest continuity signal. What he wants a track to *feel* like did not move; what he wants it to *be* did.

**Energy.** Median energy 0.803; 28 of 56 tracks at or above 0.8. The force function did not relax — it changed uniform. Danceability median 0.683 is, if anything, *groove-forward* relative to a riddim-heavy list's reputation, driven by the UKG/bassline wing's swung drums.

**Newness — "devouring" is literal.** Release years: **34 of 56 tracks (60.7%) released in 2026**, 10 more in 2024, 6 in 2025; only 6 tracks predate 2024, and exactly one of those is the 2003-rooted Benassi song in its current ARTBAT version. Median Spotify popularity 39.5 (mean 39.3; only 12 tracks at 50+, 13 below 30): this is underground intake, not chart grazing. His same-day phrase "absolutely devouring new stuff" is, unusually for a self-summary, *understated* against the release-date column.

**The vocal texture.** Instrumentalness median 0.029 — the list is overwhelmingly vocal-bearing, consistent with the standing vocal-core picture — but speechiness tells the deviation's story in miniature: 11 tracks above 0.2, and they cluster at the two ends of the week: the hip-hop seeding day (NEMS at 0.380, JR Writer at 0.458, Fergie Baby at 0.302) and the UKG tail, where chopped MC/vocal-sample density drives Cesco's "GITP" to 0.420 and pushes MPH, Sammy Virji, and the grime-tagged "Greengate Adhesive" over the line. Per the lyric qualifier, this is read as voice-as-instrument density in both cases: the drill verses and the garage chops are the same axis to his ear — a human voice used as percussion — which is presumably why a taste system that admits almost no semantic listening can admit both without strain. **Confidence: moderate** (the qualifier itself carries its stated single-source limits).

**Key/mode metadata, reported and not interpreted.** Spotify's track-level estimates give 34 major / 22 minor, with C#/D♭ the modal key (14 of 56). The predecessor report excluded key claims as unreliable on this material, and the Constitution's I–vi finding rests on a chroma progression pass this dataset cannot replicate. These numbers are therefore filed as metadata only. They assert nothing about harmony — and the harmony question they raise is logged below as debt.

## What the week was, in sequence

Reconstructed from timestamps and the clusters above, the deviation has a plot the averages hide:

- **Oct 15 — the scatter was real, and it was first.** Nine adds spanning sexy drill/jersey club at a double-time-tagged 187.7, East Coast hip-hop, big room (Sandro Silva, 128.0), stutter house, UKG, and the week's first bridge track. Read alone, this day supports the "all over the place" summary completely. It is also the only day that does.
- **Oct 16–17 — silence with punctuation.** Two tracks in two days: the heaviest dubstep add of the week and the week's most speech-heavy hip-hop add. The deviation's two poles, filed one per day, as if bookmarked.
- **Oct 18 — consolidation.** Sixteen adds; bass music becomes the plurality, the JONCASS bloc lands whole, Virtual Riot/Eliminate return — and the day's fringe (LOVE LANGUAGE disco house at 128.7, Rebūke melodic techno at 130.0) sits exactly on the old home-room coordinates, as though the 128 shelf had been demoted to a rest stop.
- **Oct 19 (overnight) — the 1 a.m. broadening.** Disco, tech house, phonk, a Sickick dark-side mix, and dubstep again, in a 43-minute burst. The fringe and the force wing in one session.
- **Oct 19 (night) → Oct 20, 00:15 — the tecktonik exposure.** By Dan's account, an evening of Cheba Man reels. The first musical act afterward is the reel's song.
- **Oct 20, 00:15–00:50 — the UKG run.** Fifteen tracks, 35 minutes, the new center at full monoculture — Cesco, MPH, Nikita, the Wicked, Sammy Virji, Hamdi inside "Push." The week ends less scattered than it has ever been in the record: one genre family, one tempo shelf, one session.

The summary Dan gave at 15:07 — "ALL OVER THE PLACE" — describes Oct 15 accurately and Oct 20 inversely. The week's trajectory is scatter → consolidation → monoculture, a funnel, not a spray. If a self-summary names the *first* day's character and the *most-looped* song, and misses the funnel's narrow end entirely, the salience law proposed above has a second leg: **the summary lags the intake by the length of the funnel.** He narrated the week he started, while living at the end of the week he finished.

## Assessment

The October 4 version concluded: a stable, measurable filter; the personnel changes, the parameters don't. The honest recursive update, sixteen days later, is narrower and stranger. **The parameters that moved are the visible ones — genre, artist, tempo shelf. The parameters that held are the felt ones — darkness (to the third decimal), energy, vocal texture, underground newness.** A listener who changed what he listens to while changing nothing about what he wants it to do to him is not a listener whose taste broke; he is a listener whose taste went looking for its old function in a new genre — and found it, at volume, in 35-minute overnight sessions, in UK garage and bass music at 140.

Three debts and four predictions are left on the record, deliberately falsifiable, so the next version of this profile grades this one instead of merely succeeding it:

**Debts.**
1. *Harmony.* No chroma pass has been run on these 56. Whether the I–vi seesaw (42.9% of the canon) operates inside the UKG/bass intake is the single largest open question in the profile.
2. *Spectral comparability.* The 56 have not been through the October 4 FFT pipeline; centroid/sub-bass claims for this week do not exist and are not approximated here.
3. *Play counts.* This version measures intake only. No listening/rotation data for the 56 exists yet; intake is not preference, per the series' oldest correction.

**Predictions** (made 2026-10-20, to be graded by the next version).
1. **Purge asymmetry.** Of this week's two force wings, the dubstep/riddim/deathstep bloc will purge at a higher rate than the UK garage/bassline bloc before the next canon-level lock — the UKG wing sits on his historical 140 shelf and his strongest historical promote genre (bass/tech house), the riddim wing on novelty energy with no standing shelf. *Confidence: moderate.*
2. **The JONCASS test.** The Mary Droppinz / SVDKO / JONCASS bloc either recurs in the next intake window (adoption) or appears nowhere (one EP, inhaled). No middle outcome is predicted. *Confidence: high that the test is decisive, by construction.*
3. **Tempo reversion or ratification.** The next full export's median tempo will be either back within 128–133 (this week reclassified as tourism) or at/above 136 (re-centering ratified). A median in 133–136 is the genuinely surprising outcome. *Confidence: moderate.*
4. **The doorway track outlasts its session.** "Love Is Gonna Save Us" (ARTBAT; Benny Benassi) will still be in active rotation after the median Bing Bong track has gone quiet — not because it fits the new center (at 128.0 it does not) but because its exposure history is unlike anything else in the list: hundreds of looped viewings against one skip-test each for the rest. *Confidence: low-moderate — this is the salience law betting on itself.*

And the standing instruction this version leaves for every future one, in the commissioning words that created it: this is exactly the stuff that should be documented and recursively analysed, each new version against the last — corrections kept beside the claims they corrected, instruments named, predictions graded. The taste will do what it does. The profile's job is to still be honest when it gets there.
