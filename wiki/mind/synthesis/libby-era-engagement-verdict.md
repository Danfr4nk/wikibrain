# The Libby-Era Disengagement Claim

**The question:** Annie says that during the months she worked for Libby — mornings
at Libby's, evenings straight to the restaurant, six days a week — Dan ignored
her when she came home and never asked how her day was, and that this is the
shit that takes a toll on someone. Was the claim factually founded, and does it
explain or justify why she began leaving?

**The short answer:** the feeling is corroborated; the causal story is not
demonstrated. The record shows he genuinely almost never asked about her day in
writing and was measurably slower engaging with her Libby talk than with
anything else. It also shows, in 137 documented messages across the era — about
one every day and a half — him offering to bring her food, groceries, clothes,
money, medicine, and help, plus a three-day stretch in July 2024 where a
"fruit stand" complaint turned into a three-day effort to get bananas to her the
next nights. He
was working full shifts through the era and drafted the legal letters that
fought for her unpaid wages. And the disengagement formulation itself appears
exactly once in the corpus, two years after the era, in an outburst she shut
down within two minutes — which reads as ending the fight, not withdrawing the
claim, but the claim is never repeated, corrected, or raised again. What she
felt may be real. As the master cause of her leaving, the record does not carry
it.

## The scene

Just after 1 AM on September 6, 2026, in the middle of an overnight fight,
Annie sent a burst of texts. They are the only place in the corpus where she
makes this claim in this form:

> "Why didn't you show this and be the partner i desperately needed. I would
> Come home after being with Libby from 8am-3:30pm. Straight to work until
> 10/11pm. COME HOME ignored. Not ask how my day way. I felt so fucking just
> alone FUCK"

Then: being asked why *he* had no clean clothes, dishes stacked up, "While I am
working 6/7 days a fucking week," "THAT'S THE SHIT THAT WILL TAKE A TOLL ON
SOMEONE." Forty-nine seconds later: "I'm Sorry." Then "Forget it." Then, by way
of explanation: "I took Benadryl for my stuffy nose."

That is the entire provenance of the claim as formulated — "ignored," "not ask
how my day," "take a toll." It never appears before that night in that form. It
never appears after. She shut the argument down within two minutes; nothing in
"I'm Sorry" / "Forget it" establishes a retraction of the underlying claim, and
this page does not treat it as one. What can be said is that the formulation
has exactly one surviving written instance, and she never raised it again.

## What she describes, and what the record says about it

**The schedule: corroborated.** Her own 2024 messages back the double shift:
"Now I'll be here 6 days a week and then Libby in mornings..: I don't want to
get worked again and pass out like last time" (May 8, 2024). "Guess I'm going to
go straight to work" (June 2). Restaurant shifts running to 10:30, 11. She
asked him, contemporaneously, to help with the house: "Since I am now working
6 days a week and working for Libby. I'm tired. So please help me out" (May 12).
Small deviations — the record says 6 days, not 6/7; Libby start times varied —
but the exhaustion she describes was real and documented in her own words, in
real time.

**"Not ask how my day way": literally true in writing.** In 40,338 messages
spanning August 2023 to August 2025, Dan never once texted "how was your day."
In the Libby window (February–August 2024), across 5,132 of his messages, he
asked a workday question twice: "How's work" (February 28) and "What are you
doing at Libby's today" (June 18). His day-inquiry rate was low in every era
measured — it is his disposition, not a 2024 departure — but the literal
complaint is textually true.

**The Libby topic specifically: he was slower there.** Her 97 Libby messages in
the window were answered within 30 minutes 42% of the time, against 71% for her
non-Libby messages. Her three heaviest disclosures — Libby's brain-cancer
diagnosis, "I think I'm dying," Libby depressed at the doctor's office — got no
text reply within 30 minutes. Her exhaustion vents drew perfunctory answers:
"Ok," "I'm v tired too," an emoji-only message, a topic change to the Amazon
delivery guy. The topic she most needed engagement on was the topic he engaged
least.

## What he did instead: the offers ledger

This is the new evidence, and it is the strongest counterweight in the file. A
systematic pass over all 5,132 of Dan's messages from February through August
2024 — every candidate manually reviewed, every count traced to a listed quote
— finds **137 messages** in which he offers to get, bring, buy, or do something
for her, reports having done it, or checks on her wellbeing with a concrete
action attached. That is roughly one every 1.6 days across the 213-day window,
concentrated in June and July when the late-night Libby routine was heaviest.

| month | explicit offers | executed favors | wellbeing check-ins | total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Feb 2024 | 1 | 3 | 0 | 4 |
| Mar 2024 | 7 | 4 | 0 | 11 |
| Apr 2024 | 2 | 4 | 1 | 7 |
| May 2024 | 4 | 7 | 0 | 11 |
| Jun 2024 | 29 | 14 | 0 | 43 |
| Jul 2024 | 25 | 15 | 8 | 48 |
| Aug 2024 | 4 | 9 | 0 | 13 |
| **Total** | **72** | **56** | **9** | **137** |

The number is deliberately conservative: 64 further messages of reciprocal
pickup logistics ("I'll get yours when I see him") were fenced out of the
headline count so no critic can dismiss the metric as weed runs.

The texture matters more than the total. "Do you want anything from the grocery
store" — three times, May through June. "Is there anything in particular you
would like me to bring you" (July 1). "Can I bring you something to eat"
(June 30). "I'm gonna go in a few mins I cam bring you a change of clothes"
(June 30). "How about I come bring you an umbrella (and a tin)" (May 30).
"Want me to cover your shift?" (May 26). "If I bring this over in the storm and
get you guac AND give you the $10 I have left over" (June 27). Flowers
(February 16). Gatorade (June 7). And twice, his own words confirm the routine
was nightly, not occasional: "I don't fucking care I ask you every single
night if you want me to bring it or not" (June 8), "Why are you going to be
like that to me? I volunteered to bring them!" (June 29).

## The banana arc: a worked example

July 15–17, 2024 is the pattern in miniature, and it happens to be the
"fruit stand" exchange — the only fruit-stand mention in the entire thread.

**July 15.** She is at Libby's; he waits outside for 90 minutes while she
struggles with a food order. He jokes: "You didn't even buy me a pollen
smoothie from downstairs."

**July 16.** She is shaky at work: "I'm so shaky I'm scared." He runs the
potassium protocol — "Stop and get some food," "Potassium," "Bananas" — and
offers: "If you need me to bring you anything let me know." She answers: "But
I didn't pass a fruit stand."

**July 17.** She asks: "Maybe get 4 bananas for later / Please e." He goes:
"You tell me what YOU want / And I can go to get you nanas and whatever else /
I'm fine I just want to get you some sustenance… I'm making my shells now and
then I'll come bring you bananas."

**July 18.** Still on it past midnight: "I'll go buy a cheap one and get your
nanas so I don't waste time." She waves it off: "Don't it's fine." Then, as
her shift ends: "If you can come meet me here. Bring banana."

**July 28.** Eleven days later he re-litigates it, contemporaneously
documenting the failure mode: "I would like a huge amount of credit for not
pointing out at the time — because I really did just want to calm things down
and didn't want to appear that I was trying to be right — that the night you
asked for bananas, you had sent me out later to get you food and drinks at the
beer depot and never mentioned the bananas or else I totally would have gotten
them at that point."

Note what this is and isn't. It is not the clean claim → refutation →
acknowledgment of family lore: in the text, *he* raises the banana point and
she never directly answers it, pivoting to his next question instead. What the
record does establish is narrower and better-sourced: the only reason the
bananas didn't arrive on the 17th was her redirect, he documented it without
rubbing it in, and he was still trying to get them the next night. A partner
who "ignores" her does not spend three days on bananas.

## Is the offers metric a fair measure of engagement?

Fair question, and the honest answer is: partially. It is a fair measure of
*expressed, other-directed, instrumental engagement* — the things a partner
does, unprompted, in the only channel fully preserved. 137 unprompted offers in
213 days is behaviorally inconsistent with "ignored" and with "there all day
doing nothing." It directly rebuts the strong version of her claim.

It is not a measure of *emotional attunement*, which is what her grievance is
actually about: "not ask how my day," "I felt so fucking just alone." A person
can receive fifty grocery-store offers and still feel alone if what she needed
was to be asked about her day and met with attention. The metric cannot see
in-person evenings, phone calls, or tone; unanswered and declined offers count
the same as completed ones; the June–July spike partly reflects message volume
and the late-night routine itself. And 137 is a floor, not a ceiling — keyword
sweeps miss phrasings.

So: the ledger kills the absolute version of the claim. It does not, by
itself, settle what she felt. Both can be true — he ran errands constantly,
and she felt alone anyway — and the article treats that as the live tension,
not a contradiction to be resolved by counting.

## What else cuts the other way

**He was not idle.** The "While you were there / All day" half of her
accusation places him at home while she worked. In July 2024 he was writing, in
the present tense, "I work at a Lebanese restaurant," "I was walking home from
work," "I showed this to my coworkers." His Au Za'atar tenure ran March 2021 to
August 2024, ending in an involuntary hour-cut. The at-home-all-day period is
real — it is the ~11 months of unemployment *after* August 2024. The employment
record disproves "idle at home throughout the Libby era"; it cannot adjudicate
what any particular day looked like, and the honest read is date displacement
of roughly twelve months, not proof he was never home midday.

**He fought for her wages.** In August 2024 Dan drafted all three escalating
unpaid-wages demand letters for her — dated correspondence, itemized
hours-at-$75/hour, an explicit statement of what remedy was not sought, a
final-notice escalation. This is the corpus's clearest instance of his forensic
register deployed for someone else's material benefit. A partner drafting legal
demands to recover her pay, in the middle of the era in question, is engaged
partnership labor — whatever the emotional register was at home.

**He was not ignoring her in general.** Median reply time to her messages in the
window: 0.7 minutes. 83.5% answered within 15 minutes. 5,132 messages to her
7,539. "Ignored" does not describe his texting behavior; it describes his
behavior on one topic. He also raised Libby himself, unprompted, seven times —
researched her identity, asked about her Libby day, coached her on the wage
fight — and described himself as "extra supportive and defensive about the stuff
with Libby."

**The chore specifics have thin contemporaneous backing.** The May 12 "please
help me out" message is real and contemporaneous — it is the one documented
household-support request of the era, and this page does not claim otherwise.
But the *specifics* of the September 2026 outburst — clean clothes, stacked
dishes — appear nowhere in 2024–2026 outside that outburst. And the laundry
grievance has a reversed history: in 2018 it was *Dan* complaining that *she*
didn't "keep up with your laundry," and in 2017 the disengagement accusation
also ran Dan→Annie: "I feel FAIRLY sure that's why you're so distant."

**The only surviving contemporaneous complaint about him in 2024 was
over-reactivity, not neglect.** August 6, 2024: "after I had gotten screamed at
by Libby you get mad over Amber taking me for ICECREAM." Possessiveness sits
strangely beside disengagement.

## His answer, that night

Dan did not deny the facts. "Even if ALL of that were true." "I am not saying
you're wrong." "I can and have taken accountability." His defense was
procedural: "I don't know how you can do what you did without even having one
conversation where you kinda let me know that i was being so egregious that it
would cost me the relationship." "I had NO clue that you would just abandon me
for being unengaged and abstinent or disrespectful."

The ledger backs the procedural point: no such conversation exists in the
written record. She never told him, in any message, that his engagement level
was costing him the relationship — not during the era, not in the thirteen
months after. The first he heard of it in this form was 1 AM on September 6,
2026, two years on, mid-fight.

## As the reason she left: not demonstrated

Two corrections to keep this honest. First, her outburst does not explicitly
say "this is why I left" — it says "THAT'S THE SHIT THAT WILL TAKE A TOLL ON
SOMEONE." It is Dan's response that connects it to losing the relationship, and
this page does not put words in her text that aren't there. Second, what
follows is her stated-reasons chronology, not a proof that the grievance played
no role — only that the record does not demonstrate it as the master cause:

1. **Before January 2025:** nothing. The record holds two logistics texts.
2. **August 2025 – August 2026: overwhelming self-blame.** "I take full
   accountability for how I have treated you Dan" (Aug 2025). "I am sorry that
   I have destroyed everything" (Dec 2025). "The start of all of this every
   single time is not because of you" (Dec 2025). "You didn't do anything to
   deserve the way I have treated you… I am the only one to blame for this… I
   do not deserve the unconditional love you have given me" (Feb 2026).
3. **She assigned the disengagement to herself.** August 2025: "I am sorry I
   have been present [sic]. Physically, mentally, emotionally. It is not okay
   and I recognize that and need to do better." Thirteen months before the
   September 2026 claim assigns disengagement to him.
4. **September 6, 2026:** the Libby outburst — the first and only him-blame on
   engagement grounds, in the formulation quoted above.

The reason-list does not evolve *from* disengagement *to* something else. It
evolves from sustained self-blame to a single retrospective him-blame
outburst. That does not falsify the grievance — remorse and grievance can
coexist — but it weakens any claim that Libby-era disengagement was her
consistently stated primary cause.

## The verdict

Was he disengaged during the Libby era? In the narrow sense she names — asking
about her day, meeting her work talk with attention in writing — **yes,
measurably**. Zero "how was your day" in two years of messages; her Libby
messages answered markedly slower; her heaviest disclosures met with no timely
text reply. In the broader behavioral sense — the offers ledger, the banana
arc, sub-minute median replies, the wage letters, unprompted engagement with
the Libby situation throughout — **no**: the record shows a partner offering
and delivering care roughly every other day in writing, across the whole era.

Does the grievance explain or justify her leaving? **Not as the master cause
or complete justification.** The formulation surfaces once, two years later,
never raised contemporaneously or in the intervening thirteen months; the
"ignored / idle all day" mechanism is contradicted by the offers ledger and by
his employment through August 2024; and her own stated reasons for the
preceding year were her conduct, not his absence. The grievance is plausible
accumulated context — an exhausted woman who felt alone even while errands got
run — but the held record does not demonstrate it as the reason the
relationship ended, or as a complete justification for how it ended.

"I felt so fucking just alone" is first-person testimony and cannot be
falsified; the feeling is taken at face value. But a feeling is not a causal
demonstration, and the record as it stands does not carry the weight of the
causal story built around it.

## Method appendix

Full mining reports (claims ledger, engagement metrics, justification
timeline, Dan's side and context, offers and favors extraction) are filed at
`~/workspace/libby-engagement/`. Key data boundaries: the 2024 Annie thread is
absent from the canonical iMessage CSV exports but present in the drive-sweep
gsheet fragment `imessages_2124702449_last6months` (40,338 rows, 2023-08-09 →
2025-08-19), which is the source of all Libby-window numbers here. The
offers/favors extraction (`offers-favors.md`) scanned all 5,132 Dan-sent
messages in the February–August 2024 window with three keyword sweeps plus
manual review of 360 candidates; 64 reciprocal pickup-logistics messages were
deliberately excluded from the headline 137. Dan-sent messages are entirely
absent from the export for September 2024 – February 2025 (a one-sided export
gap, not a quiet period). The complaint concerns in-person evenings, which no
text record can adjudicate — everything measured here is the text analogue.
"I felt so fucking just alone" is treated as unfalsifiable first-person
evidence, not tested.
