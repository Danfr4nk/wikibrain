# NYC, Second Era (2019–2025)

Six years and thirteen days, one address, one 41-month job, one
relationship in its last long form, one infidelity, one eviction he staged
himself. Dan's second New York City period ran **February 9, 2019 to
February 22, 2025** — it opens with a receipted move-prep binge and a
broker-placed lease, and it closes with a choreographed eviction and a
crash-landing in a house that was itself on the market. This is the era
of the longest stretch of adult stability in the record: the only
multi-year tenancy, the only long job, the last whole years of the Annie
relationship before its terminal phase.

It is also the wiki's **coverage-asymmetry exhibit, inverted**. The first
NYC era (2010–13) is documented by 764 dated tweets and nothing else — no
message corpus, no ledgers, no sensors — and its article names the
silence as the honest shape of the page. This era is documented to the
dollar (the Amazon move-prep ledger), to the message row (the held corpus
covers the whole run), to the shift (712 GPS-verified restaurant visits),
and to the verbatim affair transcript (4,882 Instagram records). If NYC-1
is the period told from outside the head, NYC-2 is the period told from
inside the phone. The comparison is a finding, not a lament: the record
is thick where the era was stable, which is the opposite of what anyone
would expect and worth understanding as a mechanism rather than an
accident. Stable years produce paper trails. Collapses don't keep
ledgers — until this one, which did.

And it is the page the wiki was missing. Until this article, the second
NYC era was the only major era of Dan's life without a dedicated period
page — its years lived inside the three-phase move, the 307 address page,
the restaurant entries, the collapse pages, but never held as one
container. The [[wiki/timeline/periods/nyc-first-era-2010-2013|NYC-1 page]]
names the asymmetry directly and points at this page's absence; this is
the article that closes it.

## The move in — dated to the day

The relocation is the best-dated move in the whole corpus, and it is
dated by three independent instruments that agree with each other. The
landing date is his: *"We moved to New York in February, like early
February, February 9th, 2019"* — spoken during the September 2026
receipt-reading sessions, his own dating of the move, given thirteen days
before the three-phase move carried it. The move-prep window is his too,
and it is the move's strangest artifact: *"Anything purchased from like
January 1st up until that date was like getting ready to move to New
York."* The Amazon ledger for that window holds **57 orders and
$4,757.24** — nearly half the year's entire Amazon total in roughly seven
weeks. The clusters read as a man assembling an apartment in advance of
arriving in it: a GoPro HERO7 Black on January 1 (the vlogging rig), a
Boosted Mini X at 3:06 AM on January 9 ($1,076.95), three more Bern helmets
on January 10, a 47-piece mechanic's tool set and a BLACK+DECKER drill in
the small hours of January 11–13, a longboard wall rack on January 25 —
the fleet mounted on a wall that didn't exist yet, in a city he hadn't
moved to. The 2019 spending analysis's verdict is the finding this page
restates: the duplicates stop looking compulsive and start looking like
planning. Two Mini X's is redundancy. Six helmets is a helmet for every
mode of riding. The drill, the tool set, the wall rack — the most rational
spending in either ledger, bought at 3 AM in bursts. (The complete
per-order log lives in the ledger at
`raw/self/amazon-2019-orders/amazon-2019-orders.md`; the ledger, not this
page, carries every order number.)

The lease is dated by Gmail, and it is a paper trail the corpus has never
had for any other move. The February 2019 leasing thread names the whole
cast: **Jim Blanchard** sent the listing video (CC'ing Annie from the
first message), handled the applications through the Bond NY office,
collected a **$2,300 agency fee**, vouched for Dan and Annie with the
landlord, and coordinated the March 2 move-in — the broker who placed
them, not merely the building's day-to-day man afterward. The check
amounts in the thread are stated outright: **$13,800 deposit, $11,500
rent**, to John Paci Jr. The landlord was **John Paci**, who owned and
personally managed the building for the address's entire six-year run —
the exterminator scheduler, the rent collector, the final creditor. The
address page's declared rent gap is closed: $2,450 on the last signed
lease, $2,700 on the 2024 renewal. The tenancy ran, in Dan's own words,
on the landlord's discretion throughout — *"as long as we sustain jobs
the lease would be open for us on a second year,"* *"so our landlord
won't have to deliberate about signing next years lease"* — the unpapered
address in the honest sense: papered enough to show up in Gmail, informal
enough that six years never produced a fully formal tenancy.

The third instrument is the message corpus itself. The first address rows
for the new apartment begin **February 26, 2019** (*"307 e 76th
street"*, outbound) and run through the first weeks of March as Dan hands
the address to a run of delivery contacts — Menore's supply operation and
Sam's cannabis deliveries both converging on *"307 apartment 17"*,
*"307 e 76th between 1 and 2,"* the full *"307 e. 76th street, New York,
ny 10021"* — until **March 7** he writes, *"That's our new apartment."*
The move-in the thread coordinated for March 2 has its landing by March
7. Three instruments — his voice, the receipts, the messages — date the
same move, and they agree.

What is not documented is the trigger. Nobody wrote down why *then* —
why February, why after nearly six years in a household that had held him
through the poverty floor of 2017 and the deep cycle of 2018. His framing
explains the purchases, not the decision. What the interlude incubated is
documented: the Annie relationship, begun Thanksgiving 2015, was the
vehicle — they moved north together. The GRIPNOTIC alias, developed in the
Uniontown basement years, arrived as the producer identity in the
luggage. Everything else — the shed, the board fleet, the six-year run —
was built in New York, not brought to it.

## The address — six years at 307

307 E 76th St, Upper East Side, apartment #17, was home for the entire
second New York chapter — the era's single address, which makes it the
longest continuous address in the record. Its full account lives at
[[wiki/places/307-e-76th-st]]; the period-level facts are the container
ones. The tenancy ran February 2019 to February 2025 under John Paci's
personal management — the man Annie said *"stuck his neck out for us and
held the apartment when we found it"* — with Jim Blanchard as the
unofficial super and rental broker on the ground: the man who met them on
move-in day, sent building-wide tenant notices under his own name,
coordinated Paci's access, scheduled water shutoffs and exterminator
visits, and referred to Paci as *"your landlord"* in the third person.
(The operator's 2026-08-10 capture had called him the *"world's worst
building super"*; the contemporaneous record — 259 dated messages across
two dyads, warm throughout — contradicts the superlative, and the wiki
carries the record's version. His own description, given to Suz on
2025-02-08: *"I was the quiet 'unofficial super' at my building and still
help tenants whenever I can."*)

The address is also the shared coordinate of the era's parallel delivery
operations: 76th Street between 1st and 2nd was Menore's primary service
coordinate for years, and Sam's cannabis deliveries ran to *"in front of
307"* — the residence and the supply zone the same building. And it is
the address behind two of the record's corrections: the September 2020
Train Plan crisis had a mundane cause (a roach-bombing evacuation), and
the landlord debt the Annie page once called "paid down" was still a full
$10,000 owed at move-out — rounded to the even figure in Paci's own March
5, 2025 settlement message.

## 2019: the obsession year

The first year is the move-prep thesis's other half: impulsivity and
planning share a timestamp, and 2019 is the year the thesis was proven
twice over. The two Dans of January — the one buying the drill and the
tool set and the wall rack, the one buying the sex toys in the same
month's clusters — are the same person executing the same unit of
purchase. The two-year synthesis names the cluster as the unit: something
gets decided, usually at night, and then it gets executed completely,
immediately, across as many carts as it takes. January 2019 is the
cluster at its largest scale.

Forty-five days after the move, the Shelbie sequence began — the
threesome on a weekend back in Pennsylvania, the infatuation, the April 18
refurbished MacBook ($793.94, *"I BOUGHT HER A MACBOOK LOL"*), the
$4,000-of-exotic-drugs trip, the car-keys walk-off that ended it
([[wiki/timeline/events/shelbie-annie-threesome-april-2019]]). The
obsession arrived before the address did, and the January receipts are its
packing list — that is the move chapter's finding, stated once and carried
here because the era's opening fact is that the pattern preceded the
geography. The 2019 move didn't start the obsession lifecycle. It gave it
a bigger container.

The same year holds the era's political conversion — the hard-left turn
of 2019, dated to the Bernie campaign and Chapo Trap House, the settled
anti-ethnostate, full-Palestinian-sovereignty position that the politics
record carries as his settled stance. And underneath both years runs the
figure he stated himself: the $450-a-day cocaine spend, *"through this
whole era"* — on the order of $164,000 a year, while unemployed, in a year
that also included a cross-state move. The three-phase move's observation
is the ledger's, not the wiki's, and it belongs here too: every one of
the era's events happened inside an economy of roughly $164,000 a year
that no receipt dataset can see. The geography moved; the architecture
didn't.

## 2020: the siege year

The pandemic year lands in the middle of the era's first stretch and
directly overlaps the documented market interlude. Three things happened
to the same person in the same twelve months: the lockdown, the Fran
estate paying out, and the retail-trading run. The COVID-era page carries
the year in full ([[wiki/timeline/periods/covid-era-2020]]); this page
holds the period-level facts.

The siege is testimony and the numbers are the check on it. Dan's
self-profile frames 2020 as a stress test of the "Metropolitan
Experiment" — the pandemic forcing him *"into a micro-radius siege."* The
instrument that used to quantify it (the GPS visit collapse) was retired
in 2026: the 2020–21 low visit counts are a device-change blackout from
2020-09-24 to 2021-07-20, not behavior. The instrument that stands is the
message corpus — the whole-corpus count for 2020 is **6,311**, labeled
"COVID collapse," against 20,153 in 2019 and 40,514 in 2018 — a 69%
year-over-year drop in communication volume, from a different export with
no device gap in it. It does not measure mobility. It measures contact,
and contact fell off a cliff. His June 7, 2020 real-time account dates
the siege from the inside: *"8pm curfew... it's been like living in
beirut"* — the George Floyd-protest curfew period in Manhattan, alongside
a still-undecided 2020 vote, unwilling to back Biden as of April.

2020 is the year the Fran estate actually pays, and the sequence is
unusually well dated because Rod Banks wrote each cheque and said so in
writing: five advances between February and June totaling **$32,500**
($10,000 on Feb 4, $7,000 on Mar 6, $3,000 on Mar 27, $7,500 on Apr 17,
$10,000 on Jun 23 — the last two with a live open-question discrepancy on
whether the $10,000 is one advance or two), then the August 21 court order
distributing **$144,069.31** and the September 1 landing of the final
cheque at **$134,069.31**, deposited via Suz. It is the biography's
largest documented single capital arrival, and it lands inside the era's
second year, three weeks before the market interlude opens.

The market era (September 2020–February 2021) is this period's
inheritance-funded trading run: Jerad's tip enters on the
`jfriedline@gma` chat — *'FSLY... Buy FSLY and do not touch for 2-3
years'* — Dan puts ~$15k into 112 shares, rides the meme-stock eruption
to a Robinhood balance around $25,000, loses about a third of it in a
single February session (*"But my $25k just turned into 17k"*), and comes
out with the stated thesis that cultural-trend sensing had overtaken
fundamentals in retail trading. The trade-level details verify against the
held corpus in thirteen verbatim rows
([`dat:0680-2020-2021-market-era-trades-verified`](../../../kb/data/0680-2020-2021-market-era-trades-verified.md)).
What matters at the era level is the placement: the trading fills the
window between the lockdown's contact collapse and the restaurant hire
sixteen months later — *"the money just ran out"* by early 2021, and the
Au Za'atar hire follows in March.

The year also carries the era's first household crisis of the documented
kind. On September 10, 2020, Dan told Annie the apartment was scheduled
for extermination — *"sorry to keep bugging you just wanted to give you an
update. they are bombing the apt tomorrow around 11 or so."* The couple
relocated to Annie's parents' home in Uniontown for roughly a week, and
that stay is the same trip during which the Train Plan crisis happened —
mid-argument, Dan simultaneously coordinating a second exterminator visit.
The roach bombing's mundane cause (the 2026-08-10 correction) is the kind
of detail the era's documentation density makes available: a crisis whose
cause would have been mystery in any earlier period is, here, a work
order.

## 2021–2024: the shed years

In March 2021, two years into the era, the money ran out and Dan walked
into the brand-new Au Za'atar Midtown location — twenty blocks south of
the 76th Street apartment — and talked owner-operator **Tarik Fallous**
into hiring him on the spot. Zero food-service experience, a bank account
at zero, straight into the equal front-of-house tip split he negotiated
on day one. The economics, confirmed by Dan in July 2026: $15 an hour
plus the tip pool, weekly Sunday checks of $800–1,000 for a six-day week.
That is the whole financial skeleton of the era's middle years.
Everything else — the apartment, the habits, the friendships, the affair —
hung on it.

The tenure is the longest job in the record by a wide margin — 41 months,
~690 shifts, 712 timestamped GPS visits — and its full account, people and
mechanics, lives on [[wiki/work/au-zaatar-41-months]]. The period-level
facts are the ones that make the era legible: the plywood COVID-era
"Shed" on the 58th Street sidewalk where service ran for most of the
tenure, the fictitious 9pm "night class" alibi held for eighteen months
(the era's scheduling instrument and, later, the Valeria cover story), the
Triad alliance of **Dimitri**, **Ismaila "DJ" Barry**, and Dan, and the
cast the restaurant produced and sorted — Manuel's Spanish-conducted
friendship that outlasted the job, Felipe's that died with the shed,
Lou Bahet's 1,602-message flare over 21 days, the kitchen crew's respect
he carried out.

Dimitri is the Greek manager and the holder of a distinction no one else
in the record approaches: *"the only boss or manager or supervisor who i
have ever actually really loved"* — the one loved authority figure in a
record otherwise processed through vertical-authority skepticism. He is
the positive case against the McKiernan-shaped template, and the
employment-block page's richest workplace data. Tarik is the employer case
that didn't fire: hired on a handshake, treated fairly, stayed warm to
April 2026, nearly two years past the job's end — the one boss where the
distrust template never activated, even while Dan ran his "decentralized
audit" (pocketing cash tips) against the owners' digital pool for the
whole tenure. Both truths on record; neither cancels the other.

A month after starting, Dan got Annie hired as hostess — at the original
East Village location, a separate restaurant from the Midtown shed. In a
turn Dan flags as *"truly weird,"* Annie — *"prim and proper...
reliability IS her strong suit and is my antithesis"* — was fired from that
hostess job with no notice and no reason, while Dan held the Midtown job
for years longer. The contrast sits in the record unexplained.

The job ended in two stages. First, the employer cut his hours through
June–August 2024 in a pattern Dan read in real time as maneuvering to
force a quit and dodge unemployment obligations (*"lowering my hours so
much that it's not even worth staying,"* August 6, 2024). He dropped
resumes from July 9 and filed for unemployment on August 8. The
DanAnnie-dossier amendments rule the loss **involuntary** — the fact that
reframes Annie's late-2024 "you never work" grievances as locally valid
but historically partial. Second, the territory itself died: the finished
interior opened in 2024, demand for the sidewalk tables died first, then
the city mandated the winter shed teardown, and that was that. His own
epitaph for the era: *"it was a long run and it was fun and good food and
cool mexicans and... valeria."*

What followed is only lightly documented: a mid-2025 AI-collaborative
session has Dan noting he'd been *"working for the first time since june
2024 for the last 2 weeks"* and about to hit a full 40-hour week — nearly
eleven months unemployed before returning to paid work, at an employer the
corpus doesn't name.

## Valeria — the affair the era is remembered by

**Valeria Iglesias Cid** was the Au Za'atar host-stand hostess — trained
as a hostess by Annie herself. Annie's text to Dan: *"hey i trained a
new."* Dan's framing, September 2026: *"the perfect handoff in the spirit
of Alexis>annie."* What follows is documented, not speculated.

The earliest dated evidence is an August 31, 2021 TikTok DM to Alexis, in
Dan's own words: *"I've got a new gf. She's 20 and an illegal
immigrant... I'm still with Annie but the Mexican girl is making a fast
ascent up the ranks."* Then a nine-month forensic absence — no recovered
messages September 2021 through April 2022, likely WhatsApp, no export
held. The affair peaked in person over winter 2021–22, covered by the
night-class alibi, and its message shadow is the May–June 2022 Instagram
burst: 4,882 records, with first ILYs — hers June 11, his June 13 —
*"the color has left my world,"* the Central Park kiss (*"Did u like my
kiss?"*), the basement hug, and Annie's name appearing zero times in all
4,882 records. Dan's own measure of it: *"would have literally moved to
chile for her i swear to god."* She is the one infidelity in the decade he
names as unauthorized. Her page ([[wiki/people/valeria-iglesias-cid]])
carries the full dated record, the 2026 re-contact, and the limits; this
page holds the era-level fact that the breach was the shed years'
private objective installed inside the employment — the same shape as the
tip split and the night class, three private sovereignties defended
inside one job.

## The Annie arc, 2019–2025

The Annie relationship entered the era in February 2019 and its last long
form runs the era's whole length. The beats inside the container, in
order: the Shelbie sequence, forty-five days after the move (April 2019) —
the threesome that became the infatuation that became the MacBook and the
$4,000 drug trip and the car-keys walk-off. The Train Plan crisis of
September 2020, inside the roach-bombing evacuation — a crisis the record
can date and cause because the era is documented. The 2021 hostess
hiring and unexplained firing. The Valeria winter of 2021–22, running on
the night-class alibi while the relationship ran on the surface — and her
name absent from 4,882 affair records. The concealed Eli affair's January
9, 2025 discovery: Annie found unconscious with the coworker in a building
lobby ~80 minutes after her shift, location sharing off
([[wiki/timeline/events/eli-incident]]).

Then the ending. After the affair came to light in January 2025, the
apartment went on a "shot clock": roughly **$10,000** already owed plus a
**$7,000 ConEd bill** — financial realities Dan said made continuing to
argue about staying *"not really even worth the time it would take to
debate."* The exit was not what it looked like: Dan called John Paci by
phone in January, and Paci performed a landlord eviction — what the
period record called a "forced exit" was half of an arrangement Dan
authored. Her move came first chronologically and downstream of his frame:
~**February 6, 2025**, her parents drove up to take the first load of
stuff to their house, two weeks before the exit ([[wiki/mind/synthesis/2025-move-chronology]]).
Dan's own account is that he never treated the affair as a "terminal blow"
— he proposed returning to Pennsylvania to regroup, offered to fund an
apartment anywhere she chose, and deliberately left the destination
"intentionally empty." Annie made a unilateral decision, without
discussing it with him first, to move in with her own parents, while
insisting this represented no change in the relationship's status. Dan
left the address, and New York, on **February 22, 2025**, landing alone at
his mother's house — the same 337 Saratoga Drive he had moved back into
in 2013, now with Suz's 2024 personal bankruptcy as the household's
standing condition and the house itself listed for sale the same week.
The relationship's terminal-phase record (August 2025 onward) sits
entirely in the Uniontown frame: the move did not reset the pattern, it
relocated it ([[wiki/mind/synthesis/dan-annie-fallout-verdict]],
[[wiki/timeline/periods/feb-apr-2025-return-and-rupture]]).

dormant, and the employment-block page's named gap is the honest form of
the claim: no intake obsession has been identified for 2021–23, and the
leading candidate is that the Shed itself absorbed the obsession slot.
The era is the producer-identity's negative space — the alias years
behind it, the reactivation ahead, the restaurant in between. What the era
got instead of output was the board fleet (ridden), the vlogging rig
(bought January 1, 2019, used to whatever degree the record shows), and
the regulars' favorite waiter. The ambition didn't die in New York this
time. It waited.

## The mirror: what two eras side by side show

The [[wiki/timeline/periods/nyc-first-era-2010-2013|first era]] is three
years, two apartments, the audio-career attempt, and no message corpus.
This era is six years, one address, the only long job, and the thickest
record in the wiki. The contrast is structural, not incidental. NYC-1's
move-prep is documented to the tweet and its financing is never named;
