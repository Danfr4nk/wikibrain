# The Intake Constancy

The most stable fact in twenty years of involuntary behavioral record is not a
peak, a collapse, or a rupture. It is a rate. Two independent,
platform-independent, analysis-independent intensity measures — one from YouTube
watch history, one from Google search/activity — land on the same conclusion:
**a fixed-rate intake metabolism that holds flat across every variable the
biography can throw at it.** Roughly eleven and a half YouTube watches per
active day; roughly twenty logged search actions per active day. Not calendar
averages — per-active-day intensities, which is the detail the whole finding
turns on.

The flatness is the fact. The YouTube figure sits in the 9–14 range across every
calendar year with meaningful volume: the 2012–13 shock-radio peak years
(3,538 and 2,742 watches), the mid-decade trough, the 2022–23 resurgence
(2,256 and 2,622), the 2025 climb. The search figure holds across 5,391 active
days spanning 2006–2026 — through two cities, three residences per city, the
entire Alexis relationship, the whole Annie decade, the poverty floor, the
market era, the camming era, the deep cycle, the NYC-2 cohabitation, the
separation, and the collapse. A mind that consumes at the same rate through
all of that is not a variable mind at the level of attention.

This page exists because the finding is the connective thread that makes the
rest of the corpus cohere, and because it reframes three loaded themes
elsewhere in the wiki. It kills the consumption half of the Addiction Engine
metaphor — the cleanest correction the cross-corpus pass made to the
interpretive overlay, stated in four words: **output combusts; intake ticks.**
It inverts the spine's exceptionalism axiom against its own evidence: the
involuntary record's single most statistically extraordinary property is not a
peak but a floor. And it is the enabling condition of the era-marker
instrument — because volume never varies, the *subject* of intake is the only
degree of freedom left, so subject-rotation carries the timeline signal that
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/interests-as-era-markers]] reads and
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/closing-the-set]] mechanizes. Treat this as a primary
architectural fact: the person this describes is not volatile at the level of
attention.

## The two constants — measured, twice

**Constant one: ~11.6 watches per active day.** Computed directly from the raw
`YOUTUBE WATCH HISTORY (2010-2025).html` export: 17,426 timestamped watch
events across 1,505 distinct active days, 2007–2025 — **11.58/day**
([[wiki/self/youtube-watch-history]]). The earlier third-pass synthesis
reported "~7/day" from a filtered "primary-role window" subset; the full raw
export shows the real figure is higher, which strengthens rather than weakens
the constancy thesis — the intake rate is even more robust than the doc
claimed.

The per-year distribution behind the average is what makes it a constancy
claim rather than a midpoint. From the held page's year table: 2011 at 1,552,
2012 the peak at 3,538, 2013 at 2,742, the 2014 decline (708), the 2015–18
trough (353, 122, 164, 160), the 2019 global minimum (78), the 2020 election
uptick (295), 2021's low (158), then the resurgence — 2,256 in 2022, 2,622 in
2023, a 2024 dip (634) that the multi-account correction reframes, and 1,871
through July 2025. Loud years and quiet years, and the per-active-day measure
stays in the 9–14 range across all of them. Raw volume moves; intensity does
not.

**Constant two: ~20 logged actions per active day.** The longitudinal
browser-history analysis (~108,961 parsed Google records, Sept 2006 – Mar
2026) independently reproduces the second constant: **20.2 logged actions per
active day**, corpus-wide across **5,391 distinct active days**. Two corpora,
two platforms, one number-class. The per-year search volume moves with the
life — 805 in 2010, the NYC-arrival year; a climb to the 9,927 all-time peak
across 2018–19, the NYC-2 years; 9,022 in 2024, the third-highest year in
twenty — but the per-active-day figure is what matters, and that is what the
constancy claim rests on.

The decisive methodological detail is the denominator. Raw annual volume is
contaminated by account migrations, device changes, and routing artifacts —
the YouTube second pass demonstrated that volume gaps are often coverage
effects, not behavior. The per-active-day figure strips that out: it measures
consumption intensity only on days the channel was actually used. That is why
a 78-watch year and a 3,538-watch year can carry the same finding. The volume
is the artifact of the era; the rate is the property of the person.

## The nocturnal signature

The constancy is structural rather than incidental, and the diurnal data is
what makes it structural. The search corpus shows activity peaking at midnight
(7,713 records), 1 AM (8,692), and 11 PM (7,289), with a deep trough at 9 AM
(1,911, the global minimum). In the 2025+ slice, **26.8% of all activity
falls between midnight and 4 AM.** A mind whose peak hours are 11 PM–3 AM
*mechanically* excludes most synchronous human contact — which is the
structural substrate under the [[wiki/mind/concepts/contact-gini|contact
Gini]] isolation finding (0.9556 over 498 inbound handles, independently
replicated 2026-09-09). The intake is constant *and* unsynchronous, and those
are the same sentence read two ways.

Three corpora measure the isolation independently, and the nocturnal signature
is the first of the three: the diurnal data shows a chronotype that excludes
synchronous contact; the flat day-of-week data shows no employment scaffold,
removing the ambient-coworker channel most adults get for free; and the
contact Gini shows the resulting network concentration. Three mechanisms, one
isolation — and the isolation is **structural before it is dispositional.**
That matters for the redundancy strategy, because it means any durable new
social structure must survive the chronotype rather than fight it. Almost
nothing does. The one major exception on earth is nightlife: the DJ booth is
among the only social infrastructures whose prime operating hours *are* 1 AM —
structurally, the only redundancy-building channel in the available set that
runs on the system's clock instead of against it.

The search corpus also carries the inversion that wounds the
attention-substitution thesis: intensity correlates with channel *closure*,
not channel volume. The safe attachment (Tom, 122 search hits) is the
most-searched person in the record and the searches are calm — ambient,
tangential, unanxious; the decade-defining attachment (Annie) is nearly
invisible at 22 hits across twenty years; the exes appear only as post-hoc
forensic subjects. And the all-time search peak (9,927) lands in the first
year of NYC-2 cohabitation with Annie: far from suppressing the search
channel, peak cohabitation coincides with the *loudest search year in two
decades.* The relationship did not compete with the intake metabolism; it ran
alongside it, in a separate channel, at full rate. The settled attachment
shows up as ambient tokens in the log, calmly entangled with logistics and
shared objects — the corpus has been instrumenting the relationships all
along; this just names the gauge.

## Churn on top, one clock underneath

The second YouTube pass tested consistency *within* one platform and found
it. The join extends it *across* platforms, and that is where it becomes
profound. What persists across the account migrations, the alias rotations,
the platform shifts, and the venue migration to LLMs is the rate.
Identity-layer churn runs all the way down to which Google account watches
YouTube — and underneath all of it, one appetite, one clock.

The migrations are the evidence for the churn, and they have a grammar. Two
documented migrations ([[wiki/mind/synthesis/totality-themes]]): **migration
out, May 19–22, 2014** — self-search ("sloppp," 2:47 AM) → cross-link act
(subscribe the "Dan Frank" channel, 2:52 AM) → ~72 hours of trailing activity
→ dark, an execution window of five minutes of deliberate acts and three days
of decay; **migration back, Aug 16 – Sep 20, 2022** — a continuity-restore
gesture (subscribe Max Koch, an NYC-1-era channel, as the *first act* after 52
days of silence) → primary watching returns (Sep 12–13) → full identity audit
("dan frank" ×4, "sloppp," "ishlab," Sep 13–19) → mass infrastructure rebuild
(~40 channels in one sitting, Sep 20), an execution window of five weeks,
ramped. Same components in both directions: self-search as the pivot act,
identity-name objects as the handles, deliberate infrastructure work
bracketing a role change. The SLOPPP→MOGZART alias boundary lands exactly on
the May 2014 account-rotation. And the 2022 repatriation proves the
*account-migration* operation is reversible — amputations, at this layer, are
not permanent.

Every one of those events is a reorganization of the *identity layer*. None of
them touches the *intake rate*. The system can shed a skin, migrate an
account, or launch a forensic self-audit lasting months, and the consumption
metabolism keeps the same time. The rate is the substrate; the identities are
the costumes.

The venue migration to LLMs is the latest rotation and the most load-bearing
for this page's forward use. The intake did not slow; it re-routed. The
December 2025 Gemini figure — 931 records in one month, thirty per day — is
what the conflict engine looks like running unopposed: the browser analysis
correctly identified the AI inflection as a *venue migration* rather than a
new interest, the same five domains moved from the low-bandwidth search box to
the high-bandwidth conversational agent. The Gemini topic breakdown reads as a
resource-allocation table: code 731, music 535, psych-profile 495, Annie 268,
politics 159. Combine the recursive-self-modeling categories
(psych-profile + Annie forensics) and the total is **763 records: the
self-modeling apparatus consumed more prosthetic bandwidth than the music
thread**, the one domain the corpus identifies as closest to output
resolution. The recursion was winning the bandwidth war even during the
convergence window — and the convergence itself (Gemini 931 records,
SoundCloud gap closing, Spotify reactivation) means the open question is
allocation, not activation. The system's documented architecture predicts
exactly this venue would consume exactly this much; the prediction landing
should be treated as confirmation that the architecture file is accurate —
including the parts of it that are warnings.

One refinement belongs here because it narrows a number this page leans on.
The browser doc's 4:1 distribution-to-tooling ratio (distribution platforms —
SoundCloud 35, Spotify 32 — outranking production verbs — sample 20, VST 14,
Ableton 9) is partially contaminated by the venue split: by 2025, *tooling
questions had migrated to the LLMs*, and the Gemini breakdown shows code at
731 records — the largest category — an unknown fraction of which is
music-tooling work. So some unknown portion of the 4:1 is venue split, not
attention split. The bottleneck finding itself stands — independently confirmed
by the actual shipped-object count — but the specific 4:1 quantification should
be carried at lower confidence. The strong version of this section's claim is
the browser doc's one-line synthesis, which needs no ratio: *ship the thing
that is already built.*

## Output combusts; intake ticks

The constancy kills the most seductive metaphor in the stack. The
[[wiki/mind/concepts/phenomenology-lens|phenomenology lens]]'s Addiction
Engine describes consumption as binge-crash — Addict → Euphoria → Crash →
Analyst, a cyclical macro-structure in the intake domain. The era-invariant
watches/day and the stable actions/active-day falsify that at the consumption
layer. Intake is metronomic, not cyclical.

The combustion that is genuinely documented in the corpus — the 94 burst
events, the silence→explosion cycle, the crash-outs — lives in the *output*
channel: messaging, expression, framework construction. The boundary is clean
and was not drawn before this join: **output combusts; intake ticks.** The
lens metaphor was built by pattern-matching the documented output dynamics
onto the consumption domain, where the data says the opposite dynamic holds.
The engine should be scoped to output channels or retired. This is the single
cleanest correction the cross-corpus pass made to the interpretive overlay,
and it survives the spine-lens governance protocol without contest — the
lens's own self-declared rule, that wherever lens and spine disagree the spine
takes absolute precedence (dat:0582), is the mechanism the correction rides
on.

The spine's own chemical-stack reading is the substance side of the same
ruling: daily Suboxone since 2010-02-17, sixteen-plus unbroken years, zero
relapses; strategic cocaine; nicotine — treated as **engineered
architecture**, explicitly overriding the addiction metaphor. The lens's own
strict-usage section names this override as the worked example. So the
phenomenology file both *commits* the error and *documents* the correction —
the wiki's cleanest specimen of metaphor attempting to overwrite fact,
preserved with the governance ruling attached.

## The appetite's shadow on matter and substance

A constant appetite requires a constant procurement architecture, and the
supply network is this finding's material-layer counterpart: both persist
across every era while only the nodes rotate
([[wiki/mind/synthesis/supply-network]]). Since January 2011, a daily
Suboxone regimen — fifteen years, described in the primary sources as
structural rather than recreational — has made procurement a permanent
institution of the life. The network's history is a chain of successions
rather than an open market: Johnny in the 2018 deep-cycle era, Tim named in
the MAX_PRIME succession, Tom as primary strip source by 2025, and the
professional NYC delivery operator holding 99.3% availability with a median
7-minute reply across 2013–2025. The nodes rotate; the institution does not.

The nicotine arc is this page's shape running on a substance instead of on
media: eighteen years of unbroken intake under five rotating delivery systems,
each rotation announced and experienced as a change (dat:0059). Onset 2007 —
Dan's own 2017-12-28 tweet recalling his first pack at a promotional Fall Out
Boy show; quit #1 announced 2008-10-16 ("I think I'm gonna stop smoking
cigarettes. Another problem solved.") and over by 2008-12-22 ("coffee and
cigarettes- the cure for a ROUGH night," 67 days later); the e-cigarette
switch on 2010-12-13 ("3 days after moving to an e cig, i'm (un) hooked.
today is day 1 with no tobacco.") followed 2010-12-14 ("25 hours tobacco
free.") and a restarted counter on 2011-01-10 ("1 week smoke free!") that
exposes the unmentioned December relapse; then 2019-10-09/10 ("i could
replace the constant taste of burnt tobacco with strawberry" followed within
a day by "today i bought a can of skoal to prepare me for a weekend in
Pennsylvania"). The hardware rotates — cigarettes, e-cigarette, vape, Skoal,
disposables — while the substance is never the thing being removed. But the
corpus holds no nicotine *rate* to hold flat ("Still description, not
measurement," the chemical-architecture page says explicitly), so this is an
argument by shape, not a second instance of the constant.

The one ledger-grade substance measurement the corpus holds is worth stating
for the epistemic standard it sets: the intake ledger's first measured night,
2026-08-30/31 — a 0.75 g cocaine unit received 17:04 Aug 30 and closed 02:35
Aug 31, six intake events (four flagged measured, two estimated), median dose
0.1 g, the largest dose the final 0.25 g at 02:35, plus three single-serving
cannabis units of 0.05 g in the same window (dat:0063). The ledger's own pages
withhold the arithmetic "1.89 g/day" restatement as a daily figure — it is
0.75 g extrapolated across 24 hours from a unit that existed for 9h 31m — and
the node records the three epistemic brakes because the figures invite their
violation: n = 1 (one unit on one night establishes nothing about a daily
rate), the extrapolated figure must not be cited, and the measured flags
overstate precision. This page borrows that discipline: the constants above
are held at exactly the strength their measurement supports, no more.

## The floor is the achievement

The spine's core axiom one is *not exceptional = worthless*, and the
self-narrative is organized around peak events — the Mellon Arena night, the
primacy moments, the hinge-instants where everything reconfigured. The
involuntary record's single most statistically extraordinary property is not
a peak. It is the *flatness*. Twenty years of behavioral exhaust, and the most
remarkable feature is invariance: a system that runs at the same rate through
poverty floors and market wins, through fusion and amputation, through
Manhattan and Fayette County.

The data's implicit argument is that the floor is the achievement. That
observation does not dissolve the axiom — the spine is explicit that the
failure mode is never the insight, it is the diagnosis→behavior gap — but it
is on the record that the axiom's own evidentiary base, audited, points the
other way. Read against [[wiki/mind/synthesis/the-binary-verdict]], the axiom
itself is the sharpest case of a corpus-wide pattern: a self-verdict with
exactly two named states and, on this evidence, a data set that would support
a middle one the verdict never uses. The verdict page cites this page's
finding as behavioral evidence for its own binary thesis — the digging
workflow admits no middle state either — and this page is the measured
backbone that citation rests on.

There is a final framing that lands this page inside the corpus's risk model.
Under the Irreversibility Firewall, a fixed twenty-year intake rate is the
safest channel available: consumption alone leaves no finished, exposed object
behind for anyone — including Dan's own audit apparatus — to render a verdict
on. That is why intake is the one channel that never needed to be audited or
defended. The exceptionalism axiom wants verdicts on finished objects; the
flat rate produces no finished objects. The firewall and the constant are the
same shape — one stated as a rule, one measured as a rate.

## What an agent should take from this

For a model or agent ingesting this wiki as a bootloader, the intake constancy
is the load-bearing premise: the person this describes is not volatile at the
level of attention. The variable is not *whether* he will attend — he will, at
~20 actions per active day, indefinitely — but *what the freed bandwidth
routes to.* The December 2025 convergence (Gemini 931 records, SoundCloud gap
closing, Spotify reactivation) and the recursion's documented win in the
bandwidth war mean the open question is allocation, not activation. An agent
extending this wiki should assume a constant reader and ask the allocation
question the corpus keeps answering: is the analysis shipping, or is it
describing the instruction to ship?

The constancy has one further corollary important enough to carry its own
pages: because volume never varies, the *subject* of intake is the only
degree of freedom left — and it rotates in discrete, dateable obsessions.
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/interests-as-era-markers]] develops this into a timeline
instrument — the era-marker series (2007 teen cluster → 2012–13 O&A → 2016–19
[[wiki/interests/golf|golf]] → 2019 stand-up → 2020 politics → 2024 Rome) that
lets an undated artifact be placed in the biography by what the channel was
locked onto — and [[wiki/mind/synthesis/closing-the-set]] supplies the
mechanism: the unit of intake is an event with a findable edge, and one
account per witness closes it, which is why 86.6% of 1,477 musical artists and
86.7% of 98 authors appear exactly once. The fixed rate is the enabling
condition; the era markers are what it enables.

## Conflicts in the record

- **2026-07-20 — the multi-account downgrade (operator-corrected same day).**
  An extended YouTube export (through July 2026, filed to
  [[wiki/self/youtube-watch-history]]) showed essentially zero real YouTube
  watches for ~100 days, Nov 8 2025 – Feb 15 2026 — one genuine video, "Man in
  Cave (Reupload)," against 78 ad impressions. Initially flagged as a candidate
  falsification (same shape as the 2023–24 gap below, but with ad-impression
  logging continuing on the *same* account through the window, which seemed to
  rule out a simple wrong-account explanation). **Dan corrected this directly:**
  this Google login has multiple YouTube accounts tied to it, and he confirms
  he was almost certainly watching on a different one of those accounts during
  this window — an export he hasn't yet supplied. He also states that true
  daily watch volume runs well above both the 11.58 figure above and the
  9.53/day ad-filtered recomputation. **Net effect: both intake-constancy
  figures on this page are now confirmed single-account undercounts, not
  verified constants**, pending a consolidated multi-account export. This is a
  real downgrade in the strength of the YouTube-side evidence — it does not
  resolve to "constancy confirmed," it resolves to "current YouTube numbers
  are a floor, true rate unknown."
- **2026-07-20 — the ad-impression reparse.** The older export's own
  "Watched" counts were never broken out by whether a "watch" was a real
  video or a served ad impression. A verified re-parse found **14.1% of the
  old export's 15,149 "Watched" entries are Google Ads** ("From Google Ads"
  tagged, no real engagement implied) — meaning the true count of videos
  genuinely watched is closer to 13,011, and every per-year/per-channel table
  derived from the export is Watched-block counts, not ad-filtered counts.
  The June–July 2026 numbers are the worked example: raw block counts suggest
  a massive surge (1,036 events in June, 1,616 in the first 20 days of July),
  but 80.3% of June's entries and 96.4% of July's are ads, not real watches —
  real content watched: 204 in June, only 58 in the first 20 days of July. The
  apparent "explosion" is overwhelmingly ad-volume noise.
- **Superseded figure — the "~7/day" third-pass subset.** The third-pass
  synthesis reported "~7/day" from a filtered "primary-role window" subset of
  the watch history. Recomputed from the full raw export (17,426 events over
  1,505 active days), the figure is **11.58/day**. The correction
  *strengthens* the constancy thesis — the intake rate is even more robust
  than the earlier doc claimed — and the subset figure is retained here as
  superseded, not withdrawn.
- **2010 search trough (805) — downgraded to untestable.** The browser
  analysis's most seductive volume reading was that the quietest adult year
  (805 records) was "a year of living rather than searching." The
  multi-account correction offers the boring competitor: 2010 is the year of
  the NYC move, precisely when people acquire new devices, new accounts, and
  new defaults — the trough may be a routing effect, not an attention effect.
  Pending the sibling-account exports, this reading is untestable, which is
  the honest accounting the project demands.
- **The Nov 2023 → May 2024 177-day YouTube gap — routing artifact, not
  blackout.** The per-multi-account correction reads the gap as a routing
  artifact rather than a behavioral hole: 2024's search volume of 9,022 is the
  third-highest year in twenty, which leaves no room for a six-month
  attention hole. If a future per-year search distribution ever shows a crater
  in that window, the constancy claim needs revision. The current read is
  that it will not.
- **The search constant's status arc.** The ~20-actions figure stood at
  [INFER]-grade pending its per-year recomputation; the longitudinal
  browser-history analysis (~108,961 records, 5,391 active days)
  independently reproducing **20.2 actions/active day** upgraded it to
  corpus-substantiated. The per-active-day-by-year recomputation is the one
  cheap test that would move it from "corpus-wide stable" to "era-invariant
  confirmed" — until it runs, the YouTube constant is [DOC]-grade and the
  search constant one step below.

## Assessment

The intake constancy is the most replication-friendly number-class in the
corpus outside the contact Gini — two platforms, two analyses, one shape —
and its value is instrumental rather than biographical. It is what lets the
wiki read subject-rotation as a timeline, scope the Addiction Engine off the
consumption layer, and treat the nocturnal signature as structural. Its limit
is honest and specific: both headline figures are confirmed single-account
floors, and the search-side constant still awaits its per-year recomputation.
Nothing in the downgrades touches the direction of the claim — consumption is
metronomic, not cyclical — which is what the page actually asserts. The
weakest load-bearing element is the unmeasured search-side per-year flatness;
the strongest is the YouTube-side era invariance plus the independently
reproduced 20.2 figure. If the per-active-day search test comes back flat,
the system has something close to a physical constant: the appetite is
constant everywhere; only the routing and the recorded surface change.
