# The Register Never Closes

[[wiki/mind/index|mind]]

Across every chemical register in the record, he has never closed one. Quits are substitutions; the single true zero — alcohol — is a categorical exclusion coded to betrayal, not a health decision.

The rule, stated plainly: the architecture has exactly two moves — substitute the delivery system, or exclude by verdict. Never taper-and-close.

## Controls and counterexamples

Alcohol is the control that bounds the rule — and it bounds it in the rule's favour. The one closed register closed by verdict: categorical exclusion, coded to betrayal, with no taper anywhere in its history. Closure exists in his repertoire only as a verdict, never as a taper. That is what proves the rule's actual shape.

Cannabis is the untested register: no quit-attempt history at all in the corpus, so the rule makes no claim there yet. A cannabis quit — in either direction — is a clean test.

The structural parallel is [[wiki/mind/synthesis/dormancy-not-exit]] run on chemistry: ties suspend and never disappear; chemicals substitute and never close. The same refusal of irreversibility, in a different register. Confidence: medium — structural parallel, asserted as such, not as a corpus claim.

## Prediction

The next announced "quit" will resolve to a substitution (new delivery system, same register) within 6 months of the announcement, and the announcement will use completion language first — "Another problem solved" (2008) is the template; the behavior follows or doesn't. Falsifiers of the strong form: a genuine taper-to-zero on Suboxone, or a nicotine-free interval exceeding 6 months with no substitute. Either breaks the rule as stated.

### The relapse that exists only in arithmetic

The 2011 row is the most load-bearing line in this page's evidence and it is
worth working out longhand.

Day one is **2010-12-13** by his own post. One week from day one is
**2010-12-20**. On **2011-01-10** he posts *"1 week smoke free!"*, which dates a
fresh day one to roughly **2011-01-03** **[DERIVED: date arithmetic on the two
posts; ATTESTED as posts, wiki/health/chemical-architecture]**.

So the December attempt broke somewhere between 2010-12-20 and 2011-01-03, and
**the relapse is nowhere in the archive** — it is visible only because a counter
restarted **[ATTESTED, that page]**.

Read as an experiment, this is the strongest single piece of evidence on the
page, and it cuts in an uncomfortable direction. A man posting daily to a public
feed broke a quit and produced **zero** direct statements about it. He is not
concealing: he is simply not posting failures, and an eighteen-year feed
preserved the failure anyway because a number carried its own history.

Now apply that to every other row in the table above. The rule says quits are
substitutions. The evidence for the rule is **announcements** — and announcements
are what a person types when something *changes*. A quit that succeeded quietly,
or a taper that ran without commentary, would leave the same trace as nothing at
all.

### The rule's evidence is delta-shaped, and that bounds it

This is the methodological caution the page has been missing, and
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/steady-state-invisibility]] names the mechanism: a message
or broadcast corpus records what changed, not what held, so the facts least
likely to appear are the stable ones.

Every bearing in the nicotine log is an event — an announcement, a purchase, a
supply failure, a complaint. Not one is a state. The same is true across the
whole rule:

| Register | What the record holds | Shape |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Nicotine | 2 announced quits, 5 hardware changes, 1 arithmetic relapse | all deltas |
| Suboxone | 1 dose figure, 1 appointment, 5 routing failures, 1 withdrawal | all deltas but the dose |
| Alcohol | a standing categorical exclusion | **a state — and it is the exception** |
| Cocaine | a pause, a resumption, a dose arc by self-report | deltas plus recalled states |
| Cannabis | nothing | — |

**[Row contents ATTESTED per the members table above and
wiki/health/suboxone-dose-curve; the shape column is INFERRED, applying the
delta/state distinction to each row.]**

The rule as stated — "exactly two moves: substitute, or exclude by verdict" —
is a claim about what happens at transitions. The corpus is *made* of
transitions. So the rule is being tested on precisely the data that can confirm
it and structurally cannot produce its counterexample: **a quiet taper-to-zero
would generate no announcements, no substitutions, and no record.** [INFERRED;
seams stated. This does not make the rule false — the substitutions that *are*
recorded are real — but it means the absence of a recorded taper is weaker
evidence than it reads as.]

The nicotine counter is the one place the corpus caught a state changing without
being told, and what it caught was a failure the subject never mentioned. That
is the page's own best argument for treating its silences carefully.

## The Suboxone null row, worked

The members table gives Suboxone one line: "No move at all." That line is the
rule's strangest case and it deserves its evidence.

Sixteen years of daily dosing and the corpus holds **exactly one dosage
figure** — 2mg/day, self-reported 2013-07-10, in a one-off overnight Facebook
exchange, never repeated **[ATTESTED,
[`dat:0055`](../../kb/data/0055-facebook-corroborates-the-2010-maintenance-start.md)]**.
Across the interval the record carries twelve dated bearings and one of them
carries a number **[ATTESTED, wiki/health/suboxone-dose-curve]**.

What that does to this page's claim:

**It supports the "no move" reading at the level of cadence.** The May 2026
supply failure — a supplier no-show followed by roughly 72 hours of withdrawal
while working a shift — is a functional proof that daily dosing was live
sixteen years and three months in **[ATTESTED, wiki/work/bfs-foods]**. An
intermittent or lapsed regimen does not produce that failure mode.

**It cannot support "no move" at the level of dose.** Thirteen years separate
the single figure from the present, and the dose curve is explicit about what
that silence is worth: *"A maintenance dose held for thirteen years and a
maintenance dose adjusted thirteen times with no record look identical in this
corpus"* **[ATTESTED, that page]**.

So the honest form of the Suboxone row is narrower than "no move at all": **no
announced move, no taper discussion anywhere in sixteen years, cadence
confirmed at both ends, quantity unobserved after 2013.** The rule survives on
the announcement evidence — there is no quit, no substitution, no verdict — and
it makes no claim about the number, because the record makes none.

There is a second-order point here that strengthens the rule rather than
weakening it. Every *other* register in the table produced announcements. The
nicotine row announced two quits; alcohol announced a verdict; cocaine's pause
and resumption are recalled as events. Suboxone announced nothing in sixteen
years — no quit, no attempt, no substitution offered. In a corpus this loud and
self-narrating, a register with **zero** moves of any kind is itself a finding
**[INFERRED; the corpus's loudness is attested across
wiki/mind/synthesis/self-deprecation-shield and the severance work]**.

## Why alcohol is the control and not the counterexample

Alcohol is the one closed register, and the page above says it bounds the rule
in the rule's favour. Spelling out why, because a closed register in a page
titled "the register never closes" is the first thing a sceptical reader will
reach for.

The rule does not say registers cannot close. It says **closure happens by
verdict, never by taper**. Alcohol closed at zero, for thirteen to fifteen
years, with active aversion, coded to family betrayal — four functional
alcoholics across three generations and the November 2005 rupture — rather than
to health **[ATTESTED, wiki/health/chemical-architecture]**. There is no taper
anywhere in its history.

That is a categorically different operation from the nicotine chain. A verdict
is a single irreversible act of exclusion; a taper is a graded withdrawal over
time. The record contains many of the first kind across domains and none of the
second in any chemical register.

Which connects the rule to the structural family it belongs to.
[[wiki/mind/concepts/no-delete-operation]] finds the same primitive across
cognition, protocol, strategy and archive: **write is cheap, revocation is
impossible, so control moves to the write gate** **[ATTESTED, that page]**. A
verdict is a write at the gate — it prevents the register from ever being
entered again. A taper would be a delete, and the system does not have one.
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/dormancy-not-exit]] is the relational instance, already
cited above; this page is the chemical one. Three pages, one primitive,
independently documented.

## The cocaine row: what the rule does not track

The members table gives cocaine one line — "Suspension, not closure. Paused
2007–08, resumed 2012." That row is where the rule's scope gets defined, because
it is the register whose *magnitude* moved furthest while the rule registered
nothing.

The self-reported arc: roughly **1g/day (2006–16)**, rising to **3.5–7g/day
(2017–20, the inheritance peak)**, falling back to **~0.5–1g/day (2020
onward)** **[ATTESTED as self-report, wiki/health/cocaine; the only measured
datum is 0.75g over 9h31m on 2026-08-30, n=1, and it is not a daily rate]**.

That is a swing of roughly sevenfold up and then sevenfold back down, tracking
an inheritance arriving and being spent, and **the rule has nothing to say about
any of it.** No substitution occurred. No verdict was issued. The register
neither closed nor changed delivery system; it changed volume, and volume is not
one of the rule's two moves.

Two things follow, and they pull in opposite directions.

**It bounds the rule's claim, narrowly.** "The architecture has exactly two
moves" is a claim about *closure operations*, not about the behaviour of a live
register. A register can vary by an order of magnitude, respond to money, and
stay entirely inside the rule. Anyone citing this page for "nothing changes"
is citing it wrong: a great deal changes, and none of it is a move in the rule's
sense.

**It strengthens the rule where it counts.** The 2017–20 window is the single
best natural experiment in the record — a large exogenous shock (money) applied
to a live register over three years. If the architecture had a taper operation,
the drawdown from 3.5–7g/day back to ~0.5–1g/day is where it would appear. It
does not appear as a taper. It appears as the money running out
**[INFERRED: the co-timing of the drawdown with the inheritance window is
attested on wiki/health/cocaine; reading the fall as supply-driven rather than
decision-driven is this page's inference, and the record does not carry a
stated decision either way]**.

The 2007–08 pause is the same shape at smaller scale. A pause that resumes four
years later is not a closure that failed; it is the register staying open with
nothing flowing through it — which is exactly the dormancy structure
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/dormancy-not-exit]] documents on ties. Four years of zero
did not close the register, and the resumption in 2012 needed no re-decision.

## Falsifiers, restated with what would satisfy them

The prediction above names two. Both need a measurement standard, because the
rule's whole difficulty is that its counterexample is quiet.

1. **A genuine taper-to-zero on Suboxone.** Satisfied by: two or more dated dose
   figures descending to zero, from any channel. Not satisfied by: an announced
   intention, or an absence of mentions. The corpus currently holds one dose
   figure in sixteen years, so this falsifier is not reachable with the present
   instruments **[ATTESTED, wiki/health/suboxone-dose-curve]**.
2. **A nicotine-free interval exceeding six months with no substitute.**
   Satisfied by: a dated gap in the log above with no delivery system entering.
   The longest untested gap is **2011–2015**, where the archive is a fetch
   artefact and absence proves nothing **[ATTESTED,
   wiki/health/chemical-architecture]**.
3. **A cannabis quit in either direction** — still the cleanest available test,
   and still untested.
4. **New, and the cheapest of the four: a register closing by taper in any
   domain, chemical or not.** The rule's claim is about the *shape* of closure.
   One documented graded withdrawal — from a commitment, a habit, a
   relationship — would show the taper operation exists in the repertoire and
   is merely unused on chemistry.
