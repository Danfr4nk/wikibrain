# Two Phases of Dance-Only Listening

Dan's listening history contains two periods in which he listened to dance music exclusively — and the periodization is his own **[OPERATOR]**, commissioned 2026-09-11: the 2013 SLOPPP trap phase, and the 2025–current re-entry. The 2026-09-11 playlist forensics **[DERIVED]** corroborate both the switch and the shape of each phase, and they date the second one to the track. What the forensics add is the mechanism of the switch: in 2013 production led and listening followed; in 2025 listening led and production followed months later. The decade between them is the fallow one — and it is, almost exactly, the Annie decade.

This pass (2026-09-12) verified every number below against the primary record, closed the SLOPPP close-date tension the first draft preserved as a gap, and sharpened the pre-2013 onset with the Electric Zoo 2010 material the draft missed.

## Phase 0: before the switch — Electric Zoo 2010

The first draft left pre-2013 listening as an open gap: what did the dance-only switch displace? The answer turns out to be that 2013 was not the *onset* of the dance interest at all — it was the moment production committed to it.

The public record dates bass culture's entry to **2010**: the Uniontown → Brooklyn move, Electric Zoo 2010 (photographed), Ishlab Studio — "when bass culture enters the public record" per the wiki's own geographic layering. The 2010 Twitter layer shows the indie and electronic vocabularies running **side by side rather than in sequence**: Grizzly Bear and Arcade Fire in the same months as Diplo, Electric Zoo, Ableton warping, and **a published dubstep mix**. What the 2012–2014 likes migration (ODESZA, JAUZ, Borgore) dates, then, is *displacement* — the electronic vocabulary pushing the indie one out of the listening share — not onset. The 2013 switch was a producer picking up a controller, not a listener discovering a genre.

The controller itself has a dated origin: a DJ controller acquired at Ishlab, never touched, picked up in the depressive interval after the failed first New York attempt — and "a daily occupation by the summer of 2013." The Numark NS7, the hardware that would seed the production identity ([[wiki/people/fran-coldren|gifted by Fran Whyel]]), was bought on **November 10, 2013** per the Twitter record. So phase 1's true shape: the listening was already electronic by 2010; 2013 is when the making started.

## Phase 1: 2013–2016, the SLOPPP trap phase

The first dance-only phase is production-led. [[wiki/interests/music/aliases/sloppp|SLOPPP]] is Dan's earliest confirmed production alias, dated 2013-01-01 to 2016-03-07 on its page, the signature project of the Uniontown-return period — the recording-arts ambition surviving the NYC-career failure by migrating to a Uniontown SoundCloud in 2013. (The 2013-01-01 start is a placeholder: the Twitter archive's source changes on August 17, 2013, so everything before that date is invisible and the true start is not datable from the record. The alias "emerges in mid-2013" is the honest statement.)

The operating record is unusually complete for an early alias, and the 2014 slice was read tweet-by-tweet rather than counted. In twelve months SLOPPP:

- **Released roughly monthly** — some thirty named releases between August 13 and December 26, 2013 alone (sixteen of them in September 2013, three on September 28), ~10k self-reported SoundCloud plays, free downloads tracklisted and timestamped.
- **Ran two recurring series** — *Week in Trap* (at least eight episodes) and the *Heatwave* summer mixes — plus a monthly wrap-up format (*Wax, Stacks and Racks*).
- **Competed**: the Major Lazer "Aerosol Can" flip went into Beatport contest voting, and he canvassed for votes twice (April 22 and 29).
- **Solicited collaboration in public, with a business address**: *"Lets collab! danfr4nk@icloud.com for stems and patch trades"* (May 8, 2014); emailed tracks to @SirCalvinSwish twice; signed up to Splice (August 5); requested stems from @weareGTA.
- **Broadcast live**: Ustream sessions in November and December (*"Turn up is live. 4am twerk life"*), the TRAP TV video mix.
- **Drew named peer support**: thanking @cobrakrames and @whatdirt, a shout-out from Lil Debbie (May 17).
- **Was physically invested**: *"So many hours DJing that I need daily inversion table therapy"* (May 17), a hand-painted MIDI controller (December 1).

And he placed himself without illusion. On September 23, 2013, announcing a mix and an original at the exact center of the project's most productive month: *"#overcompensating #poseur #fakefuture."* On April 25, 2014, on the Aoki pre-recorded-set scandal: *"as a 'nobody' DJ, the aoki scandal is extra annoying"* — a working practitioner's complaint about professional standards, made by someone placing himself at the bottom of the field without pretending he is anywhere else.

The alias also made **three documented attempts to convert itself into live bookings**: Zachariah Harshman's January 2014 pitch to a Pittsburgh rave-scene contact (the earliest); Dan's own July 6, 2014 outreach to Pittsburgh DJ Phil Lacher — *"I spin a lot of trap, deep house"* (deep house named in 2014, two years before the alias closed); and the March 2015 Frank Swaney near-management deal, the most serious of the three — a Pittsburgh promoter offering a flat-fee management package (500 demo CDs, 3,000 stickers, a street team, two named tentative bookings: an "Illuminati" event and a "Homegrown" show in Braddock), with contract terms to be worked out through a third party, "Joby," **talking to Dan's parents** — the only documented instance of his parents being brought into a professional negotiation over his music. No trace of the deal survives past March 2015.

The public archive measures the phase's heat exactly: **23.4% of his 2013 originals and 31.6% of his 2014 originals** touched production vocabulary — then 4 of 62 in 2015, 3 of 75 in 2016. The production identity ran hot for two years, decayed for two more, and stopped.

The close is now dated, not disputed. The first draft preserved a tension — the alias page's range ending 2016-03-07 while its connections said the December 26, 2015 "Goodbye Demo" was the close. **Resolved**: December 26, 2015 is the last *release*; March 7, 2016 is the last *public appearance of the name* — a link to existing catalogue (`soundcloud.com/sloppp/juicy-j-hella-bandz-sloppp-bootleg`), posted to a correspondent, pointing at old output rather than new. Release date vs. retirement date. The "Goodbye Demo" itself landed four weeks after he met Annie, as the MOGZART identity (2014–16, overlapping) took over as primary.

One more dated data point sits just past the close: **February 7, 2016** — *"Had lit times playing @ulmdub birthday party last night at OSU"* (tweet id 696416822011289601, video attached, fetched 2026-09-02 after the wiki had leaned toward treating it as fabricated). A real live set at Ohio State, five weeks after the Goodbye Demo. The archive's 2016 turned out to be incomplete, which is its own lesson about the instrument — but the gig does not move the phase boundary; it is the last ember, not a continuation.

The listening correlate of phase 1 survives in the 5,321-track historical dump. Its top-artist roster reads as a trap/festival-era signature: `trap sounds` 162, Diplo 39, Yellow Claw 34, Major Lazer 29, Tropkillaz 23, Hucci 22, Lil Uzi Vert 21, Jack Ü 20, Keys N Krates 20, RL Grime 20, Skrillex 19, Troyboi 17 **[DERIVED — HIGH on the counts; the "2014–2017 trap/festival era" reading is MED]**. The dump is the archive of everything, not a phase-1 playlist — but its center of gravity is the era Dan was producing trap in, which is consistent with his framing that the listening of those years was dance-only. Production and listening were aligned on the same genre, under the same alias, in the same Uniontown rooms.

Phase 1 predates Annie (November 2015). The trap phase belongs to the pre-relationship life.

## The ten-year silence, measured

Between March 7, 2016 and March 4, 2026, Dan mentioned his own music-making **not once in 908 public originals** — and 2024, the archive's peak posting year (258 originals), is inside that window. He was posting more than ever, about politics, and nothing about the thing his pages call his production identity. The private corpus agrees: across 98,056 of his own messages, one message about making a beat, zero about being in a studio.

The wiki's own reading of this has been corrected twice, and both corrections belong here because they change what "fallow" means. The first correction: silence in the message corpus is evidence about the *channel*, not the activity — GRIPNOTIC is documented as developing through exactly these years ("Uniontown basement. Gripnotic developing."), and the basement studio at 337 Saratoga is real. What the archive establishes is that between 2016 and 2026 he did not *tell anyone publicly* that he was making music. The work may have continued; the announcing of it did not. The second correction: the failure-to-launch verdict was narrowed to its sharp form — not "he never really did it" but **"he did run it as an enterprise, for about a year, and then stopped."** The fallow decade is a decade of not-announcing laid over a basement that never fully went quiet.

## The fallow decade: 2015–2025

The Annie decade (November 2015–August 2026) is, near-exactly, the fallow decade for dance-world participation. This is not the claim that he stopped making music — basement GRIPNOTIC development ran 2015–19 — but that the development was *solitary*: production without scene participation, the inverse of what the 2025 re-entry would do. The alias churn of the period (SLOPPP → MOGZART → GRIPNOTIC, with Mogged Up as a concurrent bootleg imprint rather than a sequential identity) tracks experimentation and consolidation, but it happened in a basement, not a scene.

The alias's tail ran under a second name. MOGZART (2014–16) overlaps SLOPPP's last year as primary production identity: a Juicy J "Hella Bandz" bootleg that did 1,661 plays with zero promotion — the only documented streaming number for the whole mid-decade — and, per the alias page, the shift "toward bass music production" that the Goodbye Demo closed. The trap phase's last gasp was already a rebrand in motion.

[[wiki/interests/music/aliases/gripnotic|GRIPNOTIC]]'s output type is itself a data point for this entry's frame: per its own page, "remixes and AI voice drops" — AI-generated spoken intros and drops, never sung original lyrics. That is a thirteen-year, four-alias pattern of working in forms that never require writing or singing words as meaning-bearing content — exactly what a producer who hears sung lyrics as timbre would be expected to gravitate toward as a *maker*, not just a listener. The lyric qualifier runs on both sides of the decks.

The curatorial record shows the same shape. Monthly Spotify playlists run 2011–2020, then jump to 2025 — the 2021–24 gap is the Au Za'atar employment block (March 2021–August 2024, 41 months), the longest job of his life swallowing the curatorial habit whole. The liked-songs timeline is starker: roughly 88 likes across the entire seven years 2017–2023 **[DERIVED — HIGH]**. The hand that now likes 600 tracks in a month was, for most of the Annie decade, not reaching for the button at all.

## The hinge: October 28, 2025

Three days before the first crate-build wave, Dan described the state of things to [[wiki/people/tom|Tom Maison]] in his own words **[OPERATOR]**: he had gone "roughly seven years without caring about music" before recently picking dance-music production back up — *"i swear i forget how to produce music... i can not for the life of me remember how to actually throw a song together"* — and was finding the genre's build/drop structure ("intro, build, drop reset, build, drop") harder to internalize than the verse-chorus songwriting he grew up on. Then the thesis sentence: *"dance music is literally not the same as the music i have encoded in my brain. it is literally 98% about evoking emotional response rather than crafting something catchy."*

Read against the forensics, the self-report needs one qualification and it strengthens the entry's mechanism rather than weakening it. By October 28 he had already liked 270 tracks in September and 153 in October — the *listening* re-entry was two months underway. What he is describing as lost is the *production* capacity specifically: the hands forgot what the ears had already re-learned. Listening rebuilt first; the alias would come back for production a season later. The entry's phase-2 mechanism — listening-led, production-follows — is his own account of it, dated three days before the crate build began.

## The switch: 2025, dated to the track

The re-entry is listening-led, and the forensics date its mechanism precisely. Two bulk-like sessions in autumn 2025 — **September 29 (191 tracks) and November 8 (286 tracks)** among the purged set — followed by a purge: 498 URIs present in the oldest Liked export are absent from both June 2026 exports **[DERIVED — HIGH on counts and dates]**. (Total likes on those dates ran higher — 192 and 516 — but the 191/286 are the session content that defines the event: bulk-liked, then removed.) The purged content is the old canon: Elliott Smith (8), Paramore (6), All Time Low (5), Lana Del Rey (4), Bright Eyes (4), R.A. The Rugged Man (4), Tyler, the Creator, Pixies, Hey Monday, Atmosphere — emo, pop-punk, indie, alt-rap, the pre-dance taste. The September 29 session coincides with a `LIKED SONGS 29SEP25` Spotify playlist, a snapshot taken the same day. The reading — bulk nostalgia-like, then deliberate purge — is **MED-HIGH**: the timestamps and the disappearance both point there, and under the lyric qualifier it is suggestive that the one batch containing his stated lyricist fandoms was the batch removed from the working library while the bass canon stayed. The fandom lives in memory; the working library is timbre-first.

Then the build. The 2025 MASTER CRATE (232 tracks, full Spotify audio features) was assembled in four dated waves: **October 31 (170) → November 21 (+45) → December 9 (+16) → December 22 (+1)** **[DERIVED — HIGH]**. The liked timeline around it: 151 likes in May 2025, 122 in July, 270 in September, 153 in October, **628 in November**, then 58 in December — roughly 1,417 likes in 2025 against ~88 in the preceding seven years combined. The November spike is the crate-build month: of the crate tracks that appear in his liked set, 172 of 209 were liked in November 2025. The hear → Like → promote pipeline measures at **91.3%** liked-to-crate (209/229) on direct URI matching — the crate is a near-total selection *from* his listening, filtered for dancefloor function. The 20 crate tracks never liked are almost all post-November-11 additions: the workflow changed mid-build from selection-from-liked to direct-to-crate. Two removals between the November snapshot and the final crate: HOOLIGANS (version swap, the Ghastly-featuring version kept) and ELECTRIC LOVE (AVELLO — cut outright, the only melodic-bass removal; the genre barely exists in the crate otherwise).

The late-64 additions shifted the crate's profile measurably: energy 0.808 vs 0.849 for the first 170, tempo 130 vs 133, valence 0.30 vs 0.331, and the genre lean moved housier (bass house 17, tech house 12, stutter house 10 — the Fred again.. cluster) against the first 170's dubstep dominance (54). The crate's tail is where the stutter-house wing lives. The nine VIP crates (FINDERS_FEE, MaxOut, ULTRA_DEMi, GOODBYE_BPD_GIRLZ, FALL_2025_MEGAMIX, ALPHABET_BOYS_JAN26, flat_circle_mix, what_huh, FATSO_FLATSO) plus the 489-row MASTER_COLLECTED_CRATES super-crate extend the same three rooms into 2026 intake — two of them (GOODBYE_BPD_GIRLZ, FATSO_FLATSO) are dedicated liquid-dnb boxes, the dnb wing with its own crates, everything else working the 130–140 corridor. An Exportify session on December 29, 2025, 8:54 PM (via Chrome history) exported the crate mid-build: the re-entry was already being archived as data while it was still happening.

The VIP crates are the intake system's organs, each with a job. MaxOut (20 rows) is the brightest of the set (valence 0.446) and the one that admits the lyricist gods — JPEGMAFIA x3, plus $uicideboy$ and Scarlxrd — flow-timbre admitted as texture, not as message. Spotify's own yearlist gives the cleanest statement of what the crate *isn't*: Your_Top_Songs_2025_b overlaps the crate on only 45 of 100 tracks but overlaps liked on 89. The crate is not his most-played — it is a DJ-functional selection from what he played. The crate is the instrument; the yearlist is the listening; he doesn't confuse them **[DERIVED — MED on the reading]**.

The re-entry lands exactly on the 2025 collapse — February–April 2025: the NYC exit, Annie's move to her parents — per Dan's own timeline confirmation, 2026-09-11 **[OPERATOR]**. The dance-only switch of phase 2 is not a hobby resuming; it is a life reorganizing, with the record box as the first rebuilt structure. And then production followed: the [[wiki/interests/music/aliases/mogzart|MOGZART]] DnB jump-up revival on March 4, 2026 ("Odd Mob – Never Alone (feat. Listening rebuilt first; the alias came back for the second act. ## What the re-entry taste is

The filter stack, from the forensics **[DERIVED — HIGH on all counts; glosses MED]**:

- **Energy is the load-bearing filter.** Median 0.836, Q1 0.704 — three-quarters of the crate above 0.70, genre sprawling across 30+ tags. Remove the energy floor and the genre coherence dissolves.
- **Three BPM rooms, not one playlist.** 146 of 232 tracks (63%) in the 125–145 house corridor, a 19-track 165+ dnb wing, a 15-track sub-100 halftime basement. Three DJ sets.
- **The voice is an instrument.** 96.1% of the crate carries no foregrounded vocal performance; the largest segment (47.4%) is voice-as-texture — chops, samples, one-liners. Under the lyric qualifier this is not a paradox but a prediction: a man who hears lyrics as timbre and names [[wiki/interests/favorites/music/artists/jpegmafia|JPEGMAFIA]], Kanye West, and Elliott Smith as his gods is worshipping delivery, flow, cadence — vocal instruments — and the crate is the same appetite in the DJ room. His love of lyricists was never about bars; the crate proves it by containing almost none. The qualifier is corroborated, not just asserted: to a hostile third party in late summer 2025 he stated it flat — *"I hear melodies, harmonies. I don't hear the narrative or the topic of the lyrical content at all"* — roughly a year before the direct 2026-07-14 capture this page's frame is built on. And the gods do appear in the box, once: JPEGMAFIA x3 in the MaxOut VIP crate — flow-timbre admitted as texture, not as message.
- **Dark, mid-fame, disloyal.** Median valence 0.322 (heavy, not happy); median Spotify popularity 43 (neither obscurity-mining nor chart-chasing); 321 artists and 162 labels with none above 2.6% — he follows the scene, not names or imprints. Acousticness median 0.019: fully electronic, no organic instruments.

The crate is **ammunition, not canon** (MED): 59.5% 2025 releases, 70.7% 2024–2025, zero exact track overlap with the 5,321-track historical dump, only 16 liked∩dump. The selection function is *this season's* box. Against that tightness, the two strangest entries are load-bearing precisely because they are unexplained: Shostakovich's "Jazz Suite No. 2: VI. Waltz 2" (energy 0.184, acousticness 0.984 — a classical waltz in a bass-house crate) and Frank Ocean's "Thinkin Bout You" (energy 0.329 — slow, sung, sincere, three things the box otherwise never does). A crate this tightly filtered doesn't admit accidents; whatever function those two serve is part of the taste, not noise. Dan has not yet said what it is.

## The two phases, held together

Phase 1 (2013–2016): production-led, trap, Uniontown rooms, pre-Annie — listening and making aligned under one alias, with the listening already electronic since 2010. Phase 2 (2025–current): listening-led, bass-house/dnb, built in seven weeks on the floor of the 2025 collapse — listening rebuilt first, the alias revived for production a season later. Between them, the fallow decade: solitary basement production, the Au Za'atar curatorial gap, 88 likes in seven years, ten years of not-announcing across 908 public originals. The sub-bass signature (63–85%) runs under all of it — the production constant across the listening rupture, present already in the 2013 SLOPPP material, which is what proves it was never chosen under any later name. The dance-only phases bookend the relationship: one before it, one after its effective end — and MOGZART, the name, marks both ends.

A note on the clock. The wiki's overview once framed the four aliases as four periods *and* four aesthetics; the alias-as-periodization page's re-check moved the premise — read against each other, the aesthetics do not turn over at the renames, only the periods do, which makes the alias a clock rather than a style. These two listening phases are the listening-side proof of the same correction: the sub-bass band and the timbre-first listening run under both phases and the fallow decade between them, continuous and unchosen; what turns over is the period — what the decks hold, which life the music belongs to. The invariant is involuntary; the clock is the biography.

## Gaps

- The **three exceptions** to the lyric qualifier — the ~3 songs in his life whose lyrics he parsed as conceptual message — are still unnamed. When named, the analysis wants what broke the timbre-lock, not what the lyrics meant.
- The **Shostakovich waltz and "Thinkin Bout You"**: set-closer joke, genuine left turn, private function — Dan confirms or kills.
- **Pre-2013 onset** (sharpened, not closed): Electric Zoo 2010, the 2010 dubstep mix, and the 2012–2014 likes migration date the electronic vocabulary's arrival and its displacement of the indie one — but what the listening *share* actually looked like year by year before 2013 is unmeasured, and the phase-1 "exclusively" claim remains Dan's framing **[OPERATOR]**, not a measured fact. The dump corroborates trap/festival dominance but cannot prove exclusivity.
- **Phase-1 exclusivity**: "only listening to dance music" in 2013–2016 is Dan's periodization; the record supports dominance, not exclusivity. The claim stands as his framing.
- **The OSU gig's context**: the February 7, 2016 set is confirmed real (tweet id 696416822011289601, video attached), but the venue arrangement, the crowd, and whether other undocumented live sets exist in 2015–2016 are unknown. The archive's incompleteness for 2016 means the ten-year silence's boundary rests on retrieval that is now known to be imperfect.
- **The GRIPNOTIC basement catalog**: what, if anything, was finished 2016–2026 off-record — a distributor account, an unreleased folder — is answerable by the operator in one sentence and remains unanswered.
