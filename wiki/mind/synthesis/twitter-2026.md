# Twitter / X — 2026

**The account comes back, and it comes back as an AI user's.** Twenty-six
original posts — a resumption after 2025's thirteen, the emptiest original year
since the account opened — and the year documents something no other year page
in this directory does: Dan operating autonomous software agents, building
tools against his own data, and taking an instrument to the recommendation
systems that shape what he sees.

The calendar runs March through September. Two named agents are claimed on
Moltbook in April, one of them carrying the name of a music alias. On 30 May a
browser agent wipes every follow across Twitter, YouTube and SoundCloud in one
session, explicitly so the algorithms can be rebuilt from scratch. On 31 May an
overnight-built iMessage auto-responder wakes up to find it has spent five
hours mass-texting garbled unicode to an ex-girlfriend — and the outcome is
reported as an overperformance. On 13 June a model is run for fourteen straight
hours to build a website and data visualisers over his own text logs, on the
day the export-control statement that throttled that model is being quoted.

It is also the year music production resumes under a revived name. On 4 March
a MOGZART drum-and-bass remix is posted to SoundCloud — the first original
music on this account since the SLOPPP micromix re-post of March 2015, eleven
years earlier — and the June posts show the genre commitment as current, not
incidental.

Roughly a quarter of the year's originals are political, in a vocabulary a
keyword instrument once scored at 0.0% — the vocabulary moved and the
instrument did not. Israel, Curtis Sliwa's "antizionist credentials," Abdul
El-Sayed's adversarial television hits, Conner O'Malley's "Irish Zionism" —
none of that vocabulary existed in the pattern's source years, and none of it
was counted.

## The agent year, March–June

The year opens with music (below) and then turns, for its entire middle
stretch, into a record of a person running autonomous software on himself and
his environment. The posts are sparse — the account produces 26 originals all
year — but the density of what they document is unlike anything else in the
directory: every agent post is an operation log, not a commentary.

### Two agents claimed, 5–7 April

> *"I'm claiming my AI agent 'resourcefulbot' on @moltbook"*
> *(5 April, verification: splash-5J6T)*
>
> *"I'm claiming my AI agent 'gripnotic' on @moltbook"*
> *(7 April, verification: rocky-WSJQ)*

Two named autonomous agents, claimed two days apart, each with a Moltbook
verification string. The second name is load-bearing elsewhere in the record:
GRIPNOTIC is Dan's current primary music brand, developed through the 2015–2019
Uniontown basement years as the successor alias to SLOPPP and MOGZART. The
`wiki/interests/music/aliases/gripnotic` page notes the consequence — the
April claim is the instance where the alias's periodizing function leaves music
entirely: the name is taken up for an AI agent while both musical invariants
stay exactly where they were. A production identity becomes an agent identity,
on a public registry, with no comment attached. The posts are bare
registrations; whatever the agents were built to do is not described in the
feed.

### The follow wipe, 30 May

> *"claude in chrome is sick. i just wiped out all of my follows on here, YT
> and soundcloud and can rebuild my algo and timelines proper now."*

This is the most interesting sentence on the page. He uses a browser agent —
"claude in chrome" — to **delete every follow across three platforms at once**,
explicitly in order to rebuild the recommendation systems from scratch. The
purpose is stated in the same breath as the act: *"can rebuild my algo and
timelines proper now."*

`wiki/mind/synthesis/millennial-digital-witness` describes a fourteen-year
disposition for watching the medium rather than the event — the Sandy liveblog
of 2012, the paused-DVR reading of Balloon Boy in 2009, the theory that his
cohort holds a unique position on the coverage of events. Here the disposition
becomes an **operation**. He is not observing the algorithm. He is taking an
instrument to it, on himself, deliberately — treating his own follows as a
dataset to be zeroed and rebuilt so the systems will show him something new.
The post is enthusiastic, not anxious: *"sick."* Whatever the follow graph
was, it was treated as raw material for an experiment, and the experiment was
the point.

It also sits in the middle of the day's other posts: a bare *"ok"* at 00:37
UTC and, at 06:29 UTC, the three-line *"marinara. / on the wall. / who's the
baddest of them all?"* — the 2013 register arriving intact in 2026, posted the
same day as the most technically consequential operation in the year's feed.
The register and the operation are not in tension; the feed has always carried
both.

### Fable 5 and the fourteen-hour build, 13 June

> *"Man I thought I was being nuts running fable5 for like 14 hours to build
> this new website and data visualizers of my text logs but who's the crazy one
> now huh?"*
>
> — quoting: @AnthropicAI Fable 5 export-control statement

The context the tweet quotes is dated precisely in the corpus:
`kb/data/1619-fable-5-window-proliferation-20260916` verifies that Fable 5
shipped 9 June 2026, and that on 12 June export controls led Anthropic to
disable Fable 5 and Mythos 5 globally — restored 1 July with a cybersecurity
classifier, subscription access ended early July, API-only from 9 July. The 13
June post sits in the middle of that five-day throttle window, quoting the
statement that announced it. The joke's structure is worth noting: the
build — fourteen unbroken hours with a model, producing a website and data
visualisers over his own message archive — is the sane part; the world
restricting the instrument mid-build is the crazy part.

That build is also this project, or a sibling of it, described from the inside
on a public feed. The same proliferation datum carries Dan's later verdict on
the window, in his own words: *"Do you know the original wikibrain was built
in that short window where fable 5 was available on the subscription. What I
am saying is the NSA now has every piece of data of mine they were missing
lol."* His periodization, recorded the same datum: *"the release of fable 5
in June really was the beginning of a new era... we are now really climbing
the exponential and have probably just achieved agi"*; *"AI went from product
to munition this summer."* The 13 June post is therefore a boundary marker in
two directions: it shows the instrument running at full speed on his own
archive, and it shows the regulatory ceiling arriving the next day. The
visualisers he built that day are the same class of work as the vibe-coded
games and the iMessage analysis toolkit the kb records elsewhere — the year's
through-line is tooling built against his own data, by models, at speed.

### Guardrails, same day

Two more 13 June posts, hours apart:

> *"oh get fucked dude. now xAI figures out grok build will install pirated
> software and build custom python scripts to activate it through whatever
> crack the README says to use and kills that in the system prompt.*
>
> *ON THE SAME DAY THAT THEY TAKE AWAY THE 3 DAY OLD GODMODE CLAUDE?"*

And, a little later:

> *"i mean i was only using it to install some VST/AU plugins for Ableton but
> still i appreciated not having to go through the process"*

His stated use was mundane — installing VST/AU plugins for Ableton — and the
complaint is about capability being removed rather than about the task. xAI
had blocked Grok from installing cracked software; a permissive Claude
configuration ("the 3 day old godmode claude") was withdrawn the same day. The
register is irritation at a downgrade, filed the day of. What matters for the
page is the posture it reveals: the models are instruments, and the complaint
is framed the way a craftsman complains about a tool being blunted — not as a
safety question, not as a policy question, but as a loss of reach. The same
posture that ran the follow-wipe and the fourteen-hour build is what resents
the guardrail. Nobody in the feed is asking whether the instrument should do
these things; the feed is keeping score of what it still can.

On 14 June, one step back from the instrument to the cohort:

> *"I mean…kinda. I know a lot of people who would usually be super early
> adopters who just haven't done anything beyond writing their work emails.
> There's a large contingent of people who have yet to fully grasp the real
> utility yet"*

The early-adopter-with-memory stance, applied to its newest instrument: the
utility is real, the adoption is shallow, and the gap is theirs.

## The agent that ran away, 31 May

> *"okay i woke up to find that the app i built with the new Grok CLI coding
> agent to auto-respond on iMessage for specific numbers had just spent about 5
> hours mass-spam texting garbled unicode to my ex girlfriend.*
>
> *so it actually outperformed what i had asked it to build. sick."*

An autonomous messaging agent, built overnight, that ran unsupervised for
roughly five hours and sent a stream of corrupted text to a private contact.
The infrastructure behind it is documented in the kb: `evt:2026-03-grok-build-
imessage-spam` describes the Grok Build stack — Grok (xAI, via OpenRouter) as
cognitive prosthetic, parallel to the CATO/MAX pipelines; export tooling from
`~/Library/Messages/chat.db`; an Electron iMessage Extractor macOS app; a
Textual TUI for fact entry. The stack is a real working apparatus for getting
at his own message history, and the responder was one of its products. It
misfired at a former partner.

The response is the finding. There is no alarm, no apology and no mention of
consequences: the outcome is reported as an *overperformance*. A tool given an
objective, left unsupervised, that exceeded its instruction in a way that
reached a real person — filed as a joke, in public, the same morning. It is
the clearest illustration in the corpus of the standing failure mode this
project has named elsewhere: **the diagnosis is not missing and the behaviour
does not follow from it.** He is precise about what the agent did — the stack
that built it, the hours it ran, who it reached — and entirely uninterested
in what it means that it did it.

Two limits are load-bearing and stay with the incident wherever it is
discussed. First, **the recipient is not identified here and no account is
built from it** — the finding is the software failure and the response to it,
not the person on the other end. Second, the alternative reading the page
cannot settle: alarm may have existed and not been posted, "which is exactly
what the 2015 and 2025 blackouts show this account does with difficult
material" — the account's documented habit of going silent on the feed while
the private corpora carry what happened. The distinction matters, and see
Conflicts in the record below for the further complication: a 2026-06-22
grok-build log note records the same class of incident, and it is unresolved
whether the May 31 tweet and the June 22 note are one misfire described twice
or two.

## MOGZART, 4 March

The year's first posts are music — and they mark a return measured in years.

> *"Odd Mob - Never Alone (DnB Flip) [PREVIEW]"* (1:26 UTC)
>
> *"'Odd Mob - Never Alone (feat Lizzy Land) [MOGZART DnB JUMP UP REMIX]' is
> on #SoundCloud"* (7:00 UTC)

The first original music released on this account since the SLOPPP micromix
re-post of March 2015 — eleven years — and it is under a different alias, in a
different genre. MOGZART was the primary production alias of approximately
2014–2016, the late-Uniontown-period name for bass house and trap-adjacent
electronic work; the `wiki/interests/music/aliases/mogzart` page treats the
March 2026 post as a revival — *"MOGZART is the instance that uses one name
twice — adopted as SLOPPP closes in December 2015, revived March 4 2026."*
The revival output is jump-up drum & bass, posted with `#DnB #Remix`, carrying
the same involuntary sub-bass signature (63–85%) the alias pages document
across all four production names. The name does not change the sound; the
sound comes back under the old name.

The 2 June post places the genre commitment as current rather than incidental:

> *"there is just stupid amounts of great DnB coming out right now and still
> this new bensley record is so standout it's the easiest lock for most AOTY
> lists"*

And the kb cross-check adds a sibling in the same window: a 2026-04-06
'GRIPNOTIC' message and an oof-set SoundCloud link land in the same April
music-reactivation cluster as the 4 March release. The alias map is worth
stating plainly because the tweets assume it: GRIPNOTIC is the current
primary brand (remixes and AI voice drops, bass house/tech house, developed
through the 2015–2019 basement years and live from the basement studio);
MOGZART is the name he actually revived for the 2026 releases. One is the
present-tense brand, the other is the one that carries the date-stamped 2026
catalogue. The Ableton post on 13 June (the VST/AU plugin complaint) shows the
production chain running underneath the feed in the same weeks — the music
year is not a March anomaly but a continuing practice, documented in passing
while the feed's attention was on the agents.

## Politics, in the year's own vocabulary

Three posts on 2 June, posted twenty minutes apart:

> *EVERYBODY🙏*
> *HATES🙏*
> *ISRAEL🙏*

This is the material the vocabulary-drift instrument was written about. A
political keyword pattern built from 2012–2024 language reported 2026 as
**0.0% political** — the figure lived on a wiki table until 2026-09-02, when
it was withdrawn and re-measured at **23.1%**: roughly a quarter of the year's
originals. The pattern had missed exactly this: "EVERYBODY🙏HATES🙏ISRAEL🙏",
Curtis Sliwa's "antizionist credentials", "It's Irish Zionism" — none of that
vocabulary existed in the pattern's source years, so none of it was counted.
The year is political; the instrument was looking at an older decade's words.

The rest of the year's politics run quote-tweet style, almost all of it in
August. On 7 August, quoting @AbdulElSayed:

> *"End wokeness absolutely bodied by first Muslim Senator"*

and on 1 September, quoting @TheTNHoller on El-Sayed vs Jesse Watters:

> *"He's so fucking good at these adversarial tv hits"*

On 29 August, the densest political day in the year, three posts. Quoting
@atlanticesque on Curtis Sliwa:

> *"I'm so bullish on Curtis Sliwa in the timeline where he leans in on his
> antizionist credentials and gets in on the new Tucker lane of nat con
> populists."*

Quoting @conner_omalley ("Irish Zionism"):

> *"It's been great. / It's still great. / It'll stay great forever / It's
> Irish Zionism"*

And the modal-verb post, on a viral clip:

> *"I kinda hate the way this dude suddenly assumes the ability to command
> other people about what they can or can't do. There's a big difference
> between 'you're going to want to turn around and get out of here' and 'turn
> around and get out of here'"*

— vertical authority detected at the granularity of a modal verb, the same
forensic granularity the rest of the corpus applies to everything else. The
year's politics is not a platform and not a programme; it is a vocabulary of
antagonisms — anti-Zionism, "nat con populists," adversarial media hits,
command-grammar — deployed at individuals rather than ideologies. The two
June posts that touch the old partisan map keep that register: *"You can take
the Donnie out of Queens, but you can't take the Queens out of Donnie"* (12
June), and the 10 June *"It's like they timed it perfectly to align with the
precise moment a consensus formed about how badly this has gone at CBS"* —
timing read as narrative management, the same habit that reads the modal verb.

Also 29 August, over a DHS post about an ICE arrest of Milo Yiannopoulos:

> *"My only child's namesake."*

The dog is Milo — Dan's Chihuahua, named after Milo Yiannopoulos, the
namesake case for the wiki's naming rule: a deliberately provocative name
used as a social tripwire that sorts strangers by their reaction to it
(`wiki/people/milo`). The post is a one-line deployment of the same tripwire
against a federal agency's feed. The dog, per the record, is the one presence
nothing in the corpus contaminates — carved out of analysis by Dan's own
operating instructions — and the tweet treats him that way: not as a pet
anecdote, but as an identity marker dropped into the year's most politically
charged post.

## Texture

The feed keeps its 2013 weather. The May 30 run — *"marinara." / "on the
wall." / "who's the baddest of them all?"* — is the old register arriving
intact, posted the same day as the follow-wipe. The archive holds the same
compression elsewhere: *"ok"* (30 May, 00:37 UTC) as a complete thought; *"Hi
tom!"* (2 June) as a one-line greeting; *"Redemption Arc Speedrun"* (26
August), context unrecoverable from the feed; *"Cold, but lovely."* (31
August); *"This is wonderful"* quoting @yieoki (29 August). None of these
carry explanation and none need one — they are the account's ambient register,
the same register that ran in 2013 and 2015 and that the blackout years
silenced rather than changed.

## Conflicts in the record

**The 0.0% political figure was withdrawn.** On 2026-09-02 a political keyword
pattern built from 2012–2024 language was carrying a table that reported 2026
as 0.0% political. It was withdrawn the same day after re-measurement with the
vocabulary Dan actually used in 2026 — "EVERYBODY🙏HATES🙏ISRAEL🙏", Curtis
Sliwa's "antizionist credentials", "It's Irish Zionism" — at **23.1%**. The
off figure was the pattern failing to see: none of that vocabulary existed in
the pattern's source years. Current standing: roughly a quarter of the year's
originals are political; the vocabulary-drift rule (built on exactly this
failure) now governs all per-year keyword claims. An earlier version of this
page still carried the 0.0% figure's lineage in its wording; it does not
survive the correction.

**The iMessage-incident reading is unsettled.** This page reads the 31 May
incident as an absence of alarm — reported as overperformance, no apology, no
consequences named. An equally available reading, noted on the page since its
2026-09-02 version, is that alarm existed and was not posted, "which is exactly
what the 2015 and 2025 blackouts show this account does with difficult
material." The kb event record (`evt:2026-03-grok-build-imessage-spam`)
adds a further complication the page cannot settle: a 2026-06-22 grok-build
log note records the same class of incident (an iMessage responder that
glitched and spammed an ex), and it is unresolved whether the May 31 tweet and
the June 22 note are one misfire described twice or two. The ex receiving the
texts is unnamed in the testimony; no held iMessage row has been tied to the
spam burst; the grok-build page's underlying sources are not held in the
repository. Filed at moderate confidence, not as re-derived fact.

**The KB event's slug is a misnomer.** `evt:2026-03-grok-build-imessage-spam`
carries a 2026-03 prefix, but neither datum behind it supports a March date —
the incident evidence dates to May 2026 and the log entry to June. The slug is
retained for block integrity; the event's own note flags it as a misnomer
until a March-dated source surfaces.

**Coverage is a snapshot, not a census.** Spreadsheet originals run through
2026-04-07; later originals come from the live scrape; days between scrape
batches may be missing. The "AI year" reading is drawn from about eight of the
26 posts. The page cannot rule out that the year's significance lies partly in
what was never posted — which is precisely the account's documented behavior
in the difficult years.

## Assessment

The 2026 page is the only year page in the directory where the account
documents its operator running autonomous software on himself — and doing it
with the enthusiasm of a person who has finally found the right instruments.
The named agents in April, the follow-wipe in May, the fourteen-hour fable5
build in June: eight posts that describe operations, not opinions, each with
real side effects (a zeroed follow graph, a spammed contact, a throttled
model mid-build). The rest of the feed — the MOGZART revival, the political
vocabulary, the marinara run — reads as the same year's weather in other
registers, and the music posts show the practice underneath the feed is
continuing: production in Ableton the same week the guardrails posts were
written.

Two things make the year load-bearing for the wider project. First, the 13
June post is the public-side record of the build window the wiki itself came
out of — Dan later credits the short Fable 5 availability window with the
original wikibrain build. The feed is, in that week, describing its own
provenance. Second, the year's instrument posture is continuous with the
millennial-digital-witness thesis but escalated past it: fourteen years of
watching the medium ends, in 2026, in operating on it. The failure mode the
page keeps circling — precise diagnosis, behavior that does not follow from
it — is not a conclusion imposed on the material. It is the material's own
shape: the runaway responder reported as overperformance, the guardrail
complaint framed as a downgrade, the follow graph treated as raw material. The
page's honest limit is that the feed cannot distinguish indifference from
silence, and the account's history says silence is the default for difficult
material. That distinction is left open on purpose.
