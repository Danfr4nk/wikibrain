# The Two New York Eras: 2010–2013 and 2019–2025

Dan lived in New York twice, fourteen years apart in their endings, and the two stays have the same silhouette. Each begins with an arrival organised around someone else — a father's offer, a partner's lease, a broker-placed apartment — and each settles into work that put him in a trusted, cash-adjacent, low-rank position inside someone else's business. Each ends not with a plan but with an exit, and the exits are documented in very different amounts of paper. This page is the synthesis of the two eras as one shape, built from `kb/syntheses/nyc-years-2010-2025.md` and the nodes it cites. The period pages carry the eras separately — [[wiki/timeline/periods/nyc-first-era-2010-2013|the first era]] told from outside the record, [[wiki/mind/synthesis/nyc-round-two|the second]] told from inside the phone. What neither period page can show, because each holds only its own years, is the repetition.

Between the eras sit the Uniontown years: caddying at Nemacolin, the Fran vigil, the supply-network years. In the kb synthesis's reading those years are the intermission the city made necessary — the interval between a first exit that produced complaints to two levels of government and a second arrival that produced, eventually, the longest job of Dan's life. The eras are not parallel lives. They are the same structural position occupied twice, once at twenty-one and once in his thirties, with the same kind of ending both times and a different kind of paperwork each time. Why the paperwork differs is, on this record, the most informative question the two eras answer together.

## NYC-1: arrival, 2010–2011

The first era opens the day after a dated hinge. On 17 February 2010 Dan began Suboxone; the following day Rick met him in Pittsburgh and the two boarded an Amtrak to Penn Station (`evt:2010-02-17-suboxone-start`). Months of apartment-hunting followed on Rick's offer of $2,000 a month "while you intern," and by June there was a lease at 424 Bedford Ave in Williamsburg, with Alexis Armel — the girlfriend of the pre-stabilisation arc, present for the whole arrival (`ent:alexis-armel`).

The city's first employment was studio-adjacent. There was an internship and engineer role at Ishlab Studios under Jamin Gilbert, at 10 Jay Street in DUMBO — a workplace that still has no page of its own in the knowledge base, a gap this synthesis inherits rather than fills. Then, from about April 2011 to 15 February 2012, Creative License: a small celebrity-talent and licensed-music brokering company at 71 8th Avenue in the West Village, under its president Kevin McKiernan, where Dan's contemporaneous title was Special Projects Coordinator (`dat:0498`). The job put a junior employee in motion around other people's money, travel, and contracts — the trusted-low-rank position the second era would reproduce in a restaurant shed a decade later.

## NYC-1: the Creative License exit, in four acts

The Creative License exit is the best-documented employment ending in the record below the 2026 BFS case, and the kb data node lays it out as four acts. They are reproduced here in the order the record gives them, because the sequence — not any single act — is the synthesis's evidence.

**Act Zero is the stated reason, and Dan's own testimony complicates it.** The thing he cited when he quit had to do, in his retrospective words, with *"some kind of small fraud with airplane tickets that i blew it up into a much much bigger thing but maybe i was just desensitized"* — with explicit self-doubt about proportionality. He used the airfare citation as, in the node's reading, legal ground to detach: a personality complaint would have been dismissible as sensitivity, while a financial anomaly made the departure an objective failure on the company's part. The citation is settled as real by his own primary testimony; its mechanism is not verified, and is carried as open in Conflicts below.

**Act One is the payroll dispute.** A final paycheck carrying a $104 MetroCard charge Dan says he never requested; Marty Jackson in Accounting demanding that any opt-out should have been made "in writing before you quit" and writing the anchor line — *"It's a trust issue. We don't trust you. Please do not forget the keys or any other CL property you have"*; Dan filing complaints with the IRS and the New York State Department of Labor; Rick copied throughout, functioning in the thread as tactical coach (*"You sound so desperate. That is what kevin wants. We'll talk about a strategy for this"*) — the corpus's clearest instance of Rick operating as strategist rather than distant authority. Dan closed the dispute out directly with Marty on April 9, conceding the $104 and explicitly separating the accountant from the president.

**Act Two is one line long.** On 26 April 2012, an email to Rachel Rauch: "Enjoy!!!", with compiled McKiernan voicemails attached. The node calls it the catharsis. Whether the email and the compilation exist as described is one of the era's open items — the BEST OF KTM VOICEMAILS material is sourced to a Gemini session only and was not located in the Gmail search — and it is recorded here as testimony, not artefact.

**Act Three is the disclosure.** In May 2012, unprompted, Dan told former contact Renae Holland what he had witnessed: that in December 2011 McKiernan had used Dan's own email and LinkedIn account to send recommendation-removal requests to former employees, and that employment contracts Dan had been made to send to two former interns had been altered before sending, with a clause added prohibiting recommendations without CEO approval. Renae's reply corroborates from her own tenure: *"in my time there I was Kevin falsify more documents that I can count"* — the grammatical break in the quotation is in the source as cited.

**Act Four is the retaliation.** In June 2012 McKiernan filed a formal LinkedIn intellectual-property complaint backed by two notarised documents asserting Dan had never been a Creative License employee — notarised by Katherine Palakovich, who had sent Dan his own non-compete in May 2011, using her commission on her employer's behalf against a former coworker. LinkedIn threatened termination within three business days; Dan filed a counter-notice naming the retaliation motive (`dat:0498`).

Four acts, and the shape across them is consistent: the complaint is always filed somewhere with a form — a tax authority, a labour department, a platform's counter-notice process — rather than pressed in the room. That is an attention-level observation about where the record shows effort going during the exit, and it is the same shape the corpus names fourteen years later.

## The intermission: Nemacolin and the Uniontown years

Between the LinkedIn retaliation of June 2012 and the next held employment, the record goes quiet in a specific way. The kb synthesis names 2013–2016 the biography's least-documented adult stretch, and the 2019–2021 pre-Au Za'atar stretch is nearly as thin: the city years resume in the record with the March 2021 hire, not before. What fills the middle of the decade in employment terms is Nemacolin Woodlands Resort in Farmington, Pennsylvania — golf caddy, April 2016 to November 2019 on a resume-derived span of 43 months — the main income through the hardest financial stretch, overlapping the Fran vigil, the Suboxone years, and the 2018–2020 Uniontown supply-network era (`ent:nemacolin`).

The held-corroborated beats of the caddy years — Laurel Valley day trips, the Pikewood National tournament coordination — sit beside unheld resume narrative, and the synthesis does not upgrade the second kind into the first. The intermission matters to this page for one structural reason: it is the period in which no vertical employment relationship is documented at all. The two New York eras both end in documented ruptures with a vertical figure; the years between them, in Fayette County, have work, income, and a dying great-grandmother, but no employer in the record to rupture with. Whether that quiet is a property of the years or of their documentation is exactly the kind of question the record's coverage cannot answer, and the falsifier the kb synthesis states for the two-era shape turns on it: if the gaps fill with evidence of a stable, unruptured working life elsewhere, the shape becomes an artefact of coverage rather than biography.

## NYC-2: Au Za'atar, 2021–2024

The second era's anchor institution is Au Za'atar, March 2021 to August 2024 — 41 months, hired as a busser with zero food-service experience, at the Midtown restaurant at 58th and 1st (`ent:au-zaatar`, `evt:2021-03-au-zaatar-hired`). The tenure's documented texture is unusually specific for a job the held corpus barely mentions: service out of a plywood COVID-era shed on the 58th Street sidewalk, rebuilt nightly over two to three hours, which Dan converted into an "Outside Guy" post with sole custody of the shed's cash tips, and which he never closed once in 41 months; pay confirmed by Dan directly on 14 July 2026 at $15 an hour base plus tip pool, with weekly Sunday checks typically $800–$1,000 for a six-day week; and the restaurant as his most-visited GPS location at 445 visits. Every one of those specifics is page testimony rather than held-corpus fact, a standing the entity page states and this synthesis preserves.

The cast is the era's human texture, and the record's warmth is concentrated here in a way the first era never shows. Tarik Fallous, the owner-operator, hired Dan on the spot; their held thread runs 80 messages from August 2023 to April 2026, fully verified, with warm contact continuing well past the job's end (`ent:tarik-fallous`). Dimitri, the Greek manager of the Midtown room, is in Dan's own words the only boss he ever loved (`ent:dimitri`) — held thread of 71 messages against a page claim of 116, the gap open. Ismaila Barry, the Senegalese busser-turned-server, is counted by the record among Dan's best friends (`ent:ismaila-barry`). Manuel, a coworker, became a genuine friend whose 99-row two-sided thread runs to February 2026, the friendship outlasting the job (`ent:manuel`). Inside the tenure sits its one rupture of the other kind: Valeria Iglesias Cid, the hostess at the East Village location, in what the record calls the decade's one unauthorised infidelity, the affair peaking in winter 2021–22 (`evt:2021-22-valeria-affair`, `ent:valeria-iglesias-cid`).

The era ends in August 2024 with the shed shutdown, ruled involuntary — dossier Amendment 2, ACCEPTED — with hours cut June through August, resumes circulating from July 9, and unemployment filed August 8. The contemporaneous first-person record, held, is from 21 August 2024: *"By the way they officially let me go from AZ"*; *"Tarik was really really nice about it and everything though. We parted on good terms"*; *"And I am already getting unemployment so it didn't cause me too many problems financially"* (`evt:2024-08-au-zaatar-exit`). The second era thus ends the way the kb synthesis's two-era reading predicts in structure and contradicts in paperwork: a vertical relationship ended the employment, and produced almost no adversarial documentation at all.

## The shape the two eras make together

Laid side by side, the eras produce a finding neither contains alone, and the corpus itself names its endpoints: Creative License (2011–2012) and the BFS Foods drawer dispute (2026) are filed as the first and most recent instances of one shape, with Au Za'atar's involuntary end as the middle instance (`dat:0498`, `dat:1387`). The shape, stated at attention level from the record of what was written, filed, and sent: a trusted vertical figure's undocumented financial claim is met with exit and documentation rather than confrontation in the room. At Creative License the claim was an airfare irregularity and a $104 charge, and the response was complaints to the IRS and the state labour department and a counter-notice to LinkedIn. At BFS the claim was a disputed $50 drawer shortage, and the sequence was termination and, in August 2026, a rehire offer. The amounts shrink across the instances — an airfare scheme, a MetroCard charge, fifty dollars — while the consequences do not.

The asymmetry between the two New York exits is the synthesis's second finding, and it cuts against any simple reading of the first. The Creative License exit produced a paper trail Dan still cites fourteen years later. The Au Za'atar exit produced *"Tarik was really really nice about it"* and an unemployment filing. Same city, same structural position — the worker with sole custody of cash, in the shed as at the talent agency's travel desk — opposite documentation. The record supports one attention-level distinction between the cases: in the first, the vertical figure was an adversary (the notarised claim that Dan had never worked there is as adversarial as an employment document gets); in the second, the vertical figures were, by Dan's own concurrent account, people he held in affection, and contact with Tarik continued warm into 2026. The documentation effort in the record tracks the adversarial cases and is absent in the affectionate one. This page states that as an observed distribution of paperwork, not as a claim about motive: the corpus shows where the filings went, and a motive reading would be an interior claim the instruments here cannot carry.

The vertical-authority page sharpens the same point from the profile side, at attention level: the split between vertical and lateral channels is a clock, not a switch — lateral figures are audited later rather than trusted more, while vertical claims are audited on arrival (`dat:1387`). New York is where that clock is visible at era scale. Both eras place Dan inside a vertical structure he did not control, in a role built on being trusted with cash and access; both end when the vertical relationship, not the work, fails; and the intermission between them is the one long stretch with no vertical employer in the record at all.

## Conflicts in the record

- **"Longest job": Au Za'atar's 41 months against Nemacolin's 43 (unresolved).** The Au Za'atar page calls the restaurant Dan's longest job; the Nemacolin tenure, April 2016 to November 2019, is 43 months on a resume-derived span (`ent:au-zaatar`, `ent:nemacolin`). The natural reconciliation — caddying was seasonal, roughly April–November across four seasons, while the restaurant was year-round — is an inference, not a filed fact. Current standing: both figures stand as sourced; the superlative is not settled.
- **The airfare mechanism (unverified).** Dan's own testimony settles that the citation he quit over was real, and includes his retrospective doubt about its proportionality. What the fraud actually consisted of is not verified: a Gemini elaboration involving mismarked travel-expense billing to named clients stays unverified, as does what happened to Renae Holland after her corroboration. The data node's own triple-pass correction history in one day — flagged, wrongly unflagged, re-flagged — is recorded on that page as a case of the correction instinct overfiring (`dat:0498`). Current standing: citation real, mechanism open.
- **The Au Za'atar exit's voluntariness (ruled, not held-verified).** The involuntary ruling rests on a dossier amendment marked ACCEPTED. The held corpus carries the 21 August 2024 disclosure and the good-terms characterisation; a held message from Tarik or Dimitri describing a voluntary quit would reopen the ruling. Current standing: involuntary, on dossier authority, with the falsifier named in advance.
- **The Dimitri thread count (open).** The held corpus holds 71 Dimitri-thread messages (36 inbound, 35 Dan's, September 2023 to December 2024) against the page's figure of 116 from an unheld export. Current standing: the held number is the verifiable one; the page figure is testimony, and the gap is open either way.
- **The restaurant's near-absence from the held corpus (stated limit).** Across the 192,140-record held corpus, 'zaatar' appears in roughly ten rows and 'tarik' in fifteen; the 2021 held slice is 282 rows with none in November 2021. The tenure's texture — the shed, the checks, the never-closed record — rests almost entirely on unheld sources: page testimony, dossiers, and session material, with first-person session narration in the record explicitly styled as memoir rather than deposition. Current standing: the tenure frame (dates, hire, exit) is moderately supported; the texture is low-confidence testimony and is used here only as such.
- **The gaps (2013–2016; 2019–2021).** The city years end in the record with the June 2012 LinkedIn retaliation and resume, in employment terms, at Nemacolin in 2016; the second era's record resumes with the March 2021 hire rather than with the 2019 arrival. What those windows contained is not in this knowledge base, and the two-era shape is hostage to them: stable, unruptured working life in either window would make the shape an artefact of coverage.

## Assessment

The two-era reading earns its synthesis status on one ground: the repetition is in the record, not imposed on it. The corpus itself files Creative License and BFS as the endpoints of a single named pattern across a fourteen-year interval, the Au Za'atar entity carries the middle instance with its paperwork conspicuously absent, and the intermission's lack of any vertical employer is a fact of the record whatever it turns out to mean. New York, on this evidence, is not two chapters but one experiment run twice: a trusted cash-adjacent role inside someone else's vertical structure, an ending determined by the state of the vertical relationship, and documentation effort that appears in the adversarial run and not in the affectionate one. The city extracted the same ending twice; what differed was whether anyone Dan worked for there was, in his own concurrent words, a boss he loved — and the record shows the paperwork following that difference, at attention level, in filings sent and not sent.

The reading's limits are the record's limits, and they are not small: the best-textured tenure in the biography is the one the held corpus can least verify, and the two windows that would test the shape are the two windows the record does not hold. The synthesis stands at moderate confidence, with its falsifiers named where they belong — in Conflicts, above, where a future pass that fills either gap can retire or confirm the shape on evidence rather than on silhouette.
