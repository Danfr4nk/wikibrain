# Twitter / X — 2021

**The first full year of the converted position, and it is fluent.** The
2020 declaration was an ultimatum; 2021 is the working version. There are only
34 originals on the record, and they fall into two clusters — twenty-one in
January, most of them in a single day around the Capitol riot, and thirteen
more in October through December — with nothing recorded in between. The
vocabulary, the targets, and the internal disagreements are all a settled
leftist's rather than a recent convert's: *material conditions*,
*neoliberal*, *corpo donors*, *dem soc* are used correctly, and the primary
opponent is the Democratic Party rather than the Republicans — the tell that
distinguishes a leftist from a liberal. The year's densest day, 7 January,
produces the cleanest on-record demonstration of how his conspiracy
judgments actually work: he forms one at high confidence, prices its weakest
point in the same breath, and rejects the mirror-image conspiracy aimed at
his own side's enemies on the ground that it requires its actors to work
against their own interests. And one post, from 26 October, corrects a date
this corpus currently carries about when the free-speech-absolutism position
was abandoned.

## January: the Capitol cluster

Twenty-one of the year's thirty-four originals fall in January, and six of
those land on 7 January, as the Capitol riot was still hours old. The month
opens with a three-tweet sequence on 5 January that is already the mature
position in miniature. First, on GOP rhetoric: *"all the bombastic GOP claims
that the most moderate, establishment-approved democrats having a
'radical/extreme liberal' agenda (including wanting to do a little socialism,
communism, and possibly even a marxism) is like the funniest shit ever to me."*
Then the forward-looking version: *"just wait until they are running against a
principled and unapologetic dem soc... they will probz regret using up all
their effective 'socialism bad because venezuala' talking points"*. Then the
revenge fantasy, which is also the definitional-precision instinct wearing its
most obvious costume: *"gonna exec produce a new primetime series that is just
conservatives in a restraining chair (like the theater scene from a clockwork
orange) and make them define terms they constantly use incorrectly such as:
socialism, globalism, obamagate, liberty/freedom, etc."*

On 6 January he refuses an anti-Comey op-ed's framing: *"Comey is not 'what
got us into this mess'. The Democratic party and their corporate ring-kissing
deserve blame; also - racism."* The primary opponent is named again, and it
is not the Republicans.

### 7 January, in real time

The six tweets of 7 January arrive as a genuine real-time sequence, and they
show the judgment apparatus working in order. At 16:51 UTC, on the footage of
the breach: *"not only that but the footage showing how they got through each
layer of security is just inexplicable. even the most generous interpretation
- that the entire force tasked with preventing a breach are wholly inept
semi-narcoleptic pacifists"*. At 16:59, the layman's forensic turn: *"i have
no background in security or law enforcement, to be sure. but it doesn't take
a criminal justice degree or any training whatsoever to know that the 'mosh
pit' is not a reccomended move"*. At 20:11, the entertainment-register
reaction: *"yep, although it's hard to imagine any series finale could ever
have such a shakespearean and unexpectedly satisfying ending."*

At 20:19 the hypothesis, at full confidence, with its own price attached:
*"it's so curious, though. i'm as sure as i could possibly be that the
capitol police had arranged to allow the chuds into the building while they
LARPed the violence they would usually be committing -and then blame it on
ANTIFER but what was the endgame? is that being too generous"*. At 20:25,
the contempt verdict on the rioters themselves: *"this is what happens when
the best option you have for your coup de tat crew are the hogs that organized
the charlottesville 'unite the right' disaster."* And at 21:22, the
hypothesis restated to another account's take, more carefully: *"I don't
disagree with the spirit of this take but, after seeing some of the livestream
footage that was broadcast as they breached the security checkpoints and (more
obviously) once they had gained entrance, the Capitol police seemingly
collaborated to let them inside"*.

The sequence matters because of what the 20:19 tweet does in one breath: it
states the collusion accusation at maximum confidence and then immediately
attaches the hypothesis's own weakest point — that it credits the actors with
more coherence than the evidence supports. The generous-interpretation tweet
at 16:51 had already done the same move in miniature: even the *most generous*
reading makes the force look absurd, so the observation is priced before the
conclusion. And the 21:22 version dials the confidence back from "arranged to
allow" to "seemingly collaborated" — not a retraction, but the same mind
re-pricing the claim as the footage kept arriving.

### January 6th: a conspiracy entertained, priced, and one rejected

The Capitol cluster is the best available demonstration of what
`wiki/mind/synthesis/vertical-authority-skepticism` means when it says the
"conspiracy crank" label is contradicted by his behaviour.

**He forms one.** On 7 January: *"i'm as sure as i could possibly be that the
capitol police had arranged to allow the chuds into the building while they
LARPed the violence they would usually be committing"* — a serious accusation
of collusion by a police force, stated at high confidence.

**Then he immediately prices it, in the same tweet:** *"but what was the
endgame? is that being too generous"*. The hypothesis is published with its
own weakest point attached, and the weakest point is that it credits the
actors with more coherence than the evidence supports.

**And he rejects the mirror-image conspiracy on structural grounds.** On
17 January, to somebody arguing antifa infiltration: *"Sooooooo...are you
saying that the radical leftists were agitating to keep the far-right
president in power rather than the democratic candidate? Do you seriously
think that antifa is working against their own ideological values to sling
mud on Trump supporters? Come on, dude."* The refutation is not "that's a
conspiracy theory" — it is that the theory requires its actors to work
against their own interests.

That is the distinction the whole vertical-authority page rests on: he is
not credulous about institutions and he is not credulous about the stories
told against them either. The test he applies is whether the actors'
behaviour makes sense from inside their own position, and he applies it to
his own hypothesis first. All of this is observable at the attention level —
what gets written, in what order, with what self-qualification attached —
rather than requiring any claim about interior motive.

### The rest of January

The month's remaining posts fill out the register. On 9 January, the
pipeline made personal: *"@hasanthehun Derrick Evans is in the Capitol
Building!!!"* — Hasan Piker, one of the four channels
`wiki/mind/synthesis/2020-left-turn` names as the conversion pipeline,
addressed directly. On 10 January, a bare *"Yikes"* with a TikTok link. On
14 January: *"A dedication to the years we let the mentally-ill billionaire
student of Roy Cohn run the most militarized and wealthy nation on earth"* —
the Trump epithet in its full form. On 15 January: *"Matt Gaetz is a lot
more tolerable if you just pretend he is actually a 4th grader who switched
bodies with an Arizona State undergrad"*, and a bare *"@Phloxenheim"* —
nothing else in the record explains who that handle is, and the tweet carries
no text of its own. On 16 January two reply-fragments whose antecedents were
never captured: *"And it just might"* and *"Fucking YIKES dude"*.

On 18 January, the Sanders loyalty that runs all year: *"lol that's not
bernie sanders at all. while bernie sanders has more spine than anyone in
congress...his posture is abominable. also the senate is not in session. also
a million other things"*. On 21 January, *"Legit can't tell if this is
sarcastic or not lol Oh 2021"*, and on 25 January, *"No........ No,he's not
lol"* — both reply-fragments, both antecedents lost. The operator spreadsheet
captured the text of the originals but not the threads they belonged to, so
a third of the month's output is orphaned from its context. That is a
standing limit on the whole year.

## The quiet months (February–September)

Nothing is recorded from 26 January through 25 October — no originals, and no
reposts either: the repost archive holds zero 2021 entries. Eight months of
the public record are a blank. The record does not establish whether he went
quiet, whether the archive simply failed to capture the period, or whether
the operator spreadsheet's coverage ends where it ends. What can be said is
that every one of the thirty-four recorded originals carries zero likes,
zero replies, and zero reposts in the archive's engagement fields — this was
either a small audience or a record that stopped counting engagement. The
bimodal shape of the year — a January burst and an autumn burst — is a
property of the record as retrieved, and should be read as such.

## October–December: the working position

The autumn cluster is the mature position operating at full fluency, and it
is where the vocabulary work of the year happens. On 26 October, the year's
most consequential sentence: *"Lenny Bruce was arrested...not by 'the
public', but by the state. Dave Chappelle is facing criticism which is, I'm
told, different."* Set against 16 January 2019 — *"The recent joke shaming of
Louis CK is so fucking disturbing"* — the move from comedy free-speech
absolutism happens somewhere in the thirty-three months between, and the
earliest dated instance of the new position is 2021-10-26. (The dating
question is carried in Conflicts in the record.) The same day, an untouched
small one: *"The math might be correct if they're serving glass bottles of
Mexican soda. This still should not inspire any sympathy for the owner and is
probably not what is happening here anyway."* On 30 October: *"@bludnymph I
was looking for tattoo design ideas and came across this"* — one of four
addressee-first posts in the year, the others being the @hasanthehun,
@Phloxenheim, and @turkoforpitt tweets.

November opens on the 4th with the pundit-narrative policing: *"Of all the
stupidity being pushed today by every cable news pundit searching for a
narrative to blame progressives as the cause of yesterday's election losses,
this one is impressively bad."* On the 6th, on the infrastructure bill:
*"What mechanism of the current BIF bill is going to excite voters enough to
impact voter turnout next year? None of the remaining entitlements will
substantially improve material conditions and nothing will be implemented
before the midterm election anyway"* — *material conditions* used correctly,
as a test a bill can fail. On 12 November, a small perfect one: he misreads
a tweet in somebody's likes, believes for twelve hours that his old coach is
coaching in the NFL, **telephones his mother about it**, and then posts the
correction himself: *"@turkoforpitt I saw this in your likes and
misunderstood it for 12 hours. I literally called my mom to tell her
@CoachKolesar was coaching in the NFL"*.

On 15 November, a disappointment stated as one: *"Had been (foolishly)
holding out hope that @elonmusk would eventually turn face and experience
some kind of leftist epiphany. Officially filing that fantasy as a 'no shot'
and also get fucked, Elon."* The admission that he had been hoping is the
interesting half — the position he held *before* the year is visible only in
its retraction. The same day, the year's purest loyalty statement:
*"Honestly I would sooner forgive someone for viciously insulting my mom
than I would for taking a swipe at Bernie Sanders."* On the 16th, the Musk
thread continues: *"'Bowing down' sounds like the exact opposite of the
epiphany I was describing. Also not sure what reality you're living in where
the leftist economic ideological message is even given basic legitimacy in
the US, much less demanding compliance from a billionaire lol"*. On the 23rd,
a three-word closer whose antecedent is lost: *"Ah, conservative comedy."*

December is a three-tweet mini-thread on the Fetterman/Lamb primary, and it
escalates in three moves. On the 3rd: *"I'm really not excited for the dnc
messaging initiative forthcoming for the fetterman/lamb primary. There's
nothing more threatening to libs than a hugely important race being won by an
effective progressive so they'll throw a lot of money and time to spoil"*.
On the 4th, the mechanism named: *"Agreed on the premise, but doesn't this
just create the conditions to put more DNC effort into anti-fetterman oppo
messaging? I would imagine the neoliberal forces who run the party and their
corpo donors see it as a worthy investment to block fetterman from spreading
influence"*. And the closing fear: *"To your point, fetterman is uniquely
positioned to do well and the fundraising numbers are super promising. I
hope that his potential for the left isn't the catalyst for the same
playbook that worked to outperform both Sanders campaigns to be run."*

## Conflicts in the record

- **2026-09-02 → 2026-10-07 (this page's earlier versions, corrected):**
  `wiki/interests/opie-and-anthony` dates the move away from comedy
  free-speech absolutism to **28 March 2022**, during the Chris Rock slap.
  The 26 October 2021 Lenny Bruce/Chappelle post makes the same argument five
  months earlier, during the Chappelle row: *"Lenny Bruce was
  arrested...not by 'the public', but by the state. Dave Chappelle is facing
  criticism which is, I'm told, different."* The earliest *dated* instance of
  the new position is therefore **2021-10-26**, not March 2022; the move
  itself happened somewhere in the thirty-three months between that post and
  the 16 January 2019 Louis CK defence. The O&A page's 2022 example is not
  wrong — it is just not first. Corrected there rather than left for the next
  reader to trip over. Current standing: this page holds 2021-10-26 as the
  earliest dated instance; nothing in the record pushes it earlier.

- **The Jan 6 reading, carried as an open alternative:** the reading above
  interprets the 20:19 self-qualification (*"but what was the endgame? is
  that being too generous"*) as method — the hypothesis published with its
  own price — rather than as hedging. Hedging is a real alternative: the
  qualifier could be read as softening a strong claim for an audience rather
  than as a structural check. The reason to prefer method is the 17 January
  rejection, which applies the same actors'-interests test to the opposite
  conspiracy at cost to his own side's narrative — a hedger would not spend
  that. Current standing: method reading preferred, hedging alternative
  recorded.

## Assessment

The record supports two judgments. First, the January 6th cluster is the
cleanest on-record demonstration of the vertical-authority-skepticism
distinction the corpus carries: a police-collusion hypothesis formed at high
confidence, priced in the same tweet, with the mirror conspiracy rejected ten
days later on the structural ground that it requires its actors to work
against their own interests. Credulous about neither the institution nor the
stories told against it — the test is whether the behaviour makes sense from
inside the actors' own position, applied to his own hypothesis first.

Second, 2021 is the year the position stops being a declaration and starts
being a working vocabulary. *Material conditions*, *neoliberal*, *corpo
donors*, *dem soc* are all used correctly; the Democratic Party is the
