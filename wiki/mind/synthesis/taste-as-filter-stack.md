# Taste as Filter Stack

Dan's musical taste is not a preference. A preference is a ranking — this over that, a list with a top. What the 2026-09-11 playlist forensics describe is something else: a **filtration system**, eight filters in series, each with a measurable threshold, each rejecting a different class of candidate, the survivors assembled into a working record box. The right question about any track in the 2025 MASTER CRATE is not "why does he like this" but "what did it survive" — and the right question about the taste as a whole is not what it selects for but **what the survivors are for**.

The answer to the second question is the entry's thesis, stated up front so everything below can be checked against it: **the survivors are ammunition, not canon.** The crate is an instrument — a DJ's record box, built in four dated waves across seven weeks, 59.5% released in 2025, zero exact track overlap with his own 5,321-track historical archive — and the filter stack is the mechanism that keeps the instrument loaded with current, mixable, high-energy material while the archive does the remembering. Listening and remembering are separate systems. He does not confuse them, and neither should the wiki.

The sibling entry [[wiki/mind/synthesis/dance-music-only-phases|dance-music-only-phases]] covers *when* — the two dance-only eras, the fallow decade, the dated re-entry mechanism. This entry covers *how the ear works* — the filters, the pipeline, the anatomy of the box. They are two halves of one object.

## Filter 0: the lyric qualifier (the precondition, not a choice)

Everything below runs downstream of a perceptual fact Dan stated twice,
fourteen months apart, in his own words: **he hears sung lyrics as timbre,
not language** [[wiki/mind/concepts/lyric-qualifier|lyric-qualifier]]
[OPERATOR]. The founding capture (2026-07-14) is not held in the repo's
`raw/` tree; the second capture (2026-09-11 MUSIC THREAD) is held and
byte-exact, and it added the lyric-sheet detail and the ~3-exceptions
count. (The retired page's late-summer-2025 hostile-third-party
corroboration does not stand: the held corpus returns zero rows for the
quoted sentence — see the canonical entry's Limits.) Words in songs do not resolve into semantic content — not in real time, not even with a lyric sheet in front of him. Across his entire life, roughly three songs have had their lyrics parsed as conceptual message. The three are still unnamed [UNKNOWN — see Gaps].

This is Filter 0 because it is not a filter he operates; it is the shape of the room the filters operate in. Every vocal statistic in this entry must be read as **voice-density, never content**:

- Spotify's `speechiness` measures voice-as-instrument density — how much voice-shaped sound is happening — not wordiness. A high-speechiness track is "a lot of vocal material," not "a lot being said" [DERIVED from the qualifier — HIGH confidence on the reframe, since the alternative reading is incoherent under his own report].
- His stated love of lyricists — [[wiki/interests/favorites/music/artists/jpegmafia|JPEGMAFIA]] (13 entries in the FAVS music log), Kanye West (11), Elliott Smith (7) — is therefore about **delivery, not bars**: flow, cadence, vocal grain, the voice as percussion. The admiration is real; its object is the instrument [INFERRED — MED, stated as the only coherent reading of the qualifier applied to the attested fandom].
- The crate's 96.1% instrumental rate is not a paradox under this frame. It is a *prediction*: a timbre-first ear building a DJ box builds a box with almost no foregrounded vocal performance, because foregrounded vocals are, to that ear, just a loud instrument that gets in the way of the mix.

The qualifier also governs the metadata layer, not just the audio. On 2026-09-12 Dan's own reports surfaced the **name-mishearing pattern**: "Eat Me Better" heard as "eat you better," "i'm loam" heard as "its loam" [OPERATOR]. Track and artist names arrive as approximate phonetics — the same explicit-over-inferred mechanism [[wiki/mind/concepts/autism|autism]] documents, applied to text that would normally be parsed semantically. Expect track and artist names from him to be phonetic approximations; the wiki's standing rule is to verify against Spotify before acting on them. The pattern is worth filing here because it proves the qualifier is not genre-specific or attention-specific — it runs on *reading a playlist*, not just on listening. The wall is architectural.

What Filter 0 rejects: **lyrics-as-meaning as a selection criterion, permanently and involuntarily.** No track has ever entered or left the library because of what its words say. This is the single most load-bearing fact in the entry, because it reframes every artist page in [[wiki/interests/favorites/music]] that was written on the assumption that lyrical content (a betrayal narrative, a confession) was part of the attraction. The qualifier's canonical entry [[wiki/mind/concepts/lyric-qualifier|lyric-qualifier]] already issued that caveat (carried forward from the retired lyrics-as-timbre page); this entry is the empirical weight behind it. The emo/pop-punk cluster's thematic coherence (betrayal, mortality, self-indictment) cannot be "chose these songs because of what they say" — whatever is happening is happening through vocal grain, melodic movement, structure, low end. The thematic and timbral readings stay compatible (artists writing about extremity tend to *perform* with more textural extremity), but the mechanism runs through sound, not meaning [INFERRED — MED].

Falsifier: name the three lyric-exception songs and show that their selection *was* content-driven in a way the other ~1,900 liked tracks were not. Until then, Filter 0 stands as the room, not a claim.

## Filter 1: the energy floor (the load-bearing filter)

Median energy 0.836. First quartile 0.704. Maximum 0.999, minimum 0.184 [DERIVED — HIGH, n=232, no missing values].

Three-quarters of the crate sits above 0.70. Read that again as a selection statement: **the crate has a floor, not a ceiling.** Genre tags sprawl across 30+ labels (dubstep 63, bass music 59, bass house 54, tech house 34, riddim 29, edm 25, house 24, g-house 24, drum and bass 22, stutter house 22, uk garage 21, plus a tail down to art pop 3) — and the genre sprawl is only coherent because the energy floor holds it together. Remove the floor and the genre coherence dissolves; keep the floor and thirty genres become one box [INFERRED — MED, but the dependency is directional: energy is the tighter distribution, so it is the better candidate for the primary filter].

What the floor rejects: the entire low-energy universe. The FAVS music log's emo/pop-punk/indie pole — Elliott Smith (7 entries), the 498 purged URIs' old canon ([[wiki/interests/favorites/music/artists/paramore|Paramore]] 6, All Time Low 5, Lana Del Rey 4, Bright Eyes 4) — lives below this floor and, with two named exceptions, does not enter the crate. The floor is why the crate and the fandom look like different people: they are the same ear with the floor engaged vs. disengaged. The floor engages when the box is for *playing*.

The two exceptions that prove the floor is a filter and not a law: Shostakovich's "Jazz Suite No. 2: VI. Waltz 2" (energy 0.184, the crate minimum) and Frank Ocean's "Thinkin Bout You" (0.329). Both are so far below the floor that their presence is information — a filter this tight doesn't admit accidents [INFERRED — LOW, provocation-grade; see Gaps for the open question].

Falsifier: find a third of the crate below 0.60 that isn't load-bearing-weird. The distribution says you won't.

| 145–155 | 16 | Transition zone |
| 155–165 | 5 | Drumstep bridge |

146 of 232 tracks (63%) sit in the 125–145 corridor — one continuous mixable block, ±8% pitch range covering most of it. The 165+ cluster (19 tracks: Mason/Princess Superstar/1991 "Perfect (Exceeder) - 1991 Remix" 174.1, Fourward "Lose That Control" 175.0, Great Dane "Drop Top" 178.9) is a separate dnb set. Slowest-to-fastest is not a gradient; it is **three rooms**: halftime, house, dnb [DERIVED — HIGH on the distribution; the "three rooms" reading MED].

What the filter rejects: the unmixable middle. The 145–165 band holds 21 tracks total — the crate avoids the no-man's-land between house and dnb where neither pitch-lock nor double-time feels clean. This is a DJ's tempo map, not a listener's shuffle: the bands correspond to sets, the gaps correspond to set-breaks. A listener's playlist would fill the middle; a record box leaves it empty because empty is where the crates change.

The nine VIP crates confirm the architecture from the other side: GOODBYE_BPD_GIRLZ (40 tracks, dnb/liquid funk, energy 0.891 — the highest of the set) and FATSO_FLATSO (25 tracks, pure liquid dnb, 23 of 25 from 2026, 174 BPM, energy 0.924) are the dnb wing with its own boxes; everything else works the 130–140 corridor [DERIVED — HIGH on the crate stats; the "own boxes" reading MED]. The three rooms have dedicated containers. The filter didn't just shape one crate; it shaped the whole intake system.

Falsifier: a crate majority outside 125–145 that still functions as one mixable set. The ±8% pitch math says the corridor is doing structural work.

"Crafting something catchy" is verse-chorus songwriting; "evoking emotional response" is texture, low end, drop architecture. The filter and the self-report agree.

Falsifier: a vocal-forward bloc above 15% that survives a re-cut of the thresholds. The 75% instrumental+texture bloc is the robust finding.

## Filter 4: dark valence (the emotional register)

Median valence 0.322 against a 0.5 neutral midpoint. Q1 0.175, Q3 0.502 — three-quarters of the crate sits at or below neutral brightness [DERIVED — HIGH]. He plays heavy, not happy [gloss MED].

The extremes name the register's boundaries:

- Brightest: Fred again.. & Blanco — "solo" (0.935), then Matroda & KLP "Bullshit" (0.83), bbno$ "it boy" (0.823), JENNIE "like JENNIE" (0.815). The pop flank — small, deliberate, the crate's daylight.
- Darkest: Romain Garcia "Next To You" (0.029), Heyz "Fantasy" (0.035), Circadian "Hold That Sucker Down" (0.036).

The bright tail is worth reading as a filter output rather than a filter failure. Four tracks above 0.80 in a 232-track box is not noise; it is a *permit* — the filter allows daylight in small, labeled doses (the Fred again.. cluster, the pop flank), which is precisely how you know the darkness is selected rather than default. A crate that never admitted brightness would be a habit; a crate that admits four bright tracks has a rule with an exception clause [INFERRED — MED].

What the filter rejects: euphoria as a sustained register. Compare the FAVS log's pop-punk pole (New Found Glory, All Time Low — major-key, high-valence by genre) — present in the library, absent from the box. The register filter engages for the crate the same way the energy floor does: the library remembers the bright stuff; the instrument doesn't carry it.

Convergence check against the corpus: the dark valence (0.322) sits alongside the "dark valence 0.322" finding Dan himself confirmed in the 2026-09-11 music thread [OPERATOR-adjacent — his confirmation of the forensics]. The wiki's film canon (Taxi Driver, The King of Comedy, There Will Be Blood, The Witch — precision, dread, unmasking pictures) and the art favorites (24 of 25 tagged wound/observer/collapse/glitch/rupture/fortress) run the same register in other media. The valence filter is not music-specific; it is the aesthetic register applied to music [INFERRED — MED, cross-domain].

Falsifier: a sustained bright bloc (valence > 0.6) above 20% of any working crate. The four-track permit is the finding to beat.

## Filter 5: mid-fame (the popularity non-criterion)

Mean Spotify popularity 43.4, median 43.0, range 0–89 [DERIVED — HIGH]. Dead-center mid.

This is filed as a filter but it is really the *absence* of one — and the absence is the finding. Popularity is a Spotify-side metric, not his; the claim is about the absence of a pattern [MED, stated plainly]. He neither mines obscurity (no sub-20 cluster that would signal crate-digger status-seeking) nor chases charts (no top-40 concentration that would signal hit-following). The record either works or it doesn't, at whatever fame level it sits.

What the non-filter rejects: **status as a selection criterion, in both directions.** This is worth stating because the obvious alternative readings both fail cleanly:

- *Obscurity-mining* would predict a left-skewed distribution (median well below 30) plus label concentration on boutique imprints. Observed: median 43, 162 labels with none above 6 tracks. Dead.
- *Chart-chasing* would predict a right-skewed distribution plus major-label concentration. Observed: the majors present (Atlantic Records UK 6) sit at the same 2.6% ceiling as everyone else. Dead.

The mid-fame distribution is what you get when the ear is doing the selecting and the ego is not involved — which converges with the no-loyalties filter below. A selector who followed names would show fame clustering around the names he follows. He doesn't follow names.

Falsifier: any working crate with a bimodal popularity distribution (obscure-digger + hit-chaser two-hump). The single mid hump is the finding.

## Filter 6: no loyalties (the scene over names)

321 unique artists across 232 tracks. 162 unique labels. The most-represented artist (Fred again..) is 6 tracks — 2.6% of the crate. The most-represented labels (Insomniac Records 6, Atlantic Records UK 6) sit at the same ceiling. Then rSUN 5; NGHTMRE, Diplo, AC Slater, [[wiki/interests/favorites/music/artists/lyny|LYNY]], Odd Mob at 4; a tail of 3s (Mau P, YDG, Ashez, Jaenga, AHEE, Levity, ERBES, Wreckno, INZO, Know Good, CHYL) [DERIVED — HIGH].

A curator's breadth, not a fan's depth. He follows the *scene*, not imprints or names.

What the filter rejects: fandom as an intake mechanism. Contrast the FAVS music log, where [[wiki/interests/favorites/music/artists/jpegmafia|JPEGMAFIA]] leads at 13 entries and the emo canon clusters visibly — the library *has* loyalties; the crate doesn't admit them. The 2025 re-entry's intake ran on Release Radar and the 33% skip test, both loyalty-blind mechanisms (see The Intake Pipeline below). The filter is structural, not attitudinal: even if he wanted to follow an artist, the pipeline wouldn't let artist identity drive selection — the skip test happens at 33% in, before the name on the track has had time to matter [INFERRED — MED].

The 2.6% ceiling is worth pausing on because it is *so* flat. In a 232-track box, random sampling from a scene would produce more concentration than this if any artist were being deliberately over-weighted — and less if the sampling were truly uniform. The observed ceiling (6/232) with a long tail of 2s and 1s is the signature of an ear that likes *a track* and moves on. The unit of selection is the record, never the discography. This is the same unit the digging workflow operates on (see below): audition one track, verdict one track, no artist-level priors carried between auditions.

Convergence with the alias record: the four production aliases ([[wiki/interests/music/aliases/sloppp|SLOPPP]] → [[wiki/interests/music/aliases/mogzart|MOGZART]] → MOGGED-UP → [[wiki/interests/music/aliases/gripnotic|GRIPNOTIC]]) are the same non-loyalty applied to himself — names are clocks, not identities [[wiki/mind/synthesis/alias-as-periodization|alias-as-periodization]]. The filter that won't follow another artist's name is the same operation as the one that won't keep his own.

Falsifier: any crate where one artist exceeds 10%. The ceiling has held across the master crate and all nine VIP crates.

## Filter 7: recency (ammunition, not canon)

138 of 232 tracks (59.5%) released in 2025; 164 (70.7%) in 2024–2025. The tail: 2023: 20, 2022: 13, 2021: 8, 2020: 7, 2019: 6, 2016: 5, 2017: 3, 2018: 2, 2015: 2, 2012: 1, 2002: 1 [DERIVED — HIGH]. The tail back to 2002 is a handful of anchors, not a second era.

This is a *current* crate — a DJ buying this month's records, not a nostalgia box. The build was burst-like and dated: added-to-playlist stamps cluster 2025-10-31 (129 tracks), 2025-11-05 (32), 2025-11-21 (45), 2025-11-08 (7), 2025-11-11 (2), 2025-12-09 (16), 2025-12-22 (1) — refined by the 170-vs-232 diff to four waves: **Oct 31 (170) → Nov 21 (+45) → Dec 9 (+16) → Dec 22 (+1)** [DERIVED — HIGH].

What the filter rejects: the archive. Zero exact track overlap with the 5,321-track historical dump (normalized artist+title matching; 17 title-only collisions, nearly all generic one-word titles — "shake," "lost," "goons" — probable false positives, true overlap ~zero) [DERIVED — HIGH on counts, MED on the false-positive judgment]. Only 16 of 1,913 liked tracks appear in the dump. The re-entry touched *nothing* from the archive. The selection function is *this season's* box.

The recency filter is also what makes the crate disposable by design — and the disposability is the point. A canon is built to last; ammunition is built to be fired. The four build waves, the late-64 additions shifting the profile housier (energy 0.808 vs 0.849 for the first 170, tempo 130 vs 133, valence 0.30 vs 0.331, stutter-house wing arriving in the tail), the two removals between snapshot and final (HOOLIGANS version-swapped for the Ghastly-featuring version, ELECTRIC LOVE cut outright — the only melodic-bass removal, the genre barely existing in the crate otherwise) — this is a living box being tuned, not a monument being erected [DERIVED — HIGH on the wave medians and the removals].

The VIP crates extend the recency filter into 2026 intake: MaxOut (33 from 2026), ULTRA_DEMi (31 from 2026), GOODBYE_BPD_GIRLZ (31 from 2026), ALPHABET_BOYS_JAN26 (January 2026 incoming), FATSO_FLATSO (23 of 25 from 2026). MASTER_COLLECTED_CRATES (489 rows, the merged super-crate) carries the 2026 intake layer — 202 of its 307 non-crate rows are 2026 releases [DERIVED — HIGH]. The filter didn't expire with the master crate; it is the standing intake rule.

Falsifier: a working crate with >30% pre-2020 content that isn't the ALPHABET_BOYS catalog-dig exception (that crate's job is explicitly older releases — 10 tracks from 2019–2023 — which proves the rule by being the one box allowed to break it).

## Filter 8: electronic-only (the acousticness wall)

Median acousticness 0.019. Q1 0.005, Q3 0.074 [DERIVED — HIGH]. Fully electronic; no organic instruments to speak of. Danceability median 0.701 (high but not extreme — the halftime and dnb wings score lower, correctly for their genres). Loudness median −4.98 dB: modern mastered-to-the-ceiling dance music, release-quality masters, not demos [MED on the mastering-norms gloss].

What the filter rejects: anything with a room in it. Guitars, drums recorded in space, vocals recorded dry — the entire organic-signal universe. The one track that breaches the wall is the Shostakovich waltz (acousticness 0.984, the crate maximum by far) — and the breach is so total that it reads as deliberate rather than leaky. Filters don't fail at 0.984; they get overridden [INFERRED — MED].

The acousticness wall converges with the production record: thirteen years, four aliases, sub-bass signature 63–85% — the making side never left the electronic room either. ## The Intake Pipeline

The filters above describe what survives. This section describes the machine that does the surviving — the intake pipeline Dan reported on 2026-09-12 [OPERATOR], now measured against the 2026-09-11 forensics. The pipeline has five stages, one triage rule, and no pending state.

### The ascent ladder

Dan's own taxonomy of a track's climb, reported 2026-09-12 [OPERATOR]:

1. **Release Radar** — the feed. Spotify's algorithmic new-release playlist, personalized. The intake mouth.
2. **The 33% skip test** — the triage (see below).
3. **Liked** — the working library. The canonical liked union measures 1,913 unique URIs across three Exportify snapshots (no single export is complete; the oldest holds 498 URIs absent from both June 2026 exports — see the purge below).
4. **FUNDAMENTAL FLAW (AUG / SEP)** — the current crate playlist. The intake crate: what survived triage *this season*.
5. **The running yearly playlist** — the annual layer.
6. **MASTERLIST ("DAN FRANK MASTERLIST VIP CRATE")** — the honor roll.
7. **ALL TIME TOP 100 ("DAN FRANK ALL TIME TOP 100 VIP CRATE")** — the ultimate honor. The canon, such as it is.

A track climbs rung by rung. Nothing skips. The ladder is the only promotion path — which is why the late-build workflow change matters (see below): when 20 crate tracks went direct-to-crate without passing through Liked, the ladder was bypassed, and the bypass is itself data about the build's urgency.

### The 33% skip test (the triage)

Dan's own description of the audition, 2026-09-12 [OPERATOR]: *"Play > skip to 33% in > do nothing OR like track OR like + into the current playlist."*

Three facts about this rule do most of the analytical work:

**First, the skip point.** 33% in is past the intro and into the first drop or its approach — for dance music, the structural thesis of the track. He auditions like a DJ cueing: skip the intro, test the drop. The October 28 account to [[wiki/people/tom|Tom]] Maison corroborates the structural listening from the other side: dance music is *"literally 98% about evoking emotional response rather than crafting something catchy,"* and the genre's build/drop architecture ("intro, build, drop reset, build, drop") was what he had to *relearn* — harder to internalize than the verse-chorus songwriting he grew up on [OPERATOR]. The skip test is the re-learned competence made into a habit: the ear goes to the structure, not the hook.

**Second, the triage is binary-plus.** Do nothing, or like, or like-plus-playlist. There is no "maybe" state, no save-for-later, no pending audition queue. This is the Ti-closure pattern [[wiki/mind/profile/intp|intp]] documents — verdicts, once reached, don't get relitigated — running on intake. The pipeline has no pending state because the cognition has no pending state. A track is either in or it isn't, and the decision happens at 33%.

**Third, the test is loyalty-blind by construction.** At 33% into a first audition, the name on the track hasn't had time to matter — the verdict lands on the drop, not the discography. This is the mechanism behind Filter 6 (no loyalties): the pipeline *cannot* select by artist identity because the decision point precedes identity recognition. The 2.6% artist ceiling isn't an attitude; it's what a 33%-in triage produces.

### The instruments (2026-09-14)

On 2026-09-14 Dan commissioned the pipeline's instrumentation — two static web apps that operationalize this entry's measurements. [[wiki/work/tech/projects/musictrainer-autopsy|MusicTrainer]] scores every Discover Weekly and Release Radar track 1–10 with skip/like triage and an ADDED? verdict (keep = liked + into the current playlist), running timestamped prediction locks against the outcomes — the 33% test made legible, week by week. **AUTOPSY** dissects the keepers: 16 what-did-it drivers per track (drop, sound design, bass weight, vocal-as-texture, set utility…) plus a kill-one load-bearing-element pick, ranking attributes by lift over base keep rate. If the filter stack describes the ear's architecture, the instruments measure it live — MusicTrainer captures the verdicts, AUTOPSY captures what the verdicts were *for*.

## The 33% Skip Test as Embodied Practice

Why 33%? In dance-music structure, the first third of a track is intro → build → the approach to the first drop — the track's structural thesis. The skip test ("play > skip to 33% in") lands the ear *at the drop or its approach*, skipping the DJ-intro (which exists for mixing, not listening). He auditions like a DJ cueing: skip the intro, test the drop.

This is genre-specific competence. For verse-chorus songwriting, 33% lands you mid-first-verse — you'd audition the *hook*, not the structure. For dance music, the hook *is* the structure: the drop is the thesis, the build is the argument, the intro is throat-clearing. The October 28 account says the build/drop architecture was what he had to *relearn* ("intro, build, drop reset, build, drop") — harder than the verse-chorus songwriting "encoded in his brain." The skip test is the relearned competence made into a habit: the ear goes to the structure because the structure is where the decision lives.

The triage's binary-plus form (do nothing / like / like+playlist) maps onto the structure too: at the drop, the verdict is *immediate and bodily* — the track either moves you or it doesn't, and "moves you" for dance music is a physical fact (energy, low end, the drop's impact), not a judgment. There's no "maybe" because the drop doesn't negotiate. This is why the aesthetic filters engage at Like time (the liked≈crate finding): the verdict happens at the body, in seconds, and the body doesn't do pending states.

## The Two Systems of Evaluation (rated books, unrated music)

The FAVS log's rating asymmetry — 145 rated rows, all books (120) and art (25), zero of 1,860 music rows rated — is worth a full section, because it's the library's own statement about how judgment works in each domain [DERIVED — HIGH].

The 5★ book shelf names the canon: Dan Carlin's *Death Throes of the Republic*, Jonathan Karl's *Betrayal*, *The Divider*, Whitney Webb's *One Nation Under Blackmail*, Plutarch's *Complete Works*, Annie Jacobsen's *Nuclear War*, Parenti's *Assassination of Julius Caesar*, *American Prometheus*, Goldsworthy's *Caesar* — the Roman-republic + Trump-era-journalism canon, exactly as the wiki has it. All 25 art rows are 5/5 — one per creator, the Architecture A pattern (closing bounded sets through unique witnesses).

Books get stars because book judgment is **verbal and deliberate**: you finish the book, you consider what it argued, you assign the stars. The judgment happens *after* the consumption, in language, at leisure. Music gets no stars because music judgment is **embodied and immediate**: the verdict happens at 33%, at the drop, in the body. You don't assign stars to timbre — you either keep the record or you don't.

This is the same binary-plus triage as the skip test, which means the unrated music log and the no-maybe pipeline are one operation, not two. And it converges with Filter 0 from an unexpected direction: if lyrics were processed as language — if songs were *arguments* — you'd expect the same deliberative rating behavior as books. The absence of music ratings is Filter 0's behavioral signature in the metadata: where there's no language to deliberate about, there's no deliberation. The stars stop where the words stop mattering.

The art ratings add the third point of the triangle: 25 artworks, all 5/5, one per creator — ratings as *inclusion markers*, not discriminations. In Architecture A domains (books, art), the rating is part of the closing operation: rated, filed, done. In the music domain (Architecture B), there's no closing operation — the clusters are *used*, not covered — so there's nothing for a rating to close. The rating asymmetry isn't a quirk of the spreadsheet. It's the two architectures made visible in the metadata.

## The Skip Test's Blind Spots (what the triage misses)

Every filter has its misses, and the entry's honesty standard requires naming them:

**The slow builders.** Tracks whose thesis arrives after 33% — long ambient intros, delayed drops, structures that earn their payoff late — get skipped before the verdict point. The triage is calibrated for dance music's intro/build/drop architecture; anything with a different dramatic curve is systematically under-auditioned. The melodic-techno tag's presence in the crate (8 tracks, energy 0.790 — the lowest tag median) suggests *some* slow builders survive, but the triage selects against the form.

**The growers.** Tracks that need five plays to land — the ones that become favorites through repetition rather than impact — can't survive a binary-plus first audition. The ladder has no "revisit" rung. This is the cost of no pending state: the system never changes its mind, which means it's always right about impact and systematically wrong about slow affection. The emo canon's library residence (loved, remembered, never boxed) is partly this: those tracks were acquired under a different intake regime, before the skip test existed.

**The bypass as blind spot.** The 20 direct-to-crate late additions skipped the aesthetic rung entirely — they never got the 33% test. If the skip test is the taste and the crate is the profession, the bypass is profession without taste: utility-selected, aesthetically unverified. The wave-2 dip (energy 0.806, the lowest wave median) is where most of the bypass tracks landed. The build's hottest urgency produced its least-filtered wave. The entry files this without alarm — 20 of 232 is 8.6%, inside the visa program's allowance — but the blind spot is real: the triage can't catch what never goes through it.

**The name-mishearing at intake.** If track names arrive as phonetic approximations, the skip test's verdict attaches to a *misremembered* track as often as a remembered one. The ladder's later rungs (yearly playlist, MASTERLIST) re-encounter the track by its *file*, not its name — so the mishearing washes out downstream. But at the Like stage, the unit being liked is partly phonetic fiction. The standing verification rule (check names against Spotify before acting) exists because the intake's first rung is the least reliable about identity.

## The Mouth of the Pipeline (Release Radar and the black box)

The intake pipeline's first rung is **Release Radar** — Spotify's algorithmic new-release feed, personalized from listening history. Every track the filter stack ever sees enters through this mouth. Three consequences follow, and the third is the one worth sitting with:

**1. The mouth is loyalty-blind by design.** Release Radar serves novelty weighted by past engagement, not artist identity. A new track from an unknown Lithuanian bass producer and a new Fred again.. single arrive through the same slot, judged by the same 33% test. This is Filter 6's structural guarantee: the pipeline *can't* select on names because its mouth doesn't lead with names. The 2.6% artist ceiling starts here — not as an attitude about fandom, but as a property of the feed.

**2. The mouth is recency-enforcing.** Release Radar serves *new releases*. The recency filter (Filter 7) doesn't begin at the crate — it begins at rung 1. The pipeline's mouth only opens onto the present; the past has to enter through other doors (the ALPHABET_BOYS catalog-dig crate, the manual old-canon likes). The 59.5% 2025 figure is therefore *understated* as a filter measurement: the feed pre-filters for recency before the ear ever gets a vote. The crate's recency is overdetermined — feed plus filter agreeing.

**3. The mouth is a black box he doesn't control.** This is the point. Seven of the eight filters are *his* — his ear, his skip test, his ladder, his audit. But the candidate pool is Spotify's: an algorithm whose ranking function he can't see, whose training data is his own past engagement (a feedback loop), whose coverage is limited to what's on Spotify. The filter stack is a precision instrument pointed at a candidate pool it didn't choose. The taste-as-filter-stack is really *taste-as-filter-stack-on-Spotify's-candidate-pool* — and the entry's measurements inherit that scope condition.

The feedback-loop structure matters: Release Radar personalizes from listening history, the skip test's likes *become* listening history, and the November 2025 spike (628 likes) trained the feed on the re-entry's own output. The pipeline eats its tail — the more bass he likes, the more bass the feed serves, the more bass he likes. This is visible in the liked timeline's shape: the detonation isn't just the switch flipping on, it's the loop *accelerating*. May (151) → July (122) → September (270) → October (153) → November (628): each month's intake trains the next month's feed. The filter stack is precise; the candidate pool is a mirror. The precision is real, but it's precision applied to a reflection.

What the mouth *can't* supply bounds the taste: no Bandcamp exclusives, no SoundCloud-only bootlegs (ironic, given his own output lives there), no vinyl rips, no unreleased dubs — unless they cross into Spotify. The pipeline is Spotify-contained, which the wiki already documents as his standing operational fact ("his whole music operation runs inside Spotify; local files are the exception"). The filter stack's jurisdiction isn't just the box — it's the platform. Everything measured here is Spotify-mediated listening. The entry's numbers describe the ear *through* the platform, not the ear in itself.

## The Delivery Profiles (what the gods actually sound like to him)

The FAVS poles named the lyricist gods ([[wiki/interests/favorites/music/artists/jpegmafia|JPEGMAFIA]] 13, Kanye 11, Elliott Smith 7) as the calibration cluster — voice-as-delivery, not bars. The claim deserves its per-artist expansion, because "delivery" is doing specific work in each case [INFERRED — MED, the only coherent reading under Filter 0; the fandom counts are ATTESTED]:

**JPEGMAFIA — flow as percussion.** Peggy's rapping is rhythmic information first: abrupt cadence shifts, bars that start mid-thought, vocal timbre changes deployed like drum fills. Tracks like "BALD!" or "HAZARD DUTY PAY!" don't ask to be followed lyrically — they ask to be *withstood* rhythmically. The voice is a drum machine with opinions. Under Filter 0, this is the purest form of the appetite: language present, meaning absent, rhythm total. The crate's chop/texture bloc (47.4%) is this appetite with the words removed entirely — Peggy minus the verses, which is what the ear was hearing anyway.

**Kanye West — cadence as architecture, chops as method.** The soul-sample era (*The College Dropout*, *Late Registration*) is *literally* the chop/texture technique: voices sampled, pitched up, looped as instrumental material — "Through the Wire"'s Chaka Khan chipmunk-soul is a vocal-instrument composition. Kanye's own production is the filter stack's ancestor: he was turning voices into texture before the crate existed. The later eras (the *Yeezus* industrial bark, the gospel choir as massed texture on *Jesus Is King*) keep the voice-as-material logic across every aesthetic turn. The fandom isn't for the bars — it's for the *method*: the voice as building material.

**Elliott Smith — the whisper as texture, the regulator.** Double-tracked close-mic'd vocals, fingerpicked guitar beds, melodies that resolve downward. The quietest pole in a library otherwise built on bass warfare — and the emotional regulator ([[wiki/mind/synthesis/music-as-identity|music-as-identity]] mode 3). Under Filter 0, the Elliott Smith fandom is the proof that the qualifier doesn't flatten everything into aggression: the ear hears *grain* — the breath in the mic, the fingers on the strings, the doubled voice's phase shimmer — as textural information with emotional weight. The 8 Elliott Smith tracks in the purged 498 are the working set letting go of the regulator while the library keeps it: the fandom lives in memory, which is where regulators live. You don't play the regulator in the set. You keep it for when the set's over.

The three profiles converge on one sentence: **the gods are worshipped for what the voice *does*, not what it *says*.** Flow, chop, whisper — three techniques of voice-as-material, three calibrations for the crate's texture bloc. The calibration cluster isn't a separate taste. It's the standard the instrument is tuned against.
