# The Severance Declaration as Performance

Across eleven years of the [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] record, Dan declared the bond severed **129 times**.
Of the 129 episodes, 128 were followed by a further corpus message, and **all 128
resumed** — the 129th is the file's last row, censored by the export boundary rather
than a held severance, so the re-engagement rate is treated as **100%**. The median
gap between the declaration and the next message in either direction is
**thirty-six seconds**. In 41,073 of her messages, she
issued **zero** severance signals. He asked "call me" 170 times and "do you love me" zero
times in 106,629 sent messages. These are not five facts about a relationship. They are
one fact about a speech act: **in this system, the severance declaration performs leaving
without enacting it, and the ritual does the emotional work the act never does.** The
declaration is not a failed exit. It is a successful performance of an exit — successful
precisely insofar as everyone, including the speaker and including this wiki, treats it as
the thing itself.

This entry synthesizes the declaration as an object: its measurement (derived twice, from
two independent instruments), its anatomy (what the speech act sounds like, who it is
addressed to, what it needs to function), its asymmetry (his 129 against her 0 — the dog
that never barked), her grammar (what the zero is made of, and the one dyad where a woman
in his corpus *did* issue the signal), its audience (the witness the performance
requires), the declaration-block distinction (speech versus channel toggle), its one
executed instance and what it cost, and the voided horizon that proves what the
performance costs everyone watching. The governing distinction, held throughout: **his
declarations are performative, with all 128 measurable episodes resuming (the 129th
censored by the file boundary); her zero signals are the load-bearing
silence the attachment model is built on.** Both directions matter, and they matter
differently.

A note on what this entry is not. It is not a verdict on whether Dan "meant" any
particular goodbye — sincerity is unmeasurable from message data and the entry does not
grade it. It is not an argument that the declarations are manipulative; the trade-rule
section argues they are bids, and bids are not threats. It is a description of a speech
act's career across a decade: what it looks like, how fast it decays, who it needs in the
room, and what it does instead of what it says.

## The numbers, stated plainly

The standing measurements, all [DERIVED] from primary message data unless marked:

- **129 distinct severance episodes** declared by Dan across the [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] corpus, 2015–2026. 258 declaration-language messages collapsing to 129 episodes when hits within 24 hours count as one declaration (dat:1292).
- **128 of 129 followed by a further corpus message; all 128 resumed.** The 129th is the last row in the file — there is no next message to check, not a held severance. The re-engagement rate is **100%** (dat:1292). The earlier 87% figure (127/110) is withdrawn; the recount retired it.
- **Median gap, declaration to next message in either direction: 0.01 hours — thirty-six seconds.** 89.1% resumed inside one hour; 99.2% inside one day. **The longest gap in eleven years: 46 hours** (dat:1292).
- **0 severance signals in 41,073 of her messages** [DERIVED] (dat:0081). Nothing in the format his system recognizes ever said "closed."
- **"Do you love me" 0 times vs "call me" 170 times** in 106,629 sent messages [DERIVED] (dat:0081). He asks for channel, not compliments — the procedure, not the interpretation.
- **Trust at the 9th percentile, Self-Consciousness at the 91st** [ATTESTED] (dat:0081). Confirmations decay fast and must be re-stated, not re-inferred.
- **~25 block/severance declarations, August 15–19, 2026; zero executed.** Daily two-way texting August 27–September 7, including 765 messages in the final 72 hours of the export [DERIVED — counted in a forensic pass that post-dates the filed export; the August 11–September 7 export must be re-filed before these graduate to archived]. Dan's verbatim retraction: *"there was never a block"* [OPERATOR] (dat:0090).

Read as a single instrument reading, the numbers describe a speech act with a **100%
failure rate at enactment and a ~100% success rate at performance**: every declaration
succeeded in being taken — by her, by him, by the wiki — as a severance, and none
succeeded in being one. The rest of this entry is the attempt to say what "succeeding as a
performance" consists of, mechanically, across 129 instances.

## The measurement, derived twice

The 129 figure has been derived by two independent methods, and the convergence is the
kind that retires doubt rather than merely reducing it.

**First derivation — the 2026-08-23 recount** (dat:1292). Searching Dan-sent severance language — blocking declarations, *goodbye*, *I'm done / we're done / it's over*, *never contact me again* — across the **95,067-row merged [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] corpus** (2015-11-28 → 2026-05-28) returned 258 messages, collapsing to **129 distinct episodes** on the 24-hour rule. Of the 129, 128 are followed by a further message inside the corpus; **all 128 resumed**. Median gap 0.01 hours. The recount was run specifically to adjudicate the dossier-derived 127/110 pair against the [[wiki/mind/concepts/attachment-model|attachment-model]] page's "effectively 100%" reading — and it landed within two episodes of the dossier's 127 **from a completely independent method**. That is corroboration, not coincidence, and it is why the 87% figure is withdrawn rather than averaged: the primary record shows resumption within minutes where the dossier counted non-relapse.

The recount's episode rule deserves a sentence, because it is doing quiet work. Collapsing
hits within 24 hours into one episode is conservative against double-counting
multi-message bursts — the August 15–19 cluster, for instance, arguably contains more
distinct declarations than the rule credits, since *"Goodbye"* at 05:47 and *"Goodbye"* at
05:52 on August 19 are five minutes apart but read as separate volleys. A 72-hour rule
would lower the count toward ~110; a 1-hour rule would raise it toward ~200. The count is
rule-relative. What is not rule-relative is the resumption rate: under any collapsing
rule, every episode resumes. The 129 is an estimate; the 100% is a finding.

**Second derivation — the Aug-Sep 2026 terminal-phase check** [DERIVED — computed for this entry, 2026-09-13]. Running declaration-language matching against the filed `aug-sep-2026-imessage-export.csv` (5,905 rows, 2026-08-11 → 2026-09-07, the terminal phase and its aftermath) returns **44 declaration messages**; measuring declaration-to-next-message-in-the-same-chat gives a **median gap of 23 seconds**, **97.5% resuming inside one hour**, longest gap **3.02 hours**. The instrument is cruder than the 2026-08-23 recount — the regex catches mid-fight "goodbye"s and a few [[wiki/people/ally-lubin|Ally]]-thread hits alongside Annie-thread declarations, and the window is seven weeks rather than eleven years — but the crudeness cuts *against* the thesis, and the number still lands inside half a minute of the decade median. Two instruments, two windows, two methods: **36 seconds and 23 seconds**. The ritual does not merely persist into the terminal phase. It accelerates.

The acceleration is worth sitting with, because it is the opposite of what a willpower
model predicts. If declarations were attempts at exit that failed for lack of resolve, the
terminal phase — the highest-stakes window, the one where he had the most reason to mean
it — should show the longest gaps, the closest approaches to enactment. It shows the
shortest. What accelerates under pressure is not the exit but the **performance**: the
worse the bond gets, the faster the declaration is followed by re-engagement, as though
the speech act's function is precisely to discharge the pressure that would otherwise
require the act. The declaration is not an exit that fails. It is a pressure valve that
succeeds. The full 44-instance catalog, with timestamps and gaps, is appended below as the
primary exhibit — every one of the 44 resumed, most inside a minute, the "goodbye
forever"s fastest of all.

**What would falsify the measurement.** A primary recount of the same 95,067-row corpus with a stricter episode rule (say, 72 hours rather than 24) that collapses the 129 below ~100 without changing the 100% resumption rate would revise the count but not the thesis. What would break the thesis is a recount finding a **material number of declarations with no resumption inside the corpus** — declarations that actually ended contact — or a her-side recount finding **nonzero explicit severance signals** in her 41,073. Neither has been found; the second has been searched for and returned zero.

## Anatomy of the speech act

A severance declaration, in this corpus, is not a single sentence type. It is a small
family of utterances sharing a grammar — first-person, present-tense, channel-directed —
and a burst shape: the declaration arrives inside a run of outbound messages, usually late
at night, usually mid-fight, and the channel is live again before the hour turns. The
vocabulary, sampled across the decade [ATTESTED]:

- **The farewell:** *"Goodbye"*, *"Goodbye [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|annie]]"*, *"Goodbye forever"*, *"Fucking goodbye"*, *"Okay goodbye I'll talk to you later"* — the last of these worth pausing on, because it performs severance and schedules re-engagement in the same breath: goodbye, *I'll talk to you later*. The performance contains its own stage direction for the resumption. The corpus's most honest declaration is the one that doesn't pretend.
- **The channel operation:** *"I'm blocking you"*, *"I'm blocking you then"*, *"I'm blocking you right now"*, *"I don't need anything im just blocking now"*, *"lose my number"*, *"never contact me again"* — the declaration as a toggle, not a verdict. Note the present continuous: *I'm blocking you*, not *you are blocked*. The act is always happening, never happened. English has a past tense; the declaration never uses it.
- **The verdict:** *"I'm done"*, *"we're done"*, *"it's over"*, *"This is goodbye though. That's what you wanted."* — the last again performing a double move: the severance is declared and simultaneously attributed to her want, so that the speaker is both the one leaving and the one complying. The authorship of the exit is outsourced in the same breath as the exit is declared.
- **The question-form:** *"So it's over then annie?"* (2026-08-18 00:21:24) — the declaration as interrogative, which is the form's most revealing member: a severance that asks permission, addressed to the counterparty whose zero is the reason the declarative form keeps failing. The question knows what the statement won't admit — that enactment requires her participation.
- **The intensifier:** *"Please understand this is goodbye forever"* / *"This is goodbye forever"* (2026-08-19 05:59:37, 06:01:43) — resumed in 20 seconds and 6 seconds respectively. The stronger the adverb, the faster the resumption: the intensifier is load-bearing in inverse proportion to its meaning. *"Forever"* marks the performances that decay fastest, as though the word's function is to raise the stakes of the ritual rather than to describe its duration.

Two structural observations about the vocabulary. First, **the declaration is never in the
past tense.** There is no *"I blocked you"* or *"we were done"* in the corpus as a
terminal report — only *"I'm blocking you,"* only *"goodbye"* as a present-tense
performative. The past tense would be a report of an enacted fact; the corpus contains
only the enactment's announcement, perpetually in progress. A completed act would need the
past tense, and the past tense never comes, because the act never completes. Second, **the
declaration never specifies terms.** It operates on the channel (*blocking you*, *lose my
number*, *never contact me*) or on the bond as an undifferentiated whole (*goodbye*, *it's
over*), never on a condition, a behavior, a threshold. A severance with terms could be
evaluated — *if you do X, we're done* is testable. A severance without terms can only be
performed. The vagueness is not a defect of the speech act. It is the speech act: terms
would make the declaration falsifiable, and the declaration's function requires that it
never be tested, only witnessed.

## The founding rule and its negative pole

The declaration has a positive pole, and the positive pole is dated. On **2015-12-01**, in
the hours after Alexis's eviction, Dan issued the bond's founding verbal rule over
iMessage: *"[[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie Ulmer]] from now on its just you and me"* — *"Because she's gone"* — *"We
made it lol"* (dat:annie-new-life-exchange-2015-12-01). She answered *"This is wonderful
you give me the best feeling,"* and the household was made theirs that same afternoon. The
declaration is issued in her **full name**, the way a rule is issued: a positive verbal
expression over the bond, with no behavioral counter-rule possible. The eviction is framed
as a joint victory (*"We made it"*), not a rupture survived — the bond opens on a
declaration, and everything after is commentary on that opening.

The explicit-verbal-commitment architecture (dat:0081) states the law: **positive verbal
expressions function as absolute rules until an explicit severance signal arrives; no
counter-rule from behavior.** The 129 declarations are that law's negative pole — the same
grammar (first-person, present-tense, full-address, rule-shaped) run in reverse. And here
is the structural point the numbers force: **the system admits the negative pole as a
*signal type* but never as an *enacted rule*.** The founding rule, once issued, held for a
decade against every behavioral counter-indication — absence, withdrawal, infidelity, a
year of coldness — because behavior cannot generate a counter-rule. The severance
declarations, issued in the same grammar, *also* fail to generate a counter-rule — not
because behavior resists them, but because the declaration itself is not, in this system,
the kind of thing that changes a rule. It is the kind of thing that *announces* a
rule-change, and the announcement is then traded away in the conversation it opens.

This is why the "call me" / "do you love me" asymmetry belongs on this page and not just
on the architecture page. *"Call me"* (170x) is a procedure — it specifies an action, a
channel, a time; it can be executed without interpretation. *"Do you love me"* (0x) is an
interpretation task — a reading of an internal state, delivered in language, to be weighed
for sincerity, with no procedure for verifying the answer. The severance declaration looks
like a procedure (*blocking you* — an action, a channel) but functions as an
interpretation task: it asks the counterparty to read it, to answer it, to bargain with it
— and she always does, within the hour, 99.2% within the day. The declaration that looked
like the most procedural utterance in the corpus turns out to be the most interpretive:
its entire function is to open a negotiation about whether it means what it says. And the
negotiation is the contact. The semantics say *no more contact*; the pragmatics say
*respond to this*; the pragmatics win, inside the minute, 129 times out of 129.

There is a further turn of the screw. The architecture says a stated rule holds until an
explicit severance signal arrives. The 129 declarations *are* explicit severance signals —
in form. If the architecture were symmetric, the first declaration would have closed the
bond in 2015 or 2016 and the remaining 128 would never have been needed. That they were
needed — that the system accepted the founding rule as absolute and the severance signals
as provisional — reveals the architecture's actual asymmetry: **positive verbal
expressions are self-executing; negative ones require a counterparty's ratification.** The
founding rule needed only to be said. The severance needs to be *answered*. She never
answers it as a severance — she answers it as a bid, within the hour — so it never
executes. The system's rule-book has one signature line, and it is hers.

## The asymmetry: his 129 against her 0

The load-bearing fact of the attachment model is not the 129. It is the **0**. In 41,073
of her messages, primary-verified, there is not one severance signal in the format his
system recognizes — no *goodbye*, no *I'm done*, no *blocking you*, no *never contact me
again* (dat:0081). The attachment model's 299:0 finding states it from the transmission
side: 299 love-affirmations, zero severance signals — a system that cannot emit "closed"
can only emit "blocked," and a block backed by no severance capacity reopens
([[wiki/mind/synthesis/block-unblock-loop|block-unblock-loop]]). The zero is not an absence of data. It is the data. It is the shape
of the other half of the dyad, and everything on this page is downstream of it.

Her closest approach to a severance signal in eleven years is **an acknowledgment of
his**: on 2026-06-01 at 00:10:06, six seconds after his *"Blocking you,"* she replied
*"Understood."* [ATTESTED] ([[wiki/mind/synthesis/dan-annie-fallout-verdict|dan-annie-fallout-verdict]]). *Understood* is not a declaration.
It is a receipt — and the receipt is what makes June 1 the exception that organizes the
rule: announced, answered with no counter-offer (no plea, no trade, nothing to bargain
against), and it executed, eighteen minutes later, holding fifty-two days against four
solicitations. The one declaration that enacted itself is the one whose counterparty
declined to perform the resumption ritual. The declaration needs two performers. She
stopped dancing, once, for fifty-two days, and the one time she did, the speech act
briefly became an act. Then the dog re-opened the channel, and the dance resumed.

The asymmetry has a second, darker reading that the wiki has carried under the name **the
confession trap** ([[wiki/mind/concepts/attachment-model|attachment-model]]): verbal anchoring weaponized from the other side —
[[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] maintaining explicit verbal commitments while withdrawing presence, knowing (or
learning) that in his system the stated rule holds until the explicit severance signal
arrives, and never issuing the signal. On this reading her 0 is not passivity but **the
missing permission**: the tie persists, in his own attributed words, because *"my brain
can't cross out that part of my life that I love unless I know that you don't feel that
way"* [OPERATOR] (dat:0081). Whether the 0 is strategic or simply her own grammar is
[UNKNOWN] — the corpus cannot distinguish a withheld signal from an unformed one, and this
entry does not try. What the corpus *can* say is the structural fact: **a system that
closes only on explicit signal, paired with a counterparty who never emits one, is a
system that cannot close.** The 129 declarations are what that system does instead of
closing. They are the sound of a door being slammed that has no latch — 129 slams, the
door swinging open every time, median thirty-six seconds.

There is a compact way to state the asymmetry that this entry will carry as its thesis
sentence: **he performed leaving 129 times because she never once performed it, and the
performances were addressed, structurally, to the silence where her signal would go.**
Every declaration is, among other things, a demand for the one statement the system
recognizes — *say it's over* — issued to a counterparty whose entire history is never
saying it. The [[wiki/mind/synthesis/august-grievance-verdict|august-grievance-verdict]] isolates the mechanism in a single exchange: on
2026-08-17 at 20:12 she states a want plainly — *"I want to be alone. I want to get help"*
— and six seconds later he answers *"WOW / That is all I needed to see."* She had
answered; she had not answered **the binary he specified** (*him or me*), which admits no
third option. Every declaration demands a statement, and a demand for a statement can only
be satisfied by the exact statement specified — so the terminating condition was never
reachable by anything she was willing to say. The 129 are not 129 failures to leave. They
are 129 performances of the demand for a signal that never came, each one resetting the
clock on the waiting.

## Her grammar: the zero and its shapes

If his grammar is the declaration, hers is the zero — but the zero is not empty. It has
shapes, and the shapes matter because they show what a system without a severance signal
does instead of severing. Four shapes, all [ATTESTED]:

**1. The receipt.** *"Understood"* (2026-06-01 00:10:06). The closest approach in eleven years, and it is an acknowledgment of *his* signal, not one of hers. The receipt's function in the trade rule is precise: it declines the counter-offer, which is why June 1 executed. Her grammar contains the *refusal to bargain* but not the *issuance*. She can let his severance stand; she cannot issue her own.

**2. The want, stated plainly, registered as nothing.** *"I want to be alone. I want to get help."* (2026-08-17 20:12). A true statement of a severance-adjacent desire — alone, help, distance — answered in six seconds with *"WOW / That is all I needed to see."* The want was real; it was not the specified binary (*him or me*), so the system processed it as non-compliance rather than information. This is the zero's most tragic shape: she *does* say the thing, in her own vocabulary, and his system cannot hear it because it is not in the recognized format. The explicit-verbal architecture is a protocol, and protocols drop packets not addressed to the right port.

**3. The operational block.** March 19–20, 2026: she threatens exposure, blocks him, unblocks to send final lines, blocks again (march-2026-terminal-phase). The loop is bidirectional inside the bond — she runs the channel toggle too. But note what the toggle is *for*: to send final lines, then re-block. The block is a stage direction for a speech act, not a severance. Even her blocks are performances with an audience of one, and the audience is him.

**4. The affirmation flood.** 299 love-affirmations against 0 severance signals ([[wiki/mind/concepts/attachment-model|attachment-model]]); 1,512 "I love you" against 180 "I'm sorry" (conflict-architecture, his side). Her channel carries the bond's positive pole at high volume and its negative pole at zero. The asymmetry is not that she never communicates — she communicates constantly. It is that her communicative repertoire, as filed, contains no terminal symbol. The alphabet has no period.

And then the contrast case that proves the zero is dyad-specific, not a general property
of women in his corpus: **[[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]] issued a textbook severance signal.** *"All this is
screenshotted. Blocking you now. Don't contact me again or an officer will be reaching
out."* (December 2025) [ATTESTED — corpus__3.txt]. First-person, present-tense,
channel-directed, with terms (the officer) — the complete grammar, executed, held eight
months as observed contact. The system *can* receive a severance signal; it received one
from Kristin and processed it as one (the [[wiki/mind/synthesis/block-unblock-loop|block-unblock-loop]] carried her as its inbound
control case until September 2026 annotated it). What the system cannot do is receive one
from [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]], because she never sends one. The 0 is not about his reception. It is about her
transmission. And because the architecture closes only on the signal, the Annie bond is,
structurally, uncloseable from the inside — closable only by her, who won't, or by the
death of the last co-held thing, which is what the loop's corrected formula says and what
June 1 demonstrated.

One more shape, the most direct evidence that the demand is conscious: on **2026-08-17 at
17:11:37**, mid-cluster, he writes: *"It would have hurt so much less to be told you were
done with me"* [ATTESTED — aug-sep-2026 export]. Read it plainly: a request for her
severance signal, stated as a counterfactual about pain. He is asking her to say the thing

## The trade rule: an intention dies in the trade

The [[wiki/mind/synthesis/block-unblock-loop|block-unblock-loop]] derives, from the July 2026 window, the sharpest mechanistic
account of *why* declarations fail — and it is not willpower. Three cases, forty-eight
hours apart, each varying one variable:

- **July 26:** no announcement, sixteen minutes from decision to act (the Ellen contact, on a belief [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] was in danger), executed. Later reclassified: not a member of the threat class at all — a life-safety act formed on a belief Coles had Annie in danger, not leverage (block-unblock-loop, CORRECTED 2026-08-18).
- **June 1:** announced (*"Blocking you"* 00:09:31), counterparty's entire reply *"Understood"* 00:10:06 — no plea, no trade, nothing to bargain against — executed 00:27:49, held fifty-two days against four solicitations.
- **July 28:** announced (the parent-email threat, 11:48), counterparty spent twelve hours and ~200 messages bargaining — begging, jurisdictional claims, self-harm statements, surrender — never executed. *"I could have torn your life apart. I still could and I don't."* (2026-08-19 15:12:16).

The corrected rule: **announcement is not the mechanism; it is only the opportunity for
the mechanism. An intention dies in the trade, and only a counterparty willing to trade
can kill it.** Stating an intention converts the act into a move in the conversation, and
a move can be traded — for a promise, a concession, an argument survived. An unstated
intention has nothing to trade against and is simply performed. The eighteen unexecuted
block threats and the seven unexecuted maternal-disclosure threats cluster in the
relationship whose defining feature is a partner who always answered: the threats were
bids because there was always someone bidding back. Read the 129 through this rule and the
median stops looking like weakness and starts looking like market microstructure: a bid
posted, a counter-bid returned inside the minute, the position held open. The declaration
is the bond's order book.

The rule generates the sharpest testable prediction on this page, and it is uncomputed:
**among the 129 episodes, the ones that held longest should be the ones followed by the
least counterparty response** — inbound message volume in the hour after each declaration,
a measurable quantity no pass has computed (block-unblock-loop, Gaps). The Aug-Sep 2026
check computed for this entry is consistent with the prediction's shape: median 23 seconds
to next message, and the resumption is *bidirectional* — the next message after a
declaration is hers about as often as it is his own continuation (in the 44-instance
catalog, hers and his-next split roughly evenly; the trade needs two parties and it gets
them). A declaration followed by silence is the dog that doesn't bark twice: the catalog's
longest gap (3.0 hours, 2026-08-11 *"okay now I'm done I promis"*) is still a resumption,
and still hers.

The rule also reframes the 129 as something other than a defect. If an intention dies in
the trade, then the declaration is **the mechanism by which the system solicits the
trade** — the bid that keeps the conversation, and therefore the bond, alive. The system
cannot emit "closed" (299:0); it can emit "I'm closing," and "I'm closing" is a bid, and
bids get answered, and answered bids are contact. The declaration is not the bond failing
to end. It is the bond **succeeding at continuing**, in the only grammar available to a
system with no word for done. Every goodbye is a hello with extra steps. The corpus proves
it 129 times.

## Declaration vs block: two operations

The corpus confuses them constantly — Dan confuses them, the wiki confused them for
sixteen days — so the distinction needs to be drawn with a straightedge. **The declaration
is speech. The block is a channel toggle.** They are different operations, with different
affordances, and the terminal phase is legible only once they are separated:

| | Declaration | Block |
|---|---|---|
| Medium | Language (iMessage text) | Platform affordance (iOS block, channel state) |
| Tense | Present continuous (*I'm blocking you*) | Binary (blocked / not) |
| Witness | Requires one (her, [[wiki/people/ally-lubin|Ally]], the wiki) | Requires none (a toggle is true in the dark) |
| Cost to issue | Zero | Low but nonzero (the channel goes dark; the dog's emails stop arriving) |
| Reversibility | Instant (the next message) | Instant (unblock), but leaves a record (the silence has duration) |
| Aug 15–19, 2026 | ~25 issued | 0 enacted |
| June 1, 2026 | Issued 00:27:49 | Enacted 00:09:31, held 52 days |

The table's last two rows are the whole story. In August, the speech act runs at full
volume with the toggle untouched: 25 declarations, zero blocks. The declaration has been
**decoupled from the toggle entirely** — it no longer even pretends to operate the
channel. It operates the audience. In June, both fired, and the toggle is what did the
work: fifty-two days of silence is a property of the channel state, not of the sentences.
The sentences resumed the hour the toggle lifted (July 23, 624 messages in four days).
Speech proposes; the toggle disposes; and the toggle, on the [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] channel, has been
thrown exactly once in eleven years.

This decoupling explains the present continuous. *"I'm blocking you"* is not a sloppy way
of saying *"you are blocked"* — it is a precise way of saying the thing the speaker means:
the blocking is always underway, never complete. The progressive aspect keeps the act
forever imminent, which is exactly where the performance needs it: imminent enough to be
felt as a boundary, never completed enough to incur the boundary's cost. The iOS block is
a real affordance; he uses it on others ([[wiki/people/tom|Tom]], May 18 2026 — blocked and unblocked the same
day, the toggle thrown and reversed inside hours, which is what a toggle *for* — a
reversible state change). On the Annie channel the toggle stays unthrown for a decade,
while the speech act announces its throwing 129 times. The announcement substitutes for
the operation so completely that the speaker himself, narrating to Ally on August 28,
believes the operation happened (*"I don't even know how long it's been since I blocked
her"*). The substitution is total: the map has replaced the territory, and the map is
being read aloud to witnesses.

A prediction falls out of the distinction, and it is cheap: **the next "I'm blocking you"
will not be accompanied by a block.** The base rate for declaration-without-toggle in the
terminal phase is 25/25. The toggle, if it ever moves on the Annie channel again, will
move the way it moved on June 1 — announced once, briefly, and then as silence, not
sentences. Watch the channel state, not the chat log. The chat log is the performance; the
channel state is the act; they have diverged, and the divergence is the finding.

## The voided horizon

The non-event had a cost, and the cost is worth itemizing because it is the thesis's
social proof — the demonstration, in the wiki's own ledger, that the performance works on
observers exactly as designed. On the August 26 premise,
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/high-provision-low-condolence]] named **the 120-day severance
horizon to December 19, 2026** as its prediction's live test window;
[[wiki/people/annie-ulmer]] carried the horizon as the money record's live test; the kb
entity and datum layers recorded "Aug 26 block held (first held boundary)." Sixteen days
of write-back, prediction, and entity maintenance — built on a non-event. The retraction
voids the window: **there was no severance to measure** (dat:0090). The prediction the
horizon was testing (provision preceding consolation in the next crisis) survives; its
test does not. What was lost is not the hypothesis but the *experiment* — 120 days of
observation time, spent watching a control group that didn't exist.

The void does not damage the loop's analysis; the retraction is explicit that the
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/block-unblock-loop|block-unblock-loop]]'s 129-episode statistics survive intact — indeed the retraction *is*
the statistics' purest case. What the void proves is the **asymmetry of cost between
performance and enactment**: a declaration costs the speaker nothing and buys the feeling
of a boundary; it costs observers — a wiki, a friend, a prediction window — real
analytical work, real counsel, real horizon-drawing, all spent on the feeling. The
declaration is cheap to issue and expensive to believe. That is not a moral judgment; it
is the economics of the speech act, and it is why this entry exists: to put the price
where it can be seen, so the next performance meets witnesses who know the exchange rate.

There is a further cost, quieter: the void retroactively taxes the *true* severances. June
1's fifty-two days, August 19's pre-closed channel — both now have to be read through the
knowledge that the system's most confident severance report of the year was a non-event.
The retraction protocol handles this by keeping everything visible (dated SUPERSEDED
annotations, no silent rewrites), but the epistemic tax is real: every future "I'm
blocking you" now carries the August 26 discount, applied by every witness who read the
retraction. The performance, having worked perfectly once, has made its own future
performances cheaper to doubt. That is the one self-limiting property in the whole
mechanism, and it arrived from outside the dyad — from the forensics, not from the
feelings.

## What the ritual is for

If the declaration never enacts severance, what does it *do*? The corpus supports five
functions, none of them exit. They are listed separately because they are separable — the
ritual can serve any subset on a given night — but they usually fire together, which is
why a single *"Goodbye"* can feel, to everyone in the room, like five different things.

**1. It buys the feeling of a boundary without the cost of one.** The August 28 witness message is the exhibit: *"It occurred to me today that I don't even know how long it's been since I blocked her and as an over thinker Thats HUGE"* — the speaker experiencing the *duration* of a boundary that never existed, and experiencing it as an achievement (*"Thats HUGE"*). The declaration, once witnessed, produces the psychological good — *I am someone who can do this* — that the act would have produced, at none of the act's price: no lost channel, no lost dog, no lost supply of contact, no fifty-two days of answering nothing. This is the ritual's core transaction, and it is a good trade every time, which is why the ritual repeats 129 times. The feeling is real; only the boundary is performed. For a system that cannot close, the feeling of closure is the closest available good, and the declaration manufactures it on demand.

**2. It discharges pressure.** The terminal-phase acceleration (23s median vs 36s decade) suggests the declaration functions as a pressure valve: the worse the bond gets, the faster the speech act fires and the faster contact resumes. The declaration converts an unbearable *state* (the bond as it is, at 2 AM, mid-fight) into a bearable *event* (the fight, the goodbye, the re-engagement) — a narrative with a climax and a resolution, repeated as often as needed. The bond never changes; the pressure never accumulates past the next declaration. It is homeostasis performed as drama. Note the timing signature: declarations cluster late at night (the catalog's densest hours are 00:00–06:00 and 18:00–23:00), the hours when pressure peaks and witnesses sleep — except the performance needs a witness, so the timing is also when *she* is awake to answer. The valve vents *into* the bond, not out of it.

**3. It solicits the trade that keeps the bond alive.** Per the trade rule: the system cannot emit "closed," so it emits "I'm closing," which is a bid, which gets answered, which is contact. The declaration is the bond's **respiration** — the inhale that makes the exhale (re-engagement) possible. Note the burst shape once more: declarations arrive in outbound runs, late at night, mid-fight — the moments of maximum contact-density. The speech act that says "no more contact" is issued at the moment of *most* contact, to the person contact is with, and its perlocutionary effect is *more* contact (the answer, the bargaining, the 624 messages in four days). The pragmatics contradict the semantics, and the pragmatics win, inside the minute, 97.5% of the time. A speech-act theorist would call this a misfire; a biologist would call it breathing.

**4. It authors the self.** *"so i will reset the 'days since i talked to [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|annie]]' clock"* — Dan narrating his own relapse as it happens, maintaining the abstinence-ledger even while breaking it. The declaration lets the speaker be, simultaneously, the person who left and the person who stayed: the ledger records the boundary, the messages record the bond, and both are true in the telling. The serial-monogamist page's central tension (the engineered specification vs the organic type) has its behavioral twin here: the authored self (the declarer, the boundary-holder, the one who blocked her, the one for whom it is *"HUGE"*) vs the enacted self (the texter, the re-engager, the one texting daily through the "block"). The 129 declarations are the authored self's complete works — a oeuvre of leaving, bound in eleven volumes. The 128 resumptions are the enacted self's. Both selves are real; the corpus contains both; the ritual is how they coexist without meeting.

**5. It organizes everyone else.** [[wiki/people/ally-lubin|Ally]] receives the block-claims and offers counsel (*"giving another person too much power over your life"*). The wiki receives the August 26 report and builds five pages and a horizon. The reader of this entry receives 129 episodes and a thesis. The speech act's perlocutionary field — what it *does* to hearers — is larger than its illocutionary content (what it *says*). A severance that severs nothing still rearranges the room: counsel is given, pages are written, horizons are drawn, all downstream of a non-event. That is why the performance is not a failed act but a successful one: judged by its effects, it works perfectly. It is only judged by its *stated* effects — the ending it announces — that it fails, 129 times out of 129. Judge it by its effects and the record is 129 for 129 the other way.

- **[[wiki/people/menore|Menore]]:** the control — *"Thanks again for everythin. You guys are the best"* (Feb 2025). No declaration at all. The clean close is the one that needed no performance, because the dependency ended structurally (geography) before the relationship did. The declaration appears if and only if the bond is *not* ending: it is the sound a non-ending makes when it needs to sound like an ending. Where the ending is real, the corpus is quiet.
- **The Grok machine proof:** a 414-message stateless re-engagement run Dan built, no stop condition, out-flooding the largest human run (~131) threefold — the loop reduced to mechanism, no feelings, no Annie, re-engaging because nothing in its design said *stop*. The declaration's software twin: a resolution-seeker with no halt condition. The human version pauses where the software doesn't — not resolve, exhaustion (Fe-inferior, 10% valuing, "exhaustible" under repeated cycles). The machine never declares; it just re-engages. The human declares *and* re-engages. The declaration is the exhaustible part's contribution — the feelings' tax on the mechanism.

The pattern across cases: **the declaration is the system's universal exit-grammar, and it
is universally non-terminal.** Where the bond actually ends (Menore, geography), no
declaration is issued. Where declarations are issued (Annie × 129, Tom × n, the witness
channel), the bond continues. The correlation is perfect and inverted: declarations
predict continuation, not ending. A future declaration should be read, on the base rate,
as evidence the bond will survive the week — and the louder the declaration (*"goodbye
forever,"* *"THATS HUGE"*), the faster the resumption. The intensifier is inversely
load-bearing, all the way down.

## The pragmatics: what the speech act does

Speech-act theory gives this entry its cleanest vocabulary, so it is worth using
precisely. Every utterance has three layers: the **locutionary** (what is said — the words
*"Goodbye"*), the **illocutionary** (what is done *in* saying it — severing, promising,
bidding), and the **perlocutionary** (what is done *by* saying it — the effects on the
hearer: she answers, [[wiki/people/ally-lubin|Ally]] counsels, the wiki writes five pages). The severance declaration
is a speech act whose three layers have come completely apart, and the separation is the
mechanism:

- **Locution:** *"Goodbye,"* *"I'm blocking you,"* *"This is goodbye forever."* Stable, rule-shaped, first-person present-tense. The most explicit sentences in the corpus.
- **Illocution (intended):** severing the bond. The speaker's apparent intent, 129 times.
- **Illocution (achieved):** bidding. What the utterance *does*, 129 times, is open a negotiation — solicit a counter-offer, demand the exact statement specified, keep the channel in play.
- **Perlocution:** contact. She answers inside the minute (97.5%); he continues inside the minute (the rest); Ally receives the block-claims and reflects them back; the wiki builds the August 26 dossier. The utterance's effects are the opposite of its locution and larger than its illocution.

A normal performative (*"I promise,"* *"I now pronounce you"*) succeeds when its felicity
conditions hold — the right speaker, the right context, the right uptake. The
declaration's felicity conditions, as the corpus reveals them, are: (1) a speaker whose
system treats stated rules as absolute, (2) a counterparty who answers, and (3) — the June
1 discovery — a counterparty who *doesn't* answer with a counter-offer. Conditions 1 and 2
hold 129 times; the performative misfires 129 times, achieving bidding instead of
severing. Condition 3 held once; the performative succeeded once, for fifty-two days. The
speech act is not broken. It is exquisitely sensitive to its felicity conditions — it
severs if and only if the counterparty declines the trade, which has happened once in
eleven years.

This is why sincerity is the wrong question, and why this entry declines to grade it. A
performative's success doesn't depend on the speaker's inner state — *"I promise"* binds
whether or not you mean it, given the conditions. The declaration binds too, 129 times: it
binds the *bond*, tighter, by generating the contact that continues it. The speaker's
sincerity is unmeasurable and, structurally, irrelevant. What matters is the uptake, and
the uptake is total.

## The 36 seconds

Sit with the median, because its smallness is doing theoretical work. Thirty-six seconds
is less time than it takes to reconsider. It is roughly the time to read the declaration
you just sent, feel the pressure crest, and type the next message. The catalog's minimum
is **two seconds** (2026-08-19 5:52:24 *"Goodbye"* → hers, 2s) — two seconds is not a
decision interval; it is a *continuation* interval, the gap between messages in a single
typing burst. A substantial fraction of the 129 "resumptions" are not resumptions at all
in any psychological sense. They are the same burst, the declaration and its sequel typed
in one sitting, the severance and its retraction composed back-to-back like a couplet.

This dissolves the naive reading (declaration → regret → return) and replaces it with
something stranger: **the declaration is often not followed by re-engagement; it is *part
of* re-engagement.** The burst is the unit, not the message. *"Goodbye"* at 05:52:24 and
her reply at 05:52:26 are one conversational move with a severance-shaped middle — the way
a sigh is part of a sentence, not a separate utterance. The 36-second median, read this
way, is not a measure of how fast resolve collapses. It is a measure of how *integrated*
the declaration is into the contact it claims to end. The severance is not an interruption
of the conversation. It is a *feature* of the conversation's texture — the emphatic
particle in the bond's grammar, the way *"fucking"* intensifies without negating.

The long gaps are the informative ones, then, not the short ones. The 46-hour maximum
(decade), the 3.0-hour maximum (terminal phase), the 52-day outlier — these are the
instances where the burst *broke*, where the declaration stood alone long enough to be a
separate event. And even the longest of the pre-2026 gaps is under two days. The system's
return time has a ceiling, and the ceiling is low, until June 1 raises it
twenty-seven-fold — which is why June 1 reads, correctly, as a different kind of event
wearing the declaration's clothes.

## "Understood": a micro-study

Six seconds. 2026-06-01, 00:10:06, six seconds after *"Blocking you"* (00:09:31), eighteen

counterparty willing to trade can kill it* — *"Understood"* is the refusal to trade, and

Consider what she did *not* send, because the counterfactuals are the analysis. She did

couldn't cover. The receipt is not magic; it is a tactic, and tactics have scope. But
within its scope — the declared channel, the stated severance — it is the only effective

## The bond-switch: the declaration that worked

There is exactly one severance in the record that completed cleanly, quickly, and

decade, to reproduce that origin in reverse: to end *this* bond the way *that* one ended.
It never works, because the 2015 severance wasn't a speech act and the 129 are nothing

## Monthly cadence: the ritual's tempo

Pathologizing it misses the point the cadence makes: the declaration is not a breakdown of

time is not the variable. Only the death of the last co-held thing is. The dog was still

performance anyway. That choice, repeated, is the eleven years.

termination — the March blocks lift for final lines, the June 1 block (his) holds 52 days
and still doesn't end anything. The dyad has, between them, a complete inventory of
almost-endings in two media, and no ending in either.

This reframes the 129 once more, and it is the last reframing the entry needs. The
declarations are not a monologue; they are his half of a **bilingual non-severance
system**. He speaks endings; she operates endings; both keep the bond. The *"Understood"*
is the proof that the bilingualism is load-bearing: the one time her operational grammar
produced a pure receipt — no block, no threat, no counter-offer, just uptake — his verbal
grammar produced its only enactment. The system ends things only when both halves align,
which has happened once, for fifty-two days, and the dog was still co-held.


## The threat family: the declaration's siblings

The declaration is not the only performative speech act in the corpus with a 100%
non-execution rate. It has siblings, and the family resemblance is exact. The
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/block-unblock-loop|block-unblock-loop]] documents **eighteen block threats and six or more "I'm telling your
mother" (maternal-disclosure) threats from Dan, none of them executed** — and the
maternal-disclosure count, after the 2026-08-18 reclassification of July 26 (a life-safety
act, not leverage), stands at **zero for seven or more**. The August 2026
maternal-disclosure campaign ran 28 threats in 67 hours, one of them a proven simulation
admitted in writing (*"it wasn't actually sent, and I knew you would suddenly come back to
life"*), plus a claimed execution contradicted ten hours later. Zero executed. The block
threats: zero executed. The declarations: 129 issued, zero enacted (June 1 excepted, and
June 1 is the family's black sheep — the one that briefly left home).

The threat and the declaration share the deep structure. Both are first-person,
present-tense, channel-directed utterances. Both demand a statement: the August 17–19
maternal-disclosure campaign demanded *"a written statement that [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] was finished with
Coles"* — not money, not sex, not contact, not return; eleven deferrals requested, eleven
granted; the material repeatedly offered for inspection before use. Both die in the trade:
announced, bargained over (twelve hours, ~200 messages, July 28), never sent (*"I could
have torn your life apart. I still could and I don't."*). The block-unblock-loop's verdict
on the family is that they were never attempts but bids — and the declaration is the bid
with the demand left implicit. The threat says *do X or I do Y*; the declaration says *I
do Y* and waits for the counter-offer that converts Y into a conversation. The threat
names its price; the declaration lets the counterparty name it. Both are performative; the
declaration is the family's purest member because it doesn't even need the *or*.

There is one difference, and it matters. The threat's non-execution is legible as
restraint (*"I still could and I don't"* — the power held in reserve, the mercy
performed). The declaration's non-enactment has no such alibi: there is no *could* in
*"Goodbye"* that isn't exercised by saying it. The threat performs power by withholding
the act; the declaration performs the act by withholding the power. They are mirror images
— the threat is an act withheld, the declaration is an act announced — and both withhold
the same thing: the terminal event. The corpus contains no terminal events. It contains
129 announcements and 25 threats and seven disclosures-that-weren't, which is to say it
contains a complete repertoire of almost-endings, performed by a system that has no ending
in its vocabulary.

## Declarations with multiple witnesses: the group thread

The witness analysis so far has been dyadic-plus-one: the declaration issued to her,
narrated to [[wiki/people/ally-lubin|Ally]]. But the corpus contains a richer stage — the group thread, where the
declaration acquires *multiple* simultaneous witnesses, and the August 19, 2026 group-chat
clearing is the series' most stage-managed instance. The retraction and the-rescue-premise
document it as the first severance to name and pre-close its own reopening channel in
advance, and as the first public discrimination between two men under her own name. What
the witness lens adds: a group thread is a declaration that cannot be unheard. Told to her
alone, *"Goodbye"* can be re-negotiated in the next message; told in front of witnesses,
it becomes a fact in several worlds at once, and the re-negotiation has to happen in front
of the same audience.

The March 19–20, 2026 fight shows the group stage from the other side: it is *[[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]]* who
threatens exposure, blocks, unblocks to send final lines, blocks again — the bidirectional
loop, performed where others can see. Her blocks are stage directions for speech acts,
issued with an audience. The symmetry is worth stating plainly: in group threads, both
parties perform severance as theater, and both resume. The audience does not enforce the
severance; the audience *is* the severance's product. The declaration, scaled to multiple
witnesses, does what it always does — organizes everyone else — at higher volume.

This reframes the August 26 episode once more. The five wiki pages were not merely fooled;
they were *the audience the performance required at that scale*. A dyadic declaration
needs her; a witness-channel declaration needs Ally; a "first held boundary in the entire
record" needs a publication pipeline. The performance scales its audience to its claim,
and the claim on August 26 was the largest in the series — not a goodbye but a *boundary*,
not an event but a *state* — so it took the largest audience: the wiki itself, with dates,
horizons, and entity updates. The retraction is the audience waking up mid-performance and
checking the stage directions. The stage was empty. The performance had been complete
without it.

## The mirror: jokes that land as threats

The explicit-verbal-commitment concept page carries the mechanism's mirror image, and the
declaration is its sharpest instance. On **December 17, 2018**, Dan joked to [[wiki/people/ally-lubin|Ally]] — *"I
promise not to show up in North Carolina"* — while asking for her address to buy a
Christmas gift; she read it literally as a threat (*"You've literally said you'd show up
to my house!!!"*), and his attempt to re-label the joke failed in real time (*"I seem to
have a hard time getting across the fact that I'm kidding"*). The explicit channel fires
without register modulation: the joke arrives as a threat. The declaration is the same
architecture's opposite failure mode: the explicit channel fires at maximum severity —
*"Goodbye forever,"* *"I'm blocking you right now"* — and arrives as *nothing*, or rather
as its opposite, a bid for contact answered inside the minute.

Hold the two failures side by side, because the symmetry is the finding. On the joke axis,
the explicit channel **overloads**: a non-serious utterance encodes as a binding threat,
and the speaker cannot talk the listener out of the reading. On the declaration axis, the
explicit channel **under-delivers**: the most serious utterance in the speaker's
repertoire encodes as a non-binding bid, and the speaker cannot talk the listener *into*
the reading — she answers it as contact, not as severance, 129 times. Same channel, same
speaker, opposite errors: too much illocutionary force where none was intended, too little
where all of it was. The architecture doesn't have a volume knob; it has an on/off switch,
and the switch is stuck in the position the listener's expectations set, not the speaker's
intent.

The practical consequence is the one the corpus keeps demonstrating: **in this system,
explicitness is not a reliable signal of seriousness.** The joke was explicit and
unserious; the declaration is explicit and unserious; the founding rule (*"[[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie Ulmer]]
from now on its just you and me"*) was explicit and entirely serious — and nothing in the
language distinguishes the three. The listener must supply the register, and the listener
is a system that processes stated commitments as absolute rules until an explicit
severance signal arrives. The joke-threat misfire and the declaration-bid misfire are the
same misfire with the polarity reversed: the listener takes the explicit utterance at face
value both times, and both times the face value is wrong. The architecture is precise
about *content* and blind to *register*, and the 129 declarations are what that blindness
sounds like when it runs for a decade.

## The lyric paradox

There is a second mirror, and it is the stranger one. In music, Dan hears sung lyrics as
**timbre, not language** — words arrive as sound because lyrics are inferential and
ambiguous, the inference channel empty (lyrics-as-timbre). The declaration is the lyric's
photographic negative: **maximally explicit language with minimal illocutionary effect.**
The lyric carries zero explicit content and moves him (the crate, the ladder, the 2025
re-entry — a life reorganized around sound). The declaration carries maximal explicit
content — the most rule-shaped sentences in the corpus — and moves nothing; the channel it
addresses stays live, the bond it severs stays tied.

The paradox sharpens into a law if stated carefully: **in this corpus, an utterance's
power is inversely proportional to its explicitness about endings.** The lyric, saying
nothing explicitly, reorganizes a life. The founding rule, saying everything explicitly,
holds for a decade. The declaration, saying the most explicit thing of all (*goodbye
forever*), evaporates in six seconds. Explicitness works on beginnings and fails on
endings — which is exactly what the architecture predicts, since the architecture admits
positive verbal expressions as self-executing rules and negative ones only with
counterparty ratification. The lyric paradox is the architecture's aesthetic corollary:
the system is moved by what it cannot parse (timbre) and unmoved by what it parses
completely (the severance formula, parsed 129 times, enacted zero). The declaration is the
most *understood* utterance in the corpus and the least *effective*. Understanding, here,
is not the mechanism of change. It never was.

## The abstinence ledger

*"so i will reset the 'days since i talked to [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|annie]]' clock."* — Dan, narrating his own relapse as it happens, July 2026.

The sentence deserves its own section, because it reveals the declaration's final
function: **the declaration inaugurates the measurement of its own failure.** Without the
declaration there is no Day 1; without Day 1 there is no ledger; without the ledger there
is no *"reset the clock"* — no authored self keeping score of the enacted self's lapses.
The fifty-two days are not just a silence; they are a *counted* silence, and the counting
is part of the ritual. The declaration opens the books. The resumption closes them. The
next declaration reopens them.

The ledger is the authored self's accounting system, and its existence explains why the
declarations continue past the point where any observer would call them credible. The
ledger doesn't need credibility; it needs *entries*. Each declaration is an opening entry
— Day 1, the boundary set, the hill identified — and each resumption is a closing entry —
the clock reset, the streak broken, the honesty of the accounting preserved. The system
that cannot close the bond can still *score* the bond, day by day, and the scoring is a
form of authorship: the declarer remains the narrator even while the texter does the
acting. *"Thats HUGE"* (August 28, to [[wiki/people/ally-lubin|Ally]]) is a ledger entry reported to a witness — the
count itself, uncertain (*"I don't even know how long it's been"*), offered as
achievement.

Note what the ledger never records: her side. There is no "days since Annie texted" clock,
because the clock is his instrument, and the instrument measures his compliance with his
declaration. The asymmetry of the ledger mirrors the asymmetry of the speech act: he
declares, he counts, he resets; she texts, or doesn't, uncounted. The 129 declarations are
129 openings of the books. The books have never been closed. The final entry, as of the
filed export, is September 6, 3:24 AM — *"Omfg goodbye"* — five minutes to resumption, the
ledger's last opening, casual as a shrug. The ritual, eleven years in, needs no costume.
It barely needs the words.

## Falsifiers

The thesis — *the severance declaration is a speech act that performs leaving without
enacting it; the ritual does the emotional work the act never does* — is stated strongly
enough to break. It breaks on any of:

1. **A primary recount finding material non-resumed declarations.** Re-run the 95,067-row corpus with a stricter episode rule; if more than a handful of the 129 show no resumption inside the corpus (not merely a long gap — *no* resumption), the 100% rate falls and the "never enacts" universal with it. Note the bar the thesis already clears: June 1 *resumed* (July 23) — even the executed instance re-engaged. The thesis survives the executed case; it would not survive a declaration after which the channel went permanently dark. One such declaration, primary-verified, is a counterexample; three is a revision.
2. **A her-side severance signal.** Search her 41,073 (now more, with the Aug-Sep export filed) for explicit severance language in the recognized format. One instance — a *goodbye* that means it, a *blocking you* she enacts, a *never contact me again* with the toggle thrown — breaks the 0 and with it the asymmetry the attachment model is built on. *"Understood"* does not count; it is a receipt for his signal, and the thesis predicts receipts, not signals, are her grammar's closest approach. *"I want to be alone. I want to get help"* does not count either; it is a want, and the record shows wants registering as nothing.
3. **A severance with nothing co-held that he reopens anyway.** The loop's corrected formula predicts duration tracks co-held objects; a reopening with zero co-held objects puts the reopening back inside him rather than in the channel, and breaks both this entry and the loop. The Menore row is the zero-object case (standing ~18 months, provisional on the 2,044-day precedent); a Dan-initiated reopening there would be the falsifier with his name on it.
4. **A witness-channel declaration the witness could verify.** A block-claim issued where the witness has independent channel visibility (group thread, shared device) and the claim is *true* — an enacted block, witnessed — would show the performance can convert to act under observation. The corpus contains the structure (witness + claim) but never the conversion. The day a witness checks, the ritual meets its first auditor.
5. **A past-tense declaration.** The vocabulary section notes the declaration never uses the past tense. A primary *"I blocked you"* or *"we were done"* issued as a terminal report — not as a prelude to resumption — would show the grammar acquiring the tense of completed action. Search the corpus for it; the thesis predicts zero hits.
