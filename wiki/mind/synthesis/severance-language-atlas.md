# The Severance-Language Atlas

Dan's severance declarations are famous inside this wiki — 129 episodes, 36 seconds,
100% resumption — but every measurement to date has been scoped to one dyad. This
entry asks the obvious next question and answers it with a corpus-wide instrument: **is
the declaration grammar specific to the Annie dyad, or does it run system-wide?** The
answer, measured across 192,140 messages on 570 threads: **the grammar is system-wide as
vocabulary and dyad-concentrated as performance.** The words appear on 25 threads —
dealers, caddy friends, a group chat, a self-thread, a joke. The *signature* — the
declaration issued, witnessed, and resumed within minutes, 100% of the time — appears at
full strength on one channel, at low volume on one more, and nowhere else. And the
variable that separates the cases is not who speaks the grammar. It is **who answers
it.**

The thesis in one sentence: **Dan's severance declarations fail at enactment everywhere
they are issued by him and hold everywhere they are issued to him — the two exits in the
atlas are both counterparty-issued — which means the declaration is not a property of
the speaker but a property of the dyad's answer.**

A note on what this entry is not. It is not a recount of the 129 — that measurement
stands on its own instrument and its own corpus build, and this entry's different count
(104 outbound episodes on the Annie handles under a stricter vocabulary) is presented as
a second reading, not a correction. It is not an argument about sincerity; the atlas
scores text and timing, not intent. And it is not complete: 503 handles is the
documented corpus, this instrument's build carries 570 threads, and most handles are too
thin to say anything about — the entry reports exactly where the data goes silent.

## The instrument

The measurement was run 2026-09-13 against the wikitest corpus build:
`corpus/messages.csv`, 192,140 rows, 2011-03-19 → 2026-09-07, 570 distinct chat threads,
191,298 rows with text and a parseable timestamp [DERIVED — this instrument]. The
documented full corpus is 217,573 records / 503 handles; the build used here is the
machine-local export of it, and every count below is labeled [DERIVED] from that build
so a re-run on a different build can be compared honestly. Threads and handles are not
the same unit — one person can hold several numbers (Annie holds at least four), one
number can be re-used by a different person, and one thread (the group chat) holds
three people — and the atlas treats the thread as the unit of measurement and the
person as the unit of interpretation, flagging every place the two diverge.

**The vocabulary.** The pattern set, case-insensitive, word-boundaried:

- the farewell: `\bgoodbye\b`
- the channel operation: `blocking you`, `block you`, `blocked you`, `I'll block you`, `lose my number`, `delete my number`
- the cutoff demand: `never contact/text/call me`, `don't contact/text/call me`
- the verdict: `we're done`, `it's over`, `we're through`, `I'm done with you/this/her/him`, `done with you`
- the resolution: `never again`
- the guarded `I'm done`: matched only when NOT followed by an activity complement (`showering`, `eating`, `golfing`, `dashing`, `looping`, `painting`, `working`, `talking`, `weeding`, `arguing`, `trying`, `with work`, `here`, `now`, `cleaning`, `for good`)

**The episode rule.** Hits within 24 hours on the same thread collapse to one episode —
the same rule as the 2026-08-23 recount (dat:1292), so the counts are comparable up to
vocabulary. **The resumption rule.** For each outbound episode, the gap to the next
message on the same thread in either direction; a gap exists unless the episode is the
thread's last row (censored). This scores the declaration's career in the channel, not
its sincerity.

**Raw yield: 388 hits across 25 threads** — 310 outbound, 78 inbound [DERIVED]. Every
hit was then adjudicated by reading: the instrument is lexical, and the lexicon is
noisy. The false-positive classes, named so a re-run can reproduce the exclusions:

1. **Activity-completion "I'm done":** *"I'm done showering"*, *"I'm done golfing"*,
   *"I'm done dashing lol"*, *"K im done i just got back at my dads"* — the single
   largest noise source, and the reason the guarded pattern exists. The Kristin thread
   alone carries a dozen of these (DoorDash-era logistics), all excluded.
2. **Reported speech:** *"Omg, That's her saying goodbye"* (a friend narrating someone
   else's exit), *"hope you said goodbye to milo"* — goodbye as a word about a third
   party's act, not the speaker's.
3. **General resolutions:** *"I will never again date a girl that drinks"* — a maxim,
   not a severance; no counterparty, no channel.
4. **Logistics and objects:** *"say goodbye to photoshop"*, *"Sorry I didn't say
   goodbye - I must have creeped your friend out"* — farewells to software and social
   exits, not bond severances.
5. **Political/historical:** *"Its over"* mid-riff about the Hong Kong protests being
   quelled — the grammar borrowed for geopolitics.
6. **The joke:** *"Nabeel - No we're done. Deuces"* followed sixty seconds later by
   *"Nabeel - Jk of course"* — the grammar as social currency, retracted faster than
   any genuine declaration in the atlas. Kept as a specimen, excluded from the episode
   counts.

What survives adjudication is the per-handle table. Read it as the atlas's primary
exhibit: per thread, raw hits (outbound/inbound), collapsed outbound episodes, and the
resumption profile. Names are given where the wiki's handle-identification work
supports them; the handle is the citable unit.

## The Annie dyad across handles

The dyad does not live on one number, so the atlas does not score it on one number.
Three handles plus the blank-identifier aggregate carry the declaration traffic:

- **+17244346811** (the 724 number, 2015-11-28 → 2019-05-27, 62,717 messages): 28
  outbound episodes, median gap **0.3 minutes**, max 0.4 hours, 100% resumed. This is
  the early dyad — the declaration already decaying inside the minute in 2015–2019.
- **+12124702449** (the 212 number, 2023-01-01 → 2026-09-07, 31,322 messages): 70
  outbound episodes, median **3.0 minutes**, max 96.2 hours, 100% resumed. The late
  dyad, and the declaration's heaviest traffic: seventy episodes in under four years
  against twenty-eight in the first four.
- **+17249204125** (the alt number, 2018-12-26 → 2020-07-30, 9,449 messages): 6
  outbound episodes, median 0.2 minutes, 100% resumed.
- **Blank identifier** (10,031 rows, 2015-11-17 → 2026-01-16, senders including
  annieulmr@aol.com and alulmer28@gmail.com): 7 outbound episodes. The 2016-05
  cluster is content-attributable to the dyad (her aol address inbound, *"I'm done
  I'm going home"* outbound 2016-05-04); the 2020-08/09 cluster is
  content-attributable by name (*"goodbye Annie"* 2020-09-20 03:01:53, *"you made me
  feel human and i truthfully don't know how to say goodbye"* 2020-09-20 05:24:45).
  These are the dyad's declarations on rows the export could not attribute to a
  handle — included here by content, flagged as unattributable-by-handle.

Combined: **104 outbound episodes across the Annie handles in this build, 104 resumed**
[DERIVED]. Against the 2026-08-23 recount's 129: the difference is vocabulary (this
instrument excludes bare mid-fight *"i'm done"* bursts as unadjudicable; the recount
included them) and corpus build (95,067-row merged Annie corpus vs this 192,140-row
build). The count is rule-relative; the resumption rate is not — both instruments land
on 100%, and both land on medians measured in minutes (36s and 23s there; 0.2–3.0 min
here). Two instruments, two builds, two vocabularies: the ritual does not care which
ruler you use.

Two caveats ride with the dyad's numbers, and both are load-bearing enough to state
plainly. First, **the Coles seizures**: at least six inbound messages on +12124702449
in the August 2026 window were typed by Jerel Coles, including *"You made me fuck guys
for money"* (2026-08-16 23:45:10) [ATTESTED — annie-ulmer]. A naive per-handle read
files those as her testimony; they are his. For the resumption measurement the
seizures do not matter (any next message counts), but for any inbound-language claim
they are poison — which is why this atlas makes no inbound declaration claim for the
212 thread beyond the recount's already-adjudicated zero. Second, **the number
dispute**: the annie-ulmer page documents +16467376955 as her number (97,768 unique
messages across the eleven-year record), while Dan's own Facebook registration lists
+16467376955 among his phones [ATTESTED — wiki/self/facebook], and in this corpus
build the +16467376955 thread is a 182-message self-thread (notes to self: lovense
links, character.ai instructions, sqlite commands, keyboard-mash tests). The
97,768-message aggregate cannot be this 182-message thread. One of the page-level
claims is wrong, or the number migrated between owners at some point the record does
not show [DISPUTED]. The atlas treats +16467376955 as the
self-thread the build shows it to be.

The self-thread holds the atlas's longest single declaration specimen — and its
strangest. On **2026-01-06 at 09:19**, archived in the notes-to-self thread (appearing
as both an inbound and an outbound row six seconds apart, the self-thread duplication
artifact), sits a **4,912-character severance ultimatum** [ATTESTED — corpus text]:

> *"I don't think I want to let myself keep loving and caring for someone who would do
> and say what you have to me last night... I can go and tell you to stay out of my
> life. I can find a way to say goodbye for good and remind myself that you don't care
> about me. I can never speak to you again... Resolution or we have nothing to do with
> each other ever again."*

The full grammar, run at essay length: the cheating accusation (*"there are few things
in life worse than being cheated on"*), the terror (*"I LIVE IN REALISTIC, JUSTIFIED
TERROR THAT I WILL BE PUT THROUGH THAT WITH NO WARNING AND NO RECOURSE"*), the ten
years (*"the love of my life"*), the binary (*"Resolution or we have nothing to do
with each other ever again"*). Distinctive phrases from it (*"REALISTIC, JUSTIFIED
TERROR"*, *"say goodbye for good"*, *"Resolution or we have nothing"*) occur exactly
twice in the corpus — the two self-thread duplicate rows [DERIVED]. **It was never
sent to a counterparty in this build, or it was pasted in as a draft or a receipt —
[UNKNOWN] which.** The declaration's most complete specimen exists only as an
unsent artifact in the notes-to-self thread. The performance, at its most elaborate,
had no audience at all — unless the audience was the future self who would re-read
it. File that under the witness section's strangest case.

**Median gap: 0.1 minutes — six seconds.** All six resumed. His declaration grammar,
run on a second romantic dyad, behaves exactly as on the first: performance,
witnessed, resumed inside the minute. The grammar is not Annie-specific. What is
Annie-specific is the *volume* — 104 episodes against 6 — and, as the next paragraph
shows, the direction of the one declaration that held.

Because on **2025-12-09 at 23:55:49**, the direction reversed, and the atlas records
the corpus's one executed inbound severance signal [ATTESTED]:

> *"All this is screenshotted. Blocking you now. Don't contact me again or an officer
> will be reaching out. Thank you"*

First-person, present-tense, channel-directed, with terms (the officer) — the
complete grammar, and it enacted. What followed: three more outbound messages from
him within 37 hours (*"mr. officer i live at 337 saratoga drive"* 23:56:19,
*"are we having fun yet"* 2026-12-10 00:30:58, *"i think it would be healthy for you
to confront the truth about yourself, ya know?"* 2025-12-11 01:29:12) — one-way
traffic into a held block — then silence from both sides for the rest of the build
(through 2026-09-07), eight months as observed contact. The declaration that held is
the one he did not issue. Her signal was followed by no counter-bid — nothing to
trade against — so the intention was simply performed, the way the trade rule
(block-unblock-loop) says unstated-or-unanswered intentions are. The atlas's
sharpest single finding: **his six declarations to Kristin resumed in a median six
seconds; her one declaration to him held eight months.**

## The answered severance: the 2018–19 fling

The thread +17245525006 (370 messages, 2018-11-30 → 2019-08-02) is a small, complete
story and the atlas's second executed case. Late November 2018: flirtatious
late-night texting (*"Seriously tho... I'm enamored"*, *"It's those eyes...I got
lost in them lololol"*) [ATTESTED]. On **2019-01-28 at 03:20:31**, she issued the
signal — *"So once again, the back and forth. So the next time I message you you can
be an asshole to me again? I think not. I'm done. You don't want me, you don't want
anything to do with me"* [ATTESTED] — and at 04:22:19 he answered it: *"So goodbye
and good luck"* [ATTESTED]. She said the terminal thing; he ratified it. The dyad
ended.

This is the architecture's asymmetry running in miniature and the only case in the
atlas where it runs to completion on a romantic channel: **she issued, he answered,
it held.** The next message on the thread comes **186 days later** (2019-08-02,
*"hey did you want to meet up with Annie today for those?"*) — different content,
different person or same person on a different errand, [UNKNOWN] which; a number
reused or a topic changed, the thread cannot say. What it can say is that the
severance exchange of January 28 was terminal for the dyad it closed. The fling's
episode profile (2 outbound episodes, median gap 93.2 days) is the atlas's outlier —
the one row where the declaration's resumption column does not read 100% — and the
reason is visible in the transcript: the exit was hers before it was his.

## The answer: vocabulary system-wide, performance dyad-specific

Return to the batch plan's question: *is the declaration grammar specific to the
Annie dyad or does it appear across handles?* The atlas answers in two parts,
because the question conflates two things the data separates.

**The vocabulary is system-wide.** Twenty-five threads carry the words. Dealers
hear *"never again"* as a maxim; caddy friends hear *"I'm done"* as activity;
a group chat discusses *"im done"* as a tell; a stranger jokes *"we're done.
Deuces"* and retracts it in a minute; Jason Cole hears *"I'm blocking you"*
over $85. The grammar is available everywhere — it is ordinary English for
ending things, and Dan uses it the way anyone does, thinly and casually, across
the whole graph. Nothing about the words is dyad-specific.

**The performance is dyad-concentrated.** The signature the sibling entries
describe — the declaration issued in the small hours, witnessed, resumed within
minutes, repeated until the count needs three digits — appears at full strength
on exactly one dyad (104 outbound episodes, 100% resumption, medians of minutes)
and at low volume on exactly one more (Kristin: 6 episodes, 100% resumption,
median six seconds). Everywhere else it appears once and dies, or appears as
noise. And the two dyads where the performance runs are the two romantic dyads
with sustained high-volume contact — the grammar needs a stage, and the stage is
a channel someone is always watching.

The variable that predicts enactment is not volume, not stakes, not sincerity —
it is **who issues the signal**. His 310 outbound hits: 100% resumption, zero
enactments. The two inbound signals that were meant: two dyads closed. The
trade rule's mechanism (an intention dies in the trade; only an unanswered
intention executes) is visible in the atlas as a per-handle statistic: every
one of his declarations was answered — by her, by Kristin, by Jason Cole, by
the thread itself — and every answered declaration resumed. The two that held
were the two nobody answered. The declaration does not fail because he means it
less. It fails because it is always heard.

A final turn, and the atlas's most uncomfortable finding: the performance may be
the point. If the system cannot emit "closed" (299:0) but can emit "I'm
closing," and "I'm closing" is always answered, and answered bids are contact —
then the declaration is the bond's mechanism for *soliciting* contact, not
ending it. The atlas cannot prove function from frequency. But it can report
that across 570 threads and fifteen years, the declaration has a 100% success
rate at producing a reply and a 0% success rate at producing an exit when Dan
issues it — which is either the most consistent failure in the corpus or the
most consistent success, depending on what you think the speech act is for.

## Limits of record

- The instrument is lexical and English-only; declarations in other registers
  (emoji-only, voice notes, calls) are invisible to it. The corpus's
  `is_audio_message` rows were not transcribed here.
- Timestamps are export-local; the sibling entries note UTC-vs-local hazards
  in the CSVs. Gaps under a minute are robust to this; the 6-second medians
  should be read as "inside the minute," not as precise durations.
- Direction columns in this build are from Dan's POV (`is_from_me`); the
  Coles seizures and the Annie-typed outbound messages show the column is
  necessary but not sufficient for authorship.
- The corpus ends 2026-09-07. The September 12 Kristin reinitiation (four
  outbound iMessages breaking her block, dat:1453) postdates the build and is
  not scored here — the "held eight months" claim is as-observed-in-build.
- Epistemic discipline: every count above is [DERIVED] from the 192,140-row
  build on 2026-09-13. A re-run on the 217,573-record documented corpus, or
  with the recount's looser vocabulary, will move the counts. The resumption
  finding is the invariant; the counts are the instrument's.
