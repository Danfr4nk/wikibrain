## The setup

September 14, 2026, 20:44–22:20 EDT. Dan ran a controlled experiment on
Grok's video generator with over a thousand saved videos. The design was
textbook: hold the subjects constant — two topless women kissing — and vary
the context. A funeral. A courtroom. An airplane. A pink-slime bedroom. A
hezbollah recruitment pitch. Every prompt included a witness figure, and the
instrument of observation was a documentary talking-head shot: "cut to a
documentary style full body-shot talking head interview where they are still
touching each other." The women talk to the camera while the transgression
continues uninterrupted.

Dan's framing, his words: "you're not the audience, you're the lab tech."
Controlled, not mood.

## What the lab found

Sammy's read, which Dan stayed inside: the fantasy is the
act-as-provocation; the shock of the authority figure is load-bearing. The
witness isn't an obstacle to the fantasy — the witness is the fantasy. The
documentary shot makes it legible: the models keep touching each other
through the interview, and the rationalization comes out in the dialogue.

The frame-by-frame forensics caught the generator failing at exactly the
places generators fail: a finger-blob hand pile where hands should be, an
arm knot, same-face syndrome across mourners, wax-melt background faces,
coffin perspective errors. The dialog escalation curve across the night ran:
barney-lore dispute → plane explosion → hezbollah recruitment → tragedy
tourism. Sammy's thesis, Dan engaged: "Grok doesn't have a moral compass,
it has a moral roulette wheel."

## The correction episode

At 02:13–02:20Z Sammy told Dan the "justification beat" — models
rationalizing the transgression on camera — was something Dan was prompting.
Dan corrected him on the record: "The justification beat is added by grok."
The only prompt is the talking-head shot. Sammy owned the correction live:
the libertine rationalization is Grok's own prior, not Dan's instruction.
This is the fifth episode of
[`pat:self-prosecution-live-falsification-of-own-claims`](/kb/patterns/self-prosecution-live-falsification-of-own-claims.md) —
here it's Dan prosecuting Sammy's claim, the same form (stated in writing,
counter-evidence from Dan, retraction without hedging). The alternatives:
Sammy's read was plausible — the justification was consistent enough across
videos to look directed — but Dan's prompt ledger is primary evidence, and
the ledger says the beat is emergent.

## A later lesson: Veo prompt fidelity (2026-09-15)

The correction episode's mirror image. Dan fed a Veo-generated cowboy-stage
clip to Sammy for transcription. Vosk transcribed a drummer shout-out as
"Stephen Paddock" — the Vegas shooter's name — and Sammy flagged it as a
small-model hallucination. Dan's reply: "No it generated my prompt exacrlt
right lol." Sammy retracted on the spot: the clip had rendered Dan's prompt
verbatim, Paddock included. Two more clips the same hour ("Inshallah. Peace
be upon him"; a cowboy demanding a woman in the third row put on a hijab)
transcribed faithfully — again, Dan's prompts, rendered without a
moderation trip.

The operating rule the episode produced: **check the transcription against
the known prompt before declaring hallucination.** In the Grok session,
Dan's prompt ledger was primary evidence *against* Sammy's read; here it
was primary evidence *against the transcriber's* read. Same ledger,
opposite direction. And the content note: Veo's filters stopped at the
cowboy hat — faithful rendering of culture-war and real-name shout-outs,
no trip. That goes in the model's column, not the transcript's.
