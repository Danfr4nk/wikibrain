# Supply Graph, Not Supply Chain

The existing account of Dan's procurement — [[wiki/mind/synthesis/supply-network]] — is a chain: a single continuously-operating logistics architecture, a succession of nodes from [[wiki/people/zachariah-harshman|Zach Harshman]] (2014) through [[wiki/people/johnny-dealer|Johnny]] (2018) to [[wiki/people/tom|Tom]] (terminal era), and a single point of failure at the end of it, where Tom's mid-May 2026 no-show propagated through 72+ hours of withdrawal into a lost job.

That account is correct about succession and about Tom's centrality. It underprices the fragility, because it models one layer and the record shows two.

The record's actual object is a **graph with two layers and one shared cut**. Layer one is licit: a prescribing doctor, a script, pharmacies that fill or refuse it — a routing-only medical relationship whose documented failure mode is geographic and jurisdictional. Layer two is illicit: Tom as the terminal-era strip line (plus mushrooms, cocaine, klonopin), backed in earlier eras by real parallel-node depth, all of it failing in the relational register — owe-and-silence cycles, friendship ruptures, not logistics.

The two layers are not independent, and they are not redundant either, because the same node — Tom — sat in both: the illicit layer's primary line *and* the licit layer's documented failover. When the licit layer jammed and the illicit layer collapsed in the same fortnight, there was no third layer left.

That is the May 2026 event, and it is not a chain breaking. It is a correlated two-layer cut, and it is the only supply failure in sixteen years that produced withdrawal — which is why it is the fragility model, and every single-layer failure before it is the contrast.

The thesis, stated flat: **the system survived every single-layer failure the record holds; the one failure it did not survive took both layers at once.** The true fragility was never "one supplier." It was two suppliers — one licensed, one not — whose failure modes were different enough to look independent and correlated enough through the operator's displacement to fail together.

## 1. The chain model and what it cannot express

The chain model's claims, fairly stated **[ATTESTED, wiki/mind/synthesis/supply-network]**:

Procurement is one permanent institution since January 2011, not scattered dealer contacts — a continuously-operating logistics architecture with succession, redundancy, and failure modes.

Redundancy decayed monotonically: the 2018 deep cycle ran parallel dealers ([[wiki/people/johnny-dealer|Johnny]], a second handle, [[wiki/people/teddy|Teddy]]); NYC ran on [[wiki/people/menore|Menore]]'s 99.3% availability; by 2025 the whole load sat on Tom.

The reliability inversion: distance disciplines, intimacy licenses failure. Tom is the least reliable node in the record; the professional stranger is the most reliable.

May 2026 reads as the single point of failure giving way: Tom no-shows, withdrawal follows, the BFS shift is impaired, the job ends.

None of that is wrong. What it cannot express is the prescriber.

The chain model carries a flagged contradiction — its own August 2026 addition — recording that the June 2025 messages describe a different topology: *"I had my doctor move my prescription here"* (2025-03-06), *"my doctor said the pharmacy won't fill an out of state prescription"* (2025-06-08), *"my doctor tried to send a prescription to walmart and they won't fill it either so i'm completely out of options now"* (2025-06-12), *"I am so fucked. I'm gonna have to drive down to see Tom today"* (2025-06-13) **[ATTESTED as quoted on the supply-network page; verification status per dat:0028 below]**.

On that sequence the prescription is the default path, the pharmacy counter is the chokepoint, and Tom is the *failover*. If that holds, May 2026 is not a first-line failure. It is a second-line failure with the first line already down — a materially different architecture, and a different remedy. The page held the contradiction open rather than resolving it. This entry resolves it, with the verification limits kept visible.

The chain model also cannot express *why* the May failure was different in kind from every earlier one. Sixteen years, one documented withdrawal event. The chain model explains that as luck plus one very long MTBF.

The graph model explains it as structure: every earlier failure was a single-layer failure, and single-layer failures are survivable by construction. May 2026 is the only documented case of both layers going down together.

The difference between "one failure in sixteen years" and "the only two-layer failure in sixteen years" is the difference between a luck story and a model.

## 2. The graph, layer by layer

### The licit layer: the prescriber

A prescribing doctor exists. The evidence is thin, dated, and consistent **[ATTESTED]**:

**2011:** a Suboxone appointment with drug screening — monitored treatment, the earliest institutional contact **[ATTESTED, dat:0033]**.

**2013-07-10:** *"i was on the strips when i had a doctor in nyc"* — past tense by 2013, in the same exchange that reports 3.5 years of maintenance and 2mg/day **[ATTESTED, dat:0055]**.

**2019-05-31:** *"try to go get subs from my old doctor"* — the only one of the four prescriber quotes verified verbatim against the authoritative corpus **[ATTESTED, dat:0028]**.

**2025-03-06:** *"I had my doctor move my prescription here"* **[ATTESTED as quoted; dat:0028 could not verify — the corpus holds 3 messages that day, none of them this line]**.

**2025-06-08:** *"my doctor said the pharmacy won't fill an out of state prescription"* **[ATTESTED as quoted; dat:0028: the corpus holds 0 messages that day]**.

**2025-06-12:** *"my doctor tried to send a prescription to walmart and they won't fill it either so i'm completely out of options now"* **[ATTESTED as quoted; dat:0028: 0 messages that day]**.

The relationship's content, across every dated instance, is **routing**: moving the script between pharmacies, following the operator's location. No dose discussion, no taper discussion, no review, no body — anywhere in the record **[ATTESTED, wiki/health/the-configured-body]**.

The one standing medical relationship in the corpus is a logistics relationship, which is exactly what the chemical-architecture page's two-mode account predicts: a prescriber is an input supplier, and input supply is the faculty this system has.

Three structural properties of the licit layer matter for the graph.

First, **it is thin.** Six dated contacts across fifteen years. It is not a care relationship; it is a permission slip with a phone number.

Second, **it follows geography.** *"Move my prescription here"* — the script's validity is a function of where he is. The layer's documented failure mode is jurisdictional: an out-of-state prescription meeting a pharmacy that will not fill it.

Third, **it predates and overlaps the illicit layer.** dat:0055's finding is that "the prescribed and informal topologies overlap rather than succeed each other, at least through 2021" **[ATTESTED, dat:0068]**. This is not a story of the licit layer replacing the illicit one or vice versa. Both run at once, through 2021 at minimum, which is what makes the May 2026 event a *two*-layer failure rather than a handoff that broke.

The epistemic floor, stated once and then used: the three 2025 quotes come from a census run on a superseded message dump, not the authoritative export; one of four quotes verified verbatim; two fall on days the corpus does not cover (absence in a hole is `never_observed`, not `known_not_to_occur`); one is absent from a thinly-covered day **[ATTESTED, dat:0025, dat:0028]**.

Confidence on the 2025 topology: moderate. The graph below is built on it anyway, because the alternative — treating the licit layer as nonexistent — contradicts the 2011 appointment, the 2013 past-tense doctor, and the 2019 verbatim line, and because the May 2026 scramble's shape is only legible if the layer exists.

### The prescriber relationship, close up

The 2011 appointment with drug screening is the licit layer at its thickest: monitored treatment, institutional contact, the regimen under supervision **[ATTESTED, dat:0033]**. Nothing in the record ever returns to that thickness. By 2013 the NYC doctor is past tense. By 2019 the doctor is "my old doctor" — a fallback to be tried, not a relationship being maintained. By 2025 the doctor is a routing function: move the script here, try Walmart, report the refusal.

The direction is monotonic: **the licit layer thins over fifteen years while the demand it serves never varies.** The 2011 layer could plausibly have carried the load alone — monitored, screened, institutional. The 2025 layer cannot carry anything alone; its entire function is consumed by the act of re-routing, and re-routing fails at the pharmacy counter. The layer did not just stay thin. It thinned *into* its failure mode: a logistics-only relationship is, by construction, a relationship whose whole capacity is spent on the move, so when the move is the problem, there is nothing left.

This is also where the configured-body page's Prediction 2 lands: *"The Suboxone supply will fail again through geography, not through Tom"* **[ATTESTED, wiki/health/the-configured-body]**. That prediction was written about the *next* failure. This page's thesis is that May 2026 was already its partial confirmation — the geography jammed the licit layer, and the Tom failure finished what the geography started. The prediction named the right failure mode and the wrong sufficiency: geography alone did not produce withdrawal in June 2025, because Tom answered. It took both.

One more property of the prescriber relationship, because it matters for the remedy section: **it is the only edge in the graph that requires an institution on the other end.** Every other edge terminates at a person — Tom, Johnny, Bop, Suz. The prescriber edge terminates at a doctor *and* a pharmacy *and* a jurisdiction, three institutions that must all agree. That is why it is the hardest edge to rebuild and the easiest to jam: it has three chokepoints where every other edge has one. A graph that depends on its hardest edge as a layer is a graph built backwards — unless that edge is thick enough to carry the institutional overhead, which this one, by 2025, was not.

### The licit layer's true chokepoint: the pharmacy

Read the June 2025 sequence as a failure trace and the prescriber is never the failure. The doctor *writes*; the pharmacies *refuse*.

The out-of-state prescription dies at the counter on June 8. The Walmart reroute dies at the counter on June 12. The operator is "completely out of options" on the 12th and driving to see Tom on the 13th **[ATTESTED as quoted]**.

The prescriber layer's single point of failure is not the prescriber. It is the fill jurisdiction — the fact that a script's authority ends at a state line and a retailer's policy.

This matters because it names the licit layer's failure mode precisely: **geographic displacement jams it.** Every time the operator moves — NYC to Uniontown, Uniontown to the next address — the script has to be re-routed, and re-routing is the whole relationship the prescriber provides.

A logistics-only medical relationship is, by construction, a relationship whose entire function is consumed by the act of moving. When the move is the problem, the relationship cannot solve it; the relationship *is* the move.

The 2025-03-06 line (*"move my prescription here"*) is the layer's normal operation: geography changes, routing follows. The June sequence is the layer's failure: geography changes, routing fails. The difference between the two is not effort. It is whether the pharmacy at the end of the route will honor an out-of-state script.

That is a jurisdictional fact, not a relational one — and it is why the licit layer's failure in the collapse window is correctly described as *geography*, not as anyone's fault.

### The illicit layer: Tom

Tom Maison, ~2010–2026: friend-supplier, strips, mushrooms, cocaine, klonopin — "shows up with substances and announces it" **[ATTESTED, wiki/health/chemical-architecture]**. By 2025 he is the sole strip source **[ATTESTED, wiki/mind/synthesis/supply-network]**.

The message volume — roughly 4,160 on the primary handle plus ~1,600 under phloxenheim@gmail.com — marks the friendship as the primary male bond in the corpus *and* the primary supply edge **[ATTESTED, wiki/mind/synthesis/aura-illness-compound-collapse, via wiki/people/tom]**.

The illicit layer's failure mode is not jurisdictional. It is relational, and it has a twelve-year documented pattern: the owe-and-silence cycle.

August–September 2014: *"Do you have any fucking clue how shitty I feel? I waited 2 weeks already… you need to get me the rest of what you owe me TODAY"* (Aug 20), *"I'm not doing this stupid fucking dance with you anymore"* (Sep 10), then right back to him September 15 — *"yo looking to purchase sub/tex whatevz. anything around?"* — the channel reopening the moment product was needed again **[ATTESTED, wiki/people/tom]**.

May 2026: the identical cycle — the string-along ("after court," "after a nap," "after weed whacking"), the no-show breaking point May 15–16, the May 18 block/unblock and $36 demand, the May 29 "a $30 come up" accusation, the May 30 threat to route the dispute through Tom's father Phil, then silence **[ATTESTED, wiki/mind/synthesis/aura-illness-compound-collapse]**.

Dan's own retrospective names the failure's register: what bothered him most was not the money but *"that he would be okay letting me go sick"* **[OPERATOR, wiki/mind/synthesis/aura-illness-compound-collapse]**.

The friendship-embedded supplier fails the way friendships fail — by indifference, by silence, by the owe-stonewall-escalate loop — and the supply consequence (withdrawal) rides on top of the relational event. This is the layer the chain model describes well. It is also only half the graph.

### The illicit layer's lost depth: the 2018 parallel nodes

The terminal-era illicit layer — Tom alone — is not the layer's historical shape. The 2018 deep cycle ran genuine parallel depth **[ATTESTED, wiki/mind/synthesis/supply-network]**:

**[[wiki/people/johnny-dealer|Johnny]]** (2018–2019): primary deep-cycle dealer, strips ($15/8mg, $20/12mg), "b's", fronts. ~3,462 messages. Produced supply panics when he vacationed or his phone died — *"I have no other person I can get z from. Johnny leaving for the beach"* (Aug 2019) — but the panics stayed panics because the parallel nodes existed.

**The second dealer** (+17243233522): the 2018 parallel node from the operating manual. Identity unresolved.

**[[wiki/people/teddy|Teddy]]** (2018–2020): parallel informal supplier — strips, pills, credit and holds. Willing but fragile: fines, probation, borrowed money.

**[[wiki/people/zaco|Zaco]]**: the 2018 Suboxone-strip secondary, and the network's only inbound-marketing node — 58 of his thread's 65 messages are unsolicited inventory advertisements.

**[[wiki/people/jay-lauer|Jay Lauer]]**: seven weeks of suboxone/cocaine peer-trading across the 2016–17 New Year — mutual credit between broke men, not vendor service.

**[[wiki/people/menore|Menore]]** (NYC): the professional delivery operator, 455 of 458 requests answered over 6.5 years (99.3%, median 7-minute reply) — the reliability outlier that proves distance outperforms intimacy in this market.

The redundancy ledger: in 2018 a supplier failure was an inconvenience; in 2025 it was a crisis. The decay is monotonic and documented, and it runs on the same curve as the relational concentration the [[wiki/mind/concepts/contact-gini|contact-Gini]] page documents — the top handle taking 45% of all sent messages, the graph collapsing toward a single node.

**The illicit layer lost its parallel edges over seven years while the licit layer stayed thin throughout.** By May 2026 the graph had, in effect, two edges left: the prescriber route and Tom. Both had to hold. Neither did.

### The fiscal rail: Suz

[[wiki/people/suzanne-frank|Suz]] is not a product node for Suboxone. She is the payment rail: a decade of purchases from [[wiki/people/jim-vrabel|Jim Vrabel]] in her own name, routine cocaine provision through 2015–16, and the Cash App account — installed at Dan's request in August 2018 — that the later supply-era transfers ran through **[ATTESTED, wiki/mind/synthesis/supply-network]**.

The supply-network page's corrected finding is the precise one: the family layer was not net-funding procurement; it was the *rail* procurement ran on.

The rail matters to the graph because it is the one edge that did not fail in May 2026 — and its survival changed nothing. Payment infrastructure is not product. The graph's demand (daily Suboxone) could be paid for; it could not be filled.

This is a standing property of the system worth naming: **the money layer and the product layer are decoupled**, and May 2026 is the demonstration — the fiscal rail intact, the product graph cut, the withdrawal arriving on schedule anyway.

### The node that was also the failover

Here is the graph's load-bearing structural fact. Tom occupied two positions at once:

1. **The illicit layer's primary edge** — the standing strip line, sole source by 2025.
2. **The licit layer's documented failover** — the June 2025 sequence ends with *"I am so fucked. I'm gonna have to drive down to see Tom today"* after the prescription path died at two pharmacy counters **[ATTESTED as quoted]**.

There is also the older, undated near-miss running the same shape from the other direction: a supply-line rival "jumped his place in line" and bought out a dealer's Suboxone stock, and Tom absorbed the loss covering him anyway **[ATTESTED, wiki/health/chemical-architecture]**. Tom was the backstop for the illicit layer too.

In graph terms: **Tom was the single node adjacent to every failure path.** The licit layer's recovery edge ran through him; the illicit layer's recovery edge ran through him; the illicit layer's primary edge *was* him.

A chain model reads this as "Tom was important." A graph model reads it as the actual fragility: the architecture had two layers, and both layers' redundancy terminated at the same person. That is not redundancy. That is a single node with two job titles.

When the friendship collapsed in May 2026, it did not take out one edge. It took out the primary edge of one layer and the failover edge of the other — simultaneously, because they were the same edge wearing different labels.

## 3. The edges: what flows, and where the redundancy lived

The graph, stated as edges (direction: toward Dan):

**Licit layer.**

*Prescriber → pharmacy → Dan*: Suboxone script. Chokepoint: the fill — out-of-state refusal, retailer policy. Failure mode: geographic/jurisdictional. Relationship content: routing only, six dated contacts in fifteen years, nothing clinical.

*Pharmacy refusals, June 2025*: the documented failure trace of this edge — two counters, four days, then failover.

**Illicit layer, terminal era.**

*Tom → Dan*: Suboxone strips (sole source by 2025), mushrooms, cocaine, klonopin. Chokepoint: the friendship. Failure mode: relational — owe-and-silence cycles, indifference during asymmetric periods.

*Second contact → Dan*: took payment Sunday night of the May cascade, never delivered. A burn, not an edge — it never carried flow.

*Workplace customer → Dan*: ~$35, never delivered. Same.

**Illicit layer, historical depth (the redundancy that decayed).**

*Johnny → Dan* (2018–19): strips, fronts. Failed gracefully — vacations produced panic, not withdrawal — because parallel edges existed.

*Second dealer → Dan* (2018): parallel node, identity unresolved.

*Teddy → Dan* (2018–20): strips, pills, credit. Fragile but present.

*Zaco → Dan* (2018): strip secondary, inbound-marketing.

*Menore → Dan* (NYC, 2013–2025): the professional edge, 99.3% uptime. Still operating 2026 — but in the wrong city for the May 2026 failure, which is its own lesson: **an edge that cannot reach the failure's geography is not redundancy.**

*Jay Lauer ⇄ Dan* (2016–17): peer trading, bidirectional.

**Fiscal rail.**

*Suz ⇄ Dan*: Cash App transfers, the 2018 $20–$3,000 irregular flows, the August 2018 account installation. Carries money, not product.

**Failover edges (the ones that make it a graph, not two chains).**

*Licit failure → Tom*: documented June 2025. The licit layer's recovery path ran through the illicit layer's primary node.

*Illicit degradation → Tom*: the undated jumped-line near-miss. The illicit layer's recovery path ran through the same node.

Count the distinct recovery paths that do not pass through Tom: zero, by the terminal era. That sentence is the whole fragility model.

## The product graphs are not the same graph

So far this page has modeled one graph. The record actually holds several — one per product — and they have different topologies, different chokepoints, and different failure consequences. The May 2026 cut was a *Suboxone* cut specifically. Naming the product graphs separately is what lets the model say why.

**The Suboxone subgraph.** Demand: daily, inelastic, withdrawal on interruption — the fixed-rate intake the [[wiki/mind/synthesis/intake-constancy|intake-constancy]] page documents.

Edges, terminal era: the prescriber route (thin, jurisdictional) and Tom (sole strip source by 2025). Historical depth: Johnny ($15/8mg, $20/12mg strips), Teddy (strips, pills), Zaco (2018 strip secondary), Jay Lauer (peer-trading).

This is the subgraph with the catastrophic failure mode, because it is the only subgraph whose interruption produces a physiological cascade. Every other product's graph can fail and the operator has a bad week. This one fails and he cannot work a shift.

**The cocaine subgraph.** Demand: daily, but elastic in a way Suboxone is not — the dosage arc moves with money (1g baseline, 3.5–7g in the 2017–2020 inheritance window, 0.5–1g after), which means the demand curve has a governor even if the governor is only the wallet **[ATTESTED, wiki/health/cocaine via wiki/mind/synthesis/chemical-architecture]**.

Edges: Tom, Johnny (the deep-cycle primary), the minor-node layer (Al/"Socialist" 2018, Jajuan Davis winter 2018–19, Lindsay Carolla Thanksgiving 2016), Suz (routine provision 2015–16), and — the inversion — Dan himself as the terminal-phase supplier to [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]].

The cocaine graph was always wider than the Suboxone graph, which is why no cocaine supply failure in the record produces a crisis: the product with the most elastic demand had the most redundant graph, and the product with the least elastic demand had the least. That is either a design accident or the one place the architecture's priorities were exactly backwards. The record does not say which.

**The klonopin/mushroom subgraph.** Carried on Tom's edge almost exclusively ("strips, mushrooms, cocaine, klonopin — shows up with substances and announces it"). No parallel nodes documented, no failover, no licit route.

The thinnest subgraph — but also the one whose interruption has no documented catastrophic mode, which is why its thinness never got priced. A subgraph nobody depends on for survival can afford to be fragile. The Suboxone subgraph could not, and was.

**The cannabis subgraph.** Menore's NYC line via [[wiki/people/sam|Sam]] (2019–20, menus, a delivery driver — the only node in the record to fire Dan as a customer), Jim Vrabel (two and a half years, marijuana), the Zach Harshman-era wax/dabs/flower.

Multiple edges, low stakes, no withdrawal mode. The cannabis graph is the control: this is what a non-critical product's procurement looks like — genuinely distributed, genuinely replaceable, and nobody ever wrote a synthesis page about it because nothing about it ever failed loudly.

The point of the separation: **the fragility model is product-specific, and the product that mattered had the worst graph.** Suboxone — daily, inelastic, withdrawal-gated — ran on two edges by 2025, both terminating (directly or as failover) at nodes whose failure modes were armed by the same displacement.

Cocaine — daily but money-governed — ran on a wider graph and never produced a crisis. The architecture allocated its redundancy inversely to its criticality. Whether that is because the critical product's edges were harder to build (a prescriber requires institutions; a dealer requires a phone) or because the criticality was never modeled is unanswerable from the record — but the inversion is the kind of finding a graph model produces and a chain model cannot, because the chain model has one product (cocaine, "the network's dominant product") and treats the rest as detail.

One more product-level observation, because it sharpens the May 2026 reading: the scramble's three burns were all *Suboxone* attempts — Tom (strips), the second contact, the workplace customer ("strips or tex"). Nobody in the four-day cascade was sourcing cocaine, klonopin, or mushrooms.

Under maximum load the operator's procurement collapsed to the single inelastic product, which means the graph under stress is not the full product set but the Suboxone subgraph alone. The May 2026 cut is legible, in the end, as the failure of a two-edge graph carrying the one demand that could not lapse.

## 4. May 2026: the two-layer cut

### The timeline

Mid-May 2026, across four days **[ATTESTED, wiki/work/bfs-foods]**:

**Friday–Sunday:** Tom's string-along — "after court" (Pittsburgh), "after a nap," "after weed whacking" — consuming Dan's optionality while he slid into withdrawal. The BFS page's counterfactual is exact: had Tom said "I can't" on Friday morning, Dan would have had time and options before the impairment cascade began.

**Sunday night:** a second contact takes payment, never delivers.

**The incident shift** (Sunday, ~May 17 [INFERRED]): Dan works roughly 72+ hours into withdrawal. The drawer variance that ends the job is pinned to this shift.

**Same night:** the desperate ~$35 workplace sourcing attempt — the "shitty lottery," no product. Three burns in four days.

The cascade then runs downstream: drawer dispute, schedule cut 36→7, termination "sometime in May [2026]" **[ATTESTED, wiki/work/bfs-foods]**.

And upstream of the cascade, in the same fortnight, the friendship's own rupture formalizes: May 15–16 no-show breaking point, May 18 block/unblock and $36 demand, May 29 "a $30 come up," May 30 the threat via Tom's father Phil and the last message **[ATTESTED, wiki/mind/synthesis/aura-illness-compound-collapse]**.

### Layer one down: geography

What the record shows about the licit layer in May 2026 is an absence: **the prescriber does not appear in the scramble.** Four days, three burns, every attempt illicit — Tom, a second contact, a workplace customer.

In June 2025, when the licit layer failed at the pharmacy counter, Dan's first move after the failures was the doctor and his last resort was Tom. In May 2026, with the stakes higher (72+ hours of withdrawal, a job on the line), the doctor is not contacted at all **[INFERRED from the absence; MED]**.

Two readings of that absence:

1. **The layer was down.** The June 2025 topology — prescription as default, pharmacy as chokepoint, out-of-state refusal — was still the topology eleven months later, and the same jurisdictional jam that killed the June fills was in force in May. The scramble went illicit-only because the licit route was unavailable.
2. **The layer was unused.** The prescriber relationship is routing-only and thin (six dated contacts in fifteen years); in a four-day crunch, Dan defaulted to the channel he actually used — the illicit one — and never tried the doctor.

The record cannot adjudicate between these. What it can do is price them: under reading 1, May 2026 is a two-layer failure and the fragility model holds as stated. Under reading 2, May 2026 is a single-layer failure (Tom) plus a licit layer that was *functionally* absent — which is the same graph with the same cut, arrived at by disuse rather than by refusal.

Either way, the graph had one working edge left in May 2026 and it was Tom's, and Tom's edge was the one that failed. The distinction matters for the remedy (fix the licit route vs. build the habit of using it) and is preserved as a gap.

What is not in doubt: **the licit layer's documented failure mode is geographic, and the operator was mid-displacement.** The June 2025 sequence ties the fill failures to jurisdiction explicitly (*"out of state prescription"*). The 2025-03-06 line ties the script to location (*"move my prescription here"*).

In May 2026 Dan was living at [[wiki/places/337-saratoga-drive|337 Saratoga Drive]] under a bankruptcy proceeding filed October 2024 with an August 2025 drop-dead provision — the forced sale would close June 23 and vacate him July 8 **[ATTESTED, wiki/mind/synthesis/aura-illness-compound-collapse]**. The displacement the prescriber layer could not survive was not a future event in May. It was the standing condition.

### Layer two down: the relationship

The illicit layer's failure is the part the chain model narrates well, and the graph model keeps the whole of it: Tom's owe-and-silence cycle, the twelve-year pattern (2014, 2026), the friendship-embedded supplier whose intimacy licenses failure while distance disciplines **[ATTESTED, wiki/mind/synthesis/supply-network]**.

The May 2026 instance is the cycle at its most costly — the silence consuming not just money but the optionality the BFS page's counterfactual names: a Friday-morning "I can't" would have left time and alternatives; the string-along ate them.

Note the failure's register, because it is what distinguishes the layers: the licit layer failed at a *counter*, over *jurisdiction*, with no human antagonist — a policy, a state line. The illicit layer failed in a *friendship*, over *indifference*, with the human antagonist being the absence of one — silence, not refusal.

Dan's own epitaph for it (*"that he would be okay letting me go sick"*) names the register exactly: the betrayal is physiological permission, not financial theft **[OPERATOR]**. Two layers, two failure grammars, one fortnight.

### Why the scramble is the proof

The three-burns-in-four-days cascade is doing evidential work beyond the narrative. Consider what it shows about the graph's state:

**No licit attempt** across four days of escalating desperation. Whatever the reason (down or unused), the licit edge carried zero flow under maximum load.

**No parallel illicit edge** answered. The 2018 graph would have routed around Tom through Johnny, Teddy, or the second dealer. The 2026 graph had no such edges. The second contact and the workplace customer were not edges — they were burns, attempts to *create* edges under load, which is not how edges work.

**The failover edge was the failed edge.** Tom was supposed to be the licit layer's backup (June 2025 pattern). In May 2026 the backup was the primary failure. A failover that shares its failure mode with the primary is not a failover; it is the same risk counted twice.

This is the min-cut, stated plainly: remove the prescriber route and the graph holds (June 2025 — Tom answers). Remove Tom and the graph holds *only if* the prescriber route is up (never documented in the terminal era). Remove both — or remove Tom while the prescriber route is down — and the graph is cut, and the cut produces withdrawal, and the withdrawal produces the impaired shift, and the impaired shift produces the termination.

May 2026 is the cut. Sixteen years, one cut, one job lost to it.

## 5. What the graph survived: the single-layer record

The model's checkable claim is that every documented failure before May 2026 was single-layer, and the graph survived all of them. The record:

### June 2025: the licit layer failed and the graph held

The failure trace, quoted above: out-of-state refusal June 8, Walmart refusal June 12, *"completely out of options"* — then Tom: *"I am so fucked. I'm gonna have to drive down to see Tom today"* (June 13) **[ATTESTED as quoted; verification limits per dat:0028]**.

The licit edge died at the pharmacy counter. The failover edge — Tom — answered. No withdrawal is documented for June 2025. The graph held because the failure was single-layer and the failover was alive.

This is the control case for the whole page. Same two layers, same chokepoints, eleven months before May 2026 — and the system survives, because exactly one layer is down. Whatever made May 2026 different is not in the layers. It is in the correlation.

### The jumped-line near-miss: the illicit layer degraded and the graph held

Undated, from the chemical-architecture record: a supply-line rival "jumped his place in line" and bought out a dealer's Suboxone stock — an illicit-layer degradation — and Tom absorbed the loss covering him anyway **[ATTESTED, wiki/health/chemical-architecture]**.

The primary illicit edge wobbled; the backstop (Tom, again) held. No withdrawal documented. The graph held.

Read against May 2026, the near-miss is the inversion: in the near-miss the *illicit* layer degraded and Tom-the-backstop saved it; in May 2026 Tom-the-backstop *was* the degradation, and there was no backstop behind the backstop. The system's redundancy was always one person deep.

The near-miss proves the depth worked once. May 2026 proves it was the only depth there was.

### 2014: Tom failed and the graph held

August–September 2014: the full owe-stonewall-escalate cycle, *"I'm not doing this stupid fucking dance with you anymore"* (Sep 10), weeks of unreliability from the same node that would fail catastrophically in 2026 **[ATTESTED, wiki/people/tom]**.

No withdrawal crisis is documented for 2014. The graph held — through the licit layer (the 2013 messages show a doctor in the picture, past tense but present), through the era's other nodes, through whatever edges the record no longer itemizes.

The Tom failure of 2014 is the proof that a Tom failure, alone, was survivable *in this topology*. It does not prove a Tom failure is survivable in general. The chain model sometimes reads the 2014 survival as evidence about Tom. It is evidence about 2014's graph.

### 2011: the dry Saturday

The chemical-architecture record holds an at-home test kit episode with Dan dry since Saturday night before an appointment — a single-layer stress on the supply, survived **[ATTESTED, wiki/mind/synthesis/chemical-architecture]**.

Early in the run, thin documentation, but the shape is the same: one layer stressed, system holds.

### 2018: the panic that stayed a panic

Johnny's vacations and dead-phone stretches produced the era's characteristic distress signal — *"I have no other person I can get z from. Johnny leaving for the beach"* (Aug 2019) **[ATTESTED, wiki/mind/synthesis/supply-network]**.

The panic is real and documented. The withdrawal is not, because the parallel nodes (Teddy, the second dealer) absorbed it. This is the redundancy the terminal era lost, measured in the exact unit the model needs: a primary-edge failure that *felt* like a crisis and *wasn't one*, because the graph had somewhere else to go.

Five single-layer events. Five survivals. One two-layer event. One job. The ratio is the model.

## The eras as cut sets

The graph was not one graph across sixteen years. It was a sequence of graphs, and the cut set — the smallest set of edges whose removal disconnects Dan from Suboxone — changed with each era. Walking the eras is what turns the redundancy decay from a narrative into a measurement.

**2010–2013: the monitored era.** Edges: the prescriber (2011 appointment with drug screening — the most institutional the licit layer ever gets), early illicit nodes, Tom already present as friend-supplier.

Cut set: large — removing any one edge leaves several. The 2011 dry-Saturday stress is the era's signature: a single-edge wobble, absorbed. The licit layer is at its thickest here (monitored treatment, screenings), which is worth marking because it never gets thicker again. Everything after this is the licit layer thinning while the illicit layer does the carrying.

**2014–2015: the Zach era.** Edges: [[wiki/people/zachariah-harshman|Zach Harshman]] (wax, dabs, flower — the earliest dated Uniontown node), Tom, the licit layer thinning to routing (the 2013 "when i had a doctor in nyc" is already past tense).

Cut set: still multi-edge. The 2014 Tom owe-and-silence cycle is the era's load test: Tom fails for weeks, no withdrawal documented, the graph routes around him.

Note what this proves and what it doesn't: it proves a Tom failure was survivable *in this topology*. It does not prove a Tom failure is survivable in general. The chain model sometimes reads the 2014 survival as evidence about Tom. It is evidence about 2014's graph.

**2016–2019: the deep era.** Edges: Johnny (primary), the second dealer, Teddy, Zaco, Jay Lauer (peer-trading), Menore in NYC (2018 return — "answered in one minute and resumed the exact prior service"), Tom throughout, the prescriber somewhere in the background (2019: *"try to go get subs from my old doctor"*).

Cut set: the largest in the record — four to five parallel illicit edges plus the licit route plus the NYC professional edge. The Johnny vacation panics are the era's load tests: primary-edge failures that produce distress signals but not withdrawal, because the cut set is deep.

This is the era the terminal era is measured against, and the measurement is stark: the graph lost roughly two-thirds of its edges in seven years without the demand changing at all. The demand is the [[wiki/mind/synthesis/intake-constancy|intake-constancy]] finding — fixed rate, every day, no exceptions. The supply is the decay curve. Fixed demand against decaying supply is the whole story of the terminal era in one sentence.

**2020–2024: the contraction.** Edges: Tom consolidating (sole strip source by 2025), Menore still operating in NYC (99.3% across 6.5 years, "still operating 2026"), the prescriber reduced to the routing-only relationship, the minor nodes gone.

Cut set: two to three edges, and the NYC edge is geographically stranded — Menore can deliver to 1st Ave but not to Saratoga Drive, which means for a Uniontown failure it is not redundancy but memory.

The era's characteristic event is not a failure but a disappearance: the parallel nodes do not fail loudly, they just stop being there, one by one, unmarked. Redundancy decayed the way the contact graph decayed — not by rupture but by attrition, which is why nobody priced it until May 2026.

**2025–May 2026: the two-edge graph.** Edges: the prescriber route (jurisdictionally jammed — the June 2025 trace) and Tom (sole strip source, friendship-embedded, twelve-year failure pattern).

Cut set: {prescriber route, Tom} — and Tom is also the prescriber route's failover, so the effective cut set is {Tom, Tom}. The June 2025 event is the era's load test: the licit edge fails, Tom answers, the graph holds. The May 2026 event is the cut: both edges down, no third edge exists, withdrawal follows. The era lasted roughly a year. It produced the only supply failure in sixteen years.

**Post-May 2026: the Bop recentralization.** Edges: Bop (daily, scheduled, house-calling), the prescriber route (status unknown — no rebuild documented). Cut set: {Bop}. Smaller than the two-edge graph that failed. More reliable node, worse topology.

If the model is right, this graph is *more* fragile than the one that produced May 2026, not less — it just hasn't been tested yet.

The era walk also answers a question the chain model leaves hanging: why did the redundancy decay? The record does not show a decision to consolidate. It shows attrition — nodes aging out, geography stranding the NYC edge, the friendship absorbing the load because the friendship was always there.

Nobody chose the two-edge graph. It is what was left when everything else went quiet. That is worth stating because it changes the remedy analysis: attrition is not fixed by a better node but by a topology that does not depend on any single node — which is to say, by a licit layer thick enough to carry the load alone, in the jurisdiction where the patient actually lives.

## 6. The correlation thesis: displacement as common cause

The two layers failed in the same fortnight. The question the model has to answer honestly is whether that is correlation or coincidence — whether the layers share a cause, or whether May 2026 just happened to be the month both broke.

The case for correlation **[INFERRED, MED]**:

**The failure modes are different but the arming condition is shared.** The licit layer fails on geography: its script must be re-routed with every move, and its fill dies at jurisdictional boundaries. The illicit layer fails on relationship: its primary edge is a friendship, and friendships in the collapse window were failing.

The May–July 2026 window is the compound collapse — job, home, friend, relationship severance inside 90 days **[ATTESTED, wiki/mind/synthesis/aura-illness-compound-collapse]**. Displacement arms the licit failure (jurisdiction follows the move); the collapse arms the illicit failure (the friendship dies inside it). The window is the common upstream.

**The bankruptcy timeline pre-arms the geography.** The Saratoga sale was not a May surprise: Chapter 13 filed October 2024, the drop-dead provision August 2025, the June 2026 close **[ATTESTED]**. The operator lived the entire terminal era under a displacement that was scheduled by paper.

The licit layer — whose whole function is routing a script to *where he is* — spent that era trying to route to an address that was being liquidated. Geography did not jam the prescriber layer in May 2026 as an event. It held it jammed as a condition.

**The friendship collapse is itself a collapse-window event.** The Tom rupture's dates (May 15–30) sit inside the compound window's opening movement **[ATTESTED]**. The owe-and-silence cycle is twelve years old, but its 2026 run landed in the fortnight when everything else was failing — and the cycle's cost (consumed optionality, the string-along eating the alternatives) is a collapse-shaped cost, not just a friendship-shaped one.

**The graph's own history shows the layers entangled, not independent.** Tom was the licit layer's failover and the illicit layer's primary. The layers were never separate systems; they were two faces of one graph with one shared node.

Correlated failure of entangled layers is the default expectation, not the surprising one. Independence would have required the layers to be actually separate, and they were not.

### The counter-model, held open

The independence reading: two failures, different mechanisms, coincident in time. The licit layer was thin and possibly unused rather than jammed; Tom's cycle is twelve years old and would have run its course in any month; May 2026 is when they happened to coincide, and coincidence is not correlation.

On this reading the fragility model is simpler — the graph had one real edge (Tom) and one nominal edge (the prescriber), and the nominal edge's status in May is unknown rather than down.

This entry does not rule that out. It prices it: under the counter-model, the analytical remedy is "rebuild the licit edge and use it." Under the correlation model, the remedy is "de-correlate the layers" — a prescriber in the same jurisdiction as the patient, a supplier outside the friendship, so that no single displacement can jam both. The two remedies differ, and the record as it stands supports the second more than the first, because the June 2025 trace shows the licit edge failing *on geography specifically* — the exact failure mode displacement produces.

### What would falsify the correlation

**A dated prescriber contact in May 2026** showing the licit route alive and merely unused: the correlation thesis needs the licit layer *down*, not just absent from the scramble. One pharmacy fill, one script move, one doctor message from the window breaks the two-layer reading into one-layer-plus-disuse.

**A May 2026 Tom failure with the licit layer demonstrably up and still producing withdrawal**: would show the layers were independent and the cut was single-edge after all.

**A second two-layer failure in the record that produced no crisis**: would show the correlation is not sufficient for the outcome.

**Verification of the June 2025 quotes against a complete export** in either direction: if the topology they describe is wrong, the licit layer's documented shape changes and this page's graph changes with it. dat:0028's partial verification is the standing exposure.

## 8. Why the graph beat the chain on this question

The chain model is the better account of *succession*: who came before whom, how the load transferred, what each node cost and charged. Its redundancy ledger, its node specifications, its reliability inversion are all load-bearing and all preserved here — this page imports them, it does not replace them.

The graph model is the better account of *failure*, because failure is what a chain cannot model:

**A chain has one failure mode: the link breaks.** The chain model therefore reads every failure as the same shape — a node failing — and the May 2026 event as a node failure with unusually bad consequences. That reading is true and insufficient: it cannot say why the consequences were bad this time, because it cannot represent the missing failover as a structural absence rather than bad luck.

**A graph has a cut.** It can represent "the licit edge was down, the illicit primary failed, and the failover edge was the same node as the failed one" as a *state of the graph*, not a coincidence of events. The bad consequences follow from the state, not from the month.

**A chain's redundancy is additive.** More nodes, more depth, better. The chain model's remedy for fragility is implicitly "more nodes." The graph's remedy is *de-correlation*: two edges that fail for different reasons, in different places, through different people, so that no single condition can take both. A second friend-supplier would add redundancy to the chain and nothing to the graph — the friendship register would still correlate them. The licit route rebuilt in-state, the supplier outside the friendship: that is what de-correlation looks like, and only the graph can name it.

**A chain cannot falsify itself on correlation.** Its predictions are about nodes (the next node will be less reliable than the last). The graph's predictions are about states (the next failure of a one-edge graph will be sufficient; the licit failure will be geographic). Different predictions, different evidence, different page.

None of this retires the chain. Succession is a chain phenomenon — the load really did transfer from Zach to Johnny to Tom to Bop, and the economics of that transfer (strip prices, fronts, reliability percentages) are the chain model's native content. The correct relationship between the pages is stated in the frontmatter: *contradicts* on the failure architecture, *evidenced-by* and *parallels* everywhere else. Two instruments, one object.

The chain model's last word on May 2026 is that a single supplier's no-show cost a job. The graph model's correction: **it took two layers failing together to produce the only withdrawal in sixteen years, and the two layers failed together because they were never as separate as the chain made them look.** Tom was the failover and the primary. Geography was the condition, not the event. The cut was correlated. That is the fragility model, and it is the only one the record's single two-layer failure supports.

## The demand the graph answers

Every edge in this page's graph exists to carry one thing: a daily Suboxone dose that has not lapsed in sixteen-plus years. The [[wiki/mind/synthesis/intake-constancy|intake-constancy]] finding is the demand curve — fixed rate, rotating subject, the dose holding flat while suppliers, pharmacies, states, and delivery hardware rotate around it.

The graph is the supply topology answering that demand. Read together, the two pages describe a system with an unusual property: **the demand is the most reliable component in the entire architecture.** It has never failed. Not once. Every failure in the record is on the supply side.

That asymmetry is doing structural work. In most procurement systems the demand is the variable and the supply is engineered to meet it. Here the demand is a constant — roughly 5,840 consecutive daily executions **[DERIVED]** — and the supply is the variable that decayed around it.

The sixteen-year run is usually cited as evidence of the system's strength. Read as demand-against-supply, it is evidence of the system's *exposure*: a demand that cannot lapse, served by a graph whose cut set shrank to two edges, one of them a friendship. The longer the demand holds, the more the decaying supply looks like reliability, because every day the graph doesn't fail is another day of evidence that it won't.

That is the MTBF mask from the demand side: the constant demand manufactures the appearance of a robust supply by never testing it — until the one day the test arrives, and the test is total.

There is a second demand-side property worth naming: **the demand cannot be deferred, only displaced.** When the May 2026 cut arrived, Dan did not reduce the dose or skip a day — the record shows the opposite, a four-day scramble of escalating desperation to *maintain* the rate. The 72+ hours of withdrawal is what "cannot lapse" costs when the graph is cut.

This is why the fragility model is about the Suboxone subgraph specifically and not about procurement in general: it is the only subgraph whose demand has no elasticity at all. Cocaine demand bends with money. Cannabis demand bends with availability. Suboxone demand bends with nothing — which is why its graph needed the most redundancy and got the least.

And then there is the audit cost, which is where [[wiki/mind/synthesis/the-deferred-audit|the-deferred-audit]] enters the graph. Tom sat on that page's two-clocks table as a chosen object — a friendship dating to the late 2000s whose model revised only after the mid-May 2026 supply failure, roughly eighteen years of deferred audit **[ATTESTED]**.

The audit finally ran in May 2026: the block, the $36 demand, the threat via Tom's father, the severance. But notice the *timing*: the audit ran *after* the supply failure, not before it. The friendship was audited when it stopped delivering product, which means the audit was triggered by the graph's failure rather than preventing it.

A supply graph whose quality control runs eighteen years behind its edges is a graph that cannot learn from its own failure modes — the 2014 cycle produced no edge replacement, no diversification, no licit-layer rebuild. The 2026 cycle produced Bop: a better node, the same topology. The audit, when it finally ran, audited the *person* and left the *graph* untouched.
