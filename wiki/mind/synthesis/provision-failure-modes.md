# Provision Failure Modes

Across eleven years, money moves through Dan's attachments in a pattern regular enough to state as a grammar. Transfers arrive where sustained verbal reassurance does not: "do you love me" appears 0 times in 106,629 sent messages against "call me" 170 times (dat:0081), sympathy tokens run at 0.45x baseline while unprompted helping runs at 1.79–2.49x, and the amounts ladder from a $25 introduction purchase to a six-figure decade outflow without the underlying move changing. [[wiki/mind/synthesis/provision-grammar|Provision Grammar]] states that rule across the [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]], [[wiki/people/ally-lubin|Ally]], and [[wiki/people/suzanne-frank|Suz]] channels and closes with a short list of ways the register breaks.

This entry is that list, expanded to evidence. Each of the three channels fails in a different way, and in each case the failure is the channel's own working structure turned against itself: the retention channel fails by engineered absence, the priced channel fails by evidence that does not survive, and the family channel fails by a dispute that never closes. The denominations differ by orders of magnitude. The breaks do not track the denominations; they track the tempos at which each channel keeps its books.

What follows stays at the level the held instruments can see: transfers sent or withheld, messages timestamped, invoices issued or contested, repayments recorded or absent. Where a motive is quoted, it is quoted as the speaker's stated framing in the record, not carried as an explanation.

## The grammar in brief

Three registers, three goals, three bookkeeping tempos — the minimum context the failures need.

On the [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] channel the goal the record shows is retention: transfers denominated in necessities (rent servicing, household liquidity, later procurement) repeat across a decade, roughly $139K sent against $16–20K received in operator-provided payment-app screenshots dated 2026-07-15, a net outflow of roughly $119K–$123K at roughly six to one. The figure is operator-reported and derived, not an audited ledger, and every use here keeps that status. The ledger half of the pattern surfaces late on this channel: no running tab is itemized to her while the provision runs, and the total is discovered in 2026 when the apps are opened. [[wiki/mind/synthesis/high-provision-low-condolence|High-Provision, Low-Condolence]] names the two halves — provision first, later invocation or itemization — and the Annie channel runs the second half in slow motion.

On the [[wiki/people/ally-lubin|Ally]] channel the goal the record shows is discharge at item prices: $30–$50 negotiations, a $500 arrival promise, a claimed $2,100 crisis-window total for August–October 2019. Provision here is priced per item and invoiced in real time, inside the argument that prompted it. The ledger half and the provision half are the same breath.

On the [[wiki/people/suzanne-frank|Suz]] channel the goal the record shows is maintenance of a family facility that moves lumps on no paper: a roughly $20,000 draw against an undistributed estate in August 2018, about $14,000 of it transferred Dan→Suz within weeks, $4,000 recovered on Dan's account, the remainder disputed (dat:1293; kb/syntheses/money-and-estate). Provision here arrives pre-invoiced: her October 3, 2018 message is an account statement ("Dan to Suzanne / $1000 / $14,000+ / Sum of transactions on app"), computed before any argument about it.

The substitution underneath all three is measured, not inferred. Positive verbal expressions in this corpus function as stated rules; a transfer needs no sincerity judgment because it arrives as a countable event (dat:0081). The weighted profile carries the same split at weight 8.5 — instrumental helping elevated, condolence register suppressed — and this entry borrows it only at that strength: it predicts care will appear as countable acts in the message record, which is what the ledgers show. It does not predict, and this entry does not claim, anything about felt states behind the transfers.

Each failure mode below is named for what the record shows happening to the transfers, not for what anyone intended.

## Failure mode one: absence weaponized — the Annie channel

The retention register's normal operation is presence: the transfer repeats, the channel stays open, the node stays identifiable. Its failure is the inverse operation performed with the same materials — rent money withheld for two months while a housing crisis is narrated as external — so that the need for the node is manufactured by the node.

The primary case is the staged eviction of January–February 2025, decoded by the operator on 2026-08-13 and carried on [[wiki/mind/synthesis/four-financial-inversions|Four Financial Inversions]] and [[wiki/people/john-paci|John Paci]]. The dated moves, as the record holds them:

In late January 2025, after Eli brought [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] home, Dan telephoned landlord John Paci, stated the household could no longer service the balance, and asked Paci to pretend to be in the process of filing an eviction if Annie called. Paci agreed. Dan then told Annie the eviction was punishment for her having been sick in the hallway that night. He stopped paying new rent for the final two months, banking the cash as moving capital against a balance he had decided not to pay. Annie called the landlord; her parents called the landlord; both calls were answered inside the performance. The Paci thread carries zero rows between 2024-08-18 and 2025-02-01 — a 167-day silence spanning the setup — and the resumption rows ("Do it about 9," then thanks for help twenty-eight minutes later) are the only acknowledgment left in the message record. Some messages were deleted at the time to keep the plan from her, on the operator's account.

The debt underneath was real: roughly $10,000 to Paci plus a ConEd balance, serviced before this at $650 per week — enough to keep the landlord's patience open, never enough to retire the balance, fifteen months at a flat balance. What the decode changes is not any figure. It changes what the servicing meant while it ran, and what the final two months of non-payment were. Servicing a debt intended for retirement and servicing a debt privately decided for abandonment are the same $650 leaving the account and different utterances. In the first, the transfer says the facility stays open. In the second, the withheld transfer says a deadline exists that the recipient must move against. The provision register did not pause in early 2025; it inverted. Money that normally arrived as the bond's operating register was withheld so that words — the eviction story — could perform.

A second, smaller instance shows the same inversion at supply scale and measures how long it can hold. On 2025-07-26 at 02:45 the corpus holds its only refused supply request in the terminal phase; the refusal reversed thirty-one hours later. Dan's own dated gloss on the supply register, two days after the reversal (2025-07-28), states the retention function in his words: getting her drugs "didn't really benefit me nearly as much as it was about that being the only way you would see me." The quote is carried here as his framing of his own transfers, which the transfer record is consistent with; it is not needed as proof. The proof is behavioral and thinner: provision withheld once, for thirty-one hours, then resumed, in a channel where 129 severance declarations across the decade resumed at a 36-second median (dat:1292 via kb/data/1292). Absence, in this register, is expensive to sustain because the channel's whole history is presence. The staged eviction sustained it for two months only by recruiting an outside performer and deleting messages — by spending social capital the ordinary register never spends.

What breaks, precisely: not the figures, which survive audit, and not the recipient's knowledge, since she never found out. What breaks is the transfer's legibility as the countable, interpretation-free utterance the grammar depends on. A transfer that arrives because a crisis was engineered to require it still arrives; a transfer withheld to create the crisis still counts as an event. But neither can be read at face value as the node's standing availability, because availability was the thing being staged. The failure is specific to the retention goal. A discharge channel cannot weaponize absence this way — there is no standing availability to fake — and a maintenance channel invoices too continuously for a two-month silence to pass unnoticed. Only the channel whose ledger surfaces years late can run a two-month inversion before anyone totals it. The slow-motion tempo is what makes the failure possible, and the July 2026 screenshots — the tempo finally arriving — are what make it visible.

## Failure mode two: evidence decayed — the Ally channel

The priced register's normal operation is the opposite of slow motion: offer, counter, transfer, audit, all inside the same argument. Its failure is slower than its operation. The invoice is issued in real time, but the evidence underneath it is a screenshot of a payment app, and over seven years the screenshot's authority decays until the same artifact supports opposite readings.

The dated sequence is short and complete. On 2019-10-22, mid-argument, Dan audits the arrangement himself: "lady i've sent 2100 and have like 2 pictures and im STILL trying lol." Ally replies, "You have not sent me $2100." He sends a screenshot. She replies, "Oh fuck." In 2019 the artifact settles the exchange; the conversation continues. On 2026-08-18, reading her own wiki entry in the channel's first sustained mutual window in years, she reopens the same figure: "Also there's no proof you ever sent me $2100 except your [screenshot] / So I contest." Both readings are on the record verbatim ([[wiki/people/ally-lubin|Ally Lubin]]). No 2019 Cash App export exists in raw/ to settle the amount, and the $2,100 remains his claimed crisis-window figure — bounded by his own accounting to August–October 2019, excluding the 2018 and January 2019 gifts — accepted in 2019, contested in 2026, uncorroborated at both dates.

The decay mechanism is visible in what each date asks the artifact to do. In 2019 the screenshot is asked to show that transfers happened, in a thread where the $30–$50 negotiations and the Cash App system notifications ($13–$101, 2018-12-11 to 2019-09-06) make transfers the channel's ordinary traffic; "Oh fuck" is recognition inside a live ledger. In 2026 the screenshot is asked to prove a total, $2,100, seven years after the window closed, against a recipient auditing a published page about herself. A screenshot shows a screen at a moment; it does not carry its own chain of custody, and the channel never deposited the underlying export anywhere the later audit could reach. The real-time tempo, which feels like the strongest bookkeeping of the three, turns out to depend entirely on contemporaneity. While the argument is live, the invoice is self-authenticating because both parties share the context. Afterward, the invoice is exactly as strong as its weakest artifact, and its weakest artifact is a picture of numbers.

A parallel decay runs on the same channel's second count: his "like 2 pictures" against her standing counter that she sent more, "they just prob weren't graphic nudes / TASTEFUL nudes." The image count was never exported either, and in 2026 it is disputed in the same exchange as the money. The channel priced identifiability — face visible at a premium — and kept no archive of what was delivered. Both halves of the priced exchange, payment and product, survive only as claims about artifacts neither party retained in verifiable form.

What breaks, precisely: not the transfers themselves, whose occurrence at item scale is independently attested by the negotiations and notifications, and not the relationship's terms, which both parties still describe identically in 2026. What breaks is the ledger half's promise that invoicing in real time settles anything. On this channel the invoice does not settle; it schedules the dispute for whenever the evidence has decayed enough to contest. The failure is specific to the real-time tempo for the mirror reason the Annie failure is specific to slow motion: a channel that invoices immediately has no later discovery moment at which a fuller record could correct the early one, because the early artifact was treated as the record. Slow motion at least produces the screenshots eventually. Real time produces a screenshot immediately and nothing ever again.

## Failure mode three: dispute-suspended — the Suz channel

The family register's normal operation is pre-invoicing: every transfer arrives with its running total attached, in a system that re-lends lumps on no paper at the moment of arrival (six estate advances totaling $42,500 across 2019–2020, final share $144,069.31 gross, $134,069.31 after the June advance — dat:0051). Its failure is not absence and not decay. The books are kept so continuously, by both parties, in opposite directions, that the money stops moving as a fact and sits permanently suspended between two invoices.

The transfer at the center is the roughly $14,000 moved Dan→Suz in August–October 2018, drawn against the expected estate before distribution. Her October 3, 2018 statement totals it in advance of any dispute ("$14,000+ / Sum of transactions on app") and in the same message computes what a further $1,450 from him would do to the balance. Dan's account records $4,000 recovered. No source records any further repayment of the remaining roughly $10,000. By June 20, 2019, his position is stated as a loan awaiting the inheritance ("I LENT YOU 14,000 … which i'm supposed to be paid back when the inheritance comes right"). On July 3, 2019 — one of the thread's eight highest-volume days at 105 messages — her counter is stated as receipts for theft ("I have the PayPal receipts from the $14,000 you stole from me"), and three hours later the number becomes a denominator ("$14,000 is a drop in the bucket compared to what you have cost me since 2004"). Neither claim was ever adjudicated. Neither was withdrawn. The standing state, seven years on, is two creditor claims over one transfer (dat:1293).

Read the number's career as the failure. In October 2018, $14,000 is a total: the sum of transactions, computable, shared. In June 2019 it is a loan: directional, repayable, scheduled against an event. In July 2019 it is simultaneously a theft (larger than a loan, criminal in register) and a drop in the bucket (smaller than everything else, negligible in register). The same figure is inflated and deflated in the same day's thread, because once both parties invoice, the figure's job is no longer to denominate a transfer. Its job is to denominate grievance — a unit in which later arguments can be measured. A transfer suspended this way cannot be repaid even in principle, because repayment would require agreeing on which invoice is the ledger, and the disagreement about the ledger is the channel's steady state. Her stated income across the decade, $11,000–$14,000 a year against $10,000 a year in property taxes, with roughly $157,000 in scheduled Chapter 13 liabilities filed in October 2024, is context the record supplies for why no surplus ever existed on either side to force a settlement; it explains the suspension's durability without explaining anyone's intent, and this entry uses it only for durability.

What breaks, precisely: not the record, which is the fullest of the three channels, and not the transfers, whose direction the 2026-08-18 correction settled (the $14,000 ran son→mother, opposite the channel's folk memory; dat:1293). What breaks is settlement itself as an available operation. The Annie channel discovers its total late; the Ally channel contests its total late; the Suz channel totals continuously and therefore never has a moment at which a total could be accepted as final. Pre-invoicing, which looks like the most rigorous tempo, removes the one thing the other two tempos retain: a before, during which the transfer was not yet a dispute. Here the dispute is coeval with the transfer. The failure is entropic rather than dramatic — books kept so well, by two bookkeepers with opposite conclusions, that they stop describing the money at all.

## What the three failures share

One pattern, visible only across the three: each failure preserves the transfer record and destroys a different property the grammar needs from it. Weaponized absence preserves the amounts and destroys legibility — the events can no longer be read as standing availability. Decayed evidence preserves the claims and destroys settleability — the events can no longer be closed by the artifact made to close them. Suspended dispute preserves the bookkeeping and destroys finality — the events can no longer be agreed as finished. In each case what survives is exactly what the channel was best at keeping (late totals, instant invoices, continuous statements), and what fails is the complementary property that channel never practiced.

The denominations do not predict the failures. The smallest contested figure ($2,100) and the mid-sized suspended figure ($14,000) fail harder, as usable records, than the largest figure ($119K–$123K net), which — for all its soft provenance as operator-provided screenshots — has never been contested by its recipient on amount, only discovered. Size is not the variable. Tempo is. This is an attention-level claim about the message and payment records: it describes which artifacts exist at which dates and what later messages do with them. It does not require, and does not assert, any claim about why either party kept the books they kept.

Three scoreable expectations follow, stated so the record can break them. First, any future significant transfer on a priced, no-contract channel will be invoiced inside its prompting argument and will become contestable in proportion to the absence of an exported underlying record; an exported record that settles such a figure without dispute would break the decay claim. Second, any future retention-channel provision gap longer than roughly a month will, on inspection, show staging costs (an outside performer, deleted messages, a manufactured deadline) rather than a simple pause; a long, unstaged, undisputed pause would break the absence claim. Third, the $14,000 will remain in its suspended state until an outside instrument — a court, an estate accounting, a signed writing — totals it from outside the channel; a bilateral settlement message in-thread, accepted by both parties, would break the suspension claim at any date.

## Conflicts in the record

**The $2,100 figure (2019–2026).** Claimed by Dan on 2019-10-22 as his crisis-window total; accepted in-thread the same night on a screenshot ("Oh fuck"); contested by Ally on 2026-08-18 ("no proof you ever sent me $2100 except your [screenshot]"). No 2019 Cash App export exists in raw/. Current standing: disputed, held at exactly that status throughout this entry; the priced register's existence does not depend on the figure's accuracy, since the $30–$50 negotiations attest it independently.

**The staged-eviction agency detail (2025–2026).** The operator decode states Annie's parents called Paci to test the eviction story. Dan's 2025-02-06 text to Paci states, "I had to call her parents." Both can be true in sequence; the record does not settle which party initiated which call. Current standing: the eviction performance and Paci's agreement are corroborated at four primary-message points ([[wiki/people/john-paci|John Paci]]); the call-agency detail remains operator testimony with the tension preserved.

**The direction of the Suz channel (corrected 2026-08-18).** Folk memory and the earlier spine framing ran the deep-cycle money mother→son, including a generalized "$750/week borrowing" rate. The primary record holds one such accusation (2018-12-13, a single week, in argument) and the largest movement running Dan→Suz (dat:1293). Current standing: direction corrected, rate retracted; this entry prices the channel in lumps, not rates, for that reason.

**The $650/week attribution.** The Paci servicing belongs to the Annie channel's housing facility, not to the Suz channel, despite the two channels' shared family liquidity; an earlier draft of the grammar's failure list placed it by association with the $14,000. Current standing: attributed to the Annie channel throughout, as the inversions page dates it.

**Rotations count on the Annie channel.** The estate spine's three-rotation see-saw and the inversions page's four rotations periodize the same decade differently. This entry uses the four-rotation frame for transfer dating and the spine for capital arrival, as the grammar does. Current standing: carried unresolved; no failure mode above depends on the boundary placement.

## Assessment

The record supports one judgment beyond the catalog: the provision register does not fail at random, and it does not fail where the money is largest. It fails along each channel's bookkeeping tempo, at the joint where that tempo meets time — late discovery inverted into staged absence, instant invoicing outlived by its artifact, continuous invoicing cancelled by its mirror. A grammar that only described successful provision would miss the finding the failures carry: the register's reliability in ordinary operation and its failure modes are the same property observed at different dates. What would change this assessment is a fourth channel with a sustained provision record failing in a way its tempo does not predict; the [[wiki/mind/synthesis/kristin-channel|Kristin channel]] (August–November 2025, ended over $40, ledger unexamined) is the standing candidate, and its small denomination makes it the cleanest available test of whether tempo, rather than size, governs the break.
