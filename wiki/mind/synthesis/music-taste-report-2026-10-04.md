# Music Taste Report — Chaos in October, Week of Sep 28 – Oct 4, 2026

**Subject:** Dan Frank's current taste state, measured against this week's inclusions.
**Method note:** Lyrics are treated throughout as timbre, not semantic content — that is how Dan processes sung words. All audio claims are spectral/FFT measurements on source files; nothing here is a listening claim except where attributed to Dan's own testimony. Confidence is labeled per finding.

**Dataset:**
- 58 tracks spectrally profiled in one pipeline (RMS, crest factor, spectral centroid, rolloff, band energies, chroma/key estimate, stereo width, energy arcs): the 38-track Chaos in October v1 pool (t1–t38) plus this week's 20 Discover Weekly / Release Radar winners (w1–w20).
- Playlist diff: Chaos v1 (38) → v2 (52): 20 added, 6 removed, full reshuffle.
- Revealed-preference data: Spotify weekly stats screenshots (1,444 minutes, week Sep 28–Oct 4; weekly top songs), three prior weeks' top-5 screenshots.
- Stated-preference data: Dan's named top tracks (Oct 2), his refined picks (Oct 3), two ALL TIME TOP 100 VIP CRATE additions.

## 1. The filter is frozen; the roster churns

The headline finding of this week's intake: **the 20 winners are statistically indistinguishable from the 38 incumbents on every measured axis.**

| Axis (median) | Incumbents (38) | Winners (20) |
|---|---|---|
| Tempo (DSP est.) | 130 bpm | 131 bpm |
| Spectral centroid | 3,159 Hz | 3,226 Hz |
| RMS | 0.323 | 0.324 |
| Crest factor | 3.09 | 3.08 |
| Stereo width | 0.348 | 0.349 |
| Sub+bass share | 27.9% | 25.5% |
| Presence+brilliance | 38.2% | 38.1% |

Spotify's two discovery surfaces threw a week of heterogeneous material at him; what survived his skip-test filter lands dead-center on his standing coordinates. **Confidence: high** (58 tracks, one consistent pipeline).

Interpretation: this is not range expansion. It is parameter confirmation. The taste function — tempo utility bands, a dark-warm spectral center (~3.0–3.2 kHz centroid), high but not brickwalled loudness, sub weight around a quarter of total energy — did not move a single notch. What moved is *personnel*: which artists occupy the slots.

**Rival check:** the sameness could be Spotify's algorithm feeding him his own center rather than his filter imposing it. Rejected as primary explanation: his cuts are active interventions (below), and he prunes *toward* the same center the filter keeps. The algorithm proposes; the center is his. **Confidence: moderate-high.**

## 2. Tempo architecture: the triumvirate holds

Of the 20 winners, 17 sit between 129.5 and 143.5 bpm DSP (the 128 house shelf and the 140 force shelf); one (Do You, Upgrade (UK)) lands at 172.5, doubling the pool's 174-class membership alongside Turbo Speed.

The three below-floor exceptions are informative precisely because they're exceptions: Tokyo Girl (115, and the darkest winner measured, centroid 2,051 Hz), This High - Slowed (120), Catwalk (123). Slow material enters only as a deliberate breather or — see §5 — under an artist about to be exiled.

His own framing (Oct 4, post-SNL): the 128/140/174 split is not genre loyalty but "a fundamental utility difference between them structurally." The intake data agrees: tempo is the hardest gate in the filter. **Confidence: high on the distribution; the utility reading is his testimony, consistent with the numbers.**

## 3. The cuts are authorship

The six removals were not random churn. The loud extreme came off the top — Heat Check (RMS 0.483, the loudest and brightest of the original 38, crest 2.07), Ignite (0.437), TAKA Remix (0.391) — and the quiet outlier came off the bottom (Out of My Mind, 0.241, the pool's former breather).

Then the detonation function was immediately re-imported at a different shelf: **Do You (w14) measures RMS 0.4766, crest 2.10** — Heat Check's exact job description, now at 175 bpm drum & bass instead of 141. The slot survived its occupant's execution.

Read: he trims extremes as *tracks* while preserving them as *functions*. The mix still needs exactly one loudest moment; it just can't be that one anymore. **Confidence: high on measurements; moderate on motive.**

## 4. The vocal axis: timbre, warmth, layering

Revealed preference (his own play counts, week Sep 28–Oct 4): CANNA "all that i can say" 21 plays, Nights Like 18, Pull Me In 13, Meet Me In The Dark 13, We Stay Inside 12. His refined stated picks (Oct 3): CANNA, Nights Like, Subsurface, nimino. The overlap is the signal: **the 126–130 bpm, female-vocal-led, warm-dark cluster is the revealed center of the taste** — the voice functioning as lead melody/timbre, per the qualifier at the top of this report.

His two ALL TIME TOP 100 additions this week (Subsurface "You Got Me Singin' Like (Ayoo)", CANNA) both sit at 126–128 bpm with centroids ~2,900–3,100 Hz — intimate loudness, not peak-time. His own summary of the distinction stands: the 140 monsters are events; these are company.

**Layering measurement (new this week):** on Dan's commission, a phone-capture clip he flagged as possibly "the best vocal layering in this thing" was fingerprinted (sliding-chroma, top match: We Stay Inside at ~2:40 in, margin thin) and the candidate vocal tracks were then measured on clean source files. Distinct simultaneous spectral peaks in the 100–1,000 Hz fundamental band, per frame: **We Stay Inside 7.9** — Nights Like 5.0, Subsurface 4.7, CANNA 4.0. We Stay Inside is the layering outlier of the pool (widest presence-band spread, 0.139), while CANNA is the purest solo voice (fewest peaks, most tonal). His ear identified the densest arrangement in the crate; the FFT agrees. **Confidence: moderate-high** (capture-based ID is corroborated, not proven; the clean-file metrics are solid).

Emerging axis to watch: arrangement density in the vocal band may be a distinct preference variable from vocal presence itself — the pool contains both poles (CANNA's single exposed voice vs. We Stay Inside's stack) at the top of his play counts. Two different pleasures, both his.

## 5. The binary: the Lonelysoul. exile

The week's cleanest specimen of the mechanism documented in *The Lame Detector*: Lonelysoul. went from four tracks deep in v1 (five in v2, including the new import This High - Slowed) to "I hate them now" within a day (Oct 4, 05:36–05:37 EDT). Stated tripwires, his words: **too slow**, and **overproduction** — "putting out too much material and looks like a try hard or scammer."

The numbers partly understudy the verdict: Say My Name Again carries 0.6% presence-band energy, the thinnest measured in the pool; the new import is literally a slowed version at 120 bpm, below the tempo floor. But the exile cause is not primarily sonic — it is the optics of volume. Output rate crossed from *catalog depth* (which he rewarded with five slots) to *try-hard signal* (which his detector reads as lameness), and the switch flipped wholesale. There is no demotion tier, no "their old stuff was good": occupation or exile, the same binary he described in himself the night before ("extreme single-interest binary that is pervasive in what I do").

Note the asymmetry with §3: *tracks* get trimmed functionally; *artists* get exiled categorically. **Confidence: high on the event and the stated reasons; the mechanism reading is consistent with his self-diagnosis.**

## 7. Sequencing consequences

Platform constraints as reported by Dan (Oct 4): Spotify's mix editor tempo-matches within ~10 bpm across a transition, then rides back to native tempo afterward; "they just don't get the grid." Combined with this week's pool state:

- Nights Like now opens the playlist; the original opener (zone) sits at #48. Whether v2's order is the mix sequence is **unconfirmed** — v1's order was addition order, not sequence. All sequencing readings here are conditional.
- If the order holds as sequence: Glow no longer closes (it's #35); the mix ends on a 140 tail (Flash, Counting) after the Do You detonation at #45 — a force ending replacing the old warm landing. OUTTAHISMIND at #43 is the last breather before the wall.
- The ~10 bpm clamp makes the 129→140 steps the mix's load-bearing joints; the 140→175 jump to Do You cannot be smoothed by the editor and will read as a slam or a drift — placement suggests that's intended.

He has now heard the current flow end-to-end for the first time ("I've heard it now and it sounds good") after building the previous mix deaf by key/BPM matching. The deaf-build era's first listening verdict is positive.
