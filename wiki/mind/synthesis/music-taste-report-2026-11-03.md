For nineteen days Bing Bong only grew. Fifty-six tracks by Oct 20, sixty-nine by Oct 23, ninety-seven by the night of Nov 2 — and in all that time exactly three tracks had ever been removed, quietly, somewhere between the Oct 23 export and the Nov 2 one. Then, at about five in the morning on Nov 3, in a single minute of Spotify adds (10:05–10:06 UTC), Dan built a new playlist out of the old one's bones: **"Samantha's Charm Offensive," 31 tracks**, and said what it was in one breath — a revised version of Bing Bong; there were too many skips; he cut out the stuff he didn't want.

Every prior version of this report measured intake: what he reached for. This one measures the opposite instrument. A skip is a revealed preference under zero cost — no rating, no review, just a thumb moving on. The keep-set below is what survived roughly two weeks of embodied listening; the cut-set is what discovery excitement added and listening rejected. The distinction matters because the series has been here before: the Taste Constitution's correction log records that axis-fit predicted crate *membership* while the crown stayed Dan's call. Additions are predictions. Survival is the verdict.

One framing note, stated once and held throughout: Charm Offensive is a *separate* playlist. Whether Bing Bong itself was also pruned after the 97-track export is not in evidence; every "cut" below means "absent from the new list," nothing more.

**Time was the loudest filter.** Survival by add date: October adds kept 13 of 72 (18%); Nov 1–3 adds kept 16 of 25 (64%). The mid-October binge that built the list (56 tracks, Oct 15–20) mostly died; the last seventy-two hours of intake mostly lived. Version-4-era tracks survive at 12/69 (17%); post-version-4 adds at 17/31 (55%).

**Sung, not spoken — and a voice must exist.** The rap/talk crossover wing was executed (GLOCKTALK; Kill Switch; the hyphy edges). Zero near-pure instrumentals survived: five were in Bing Bong, none are in Charm Offensive. 27 of 29 carried tracks are vocal-led, and the featured vocalists are overwhelmingly women — The Duchess, Dub Princess, Jem Cooke, Linney, Veronica Bravo, No/Me — the Constitution's female-vocal-at-the-summit pattern holding inside a riddim corpus.

**Heavier, not bouncier.** Danceability *fell* in the keeps (mean 0.62 vs 0.70) while loudness rose — Spotify's bounce metric penalizes exactly the heavy, irregular drops that survived. Valence did not move (0.336 vs 0.327) and popularity did not move (42 vs 41): neither mood nor fame sorted anything.

**The dabble wings were purged.** UK garage as an identity: 22 cut against 3 kept carrying the tag. Bassline 7 cut / 0 kept; tech house 6/0; EDM trap 4/0. Garage as *seasoning* survived (GOOD TIME; Body (next to mine); Need Your Love). What stands is one animal — American bass music: dubstep, riddim, bass house, with melodic vocal bass at the edges.

**The tempo middle was the graveyard.** Survival by band: 120–134 bpm (the Constitution's home room) 6/27, 22% — the worst rate measured. The 135–145 spine held (13 kept), and the extremes survived best: the 170s drum-and-bass at 4/8 (50%), the slow pair (BREATHE at 90, Stuntin at 97) at 2/5 (40%). In this corpus, mid-tempo was neither heavy enough nor fast enough to live.

**The titles tell the same story semantically.** Kept: Cry, You Got Me, Love Me In The Dark, Need Your Love, Bad Habit, Out of Time, Wide Awake, Lose U, Body (next to mine), Bring Me Home, Siren Call. Cut: GLOCKTALK, Kill Switch, Bugatti, Motorola, PARTY, JUMP ON IT. Same darkness in both piles (the valence row above) — aimed differently. The cull kept the darkness aimed at a person and cut the darkness aimed at the room.

**Loyalty is per-track, never per-artist.** SVDKO kept exactly one track (GOOD TIME, with ALOTT) while the entire Mary Droppinz / SVDKO / JONCASS bloc was cut. Repeat survivors: COSTA ×2, Eliminate ×2, NXSTY ×2. Phosphr lost "Identity" and kept "Panic Champ" — the track Dan had singled out in a call the night before ("Panic Jam," in his words). Canon-orbit names in the keep pile: LYNY (Disc Jockey, released Oct 30), ero808 (DUST), Zeds Dead.

## Grading the predictions

**Version 3's purge asymmetry — GRADED AT LAST: INVERTED.** Two versions carried this prediction untestable through zero-purge windows. The cull grades it, and it comes back backwards: UK garage-tagged tracks survived at 3/25 (12%), dubstep-tagged at 9/24 (37.5%), riddim-tagged at 5/13 (38%). Dubstep and riddim did not purge faster than UK garage; UK garage purged roughly three times faster than either. The asymmetry the prediction sensed is real — it points the other way.

**Version 3's JONCASS question — CLOSED.** Version 4 graded the bloc a single-release inhalation on no-recurrence. The cull completes the file: LOUDER, CHECK, READY, DOWN, and the Bugatti remix are all cut — 0 of 5. Inhaled Oct 18, never recurred, now removed. SVDKO's survival runs entirely through GOOD TIME, a post-bloc window add with ALOTT.

**Version 3's doorway track — CLOSED, AGAINST THE BET.** "Love Is Gonna Save Us" (ARTBAT; Benny Benassi) survived versions 3 and 4 and was cut in the cull. The salience-law bet it carried — that the doorway track outlasts its session — fails on retention, the only instrument this series has ever had. The rotation debt that always blocked the stronger version of the claim stands below, unpapered.

**Version 4, prediction 1 — the NXSTY test: ADOPTION LEG, WITH A TWIST.** BREATHE — a standalone NXSTY single (released Oct 2), added Nov 2 evening — is a recurrence beyond both inhaled releases, and it survived the cull. By the prediction's own construction, that is adoption. But the inhaled material split: MIXTAPE ONE's "DUST" (with ero808) kept; both Break The Body EP tracks (Shatter, Floor Burn) cut; EMERALD 2.0 was one of the three quiet pre-cull removals. The artist was adopted; the EP was not. NXSTY-credited survival: 2 of 4.

**Version 4, prediction 2 — first-purge target: ORDERING FALSIFIED, SUBSTANCE CONFIRMED.** The prediction named the Oct 20 afternoon scatter session as the first removal source. The actual first removals — the three quiet ones between Oct 23 and Nov 2 — were EMERALD 2.0, "real 4 me - BKAYE VIP," and "Let's Get This": none of them from that session. But when the main cull came, the named session died entire: Skittles, Hit Em, Dangerous, and Nice To Meet Those are all cut. The prediction found the right corpse and the wrong time of death.

**Version 4, prediction 3 — the daylight test: HOLDS, FRAYED.** Post-version-4 adds land 11/31 overnight (35%) — a majority outside 00:00–06:00 ET, so the shift stands by the test's own terms. The texture is less clean than the grade: the window's two big sessions (Nov 2: 14 adds; Nov 3: 11) dwarf the late-October grazing, and the final pre-cull session — Nov 3, 03:38–03:49 EST, eleven tracks in eleven minutes — was overnight. Daylight grazing was real; the binge came back for one night and built the cull's evidence base.

**Version 4, prediction 4 — the brightness test: SET-POINT LEG.** The window slice's median valence is 0.336 — under the 0.35 line. The 0.393 slice of version 4 was a bright excursion; the intake set-point near 0.32–0.34 holds, and the full keep-set medians at 0.336.

**Version 4's tempo ratification — AMENDED.** Version 4 ratified the 140 shelf as intake center. The cull keeps the shelf (median 140.0 among keeps) but reveals what intake medians hid: the 120–134 band underneath it was mostly tourist material, surviving at 22%. The 140 center is real; it is narrower and more heavily defended than version 4 knew.

## Conflicts in the record

- **Bing Bong's own state — unresolved (2026-11-03).** Charm Offensive is a new playlist, not an edit receipt. The 97-track Bing Bong may still exist intact, may have been pruned in parallel, or may have been superseded. The exports cannot say. This report grades the keep-set only.
- **The three quiet removals — timing unknown (2026-11-03).** EMERALD 2.0, "real 4 me - BKAYE VIP," and "Let's Get This" vanish between the Oct 23 and Nov 2 exports; no export timestamps a removal. They are recorded here as the first removals in the playlist's history on set-difference evidence alone.
- **This version vs. the Taste Constitution — still no amendment (2026-11-03).** The cull is retention evidence about one nineteen-day corpus, and it cuts both ways against the Constitution: it confirms the female-vocal pattern and the per-track crown principle, and it shows the 125–130 home room failing to protect a single band of Bing Bong tracks. The canon, the true #1, and the I–vi finding stand untouched. Crowning and kicking remain Dan's acts; this version notes for the record that he has now performed a kick at scale, and it was not aimed where the home room would have aimed it.

## Assessment

Versions 1–4 described a man changing what he listens to while changing nothing about what he wants it to do to him. The cull adds the missing half of the instrument: what he wants it to do, measured by refusal. Ninety-seven tracks of reaching resolved into thirty-one tracks of keeping, and the keep-set is coherent in a way the intake never was — one genre animal, sung by women, dark toward a person rather than a room, too heavy for its own danceability score, sitting on a 140 spine with the extremes kept and the middle shot out. The two new tracks he chose to lead it — "Cry" and "You Got Me," both added in the same minute as the twenty-nine survivors — say the distillation was not only subtraction. He re-led the list while he pruned it.

The recursive series also did what it was built to do: of the seven predictions graded here, two closed clean (JONCASS, brightness), one closed against its bet (the doorway track), one graded inverted (purge asymmetry), one split (NXSTY: artist yes, EP no), one held frayed (daylight), and one found the right corpse at the wrong time (first purge). A profile that can score itself like that is worth more than one that is merely right.

**Debts carried forward.**
1. *Harmony.* Still no chroma pass on this corpus (now 97 + 31 tracks). The I–vi question remains the profile's largest open measurement, and the cull makes it sharper: the keep-set is exactly the sample a harmonic pass should run on first.
2. *Rotation.* Still no play counts. The cull is the strongest retention evidence yet; it is still not preference measured in plays.
3. *Bing Bong's fate.* Whether the parent list still exists, and whether it keeps growing, is unrecorded.

**Predictions** (made 2026-11-03, to be graded by the next version).
1. **The thirty-one are sticky.** Charm Offensive will lose fewer than 15% of these 31 tracks in the next thirty days — the skip test has already been run on them by the only instrument that matters. A second cull at Bing Bong's rate (~70%) falsifies the entire survival-as-evidence frame of this version. *Confidence: moderate-high.*
2. **The openers lead or die.** "Cry" and "You Got Me" — the only two tracks never skip-tested before coronation — either remain in the list's first five positions at the next export or are cut entirely; no quiet middle demotion. *Confidence: low-moderate.*
3. **NXSTY recurs as a solo credit.** The adoption leg predicts a further NXSTY add outside MIXTAPE ONE / Break The Body material in the next window; another whole-release inhalation instead reclassifies BREATHE as a third inhalation's leading edge. *Confidence: moderate.*
