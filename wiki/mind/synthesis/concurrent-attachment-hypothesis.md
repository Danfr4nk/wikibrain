# The Concurrent-Attachment Hypothesis

[[wiki/people/annie-ulmer]] cites "the concurrent-attachment hypothesis" as
if it were a dedicated page. It was not one. This is it: the formal
statement, the recomputed base rate it rests on, the predictions it makes,
the falsifiers that kill it, and the dated 90-day scoring protocol that
settles it. Nothing here is argued by narrative. Every claim carries its
marker, every count carries its method, and the hypothesis is stated so
that the record can break it.

The question, in one sentence: **does Dan's attachment system run one live
channel at a time, activating the secondary channel only after the primary
bond fails — or can it run two live channels simultaneously, with the
secondary channel carrying load *during* the primary bond's instability?**
The first is the old rule ([[wiki/people/ally-lubin]]'s formulation: *"the
channel activates when the primary bond fails; every burst is a failure
somewhere else"*). The second is the hypothesis. August 18–19, 2026 — 408
messages to Annie and 552 to Ally, interleaved hour by hour — is the one
window in seventeen years where the two cannot be told apart by assertion,
which is why the hypothesis exists and why it gets ninety days.

## The three contenders, stated so they can be killed

**H0 — sequential (the null).** One channel carries relational load at a
time. The secondary channel activates only *after* a primary-bond rupture
or severance, never during. The August 18–19 dual-load is a measurement
artifact of a transfer in progress: the old occupant's thread still warm
while the successor bid opens. Under H0, the next primary instability
produces an Ally burst only after the instability resolves, with no
concurrent load. H0 is [[wiki/mind/synthesis/single-channel]] stated as a
timing claim rather than a volume claim.

**H1 — concurrent (the hypothesis).** The system sustains parallel live
channels. The secondary channel activates *during* primary-bond
instability and carries load alongside the primary, hour by hour. Under
H1, the next primary instability produces an Ally burst inside the
instability window, visible in the primary thread on the same days. H1 is
[[wiki/people/ally-lubin]]'s own prediction, adopted with the mechanism
attached: *"the next Ally burst will not require a severance to precede
it — it will appear during, not after, whatever the next instability is,
and it will be visible in the Annie or successor thread on the same
days"* [OPERATOR-formulated, filed on the Ally page].

**H_term — termination artifact (the rival).** August 2026 is a one-off
produced by the terminal rupture of an eleven-year bond, not a standing
pattern. The concurrency reflects the extremity of the ending, not the
architecture of the system. Under H_term, the next primary instability —
if a new primary bond forms — shows the old sequential pattern, and no
dual-load episode recurs. H_term is the ally-lubin page's own caveat:
*"If the overlap reflects a standing pattern rather than a one-off
termination artifact"* — the page left the disjunction open, and this
entry scores it.

**Priors** [INFERRED — subjective, stated so they can be argued with,
not smuggled]: H0 0.70, H1 0.15, H_term 0.15. The justification is the
denominator: seventeen years of documented operation contain exactly one
48-hour dual-load episode. A system that ran one way for ~6,200 days and
another way for two does not get its prior set by the two. The August
window is evidence, not a new base rate — and the protocol below is how
the base rate gets rebuilt, one observation at a time. Anyone who wants
different priors can substitute them; the likelihoods are where the
argument lives, and they are stated next to each observation.

**What "instability" means for scoring.** Not a mood, not a vibe — a
dated observable, one of: (a) a severance declaration in any primary
thread (the 129-episode loop's instrument); (b) a rival bid for the
primary slot (the [[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]] 2025 shape: exclusivity demands + volume
dominance); (c) a documented rupture event (a dated break, a departure,
a disclosed infidelity, a third-party intrusion like the June 2026 Coles
messages). "No instability" means none of the three is dated within the
scoring window. This definition is deliberately narrow: the hypothesis
dies faster under a narrow definition, which is what a killable
hypothesis wants.

**What "activation" means for scoring.** A Dan-originated burst to Ally
of ≥9 messages within 48 hours following ≥60 days of Dan-silence, with
romantic or pursuit content (marriage/elopement framing, priced pursuit,
or the confessional register at volume). The ≥9 threshold is set by the
smallest filed activation (June 1, 2026: nine messages). The ≥60-day
silence rule excludes the long solo runs from counting as multiple
activations. Ally-initiated contact counts separately — it is F2's
watch-item, not an activation under H1.

criteria. If the method is wrong, the count is wrong in the open, which
is the point.

### Criterion B (broad): sensitivity check

**Broad criterion:** every Dan-originated burst ≥20 messages after ≥60
days of Dan-silence, regardless of pursuit content. This adds C2 (the
Dec 2018→Jan 2019 re-entry episode, 120+ messages) and C4 (2020-06-08,
51 messages):

|---|---|---|
| C2 (2018-12-06→2019-01-09) | 2018 deep cycle: financial chaos, no dated primary-bond rupture. NYC Round Two move comes Feb 2019, after the window. | **No** |

**Result under criterion B: 3 of 6** [DERIVED]. The two added episodes
are both non-adjacent, which halves the rate from 75% to 50%.

The sensitivity is the finding, not a footnote: **the base rate is
criterion-sensitive between 50% and 75%, and the 3-of-4 figure depends on
the pursuit-content criterion that selects the literature's four.**
This entry keeps 3-of-4 as the stated rate *with the method attached*,
because the method (pursuit episodes, not mere contact) is the one the
hypothesis is actually about — H1 predicts pursuit-activation timing,
not that every text clusters at ruptures. But anyone scoring the
hypothesis against the broad count gets 3-of-6, and the protocol below
scores new observations under both criteria. A base rate that hides its
sensitivity is a base rate doing rhetoric.

### What the base rate does and does not support

What it supports: **the Ally channel's pursuit activations track
primary-bond ruptures.** Three of four (narrow) or three of six (broad)
sit within ±7 days of a dated instability, and the June 2026 leg is
timed to the minute. The tracking is real under either criterion —
50% is still far above chance for ±7-day windows scattered across
eighteen years. [DERIVED — the chance baseline: ~14 rupture-adjacent
days per year × 18 years ≈ 252 of ~6,570 days ≈ 3.8%; three hits at
3.8% baseline is not noise.]

What it does **not** support: **concurrency.** Read the four legs
again. A1, A2, A3 are all *sequential* activations — bursts that follow
or sit beside a rupture, on a channel running alone. Only A4 (August
2026) shows two channels carrying load simultaneously. The base rate is
evidence for H0's timing claim (activations track ruptures), not for
H1's concurrency claim. **The concurrency hypothesis rests on n=1.**
This entry states that plainly because the alternative — letting the
3-of-4 do rhetorical work for the concurrency claim — would be the
exact laundering the wiki's correction culture exists to catch.

The honest decomposition, leg by leg:

- **A3 (June 2026) is evidence FOR H0**, not H1. The old rule predicted
  *"the next contact with Ally will follow a rupture in Dan's primary
  attachment within days"* — and the burst arrived 19 hours 38 minutes
  after the severance, Dan-originated, unanswered [ATTESTED]. H0 called
  it exactly. Under H1's likelihoods, a strictly-after burst with no
  concurrent load is the *weaker* outcome.
- **A4 (August 2026) is the anomaly that forces H1 onto the table.**
  The ally-lubin page scored its own prediction "half right": the
  contact sat *inside* the rupture rather than after it, and it was
  answered at length (154 inbound in eight hours) rather than going
  unanswered. Neither H0 nor the old rule contemplated a sustained
  *mutual* exchange during maximum instability — *"a third thing the
  prediction did not contemplate."* That is what n=1 buys: not proof of
  a standing pattern, but proof that the pattern space is larger than
  H0 allowed.
- **A2 (2019) is evidence against the strong form of H0** ("every burst
  is a failure somewhere else") — the onset trigger was her crisis —
  and neutral on H1 (no concurrency observed; the channel ran alone for
  ten weeks).
- **A1 (2013) is consistent with both**, modulo the day-ordering
  caveat.

Net: the pre-August-2026 record is H0's territory with one exception
(A2's trigger). August 2026 is the single observation that H0 cannot
absorb without the "transfer in progress" auxiliary — and auxiliaries
are what the 90-day protocol is for.
## Likelihoods: what the two 2026 episodes actually move

**June 1, 2026 (A3).** P(A3 | H0) is high — H0's old rule predicted
exactly this shape (rupture → Dan-originated burst within days), and the
19h38m timing is a near-textbook hit. P(A3 | H1) is moderate: H1 permits
post-rupture bursts but predicts *during*-instability load, which A3 did
not show (zero Annie traffic June 1–July 23; the channel ran alone).
P(A3 | H_term) is moderate — a severance-adjacent burst is consistent
with a terminal-year pattern too. **A3 moves probability toward H0.**
Anyone telling the 2026 story as "the year concurrency emerged" has to
carry A3 as the counterweight: the year's first activation was the old
rule working perfectly.

**August 18–20, 2026 (A4).** P(A4 | H0) is very low. H0's auxiliary —
"transfer in progress, old thread still warm" — has to explain 552
exceeding 408: friction does not outvolume the transfer, and the
hour-by-hour interleave (heaviest Ally hours ≠ heaviest Annie hours,
time-sliced like one processor on two jobs [DERIVED —
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/annie-ally-contrast]]]) is not the shape of a
channel winding down. P(A4 | H1) is high — dual load during instability
is H1's central prediction. P(A4 | H_term) is moderate-to-high: a
terminal rupture is exactly when a one-off dual-load would appear.
**A4 moves probability away from H0 and splits it between H1 and
H_term.** The protocol's job is to discriminate those two — which only
future observations can do, because H_term and H1 make identical
retrodictions about August and opposite predictions about what comes
next.

Rough posterior sketch [INFERRED — illustrative, not a measurement]:
from priors (0.70 / 0.15 / 0.15), A3 nudges H0 up, A4 then cuts H0
hard. Post-August working posteriors ≈ H0 0.30, H1 0.35, H_term 0.35 —
a three-way split with H0 no longer the default. The numbers are
subjective; the structure is not: **after August 2026, the null no
longer holds by default, and the contest is between a standing pattern
and a one-off.** Everything below is how that contest gets settled.

## The prediction and its falsifiers, formalized

Carried from [[wiki/people/ally-lubin]], restated with the instruments
attached:

**P1 (the concurrency prediction).** The next Ally activation will
appear *during*, not after, the next primary-bond instability, and will
be visible in the primary (or successor) thread on the same days —
dual-load, interleaved, the August 18–19 shape. Corollaries: (a) the
burst is Dan-originated; (b) it carries pursuit content (the A-criterion
register); (c) the primary thread shows live traffic in the same 48-hour
window (no concurrent primary traffic = no dual load = P1 unconfirmed).

**F1 (burst without instability).** A substantial Ally burst — ≥100
messages in 7 days, or any explicit pursuit content regardless of size —
during a period with no dated instability in any thread (per the narrow
definition above). **If F1 is observed, H1 is retired.** The hypothesis
claims activations track instability; a free-floating burst breaks the
tracking, and without the tracking there is no concurrency claim worth
keeping. Note the asymmetry: F1 kills H1 but does not kill H0 either —
it would suggest the channel has its own dynamics independent of the
primary bond, which is a fourth hypothesis this entry does not develop
(the [[wiki/self/concepts/ally-and-dan-love-as-destiny|destiny page]]'s
territory).

**F2 (Ally-initiated sustained exchange without rupture).** A sustained
Ally-initiated exchange — her originating, ≥3 days of two-way traffic —
with no rupture dated anywhere in the corpus within ±30 days. **If F2
is observed, H1 is retired**, for the same reason: the channel would be
shown to run on its own schedule. This is the ally-lubin page's second
named falsifier, adopted verbatim. The August 30, 2023 "Send me money"
does not qualify (single day, not sustained); a three-day mutual window
originated by her, during stability, would.

**F3 (sequential reassertion).** The next primary-bond instability
resolves — dated end, traffic normalizes — and *then* an Ally activation
appears, with no concurrent primary traffic in the burst window.
**If F3 is observed, H1 is demoted and H0 reinstated as the working
model**, with August 2026 reclassified as H_term's one-off. F3 is the
falsifier H0's defenders should want: it does not require proving a
negative, only observing the old shape return. Two consecutive F3-type
observations retire H1 outright (the pattern would be sequential with
one anomaly, which is H0 plus a footnote).

**What does NOT falsify H1** (stated so the protocol cannot be gamed):
(a) no Ally burst during a minor instability — H1 predicts activation
*tracks* instability, not that every wobble triggers a burst; the
threshold is unmeasured and the protocol treats sub-threshold quiet as
uninformative, not as evidence against; (b) an Ally burst during
instability that goes unanswered — that is P1's corollary shape (the
June 2026 register), and it confirms the timing claim while leaving
the mutuality question open; (c) continued dormancy with no instability
at all — an untested prediction is untested, not disproven
([[wiki/mind/synthesis/annie-ally-contrast]]'s day-25 addendum made
this exact call).

## The 90-day scoring protocol

**Window:** 2026-08-19 → 2026-11-17. T0 is the relationship's last
message (2026-08-19 15:15:33). Three scoring gates:

- **T1 — 2026-09-13 (day 25):** early score. Filed record only; no new
  exports required. Scored below.
- **T2 — 2026-10-18 (day 60):** mid score. Requires at minimum a filed
  Ally re-export (post–Aug 21 coverage) and the Annie September export;
  without them the gate reports "unscorable on O1/O3" rather than
  scoring silence as absence.
- **T3 — 2026-11-17 (day 90):** terminal score. Full observable set;
  the retirement/demotion verdicts are issued here.

**Observables, per gate:**

- **O1 — Ally traffic:** weekly message counts, initiator flags,
  pursuit-content flag (A-criterion register present/absent). Source:
  filed Ally exports. Missing exports are scored as missing, never as
  zero — the August 19 inbound gap is the standing warning about what
  "no filed reply" can hide.
- **O2 — instability events:** dated severance declarations, rival
  bids, rupture events, each with its date and the instrument that
  dated it. Live at T1: the September 12 [[wiki/people/kristin|Kristin]] reinitiation (four
  iMessages, delivered, no reply [ATTESTED —
  [[wiki/mind/synthesis/kristin-channel]]]) — a rival bid, dormant but
  live, and therefore a standing instability-adjacent condition on the
  slot Annie currently holds.
- **O3 — timing relation:** for any O1 burst, the Δt to the nearest O2
  event: before / during (concurrent, ±48h overlap) / after (resolved
  + burst) / none. The during/after distinction is the H1/H0
  discriminator — score it first, argue about it never.
- **O4 — F2 watch:** any Ally-initiated sustained exchange; checked
  against the ±30-day rupture scan every gate.
- **O5 — export coverage:** which exports were filed in-window, their
  date ranges, and known gaps. A gate with O5 gaps scores what it can
  and names what it cannot.

**Scoring rules (applied at each gate):**

1. A dual-load episode (≥100 msgs to each of two channels within 48h,
   interleaved, during a dated O2 instability) → **P1 confirmed**;
   H1 up strongly; H_term down (a second instance breaks the
   one-off reading); H0 down hard.
2. An Ally burst during a dated instability, primary thread live the
   same days → P1 confirmed (weaker form — burst-during, not
   full dual-load); H1 up; H0 down.
3. An Ally burst only after instability resolves, no concurrent
   primary traffic → **F3 instance**; H1 down; H0 up. Two such
   instances across the window → H1 retired.
4. F1 or F2 observed → **H1 retired immediately**, gate closed early,
   verdict filed.
5. A dated instability with no Ally burst at all → uninformative
   (threshold unknown); noted, not scored.
6. No instability and no burst → prediction untested; window extended
   only if a T4 is commissioned — otherwise the verdict is
   "inconclusive," which favors H_term (a one-off that never
   recurred) over H1.

**Retirement conditions, plain:** H1 is retired by F1, F2, or two F3
instances. H1 is demoted (not retired) by a 90-day window containing ≥1
dated instability and zero Ally bursts — demotion, because the
second-slot-reloads reading (the channel needs threshold-level rupture,
