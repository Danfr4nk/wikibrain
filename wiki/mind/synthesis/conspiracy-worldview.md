# Dan's Conspiracy Worldview

On September 22, 2026, Dan gave verdicts on 33 conspiracy theories in a
single live session — believing 18, rejecting 5, agnostic on 9, one case
explicitly gut-versus-official split, several more carrying the same
structure. Entry #33, January 6th, was added at his request from the wiki
corpus, where it was already his most documented position. The full
verdict log lives at
[[wiki/mind/politics/conspiracy-verdicts]]; this page is the synthesis
of what those verdicts reveal about how he thinks.

The short version: power is exercised through real institutions —
intelligence services, party machines, corporate lobbies — using mundane
mechanisms (structural incentives, legal-but-engineered rule changes,
managed belief). The exotic, mystical versions of conspiracy theories are
often themselves operations: limited hangouts and controlled opposition
designed to discredit the boring-but-true ones. His decision rule is
mechanism plus motive plus historical precedent, not vibe. He leaves
questions open rather than filling them, and he treats the official story
as a hypothesis to test — not a default, not an enemy.

None of this is an endorsement of the verdicts as fact. They are
positions, recorded with the seams showing — his confidence labels kept
verbatim, his open questions left open. What the page takes seriously is
the *structure* of his judgment, because the structure is the interesting
part: it is a coherent epistemic posture, unusual enough to warrant
mapping, and it runs on the same forensic engine as everything else he
does.

## Why this worldview is worth mapping

The typical conspiracy theorist is an object of study for journalists and
psychologists; the map is usually drawn by outsiders. Here the map can be
drawn from the inside: 33 verdicts, delivered in one sitting, with
motives cited per case, confidence labels volunteered unprompted, and a
corpus cross-check confirming which verdicts are settled, which are new,
and which moved. That cross-check found exactly one settled prior
position (2020 election, near-verbatim match to an earlier Gemini pinned
session), one genuine 13-year evolution (Boston bombing — from 2013
tweets mocking Boston trutherism to a 2026 FBI-asset theory), roughly 20
verdicts new-on-record, and zero cases where he was previously vocal
about something he is agnostic on today. The agnosticism is not a
retreat; it is the native state.

The worldview also matters because it is the same cognitive machinery as
his forensics. The anomaly detection, the procedural-evidence standard,
the coincidence-cluster reading ("when every element has to be innocent
simultaneously, treat it as signal") — the conspiracy verdicts are
[[wiki/mind/concepts/dans-law]] and
[[wiki/mind/concepts/forensic-method]] run on public events instead of
personal corpora. Mapping the worldview is mapping the engine's output
on a fresh substrate.

## The eleven threads

### 1. The mundanity principle

The single best predictor of his belief across all 33 verdicts is how
boring the mechanism is. Believed: mail-in ballot rule changes, insular
elite power structures, covert-war funding streams, FBI informant
handling. Rejected: lizard people, chemtrails, 5G sickness, vaccine-autism.
Exoticism is a *demerit* in his system — the opposite of the typical
truther, for whom the exoticism is the appeal.

This is doing real epistemic work, not just aesthetic preference. A
boring mechanism is one that can exist inside institutions without
requiring anyone to be a genius or a monster: structural incentives, legal
rule changes, budgets that need to be spent, careers that need
protecting. "Conspiracy in plain sight" is his phrase for the 2020
election verdict — and it generalizes. The Bilderberg/Illuminati verdict
is the cleanest statement of it: real, but "not even hidden" — just
powerful people maintaining insular structures, openly. The mystical
version (lizard people, secret oaths) he rejects precisely because it
adds unnecessary exoticism to something that explains itself without it.

### 2. The hybrid template — the signature move

He almost never claims total fabrication. His signature structure is: a
real event, with an operation layered on top, and the blur between the
layers doing the work.

- **9/11:** planes legitimately hijacked by Wahhabist jihadis *and* the
  buildings rigged for implosion *and* Mossad as the driver, with CIA
  involvement the explicit open question.
- **J6:** a real rally, a real march, a real crowd *and* an inserted
  lethal core — the "Sandwich" — where the conflation of the organic
  layer and the inserted layer *is* the mechanism.
- **Boston:** a real bombing *and* Tamerlan Tsarnaev as an FBI asset gone
  rogue/uncontrolled — "obviously FBI-involved."
- **2020 election:** real votes *and* engineered rule changes, the crisis
  expanding the action space while the actors positioned to exploit it do
  the exploiting — legally.

Three independent invocations of the same structure (9/11, J6, Boston),
plus the 2020 variant, suggests this is a cognitive primitive for him,
not a per-case conclusion. It is also the rarest conspiracist structure:
the genre's default is crisis actors, no-planes, staged-everything. His
default is the opposite — the event was real; the operation rode on top.
Harder to dismiss, harder to prove, and he seems to understand both of
those things. The epistemic discipline of the hybrid is visible in the
Epstein revision: he retracted his own exfil-through-ventilation claim
*the same day he made it* because he realized he didn't have the
information to confidently claim it. The exotic layer got cut; the
mundane layer (he did not hang himself) survived. That is the mundanity
principle editing a verdict in real time.

### 3. A standing suspect class — but plural

CIA, FBI, Mossad, DNC forces, LAPD, Ted Turner. No single "they." The JFK
verdict alone names three distinct parties — rogue CIA elements,
organized crime families, possibly Israeli intelligence — in coalition,
each with a distinct motive (Bay of Pigs, detente, RFK's crime war; the
motives are doing the coalition-building). He builds coalitions, not
monoliths, and assigns each actor its own reason for being there.

The plurality matters because it is falsifiable per actor: you can remove
the mob from the JFK coalition without collapsing the whole thing, and
he does this kind of surgery freely (the Epstein retraction is the
template — remove the exfil, keep the murder). A monolith can't be
surgically edited; a coalition can. His worldview is built from parts,
and he knows the parts come apart.

### 4. Cui bono plus precedent, not pattern-matching

His motive lists are historically literate: Bay of Pigs plus detente
plus RFK's war on organized crime (JFK); post-Iran-Contra funding
continuity (CIA and the crack epidemic); on-ramp logic (Pearl Harbor).
He cites documented precedents as the license for inference — COINTELPRO
for J6, trial-testimony FBI informants in the Oath Keepers/Proud Boys,
the institutional record of covert-war funding. This is why he is not
default-suspicious about celebrity deaths: no motive-precedent fit, no
verdict. JFK Jr., Diana, Monroe, Paul Walker, Biggie — all agnostic,
all "don't know enough." Only Tupac (LAPD via Suge Knight) and Cobain
(gut-lean) break the pattern, and the breakage is instructive: Tupac has
a named suspect class with a motive; Cobain is explicitly labeled gut,
not verdict. The machinery is cold, not reflexive.

### 5. Lone-actor rejection, calibrated by victim significance

He rejects lone-shooter stories for JFK, RFK, and MLK — the three
system-level threats to entrenched power — but accepts the official
story on Sandy Hook and stays agnostic on most celebrity deaths. The
discriminator is clean: does the removal serve an institutional interest?
The 60s victims share a through-line — anti-war, anti-corruption, left —
and they are, not coincidentally, his political ancestors. His
conspiracy beliefs track his politics: the people he believes were
killed are the people he would have marched for. That correlation is
worth stating plainly because it is both a feature and a warning about
the system — the motive-finding machinery and the political identity run
on the same fuel, and the output favors his own side's martyrs. He would
probably agree; his verdicts come with confidence labels, and the labels
are where the honesty lives.

### 6. The meta-layer: theories about theory-management

Pizzagate is "a real thing" that "was just a limited hangout": Comet
Ping Pong as a literal pedophile gathering ground, probably not true —
but the surrounding reverberations (the artwork, the pedestals, John
Podesta's brother's house, the Epstein disclosure emails where pizza is
used constantly as innuendo) are real. QAnon is "likely controlled
opposition" — built to distract from the real Pizzagate and turn it
into a complete farce for normal people. This is a theory about the
production of theories. He models *belief itself* as a managed
battlespace: the exotic version of a theory is sometimes an instrument
deployed against the boring-but-true version.

This connects directly to the wiki project. Whoever controls the record
controls what is believable; the conspiracy theorist is usually the
*object* of limited hangouts and farce-association operations, and Dan
theorizes those operations as instruments. He is one level up from the
genre. It is also the most sophisticated move in the set, and the one
hardest to argue with from outside — because it names the mechanism by
which outside argument gets neutralized.

### 7. Emergency-opportunity theory

The 2020 verdict and the COVID verdict share a structure: a crisis
expands the action space, and the actors who benefit are the ones already
positioned to exploit it. Not "they released it to steal the election" —
subtler. The crisis is the medium; the engineering happens inside it,
legally. COVID: gain-of-function research at Wuhan, funded by the same US
forces who controlled the response (he names Fauci). 2020: the pandemic's
impact forced changes in state voting systems; mail-in ballots opened to
everyone; removing the hassle of in-person voting plus the George Floyd
climate drove massive Democratic vote spikes; at those margins, Georgia
and Arizona flipped on mail-in returns alone. His second-order read is
the interesting part: Democrats then assumed Arizona and Georgia were now
blue states, but they weren't — the 2020 outcome there was purely the
mail-in effect, and 2024 proved it. This is "conspiracy in plain sight"
generalized into a theory of how power moves in crises.

### 8. The agnosticism gradient as data

Nine of 33 verdicts are blank — "don't know enough," no manufacture of
certainty. Four more carry an explicit gut-versus-official split: the
Federal Reserve (gut says the conspiracy is sensible, officially
agnostic), fluoride (same structure), Cobain (gut says murdered at
Courtney Love's behest, officially agnostic), Seth Rich (50/50, gut
leans conspiracy). The corpus cross-check found *zero* cases where he
was previously vocal about something he is agnostic on today — the
agnosticism is genuine, not a retreat from an embarrassing old position.

This is the veracity-ledger pattern in the wild. His standing record on
himself: stated certainty runs ~0.25 actual, "confident" ~0.69, hedged
~0.75. The gut/official split is the same honesty mechanism one level
down: the gut gets logged, the verdict stays blank, and the seam between
them is data. For wiki writeback, the confidence labels are load-bearing
— the split is a first-class datum, not a hedge to be resolved.

### 9. New-on-record versus settled

Only the 2020 election position was settled and documented before the
live session — a Gemini pinned session (Node 8) matched today's verdict
near-verbatim, and today's version extends it with the 2024 second-order
consequence. Roughly 20 verdicts are new-on-record: articulated for the
first time in this session. Boston is the genuine evolution: 2013 tweets
*mocking* Boston trutherism, today an FBI-asset theory — the arc worth
pinning down in the dissection phase is what reporting (likely the
Waltham murders and FBI-handling coverage) moved him. J6 is the most
documented: three AI platforms, ~18 months, position unmoved. So the
worldview has a long-stable core and a large periphery being articulated
for the first time *today* — which means the periphery should be
treated as provisional positions, not settled doctrine, and the wiki
should let them sit before hardening around them.

### 10. The Israel thread — newly on-record

Mossad as a driver in JFK (possible) and 9/11 (deep, via the desire for
regime change in Iraq). This sits inside his settled anti-ethnostate /
full-Palestinian-sovereignty position, and the corpus holds no prior
statements on this thread — today is the first articulation. Notably, he
keeps it at the agency-and-motive level: a service pursuing a state
interest (Iraqi regime change), never the mystical register. The
dissection needs to hold that framing — it is consistent with thread 3
(plural suspect class, distinct motives) and thread 1 (mundanity: states
do this; it is what intelligence services are for). It is also the most
politically charged thread in the set, which is exactly why the
confidence labels matter: "Mossad deeply involved" (9/11) is a *believe*;
"possibly Israeli intelligence" (JFK) is a *maybe* inside a believe.
The gradations are the data.

### 11. Economic reductionism

When in doubt, he reduces to money and institutional self-preservation.
The Georgia Guidestones verdict is a name and nothing else — "Ted
Turner" — the sparsest verdict in the set, a placeholder where the
mechanism hasn't been worked out yet. Big Pharma suppressing cures: he
can't take an official position, but calls it "the only realistic
scenario," with the characteristic dry aside that "the people in big
pharma are the most ethical of our bunch." The Illuminati, demystified:
the most powerful people in the world preserving their wealth through
insular power structures. The CIA-crack verdict: the major funding
mechanism for covert wars after Iran-Contra blew up the old channels —
budgets need feeding. This is the thread that connects the conspiracy
worldview to his left politics: the villains are capital and its
enforcement arms, and the mechanism is always the same — institutions
protecting their revenue and their position.

## Against the typical conspiracist

**The real similarities, not denied:** the official story is a
hypothesis, not the default. Intelligence agencies are the standing
suspect class. Cui bono is treated as evidence. Pattern-seeking across
events (Pearl Harbor's on-ramp logic reappearing in 9/11). A taste for
the hidden variable. He shares the genre's raw materials.

**The load-bearing differences:**

1. *Mundane over exotic.* The typical conspiracist believes the hidden
   truth is wild. Dan believes the truth is usually boring and visible,
   and the wild versions are often the cover (QAnon as farce-association
   against the real Pizzagate). A genuine inversion of the genre.

2. *He accepts official stories.* Moon landing real. Sandy Hook a real
   shooting — "some strange things about it," but the staged claim
   rejected. Vaccines don't cause autism — "very false." Chemtrails are
   condensation — "bullshit." Roughly 15% of his verdicts are outright
   conspiracy-rejections. The typical conspiracist worldview has no
   "yes, that one happened as reported" category this large.

3. *Agnosticism instead of gap-filling.* The typical conspiracist fills
   every gap with certainty. Dan leaves 9 of 33 blank and labels gut
   feelings as gut feelings. His epistemic posture is closer to an
   intelligence analyst's confidence ratings than a truther's.

4. *No monolithic "they."* Typical: one cabal behind everything. Dan:
   distinct actors, distinct motives, sometimes in coalition, sometimes
   opposed. He never collapses them.

5. *Real-event plus layer, not fabrication.* Typical: crisis actors, no
   planes, staged. Dan: the event was real; the operation rode on top.
   Harder to dismiss, harder to prove.

6. *The meta-move.* Typical conspiracists are the objects of limited
   hangouts; Dan theorizes them as instruments. One level up.

7. *Left-political valence.* The typical American conspiracist
   worldview is right-coded. Dan's is left-coded: the victims he
   believes in are anti-war/anti-corruption left figures; the villains
   are intel agencies, DNC forces, Big Pharma, the banking order. His J6
   position is the tell — it fits neither the left's reading (pure
   fascist coup) nor the right's (total fed setup or total innocent),
   and he treats that epistemic loneliness as corroboration: a position
   that flatters nobody is less likely to be tribal.

## Tensions and honest gaps

Recorded here because the seams are the data — these are the dissection
queue for the forensic pass, not problems to be smoothed over.

1. **Epstein's exfil is his most exotic claim** — a jailbreak through a
   ventilation shaft strains against the mundanity principle. He
   retracted it himself, which is the system working; but "murdered by
   his cohorts" as the mundane fallback still needs its mechanism
   worked out. The retraction is documented at the verdicts log.

2. **The 9/11 open question** — "just Israel, or was the CIA involved
   too?" — is the load-bearing unknown of his most complex verdict. The
   rest of the 9/11 position (real hijackers, rigged buildings, Mossad
   driver) is stated with confidence; this one he leaves open, which is
   honest but also where the whole structure is most vulnerable to
   motive-stacking.

3. **J6's four murky spots** (from the corpus pull, recorded at his
   instruction): the vanguard boundary — which Proud Boy actions are
   organic vanguard versus directed insertion is never separated, and the
   cuffed-then-released window smasher sits exactly on it. The hand
   behind the Psy-Kill — Byrd as directed asset versus an organic
   officer whose shooting was opportunistically weaponized — with the
   two AI passes in tension (ChatGPT 2025 leaned opportunistic
   weaponization; Gemini 2026 leaned directed). Pre-planned versus
   real-time insertion. Careless versus precise — his own 2024 text
   calls it a "careless plan," while the Wildfall codification implies
   surgical precision. The directed-versus-opportunistic tension is
   unresolved, and the wiki records it as unresolved.

4. **Boston's evolution timing** — what specifically moved him between
   2013 mockery and the 2026 FBI-asset theory? The likely candidates
   are the Waltham-murders reporting and FBI-handling coverage; the
   dissection should pin the inflection.

5. **The Turner verdict** is a name without a mechanism — the thinnest
   entry, background pass needed before it means anything.

6. **Cobain** is the purest case study of the gut/official split —
   worth the full backgrounding either way, precisely because the
   verdict is blank and the gut is loud.

7. **The Israel thread** is newly on-record and the most politically
   charged — the dissection holds his agency-and-motive framing and
   does not drift into registers he didn't use.

## Limits

This page synthesizes 33 verdicts from a single live session plus the
J6 corpus. Roughly 20 verdicts are new-on-record — first articulations,
not settled positions — and the page treats them as provisional. The
dissection phase (per-case backgrounding) is queued but not done; the
tensions above are the queue. Verdicts are positions, not findings: the
wiki records what he believes and how confidently, and the confidence
labels are part of the record. Nothing here is endorsed as fact about
the world.
