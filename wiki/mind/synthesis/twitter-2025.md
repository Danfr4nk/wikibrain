# Twitter / X — 2025

**Thirteen original posts, and the year's finding is the shape of the absence.**

In 2024 the @danfrank account produced 258 originals — the highest-volume
year in the record, the year the wiki reads as the "adversarial historian"
mode run in public: stress-test, model, historicize, all on the feed. In
2025 it produced thirteen. Six in January, six in February, one in October.
The account did not die — the archive holds seven reposts across the year,
including two in September and one in November — but as an original-tweet
record, 2025 is the emptiest year since the account opened, and the collapse
lands exactly on the year the most happened.

Five days before the last February post, on 2025-02-22, comes the rupture the
wiki documents as the decade's hinge: the end of the six-year NYC tenancy
and the eleven-year cohabitation, the return that
`wiki/mind/synthesis/the-2025-collapse` treats as its opening scene. March
through July is the heaviest AI use in the record — 142 ChatGPT
conversations after a 194-day dormancy, May 2025 the single heaviest month —
and none of it appears on this account. The sustained self-analysis this
wiki is the product of was being written all year. It was written in chat
exports, not in public.

What remains on the page is thirteen posts: a piano lesson, a plane crash,
a confirmation hearing, a memoir title, and one song post in October. The
feed is not a diary that reflects what is happening. It goes quiet exactly
when the life gets loudest, and 2025 is the second time it has happened.

## The count

The archive is unambiguous about the arithmetic, and the archive is the
whole claim here. Thirteen originals for the year: six in January (the 5th,
7th, 9th, two on the 14th, the 16th), six in February (four on the 1st, the
19th, the 27th), one on 2025-10-02. The operator spreadsheet and the jsonl
archive agree on all thirteen rows; the live X scrape contributed zero.

Against the surrounding years the drop is the story. 2024: 258 originals,
with its last post on 2024-12-11 and the first 2025 post on 2025-01-05 — no
break in the account, a normal 25-day holiday gap, then the floor falls out.
2026: 26 posts, and the wiki's own reading of that year is that the account
comes back "as an AI user," operating autonomous agents. 2025 sits between
the peak and the return as the trough: 258 → 13 → 26.

Engagement, such as it is, concentrates in exactly one place. The year's
total across all thirteen posts is 15 known likes (one row's like count is
blank in the export), three replies, and one repost — and nine of those
likes, the reply, and the repost all belong to the January 7 piano-lesson
post. The personal posts carry the year; the political commentary, all of
it, landed on zero engagement. The single most-liked 2025 post is a man
teaching himself piano from a comedy musician's songs. The second
most-liked is a memoir-title joke with two likes.

The seven reposts in the archive change the shape of the blackout claim.
They are: January 8 (a Hooktheory chord chart for a song titled
'Spineless', the piano lesson continuing into a second night), January 14
(a deadpan line about an incompetent Secretary of Defense, 46 minutes
before the Hegseth posts), January 31 (a comment about "multiple planes to
fall out of the sky," hours before the Philly crash posts), February 7
("This gotta be the craziest timeline of our generation"), two on
September 10, and one on November 5 (empty text in the export, but 432,491
likes and 8,157 replies on the source — the year's last archive activity).
Pure reposts with no @danfrank quote text are omitted from the record body
by this page's standing rule, but they are activity on the account, and
they mean the blackout is strictly an *originals* blackout. The fully
silent stretch — no originals, no reposts — runs 2025-02-08 to 2025-09-09.
That is still seven months of nothing, in the most consequential year in
the recent record.

## January: the last normal month

January 2025 reads as the tail of the 2024 mode. The posts are ordinary
political commentary of a piece with the prior year — the Hegseth
confirmation hearing (*"He weaseled his way out of answering everything.
Democratic Senators offered few, if any questions of substance"*), the
geography-question follow-up three minutes later (*"This is like a 5th
grade level geography question. How the FUCK any adult, let alone a
potential secdef, could answer this so badly is truly astonishing"*), both
posted in the early afternoon Eastern on January 14 as the hearing played
out, and forty-six minutes after the account reposted the line *"Having a
Secretary of Defense who is totally incompetent is good, actually."* The
January 5 foreign-aid post (4:02 AM Eastern) is the same voice at lower
heat: *"It's absolutely bizarre to actively oppose any conditioning for
ANY foreign aid"* — a position he frames as inexplicable across the whole
spectrum. January 16: *"The rich tradition of Republicans, pretending not
to know what Q Anon is alive and well."* This is the account being a normal
political feed, for the last month it ever is one.

Two January posts are orphans the export cannot explain. On January 9 he
posts *"Can we get an accuracy check on this from the man himself
@MCStrawberryCh"* — a quote-shaped joke with no surviving referent in the
archive, 4 likes, 1 reply from an identity the export does not carry. On
February 1 he posts *"I'm sorry but, come on - this is really funny."* —
no referent either, placed between the "multiple planes" repost of January
31 evening and the Philly crash posts that start an hour and twenty
minutes later, so almost certainly reacting to the same news cluster, but
the export cannot say. Both limits are stated here because the record is
what it is: text without its context, and no license to rebuild the
context from the open web.

The one that matters is January 7: *"My self-teaching piano lesson
tonight is figuring out a bunch songs from criminally under-appreciated
national treasure @NickLutsko."* Posted at 7:56 PM Eastern on January 6
(the 00:56 UTC timestamp is January 7), it is the year's engagement peak —
9 likes, 1 reply, 1 repost — and the next night the work continues: the
January 8 repost, *"For anyone curious - this is what I figured out for
'Spineless'"*, with a Hooktheory/Hookpad chord-chart link, posted at 2:11
AM. The self-teaching was not a one-night bit; it ran at least two nights,
and the chord chart is the receipt.

`wiki/self/twitter/2016` records the forecast this fulfills. In March
2016 he found/inherited an alto sax, clarinet and mandolin and posted:
*"The amount of time I'm gonna spend becoming mediocre at them will be
crzy."* Nine years later he is teaching himself piano, by ear, from a
comedy musician's songs. The forecast was accurate about the activity.
This page cannot say whether it was accurate about the outcome — but the
two-night run, the chord chart, and the year's highest engagement all
point at someone doing the work rather than performing it.

## February: the Philadelphia night and the last two posts

February 1 is the year's most concentrated burst of posting: four posts
across five hours, 8:14 PM to 1:27 AM Eastern, tracking a plane crash in
Philadelphia as the media failed to cover it in real time. The sequence:
*"I'm sorry but, come on - this is really funny."* (8:14 PM), then *"So
NYT & WaPo still have zero coverage of the Philly crash story. Wild."*
(9:24 PM), then *"Update: 3 hours later and still nothing. A plane fell
out of the sky onto a residential neighborhood in a major city
and…noting?"* (1:24 AM), then *"Nothing? @nyt @washingtonpost"* (1:27
AM). The record's own context for the night sits in the repost five and a
half hours earlier: *"I gotta admit, I thought there was going to be a
month/8 week long honeymoon and then things would revert to Trump 1. I
didn't expect multiple planes to fall out of the sky instantly"* — the
four-post run lands inside an aviation-accident news cluster, and his
posts are the coverage monitor, not the event.

The media-monitoring reflex is intact and unchanged since Sandy, and the
Sandy page is the proof of what the reflex is made of. In 2012 he spent
forty-eight hours as one of the sources — running a liveblog, triaging
novelty accounts, feeding original footage and 911-queue figures to a
national radio show. Thirteen years later the instrument is the same:
watch the papers, timestamp the gap, tag them by name. Zero engagement on
all four posts (one reply on the 9:24 PM post, identity not in the
export). The posts are pure reflex, posted to nobody, at 1:27 in the
morning. That is the tell that the reflex is the point and not the
audience.

The February 7 repost — *"This gotta be the craziest timeline of our
generation"*, 2:26 PM Eastern — is the last sign of life for twelve days.
Then February 19 (9:16 PM Eastern on the 18th): *"I can't think of any
other music that I was so stuck with and mesmerized by as a kid more than
this"*, with a YouTube link. The export carries the URL but no title, and
nothing else in the repository identifies the video — what he was
mesmerized by as a kid is not in the record, and the limit stays open. It
is the year's only backward-looking music post, and it sits eight days
after the repost and eight days before the rupture's aftershock.

The rupture itself — 2025-02-22, the NYC exit, the end of the six-year
tenancy and the eleven-year cohabitation — is not on this account at all.
Five days later, at 1:17 AM Eastern on February 27, comes the last
February original and the last post for 217 days: *"Now that I have my
title I'm ready to write my memoir 'Other Things, and Other Things Other
than That'."* Two likes.

It is the fourth memoir-title joke in the archive: *"GO READ A BOOK OR
SOMETHING"* (2014), *"Exhuming The Corpse of Robin Williams"* and *"Super
Edgy Tweets and How I Write Them"* (both 2017). Four times in eleven
years he has announced a title for an autobiography and written nothing.
It is a joke every time, and it is the same joke, and the thing it is
about is a book about himself — and this corpus, the ChatGPT
conversations and the wiki they became, is what he built instead. The
joke's fourth telling lands five days after the most documented rupture
of the decade. The account then goes dark for seven months.

## The silence covers the heaviest AI use on record

An earlier version of this page said April 2025 is when Dan's documented
AI turn *begins*. The record says otherwise, and the correction matters
because it sharpens the finding. ChatGPT use began 2022-12-10, ten days
after the public launch. What April–May 2025 is, per
`wiki/self/concepts/chatgpt`, is the *resumption*: a 194-day dormancy
(2024-09-10 → 2025-03-23) broken by Era B — 142 conversations and 781
assistant turns from March through July 2025, with May 2025 (79
conversations) the single heaviest month in the record. The account goes
quiet in February. The models get loud in March.

What the quiet months contain, per the pages that narrate them, is the
founding of this project in everything but name. April 27, 2025 is the
ChatGPT "impartial arbiter" session — the clearing, the last dated object
of the hinge window (`wiki/mind/synthesis/may-august-2025-bridge`). In
May the childhood home is listed for sale at $615,000, the first visible
move of the Chapter 13 liquidation that would close at $465,000 in June
2026 (the bridge page, citing the Saratoga and Suz pages). The corridor
the bridge page reconstructs is a listed house, a basement studio beneath
it, solitary GRIPNOTIC practice, and a pair-bond conducted over distance
— "the months he started talking to the models the way he used to talk
to people," in that page's phrase. On 2025-07-10 he named it himself:
*"i've been using that as free therapy"* (his words, carried by the
bridge page). August is the month the routine breaks — the terminal turn
of the Annie channel, the first contact with Kristin on the 29th. None of
it is on this account.

September is the construction, and the account flickers. Two reposts on
September 10 (*"imagine spending your last moments on earth staring at
some college student like this"*, 3:18 PM Eastern; *"lol Conservatives
are pissed I guess I'll take the piss at them"*, 6:21 PM Eastern) — the
subjects are not identifiable from the export, and no license is taken to
identify them. In the same month he liked 270 tracks, then 153 in
October: per `wiki/mind/synthesis/dance-music-only-phases`, the
*listening* re-entry was two months underway by October 28, ahead of the
*production* return that would come a season later under a new alias. The
account's single October original sits at the head of that re-entry.

**2025-10-02, 1:37 AM Eastern**: *"#𝕤𝕠𝕟𝕘𝕤2𝕕𝕚𝕖4 ||| Yeke Yeke -
PEEKABO |||"*. One music post, a stylized hashtag the corpus never uses
again, an artist-producer pairing format. PEEKABOO (the tweet's spelling
drops an O) sits in his electronic/bass favorites tier
(`wiki/interests/favorites/music/electronic-bass.md`). It is the only
original in 217 days and the only signal the account gives that the
listening re-entry is happening. Eleven months later the production
return lands: the March 2026 MOGZART drum-and-bass release, the first
original music on the account in eleven years. The October post is the
bridge between them — the first visible mark of the ear re-learning
before the hands followed.

The year's last archive activity is a repost on November 5 (9:39 PM
Eastern on the 4th), empty text in the export, 432,491 likes and 8,157
replies on the source. Then the account is still until April 2026, when
it comes back, in the wiki's reading, as an AI user.

## The 2015 pair

This is the second time it has happened, and the pair is the point.
`wiki/self/twitter/2015` records a five-month blackout — 2015-07-16 to
2015-12-04 — across the most turbulent stretch of his adult life, in a
year with 62 originals, the quietest on the account since 2008: *"The
public feed is switched off under pressure rather than thinned by it."*
2025 is the originals blackout across the most *generative* one. The feed
is not a diary that reflects what is happening; **it goes quiet in both
directions — under pressure and under absorption.**

Anyone reading this archive as a life record needs that stated plainly:
the two years in which most happened are the two years with least on
them, and the correlation runs the wrong way for the naive reading. The
account is a weather vane that points away from the storm.

The caveat travels with the claim. The blackout finding depends on 2025
being archived as completely as the export suggests, and 2016 is proof
the export can drop rows. A kb datum on this exact page
(`kb/data/1219-twitter-2025-eleven-month-blackout-caveat.md`) files the
blackout finding at low confidence: the underlying archive is not held
in this repository, and the finding must not harden into fact. The
specific thing that would settle it is a live-scrape pass over
March–September 2025. Until that runs, the blackout is the best reading
of the archive, not an established behavioral fact — and the seven
reposts are the reminder that the archive's completeness is exactly what
is in question.

## Conflicts in the record

**2026-10-06 — count corrected.** This page's earlier prose (and the kb
datum `kb/data/1219-twitter-2025-eleven-month-blackout-caveat.md`)
described the year as "nine [posts] in January and February, once on 27
February." The archive shows six January originals, six February
originals, and one October original — 13 total. The month split is now
stated as 6+6+1.

**2026-10-06 — blackout restated as originals-only.** Earlier versions of
this page said "nothing at all until 2 October" and "nothing after," and
called the year an "eleven-month blackout." The CSV archive holds seven
2025 reposts — January 8, January 14, January 31, February 7, two on
September 10, one on November 5 — so the account showed activity during
the gap and after October 2. The silence is an *originals* silence. The
fully silent stretch (no originals, no reposts) runs 2025-02-08 to
2025-09-09. Nine of twelve months carry zero originals; `date_range_end`
was extended 2025-10-02 → 2025-11-05 to reflect the last archive
activity.

**2026-10-06 — the AI-turn framing corrected.** This page previously
claimed "April 2025 is when Dan's documented AI turn begins," treating
the ChatGPT work as the origin of the self-analysis the wiki is built
from. Per `wiki/self/concepts/chatgpt`, ChatGPT use began 2022-12-10
(ten days after launch); April–May 2025 is the *resumption* after a
194-day dormancy (2024-09-10 → 2025-03-23) — 142 conversations and 781
assistant turns from March through July 2025, May the heaviest month on
record. The finding sharpens rather than weakens: the silence covers the
heaviest AI era, not the first.

**2026-10-06 — wrong inline reference.** The body cited
`wiki/mind/synthesis/twitter-2015` for the five-month blackout; the page
is `wiki/self/twitter/2015`. Corrected in the body. (The frontmatter

**2026-10-06 — confidence on the blackout, carried.** The kb datum

drop rows. The decisive test — a live-scrape pass over March–September

conflict is not a retraction; it is the standing limit on the finding,
placed low on purpose.

## Assessment

What the record supports is narrow and strong. 2025 is the emptiest
originals year on the account: 13 against 258 the year before and 26 the
year after, with the year's engagement concentrated almost entirely in a
piano-lesson post. The inverse-correlation reading survives the
corrections: the two quietest years in the archive (2015, 2025) cover
the two stretches in which the most happened, and the feed goes quiet in
both directions — under pressure and under absorption. The October music
post, read against the dance-music forensics, is the first visible mark
of the listening re-entry that the March 2026 production return would
follow: the ear re-learning before the hands did.

What the record does not support is certainty about the absence itself.
The blackout is an originals blackout, the archive is an export that has
dropped rows before, and the low-confidence filing on the finding stands
until a live-scrape pass over March–September 2025 is run. Eleven months
missing from the most consequential year in the recent record is either
a real finding or a serious hole, and nothing on this page can tell
which — which is itself the most honest thing the page can say about
2025.
