# Scenario-Diagnostic Trials (four-component instrument)

On 2026-10-01 Dan handed over an old export from a different instrument than the scenario-ratings work: 24 pairwise forced-choice trials from the four-component diagnostic, the tool he built to map his own arousal architecture axis by axis. The trials are old — his words, it had been forever since he ran any of them, and the set was never finished. The export itself was generated 2026-10-01; the trial dates are unknown. What follows is the complete log, the axis-by-axis read (every count verified by script), and the cross-check against the newer v3 ratings corpus, where two separate instruments measuring the same man agree — and where they don't.

## The instrument

The diagnostic is a pairwise instrument: two erotic scenarios, pick the hotter one. Each scenario is coded on the four-component model of his sexuality — S (arousal substrate), P (pursuit direction), A (scene authorship), E (contact envelope). The v1 instrument runs four axes; the v2 instrument added a fifth, N (taboo charge: is a category being broken, and by whom). The export is v1-format — four axes only — which means the taboo-charge axis was never trialed in this instrument at all.

Trials ran in two phases. Phase 1 is multi-knob: twelve pairs that differ on two to four axes at once, exploratory. Phase 2 is single-knob: twelve pairs that differ on exactly one axis, isolating it. But phase 2 only ever isolated S. The P, A, and E single-knob rounds were never run — the set stops where the headline was found.

The instrument carries a baseline: his coordinates from October 20, 2019 — S1·P1·A1·E2. Woman central, he experimenting and not pursuing, Dan orchestrates, contact includes oral (the shape of the 2019 act). Everything below is measured against that.

## The v2 instrument and the axis that was never trialed

The v2 instrument kept the four axes and added a fifth: **N — taboo charge**, defined as "the orientation-violation mechanism: is a category being broken, and by whom?" Its levels are N1 (mutual crossing — both cross a boundary), N2 (he crosses alone), N3 (routine — no charge left). The v2 baseline extends the 2019 coordinates to S1·P1·A1·N1·E2, and the v2 scenario bank re-codes all 28 scenarios on five axes.

None of that was ever trialed. The export is v1-format — four axes, no N column — so the taboo-charge axis, arguably the most theoretically loaded of the five (it names the mechanism the whole instrument is circling), has zero pairwise data. The v3 ratings do carry N_x on every item, and there mutual crossing (8.45, n=42) beats crossing alone (7.75, n=4), but that is correlational single-rating data, not forced choice. The N column of the diagnostic is empty, and it is the single biggest hole in the set.

One more instrument detail worth keeping: scenario s28 — "You arranged and directed it — and it is being filmed, as in 2019" — shows the instrument anchors itself to the 2019 experience explicitly. The 2019 act isn't just the baseline coordinates; it's a scenario in the bank, the reference point the other scenarios are implicitly measured against. That matters for reading the E-axis drift below: when the trials say touch-only beats includes-oral, they're saying it against a bank where the filmed-2019 scenario is the exemplar of E2.

## The axis key

In the instrument's codes, then in plain English:

- **S — arousal substrate.** S1: a woman he finds attractive is the central participant. S2: she is present but peripheral — watching, filming, not participating. S3: no woman present at all. Plain version: how structurally present she is.
- **P — pursuit direction.** P1: the male participant is gay-identified, experimenting, not pursuing him. P2: the man is bisexual, with mild interest in him. P3: the man is openly attracted to him and pursuing. Plain version: how much the guy wants him.
- **A — scene authorship.** A1: he orchestrates it. A2: she orchestrates it. A3: spontaneous, unplanned. Plain version: who set it up.
- **E — contact envelope.** E1: mutual touch only. E2: includes oral. E3: open-ended, no preset limits. Plain version: how far it goes.

## The axis-by-axis read

All counts below were verified by script against the export.

**S — arousal substrate: S1 ≻ S2 ≻ S3, perfectly ordered, 17 of 17 contests.** S1 won 13, lost 0. S2 won 4, lost 4. S3 won 0, lost 13. Head-to-head: S1 beat S2 4–0, S1 beat S3 9–0, S2 beat S3 4–0. Twelve of those contests are clean single-knob trials; the other five come from phase 1 and agree without exception. This is the strongest result in the set: her structural centrality dominates everything else. A woman he wants, at the center, beats her watching from the sidelines, which beats her absence — with zero noise across every pairing the instrument tried.

**P — pursuit direction: P2 ≻ P1 > P3 (4–1 / 2–3 / 2–4).** Mild interest beats experimenting-not-pursuing 2–0 and beats openly-pursuing 2–1; experimenting-not-pursuing beats openly-pursuing 2–1. The shape is an inverted-U: a little pursuit is hotter than none, and open pursuit is the coldest. In plain terms, the guy should be a little into him but not a lot.

**A — scene authorship: A2 ≻ A1 > A3 (6–3 / 4–6 / 1–2).** She orchestrates beats he orchestrates 5–3 head-to-head; spontaneous trails both. A drift from the 2019 baseline, where he was the orchestrator.

**E — contact envelope: E1 ≻ E2 > E3 (5–3 / 3–4 / 1–2).** Touch-only beats includes-oral 4–2 head-to-head; open-ended trails. Also a drift from 2019, where the envelope included oral.

The P, A, and E reads carry a structural caveat: they come entirely from the multi-knob phase-1 trials, where two to four axes moved at once, so the tallies are confounded — directional, not clean. Phase 2 was supposed to isolate them and never did.

## Cross-check against the v3 ratings

The v3 ratings corpus (46 rated scenarios, September 2026) shares five axes with the diagnostic. Mean rating by level:

- **S_sub: woman central 8.62 (n=34) > peripheral 7.91 (n=11) > no woman 6.00 (n=1).** Agrees with the diagnostic. The S ordering now replicates across two instruments, two eras, two methods — it is the most established finding in his psychosexual data.
- **P_pur: mild interest 8.58 (n=12) > he experiments 8.45 (n=20) > he pursues you 8.14 (n=14).** Agrees — the inverted-U replicates.
- **A_auth: spontaneous 8.88 (n=8) > she directs 8.37 (n=27) > you direct 8.09 (n=11).** Disagrees with the diagnostic, which put spontaneous last. Unresolved.
- **E_env: open-ended 8.86 (n=7) > includes oral 8.39 (n=33) > touch only 7.83 (n=6).** Disagrees on the poles — but includes-oral sits in the middle in *both* instruments. The argument is about the extremes, not the middle.
- **N_x: mutual crossing 8.45 (n=42) > you cross alone 7.75 (n=4).** Ratings-only; the diagnostic never trialed it.

The v3 corpus also contains three usable single-knob deltas on the shared axes — modifier items with ratings, each n=1, so anecdotes, not evidence, but all three point the same way as the diagnostic: turning S_sub from woman-central to no-woman cost −1 point; turning A_auth from she-directs to you-direct cost −2; turning E_env from includes-oral to open-ended cost −2. The first two agree with the diagnostic's direction (S1≻S3, A2≻A1). The third agrees with the diagnostic too (E2≻E3) — and notably disagrees with the v3 correlational mean, where open-ended outscores oral. That tension is worth naming: the only causal E datapoint in the ratings sides with the trials against the ratings' own averages. With n=1 it settles nothing, but it keeps the E question genuinely open rather than letting the correlational mean close it.

What the agreements establish: the S ordering and the P inverted-U now rest on two instruments, two eras (the trials are years older than the September 2026 ratings), and two methods (forced choice vs. gut rating). That is about as established as a stated-preference finding gets at n=1. What the disagreements establish: A and E are not measurement noise — the same man, the same axes, opposite answers. Something differs between the instruments: the trial scenarios are all MMF scenes built from his 2019 experience, while the ratings corpus ranges across 78 scenario families; the trial is a forced binary under the four-component frame, the rating is a 1–10 gut score under no frame. The contradiction is data about framing sensitivity, and it should be treated as such rather than resolved by picking a winner.

## The drift since 2019

Against the October 2019 baseline (S1·P1·A1·E2), the current read is roughly S1·P2·A2·E1. The woman at the center held firm. Everything else moved one step: toward mild interest rather than no pursuit, toward her orchestrating rather than him, toward touch-only rather than oral-included. The honest footnote: the last two moves are exactly the two axes where the instruments disagree, so the drift is real on P and provisional on A and E.

## Limits, stated plainly

Stated preference, n=1 — this predicts ratings, not behavior. The trial dates are unknown (the export was generated 2026-10-01; the trials are older, "forever" in his words). Twenty-four trials is small, and the P/A/E tallies are confounded by multi-knob design — the single-knob rounds that would clean them were never run. The N axis exists in the v2 instrument and in the v3 ratings but was never trialed in the diagnostic. The v3 ratings cross-check rests on 46 rated scenarios with thin cells (n=1 for "no woman," n=4 for "you cross alone"). The A and E disagreements between instruments are real contradictions, not noise to be sanded off — they mark the boundary of what this data can say.

## What would close it

Single-knob diagnostic rounds for P, A, and E — the phase 2 the set never got. First-ever N-axis trials in the diagnostic. And finishing the v3 ratings: 127 of 156 modifier items are still unrated, which would firm up the thin cells at the A and E extremes.
