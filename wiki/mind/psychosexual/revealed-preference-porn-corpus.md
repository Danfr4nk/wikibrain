# Revealed-Preference Porn Corpus (2026-10-01)

On 2026-10-01 Dan made a bet against his own browsing history. The live
scenario-diagnostic session that morning had gone flat — twelve trials of the
same room with one knob turned, and he'd said so plainly: it didn't get him
hard because it was one scenario with two or three tweaks. The proposed fix
was a new scenario bank built from his *revealed* ontology instead of the
instrument's a priori axes, and the source was sitting in the wiki's raw tree
the whole time: his Google My Activity export, 108,821 rows from September
2006 to July 2025, carrying sixteen years of porn consumption — what he
watched, what he searched, what he went back to.

Before the analysis ran, he predicted his own categories. His words, in
order: lesbian, twin, cuckold, MMF, age play, amateur, trans — then, a moment
later: brother/sister, father/daughter, etc. And the concentration claim to go
with it: "That pretty much covers the entire list. I don't have many other
genres."

The analysis was built bottom-up specifically so his forecast couldn't anchor
it: categories derived from his search terms, video titles, and URL slugs,
with the S/P/A/E/N diagnostic axes deliberately not forced onto the data. The
subagent that ran it never saw his prediction list. Then the data graded him.

He knew his backbone and his early era cold. He missed the center of gravity
of his own late era entirely — then corrected the analysis himself, mid-read,
with the distinction that made the whole thing more valuable than the
forecast: genre is *what* he watches; the deep dive had found *how* he
watches, and those are different dimensions. This article is the complete
record: the corpus, the category map in full, the three eras, the scored
forecast, the genre × frame ontology his correction produced, and what it all
means for the instrument.

## Source, method, limits

The source is `raw/myactivity-2026-09-12/myactivity-unique.jsonl.gz`: Dan's
Google My Activity export, 108,821 rows spanning 2006-09-10 to 2025-07-27,
each row carrying a timestamp, an action ("Searched for," "Visited,"
"Watched," and others), a text field, and a URL. The adult subset is 4,258
"Visited" rows across 931 adult domains, identified by keyword scan plus known
domains with false positives removed — youtube.com, dictionary sites,
sporting-goods stores ("dicks sporting goods" is the store, eight visits),
documentary sites, and the comedian Anthony Cumia all excluded. The query
corpus is 715 unique adult-site search queries (1,043 hits) plus 1,963 unique
porn-related Google searches (2,660 hits). Counts below are query-hit counts
unless marked as visit-hits.

Three limits shape everything that follows, and they are stated up front
because the numbers mean nothing without them.

**First, the adult trail goes cold in 2023.** Zero adult rows from 2023
through July 2025, despite 5,253 total rows in that window. He moved to
incognito mode, another browser or account, or stopped syncing — the revealed
data is three-plus years stale at the recent end. Late-era findings (2020–
2022) are "last known," not current. When he was asked about the gap, he
didn't answer before the article was written; the question stands.

**Second, search-page titles are queries, not confirmed watches.** A search
for a thing is evidence of interest in the thing, but it is one step removed
from consumption. Visit-hits (actual page loads on adult domains) are the
harder signal, and both are reported where the distinction matters.

**Third, title keywords under-describe acts by construction.** A video
titled for its performers tells you nothing about what happens in it, and
cam-rip pages are titled by model name ("Videos Tagged with lannadunof"),
not by genre. Act-level counts are floors, not totals. The corpus is
organized, overwhelmingly, by *who* — configuration over acts — and that
itself is a finding (section 6).

## Three eras

**Early (≤2014, 659 adult visits): fantasy-taboo.** Twins (118 visit-hits)
and sisters/incest-adjacent (98) dominate proportionally; lesbian is
present (154) but not yet the overwhelming medium. The diet is organized
around forbidden configurations — the charge is in the *relation* between
the participants.

**Mid (2015–2019, 1,593 adult visits): widening.** Lesbian grows (214), cam
explodes (339), escorts appear (74), MMF grows (87), cuckold appears (13).
The repertoire widens from fantasy-taboo toward configurations and
real-world-adjacent content. This is the era where the stated profile's
shapes — MMF, cuckold-adjacent jealousy dynamics, the escort thread — all
enter the diet.

**Late (2020–2022, 2,006 adult visits): narrowing to voyeuristic
creator-direct.** Cam-rip dominates (945 — nearly half of all late adult
visits), OnlyFans surges (129), lesbian holds steady (177), twins fades
(39). The fantasy-taboo configurations of the early era recede; what grows
is *mode of access*: leaked privates, paid creator content, archived shows.
The diet narrows in genre and deepens in frame.

**2023–2025: the cold trail.** Zero adult rows. The most recent three years
of the revealed record are blank — incognito, another browser or account,
or a real change in habit. Every late-era conclusion above is "last known,"
not current.

## The forecast, scored

His prediction, made blind before the analysis: lesbian, twin, cuckold, MMF,
age play, amateur, trans, brother/sister + father/daughter etc. — "pretty
much the entire list."

**Called correctly:** lesbian (the backbone, #1 by a wide margin), twins
(#3, and "lesbian twins" the hottest exact query in the corpus), cuckold
(real, persistent, #8), MMF (#6, bi-male variant dominant as stated),
amateur (confirmed against studio tags), the incest-adjacent cluster
(sisters and mother-daughter both present, #7).

**Overranked:** trans — 16 rows, a 2019–2020 blip, the smallest standalone
category. Cuckold is real but small (57 hits across eight years); the
forecast's #3 slot oversells its volume, though the forum participation
argues for weight beyond raw counts. **Age play did not chart** as a
standalone category — the incest-adjacent content is relational
(sister/mother-daughter), not age-framed, and no distinct age-play cluster
surfaced. Called as a miss.

**Missed:** escorts (#4, 257 hits, seven-year thread with named searches)
was nowhere in the forecast. VR (35) wasn't named. The seduction/casting
narrative cluster (#9) wasn't separated out.

**The concentration claim** — "that pretty much covers the entire list, I
don't have many other genres" — is where the first scoring went wrong, and
his correction is where the analysis got better. The initial read counted
cam-rips (#2, 730 visits) and OnlyFans (#5, 219) as missed genres and
declared the claim busted. His reply: "But the cam clips are all lesbian.
Or mostly."

He was right, and a spot check of the data supports the shape of the claim
if not the letter: cam-rip pages are titled by performer name, not genre,
so genre tags undercount systematically — but the genre-tagged cam searches
that do exist ("best chaturbate lesbian," "lesbian chaturbate") point
lesbian, and the OnlyFans queries ("best lesbian onlyfans") do too. The
correction reframes the misses: **they aren't missing genres, they're a
missing dimension.** Genre is *what* — lesbian, twins, MMF. The deep dive
found *how* — recorded-not-live, archivist-voyeur, creator-direct,
real-world-adjacent. He predicted the menu. He didn't predict the way he
eats. The concentration claim holds on genres and fails on frames — and the
frame dimension is the more valuable discovery, because no instrument has
ever tested it.

Final score on the forecast: 7 of 8 genre calls present in the data (age
play the miss), concentration true for genre, blind on frame. He knows his
early era and his backbone. His late era's *mode* was invisible to him.

## The missing dimension: genre × frame

The ontology the corpus actually supports is two-dimensional. Every
consumption event has a genre (the configuration: lesbian twins, MMF,
cuckold) and a frame (the mode of access: studio scene, recorded cam
private, creator-direct feed, escort directory, VR). The old diagnostic
instruments varied neither — they held both constant at "studio-imagined
scenario, you as participant-or-arranger" and varied only the configuration
details. That is why twelve trials felt like one scenario with two or three
tweaks: the bank had one frame.

The frames in the data, ranked:

- **Archivist-voyeur (recorded/leaked):** 730 rip visits vs 136 live. The
  dominant late-era frame. The charge includes *keeping* (88 archival
  queries) — possession of the private moment, not participation in it.
- **Creator-direct (paid):** 141 OnlyFans visits, late surge. Parasocial
  access — the transaction is part of the frame.
- **Real-world-adjacent (directories/reviews):** 82 eros.com visits, named
  escort searches across seven years. The frame where fantasy touches
  logistics.
- **Studio scene (implied):** the tube default, present but unmarked —
  notable mostly for losing share to the frames above.
- **Immersive (VR/POV):** 35 hits. The frame as technology.
- **Narrative (seduction/casting):** 57 hits. The frame as story — the
  setup is the content.

The cross-check against the stated profile is where this gets load-bearing
for the instrument. The diagnostic found "mild interest beats pursuing" and
"stranger beats friend"; the corpus shows a man who watches recorded
privates five-to-one over live shows, never participates in the cam rooms he
archives, and whose forum participation is in the *cuckold* community — the
frame of watching, not doing. The voyeur frame isn't a quirk of the porn
diet. It is the through-line of the stated preferences too. The old bank
couldn't see it because it never varied the frame.

## Cross-check vs the stated profile

- **MMF≻MM:** confirmed (211 configuration hits; "bisexual mmf," "mmf
  wife"). But lesbian content out-volumes MMF roughly four to one —
  consumption is mostly *watching women together*; the *participation* frame
  is MMF. Watching and doing are different appetites, and the corpus keeps
  them separate.
- **bi≻gay male participants:** weakly present ("almost bi," "bisexual
  mmf," "homoerotic"). Straight male participants: never searched — the
  instrument's missing cell (no straight-man scenario has ever been built)
  is a real gap in the corpus too.
- **Stranger≻friend:** "stranger threesome" (3) present; zero
  friend-framed searches. Consistent.
- **Bounded-oral 2019 shape:** oral acts barely surface in titles (9) — no
  contradiction, but no confirmation either; the corpus doesn't organize by
  acts.
- **Anal hard limit:** 7 anal visit-hits in sixteen years. Negligible.
  Consistent.
- **No exhibitionism:** "fan gangbang" and "fan fuck" queries are watching,
  not being watched; voyeur/caught (23) is watching others. He is the
  watcher, never the watched. Consistent — and now with a mechanism: the
  archivist-voyeur frame.
- **Mild-interest≻pursuing:** not directly testable in the corpus, but the
  730:136 rip-over-live ratio rhymes — he watches from outside and never
  engages.
- **A axis flat:** n/a in corpus. Nothing to add.

## Instrument implications

The new scenario bank gets built from this ontology, not the old axes. The
design rule the corpus dictates: **write scenarios around configurations
and framings, not acts** — his revealed ontology is who and watched-how,
not what-they-do. And cross genre with frame: the same lesbian scene as a
studio shoot vs. a leaked cam private he's watching vs. her OnlyFans feed
tests whether the frame moves him independent of content. That test has
never been run.

Seventeen bank categories fall out of the map directly: lesbian identical
twins (the missing #1 — his hottest exact query, absent from every
instrument); lesbian seduction narrative (straight-girl/casting/massage
framings); lesbian established couple (narrative-vs-bodies contrast);
lesbian group (3+, orgy energy); MMF bi-male mild-interest (the stated
sweet spot, the control); MMF straight-male participant (the missing cell —
his stated top orientation, never built, never searched); MMF gay-male
experimenting (orientation floor); MMF stranger-vs-friend male participant
(the friend penalty, tested clean); cuckold/hotwife frame (her with him,
him watching — the jealousy charge, FFM variant included); cam-show frame
(recorded private, him as viewer — the dominant consumption mode);
creator-direct/OnlyFans frame (parasocial direct — the late-era shape);
69/face-sitting (top lesbian act in corpus); foot (minor act cluster —
tests whether acts move him at all); VR/POV frame (the tech-curious
thread); trans woman + girl (the 2019–2020 cluster); male-male stranger
with no woman (his stated MM preference, clean); escort frame
(transactional clarity — the real-world-adjacent thread; tests whether the
frame adds charge or kills it).

And the structural fix for the complaint that started all of this: vary
the room. The old bank's 28 scenarios were one frame with remixed details.
The new bank's scenarios get written as distinct scenes — different rooms,
different girls, different framings — coded clean on genre × frame so the
measurement survives the variety.

## Limits and open questions

The data ends its adult trail in 2022 — three years stale, reason unknown.
The 2023–2025 question (incognito? another setup? a real change?) is open
and material: if the late-era frame dominance is really "last known," the
bank should weight it accordingly. Act-level data is thin by construction;
a corpus organized by titles can't see what titles don't say. The trans
cluster is small enough that its bank category is exploratory, not
load-bearing. The escort thread's real-world-adjacent nature means its
scenario frame tests logistics-adjacent charge, which may not transfer to
pure fantasy. And the forecast itself is now part of the record: he
predicted seven of eight genres, missed the frame dimension, and corrected
the analysis — the genre × frame ontology is his contribution, not the
instrument's, and it's the reason the new bank will be built the way it
will be.
