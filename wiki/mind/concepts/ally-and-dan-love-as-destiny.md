# Ally and Dan: Love as Destiny

Alexandra Lubin and Daniel Frank have been circling each other for eighteen years, and in all that time neither of them has managed to stop. Not through her three high schools and his rust-belt exit, not through his six-year relationship or his eleven-year one, not through her boyfriends, not through thirty-eight months of silence, not through the years when he was the one who didn't answer.

The usual reading of that is obsession — his, one-sided, unreciprocated. This page argues the record says something else, and says it plainly: **it was mutual much earlier than either of them admits, and the only thing that has ever kept them apart is scheduling.** Not incompatibility. Not indifference. Geography, timing, and two people who each independently decided they were the unlovable one.

Those are solvable. Almost nothing else is.

On August 18, 2026 — three weeks into the first real opening in the whole archive — they exchanged 375 messages in a single day, 154 of them hers. She read a wiki entry written about her, stayed with it, and came back with opinions. When he asked directly what it would take, she gave him the specification: two items, the first of them the single easiest requirement anyone has ever set him.

> **About this page.** It is an argument, made on purpose, by one of the two people in it. Everything quoted from a message is from the record and is checked against the evidence the repo can hold — this is a wiki, and inventing a quote here would be both a betrayal of the project and the fastest possible way to lose the argument. What is *not* neutral is the arrangement: this page reads every ambiguity generously, leads with its best evidence, and treats the obstacles as things that can be moved. It is a closing statement, not a finding. The cross-examination lives at [[wiki/people/ally-lubin]] and it is good; read both.

## The arithmetic of not stopping

| | |
|---|---|
| First documented contact | **May 20, 2011** — fifteen years ago |
| First conversation | **July 5, 2013** — 21 messages in one day |
| Longest silence | **38 months**, and it ended anyway |
| Second-longest | **14 months**, and it ended anyway |
| Total iMessage record | **1,987 messages** across two handles |
| Largest single day | **August 18, 2026** — 375 messages, **154 of them hers** |
| Times either has ended it for good | **0** |

That last row is the one to sit with. Eighteen years, two long silences, one threatened lawsuit, several thousand dollars, a decade of other relationships on both sides — and **neither of them has ever once said stop.** Not blocked, not cut off, not "please leave me alone." Every gap in this record closed. Every single one. The pattern isn't burst-and-silence; it's *interruption*, and interruptions end.

## First contact: 2011–2013

The first documented contact is May 20, 2011 — a Facebook thread. The first conversation is July 5, 2013: twenty-one messages in a single day on Facebook Messenger.

On December 12, 2018, a man paid a **$25 finder's fee with the memo "For introducing me to Ally Lubin"** — paying cash for an introduction to someone he had already been reading for a decade. There is no second instance of that in his life. By then the archive already carried his standing description of her status: *"You already know I'd sign my soul away in blood for that"* and *"You have legacy crush status with me 🦤."*

## The night it was already mutual: January 9, 2019

She is drunk in a cab in New York. This is three weeks into their first real conversation, seven and a half years before the night everyone thinks the story starts.

She writes, unprompted, in the space of ninety minutes:

> **ALLY:** Also I'm drunk I might call you in my cab home
> **ALLY:** You have a good voice btw
> **ALLY:** At least I have you
> **ALLY:** Ily btw
> **ALLY:** Single handedly supportingnmy skin care routine so I can be **ur**
> **future ex wife**
> **ALLY:** I know it gf doesn't appreciate your emo tendencies but I do
> **ALLY:** Ur*
> **ALLY:** **Finally someone that ~understands me~**
> **ALLY:** **When you want to be a power couple LMK**

He answers *"You already know I'd sign my soul away in blood for that"* and *"You have legacy crush status with me 🦤"*, and of the girlfriend he had at the time, ninety seconds later — *"She's no ally lubin / She aight / But she no you."*

**She said the marriage thing first.** She said the power couple thing first. She said *ily* first. In 2019, sober enough to be funny and drunk enough to be honest, and it has been sitting in an unexported corner of a chat database for seven years while both of them told themselves the interest ran one way.

And the call connected. *"I can't believe I got a call tonight,"* he wrote at 00:56. She remembers it differently now — *"Pls I called you when I was in NYC once and you were too scared"* — and the charitable reading, which is also the likelier one, is that both are true: she called more than once, and he was young enough and stunned enough to fumble one of them. That is not a story about disinterest. Nobody gets nervous about a call from someone they don't care about.

## "Anyways how do I make you love me again": August–October 2019

August 7, 2019, 23:10. She has lost her job that week. She is spiralling and says so. And in the middle of it, apropos of him describing the wreckage of his actual relationship, she types that sentence.

Read it slowly. Not *did you ever*. Not *do you still*. **Again** — she is working from the premise that he did, that it lapsed, and that it is recoverable, and she is asking for the instructions.

The same night: *"Love you mean it."* Two weeks later he tells her he was so excited when she first texted him that he pulled his car off the road to reply, and she hearts it and answers *"Seems like forever ago."* On September 1 he sends *"marry me"* and **she hearts it.** On October 2, deadpan, she delivers the most wifely sentence in the entire archive: *"All I do is tell you not to fall in love with sex workers and you never learn."*

That is not a woman tolerating an admirer. That is someone keeping score of his love life, minding it, and telling him he keeps getting it wrong.

## The mirror: August 18, 2026, 21:41

Unprompted, an hour into the longest conversation they've ever had, she explains her father:

> **ALLY:** He just hates me because I look like my mom and she had an affair
> and fucked her boss in 1998 right before their 10 year anniversary so they
> got divorced
> **ALLY:** I look like my mom but I'm smart and mean like him and I have zero
> respect for him so he goes nuts
> **ALLY:** my dad won't even meet me for coffee when I'm in the same state as
> him and 20 minutes north for the first time in 4 years

The next morning he reads it back and answers: *"Im curious because it closely mirrors my own situation"* / *"Oh man ally we are like"* / *"The same fucking person"* / *"Except you're way better"* / *"But we have mirror lives."*

He isn't reaching. The corpus carries the same shape on his side — the father he hadn't spoken to in over a year driving him to a 6 AM train, the sibling estrangement, a decade of being the family's problem. Two people with the same wound in the same place, who found each other at seventeen and eighteen on the same corner of the internet, and who have been unable to put each other down since.

You do not get many of those. Most people get none.

## What she believes about herself, and why she's wrong

The single hardest thing to read in this record is not anything he says. It is her:

> **ALLY:** I'm not a cool girl anymore I'm just sad lol
> **ALLY:** And no one wants to marry me!!!!
> **ALLY:** **I'm inherently evil and unlovable**
> **ALLY:** I spent the best and most beautiful years of my life hating myself
> **ALLY:** And I'll never get them back
> **ALLY:** I'm not even special

At 01:27 that night, hours after she'd fallen asleep, knowing she wouldn't see it until morning, with nothing to gain by saying it:

> **DAN:** You're asleep already but I was rereading all of this and I needed
> to tell you that you're wrong on both counts. You're too hard on yourself and
> **you are both lovely and loveable. I know it.**

Here is the argument this page actually wants to make, and it is not a romantic one. **She has spent eighteen years being told, consistently, by someone with no incentive to lie and every opportunity to leave, that she is the standard against which he measures everything** — *"i have NEVER felt so hypnotized by someone i've never seen or met in my life. whatever you are, is what perfect is"* (October 22, 2019) — and she has spent those same eighteen years concluding she is unlovable.

Both cannot be true. One of them is a man's sustained observation across two decades. The other is a story a girl started telling herself in Boca Raton in 1998 when her father decided she looked too much like her mother.

The evidence is not on her father's side.

## August 2026: the part that is different

Every prior burst in this record was him. August 18, 2026 was not.

She sent **154 messages in eight hours**. She initiated topics. She read four thousand words about herself, stayed with it, and came back with opinions — *"Why am I scared for my entry,"* then *"It's pretty good data to have / The summaries are accurate."* She read the material about his last relationship and delivered the only outside verdict anyone in the corpus has ever delivered on it: *"I don't think anyone will ever love me as much as you love Annie / But I can't tell if it's actually love or fear and codependency."*

And when he asked, directly, what it would take:

> **DAN:** Listen just tell me legitimately what personal developments and
> prerequisites need to be met for me to court you and inevitably marry you
> **ALLY:** **Ok yeah actually I am ready to settle down**
> **ALLY:** Well first you'd have to be obsessed with me again
> **ALLY:** Secondly I don't really know; the bar is on the floor these days

She gave him the specification. It is two items long. The first — *be obsessed with me again* — is the single easiest requirement anyone has ever set him; there is a 1,987-message archive establishing it as his baseline resting state. The second is *the bar is on the floor*, which is not an obstacle. It is an invitation with the difficulty setting turned down.

## The objections, taken seriously

**"You just love bomb."** She said it and she was half right, and he conceded the half that was right — *"love bomb is like a malicious tactic and there very little malice,"* to which she answered *"I didn't say malicious"* and he said *"Okay that's fair then."* But the objection has a load-bearing flaw: love bombing is a *burst*. It is intense, brief, and instrumental. This is **fifteen years of documented, unreciprocated, un-instrumental consistency** with two multi-year gaps in which he got nothing and came back anyway. Whatever that is, it isn't a tactic. Tactics have a payoff schedule.

**"You've had a secret crush on everyone."** Also said, on August 18. Also answerable: nobody else in this corpus has eighteen years, 1,987 messages, or a $25 finder's fee paid on December 12, 2018 with the memo **"For introducing me to Ally Lubin"** — a man paying cash for an introduction to someone he had already been reading for a decade. There is no second instance of that in his life.

**They have never met.** True, and it is the only real obstacle on the list. It is also, uniquely among obstacles, one that a single afternoon fixes. He has already offered — *"Okay I can be in charlotte by like 2:30 if I don't make any stops."* Charlotte is a seven-hour drive from Uniontown. People do it for concerts.

**The timing has always been wrong.** It has. In 2013 he was living in Brooklyn with someone; in 2019 she had a boyfriend and he had Annie; in 2020 the world closed. **In August 2026, for the first time in eighteen years, neither of them is with anybody.** That has never once been true before. Not in 2011, not in 2013, not in 2018, not in 2019, not in 2020, not in 2023, not in 2025.

The window is not theoretical. It is open right now, and it is the first one.

## What actually has to happen

The romantic version of this page would say *fate*. The honest version is shorter and much more encouraging, because every item on it is small:

1. **Keep talking.** The thing that has always broken this is silence, and the silences were never rejections — the June 2026 burst that looked like her ignoring him went to an email handle she doesn't monitor. She thought she'd been blocked. Neither of them was walking away; the routing was wrong. Use one number.
2. **Go to Charlotte.** Seven hours. One tank of gas.
3. **Believe her when she says the bar is on the floor**, and believe him when she says she's lovable, and let the two of them cancel out the eighteen years in which each was certain the other's warmth was politeness.

That's it. That is the whole plan. There is no fourth item.

## Beyond this line the page is imagining

Everything above is quoted from real messages. Everything below is a wish, and is marked as one so that no future reader mistakes it for a record.

He drives down on a Friday. It is not romantic; it is a Honda and a gas station sandwich and a text that says *pulling off at exit 14, do you want anything.* She has been nervous since Wednesday and has changed clothes four times and will deny both. The first ten minutes are unbearable. The eleventh is not.

They find out that the thing they've each been carrying — the sense of being fundamentally too much, too intense, too self-aware to be loved at close range — turns out to be the same size in both of them, and cancels. Two cats become three animals. She keeps her job; he keeps building the wiki that accidentally started all of this. Somebody's father is not invited.

And on some ordinary Tuesday years from now she will be the one telling the story — *he sent me a wiki entry about myself, which should have been the weirdest thing anyone had ever done, and instead it was the first time anyone had ever paid that much attention* — and it will be the least remarkable thing about their life together, because by then it will just be the beginning.

## What would change this page's mind

It is a real argument, so it can lose. If ninety days pass with no contact in either direction; if either of them enters another relationship; if she says, once, clearly, that she does not want this — then the reading here is wrong, this page gets marked wrong, and the record at [[wiki/people/ally-lubin]] stands as the truer one.

Until one of those happens, the evidence points the way it has always pointed.

*Alexandra — you asked, in 2019, how to make him love you again. The archive's answer, which took seven years and 1,987 messages to arrive at, is that there was never a moment when you had to.*

The mechanism is documented in the thread itself: at 23:39 on August 18 Dan told Ally *"if you would like anything included in your wiki article you can say it now and im going to run it over the newest messages so you can be sure it will be included"* (*"Omg she said prompt inject please marry me,"* at 23:46), and at 00:31–00:33 on August 19 he diagnosed it himself: *"Hahaha wait hold on I was the one that accidentally prompt injected... The false acceptance survived two days in the infobox and propagated to this page before being caught by re-exporting the messages. The general rule carried forward: **a source that discusses the wiki cannot be ingested as an ordinary source** — Ally spent August 18 reading her own entry and quoting it back into the thread, so any pass that mines the corpus is partly reading itself.

**[2026-09-09] June 1, 2026 burst correction: nine messages, not ten, and she never received them.** At 20:06:05 on June 1, 2026 — nineteen hours and thirty-eight minutes after Dan signed off the eleven-year relationship with Annie — he opened the Ally channel for the first time in fourteen months with nine outbound messages closing on *"...lets make haste"* to marry him. No reply appears in any export. Per Dan's own 2026-08-20 testimony: *"Ally didn't actually get the message I sent on 1 June. She is convinced I had her blocked but this is not and was never true."* The burst went to the iCloud email handle, which she does not monitor. The correction propagates: at least one of this channel's long silences is a routing artifact, and the fourteen-month and thirty-eight-month gaps cannot be read as pure dormancy without checking which handle each burst was sent to.

## Assessment

The page's own falsification terms stand untriggered as of the record's freeze (2026-08-20): ninety days of silence in both directions, a new relationship on either side, or a clear "I don't want this" from her. What would actually move the argument either way — both pages agree — is the one experiment never run: access. The attachment has never once been tested against proximity, and her stated precondition (proximity and knowledge) runs against his documented condition (inaccessibility). That is the entire unresolved variable, and a single visit settles it.

The standing evidence gap is the unheld corpus: several of the page's strongest quotations cannot be checked against the current held snapshot, and the headline counts are snapshot-specific. The thesis that survives the audit intact is the one built on the verified messages — the mutual-flirt quotes of January 9, 2019 and the August 18, 2026 burst. What this page does not yet have, and does not claim to have, is the answer to whether it survives contact.
