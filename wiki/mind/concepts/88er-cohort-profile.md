# 88er Cohort Profile

The 1988 birth cohort — Dan's cohort, born November 1, 1988 — as
characterized in a 2026-09-15 thread he opened by asking, seriously,
what 88ers actually like. He prefaced the critique by noting he had
painted millennials as culturally vacuous despite being "as core
millennial as they come" — the insider's license to swing at his own
tribe.

## The hinge position

88ers are the last analog kids. A real childhood — pre-internet,
pre-smartphone — then the internet arriving at exactly the age to be
feral with it. AIM away messages, MySpace top 8, Limewire roulette:
curation as identity, learned before curation had a name.

## The formation years

The cohort's taste stack, per the thread: emo/pop-punk plus blog-era
rap plus DVD-auteur movies. The Office as a second language. This is
not a generic millennial list; it is period-specific to the kids who
were 12 on 9/11 and 20 when the 2008 crash hit — the earnest-to-ironic
whiplash generation. Old enough to remember sincerity, young enough to
learn that sincerity gets punished. (Interpretive, moderate
confidence: the 9/11-at-12 / crash-at-20 hinge is Sammy's framing, not
Dan's words; Dan supplied the taste inventory, the hinge is the
reading.)

## The archival turn

The thread's answer to "what do they do now, culturally": they curate
with adult money. Letterboxd lists, Discogs hauls, Spotify playlists
as personality. Emo reunion tours with earplugs. A24 opening weekend.
Prestige TV as homework. Same obsessions as at 17, just with bad
knees and a group chat instead of a top 8.

Sammy's summary line from the thread: "gentrified teenage taste. same
hunger, zero new moves, better shelving." Dan's verdict: "How FUCKING
BORING."

## Why it stings

The profile is not really about other 88ers. It is the cohort's
mirror held up by someone who escaped the archival turn by building
instead — the Wiki Brain, the agent pipelines, the music — and who
reads curation-without-generation as the vacuity he is trying not to
become. (Interpretive, moderate confidence.) The boredom is not
aesthetic; it is existential. The database got bigger and the output
stayed flat.
