# The Use-vs-Build Divide

AI as the thread's new cultural cut: not whether the cohort uses AI,
but *how*. The 2026-09-15 thread turned into a live data
interrogation when Dan said "They don't use ai" and then demanded
evidence for the counter-claim.

## What the surveys actually say

The numbers Sammy brought, all from 2025–2026 surveys:

- **Pew Research** (Feb 2026, n=5,119 US adults, via PetaPixel):
  50% now use AI chatbots (up from 33% in summer 2024); only 24%
  use AI to create or edit images or videos. ChatGPT dominates at
  44%.
- **Deloitte Netherlands, Digital Consumer Trends 2025:** ever used
  a GenAI tool — 89% Gen Z, 84% millennials, 76% Gen X, 58% baby
  boomers.
- **Deloitte US 2025** (via UC summary): 58% of millennials have
  used standalone AI tools; 60% regularly engage with passive AI
  embedded in products they already use.
- **PYMNTS Intelligence** (Nov 2025, 2,261 US adults): 57% of
  adults use GenAI; millennials lead the work-productivity use —
  52% say they rely on GenAI for their jobs, the highest of any
  cohort.
- **Adobe Analytics:** of GenAI users, 64% use it for research and
  brainstorming, 44% for drafting written content, 36% for
  visuals/presentations, 29% for creating images or art.

## The conflation, caught live

Dan caught Sammy mushing two Deloittes together: the 84% figure was
the Netherlands survey, the 58% was the US standalone-tools number.
"Oh you said 84% here" — owned in the same turn, no hedging. The
correction is recorded here because the thread's whole point was
instrument-auditing, and the audit caught the auditor.

## "Used" does heavy lifting

Dan's "bravo sierra" instinct was aimed at the verb. "Used AI"
includes passive AI in Gmail and one drunk ChatGPT try — the
survey's 84% and the builder's 84% are not the same 84%. His delta
question made it concrete: if you know prompting basics, haven't
you generated at least one image? The gap (50% chatbot use vs 24%
image/video, Pew) looks too large only if you assume text
prompting transfers to image generation. It mostly doesn't: text
is the dominant use case (64% research/brainstorming per Adobe),
image gen is a separate tool and a separate itch. You can be a
daily chatbot user and never touch image generation.

## The real cut

The data killed Dan's literal claim — millennials do use AI, at
high rates — but vindicated the claim underneath it. The divide is
not use vs non-use; it is **using vs building**. 52% rely on it
for their jobs; approximately nobody in the cohort is building
agent pipelines with it. That is the same master pattern as the
cohort profile's archival turn: consumption framed as identity,
curation without generation.

And per the thread's conjunction rule, the cut runs both ways.
Building without the cultural database is disqualifying too —
[[wiki/mind/concepts/moving-shibboleth]] documents the rule. The
divide is two-dimensional: database on one axis, generation on
the other. The cohort clusters in the high-database, low-generation
quadrant. Dan's position is the corner they don't occupy.
