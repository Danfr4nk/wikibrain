# Phonetic name drift

> **Incidental entry** — a single dated observation about how Dan's memory stores names: the sound survives, the spelling doesn't, and the gap gets rebuilt. Complete at this size.

**What it is.** Phonetic name drift: after a name's exact form is lost, the brain retains its sound-shape — syllable count, stress, consonant skeleton — and reconstructs a plausible replacement from the nearest high-frequency forms. The gist is real memory; the surface is a rebuild wearing the gist's clothes.

## The observation (2026-10-04)

At ~02:04 EDT, Dan reported he had spent roughly fifteen minutes trying to recall the name of Hitler's murdered niece, and produced his best reconstruction: **"Hayley Robley."** The actual name is Angela **"Geli" Raubal** (d. 1931, aged 23, in Hitler's Munich apartment). His response on being told: "Is it that similar?"

It is, and the similarity is structural, not vibes:

- **Syllable shape:** both are two syllables plus two syllables, with the same stress contour.
- **The ending:** both first names end on the "-lee" sound (Ge-li, Hay-ley), and both surnames close on an L/lee tail (Rau-bal, Rob-ley).
- **Consonant skeleton:** Raubal and Robley carry the same consonants in the same order — R, then B, then L. The vowels and the exact spelling drifted; the frame didn't.

What got substituted is as diagnostic as what survived: a rare early-20th-century German name was rebuilt out of two of the most ordinary modern English names available. The reconstruction engine prefers frequency. Given a sound-shape and a blank, it reaches for what it has heard most.

## Why this entry exists

This is the name-level instance of the pattern this wiki keeps finding at larger scales, and it is worth a dated record because it arrived clean: a self-reported recall attempt, an identifiable target, a checkable reconstruction. Dan's documented split — analysis and structure reliable, surface detail (dates, timestamps, confidence levels) prone to confident drift — is usually measured in predictions and narratives. Here it is in two syllables. The memory kept *who* she was and how her name *went*; it lost how her name *was*.

It also shows the countermeasure working. Dan flagged the uncertainty himself, sat with the failed recall for fifteen minutes, and asked for a check rather than asserting the rebuild. The drift is not a carelessness problem; the retrieval felt like remembering, and the correction only exists because he asked.

**Limits.** One instance, self-reported effort, no way to reconstruct the exact retrieval path — the resemblance judgment (syllable shape + consonant skeleton) is qualitative, not measured. His memory of the episode itself (the fifteen minutes) is taken as given; it is, fittingly, the one part that can't be checked.

## Why incidental

It is one clean datum, complete at this size. It promotes to a major article if dated instances accumulate into a series worth a synthesis — the drift pattern measured across names, dates, and confidence claims — or if the cognitive profile's reliability model is revised to carry it formally.
