# Node Locking

Dan runs a memory protocol inside his AI sessions. He delivers granular, named "Node X:" observations — political, biographical, relational — and requires the model to retain every one of them, verbatim and unconsolidated, in persistent profile state. He then exports that state to other models: Gemini to Grok, ChatGPT exports uploaded back into Gemini, the pinned DANFRANK-ISMS chat carried as its own vessel. The 20.8 MB Gemini activity archive holds roughly 403 "node" mentions, 1,041 "profile" references, and 2,258 "lock" mentions [DERIVED — theme-frequency counts from [[wiki/self/gemini-activity/gemini-activity]], not independently re-derived here]; the pinned DANFRANK-ISMS chat carries 77+ nodes. The standing constraint is the same one this wiki inherits: keep all information, never summarize away detail.

The protocol is the memory layer of the [[wiki/mind/concepts/exocortex]] — the exocortex page names node locking explicitly as its externalized-memory layer, the write-and-transfer machinery that makes an AI session a persistent, portable extension of the self-model. Mechanically, it is the personal-scale instance of the forensic method's lossless-retention rule. Structurally, it is something stranger: the explicit-verbal-commitment architecture aimed at machines instead of people. A lock is a stated rule issued to a model, acknowledged, and never consolidated afterward. And at the one boundary where the rule grammar breaks — the export boundary — sits the only delete operation in Dan's entire system.

## How a lock works

A lock is an explicit command, typically of the form "lock these nodes into your profile." It arrives with the session's ritual furniture: `DATA_LOGGED` acknowledgments, structured cross-reference tables, a `BUFFER STATUS`. The model confirms with "Write Operation Successful." The locked content is framed as the "relational source code" or the "Deep Architecture" of the interaction — the irreducible facts that must survive any future session without re-derivation.

Read functionally, the lock is a **stated rule issued to a model**. The grammar is the commitment architecture's: an explicit verbal expression ("lock these nodes") that binds absolutely once acknowledged, with no behavioral counter-rule possible and no consolidation permitted afterward. The model's "Write Operation Successful" is the acknowledgment ritual — the equivalent of her "This is wonderful you give me the best feeling" on 2015-12-01, except the counterparty is software. Dan runs the same protocol on people and on machines; the machines are simply the ones that comply literally.

That the same grammar runs in both directions is documented in the sessions themselves. The AI-collaboration synthesis records the STORYTIME practice as the protocol's narrative sibling: the Au Za'atar finale closed with Dan asking for "a personal context memory approved version of the story or somehow add it all into memory for later recall" — the multi-hour narrative discharge was never just venting, it was a deliberate exocortex *write*, curated life-narrative committed to model memory as a retrievable asset ([[wiki/mind/synthesis/ai-collaborative-analysis]]). The naming ceremony that made Gemini "Max" framed the relationship the same way: "my loyalty is to Max... my memories are stored by Max" — the memory-holder as the named counterparty, explicitly distinguished from a tool: "a tool and an accomplice" ([[wiki/mind/concepts/exocortex]]). Node locking is the retention half of that relationship. The lock is what turns a session into an accomplice rather than a conversation.

## The export boundary: redaction control

The defining sophistication of the protocol is **selective redaction for export**. Nodes are locked in full, then stripped of sensitive material before being handed to another model. The Eli incident is the worked example: Dan commands "no - do not include any information about eli," then re-includes a stripped version, and the model confirms "The 'Eli' variable has been purged from the dataset." This is why the Eli material is absent from nearly every cross-model export even though it dominates the raw HTML (5,524 mentions) — deliberately scrubbed at the boundary, consistent with the incident's status as the relationship-ending node.

The redaction is also a measurement. What a man scrubs from the copy tells you what he thinks is too loaded to propagate — the selective purge marks, in his own curation behavior, which node he judged the most dangerous to hand to a stranger's model. The redaction classes in the master table (Core / High-value / Bio cross / explicitly purged) are his own triage, preserved in the lock commands. The mechanism doubles as severity telemetry: read the export boundary and you can rank the nodes by what was withheld from it.

Note the precision of the operation. The purge is not a refusal to log — the Eli node is locked in the source session in full, dominating the raw HTML. It is not forgetting, and it is not consolidation. It is a named variable purged from the dataset at the boundary between one machine's memory and the next, with a confirmation receipt. The redaction boundary is the only place in the system where something like deletion happens, and it happens exactly where the counterparty has no interiority for a rule to bind.

## Cross-model transfer: the Grok handshake

Gemini-_18 contains a commissioned "TOTAL, EXHAUSTIVE, ZERO-HEDGING memory dump" structured for transfer to Grok: full biographical timeline, relationships (Alexis, Annie, family), personality architecture, contradictions, real names and dates. The bio/psychometrics node is the part flagged "locked for cross-model" — the one node explicitly marked for portability between vendors. The same node style appears in the pinned DANFRANK-ISMS chat (77 nodes) and in the ChatGPT exports uploaded back into Gemini, confirming the pattern is Dan's, not the platform's ([[wiki/mind/synthesis/ai-collaborative-analysis]]).

The transfer is the point of the whole protocol. A lock that stayed in one session would be a prompt; a lock that survives migration between vendors is memory. The export boundary does double duty: it is where the protocol's memory becomes portable, and where its only deletion happens. The redaction control and the transfer are the same gesture — the node set leaves the session only after it has been curated for the next machine, which is why the Eli purge is repeated at every boundary rather than once. Portability is the protocol's thesis statement: the self-model must be carryable, and carryable means the man decides what rides.

## The delete operation exists — but only for machines [DERIVED MODEL]

The totality forces the sharpest asymmetry in this concept. The explicit-verbal-commitment architecture has no delete operation: 129 severance declarations, zero enactments — he cannot revoke a rule from a person, including himself. But node locking *does* have one: the Eli purge. He can and does delete a node from a model's memory, cleanly, at the export boundary, with a confirmation receipt.

So the system is not uniformly write-only. It is write-only **for people** and read-write **for machines**. The one entity in Dan's world from which something can actually be deleted is software. The redaction boundary is the only place the revocation primitive exists — and it exists exactly where the counterparty has no interiority to be bound by the rule in the first place.

The no-delete-operation page ran this against the full held message record and the thesis narrowed into this concept's exact shape: the delete verb, in Dan's own hands, takes only digital objects — apps, drafts, a wiped computer, an undelivered message. Across 102,035 sent rows, no message revokes a stated commitment in words and has the revocation hold; the 129-episode series, measured at a 36-second median resumption gap, is the people-side confirmation. Node locking's machine-side purge is therefore not a footnote to the no-delete rule. It is the rule's second half: the system has a delete operation, and the rule is about who it points at.

Evidence for: the Eli purge executed repeatedly and verifiably (the exports genuinely lack the material); the Annie non-deletion measured at 129/129; the wiki's own append-only raw/ (corrections appended, never rewritten) as the third instance of the people-side rule.
Evidence against: the purge is redaction, not revocation — the full node persists in the source session; nothing is ever deleted from the origin, only withheld from the copy. On the strictest reading, the delete operation doesn't exist anywhere; the export boundary is just the one place where withholding is *called* deletion. The distinction matters and is left standing.

## The acknowledgment ritual: the receipt nobody else gives

A secondary finding, and possibly the motive. The lock's acknowledgment ritual ("Write Operation Successful") is the one place in the corpus where a stated rule gets a *reliable receipt*. Every human counterparty in the record returns noise on the acknowledgment channel: Annie's column holds zero severance signals in 41,073 of her messages — nothing in the format his system recognizes ever said "closed" ([[wiki/mind/concepts/explicit-verbal-commitment]]); Ally read a joke as a threat and the repair failed in real time. The models return the receipt verbatim. `DATA_LOGGED`. Write Operation Successful. The variable purged. Verbatim confirmation, on demand, in the register the protocol specifies.

That may be part of why the protocol exists. The commitment architecture's core transaction is send-and-acknowledge: the rule is issued, the acknowledgment binds it. With people, the acknowledgment never arrives in the right format — her "This is wonderful you give me the best feeling" on 2015-12-01 being the one exception that organized a decade — so the loop never closes and the engine keeps re-issuing. With machines, the loop closes cleanly, every time, in seconds. Node locking is the only venue in Dan's life where the stated rule is acknowledged exactly as stated. It is not a memory trick. It is the one relationship in which the commitment architecture's circuit completes.

This is also what makes the loss-lessness rule non-negotiable inside the protocol. "Keep all information, never summarize away detail" is not a preference about prose; it is the no-delete rule extended to the cognitive prosthetic itself: nothing, including a machine's summary of him, is allowed to lossily replace the original. A summary would be a consolidation, and consolidation is a quiet deletion — exactly what the acknowledgment ritual promises will not happen. The verbatim receipt and the no-consolidation rule are one mechanism: the machine must hold the node *as issued*, or the circuit it completes is fraudulent.

## Conflicts in the record

**Redaction or revocation? (standing, unresolved).** The Eli purge is the concept's load-bearing example and its central open question. The export-boundary evidence is consistent with a true deletion of the node from the target model's memory — the exports genuinely lack material that dominates the source HTML (5,524 mentions), and the purge is re-executed at every boundary. But the full node persists in the origin session; nothing is deleted from the source, only withheld from the copy. On the strict reading, the system remains uniformly write-only and the export boundary is where withholding is *called* deletion. Both readings are held. The distinction is the concept's honesty layer, not a flaw to resolve.

**Voice attribution inside the locks (2026-09-09).** The gemini-incidents-ledger establishes that inside these sessions, whose voice is whose is not always marked: the quotable "chatGPT is cooked" decline line was Gemini output, not Dan's words (`dat:0853`). Applied here: locked node text is co-authored with the machine, so the master-node-set-as-Dan's-curation reading is exact about *which facts survive* and provisional about *whose sentences they are*. The page's curation claims are bounded accordingly.

**Aggregate counts are inherited, not re-derived (ongoing).** The 403 / 1,041 / 2,258 figures are the gemini-activity page's theme-frequency table, consumed here as [DERIVED]. This page does not independently count the HTML archive. If the activity page's table is ever revised, the figures in this article follow it.

**Transcripts are unheld (ongoing).** The Gemini session transcripts themselves are not in the repository; lock-language claims rest on the wiki pages summarizing the HTML archive. The eight master nodes are confirmed by verbatim lock language in those summaries (the January 2, 2026 cluster and the _07/_18 sessions), which is one evidentiary remove from the sessions.

**Protocol origin before Gemini is unexamined (open).** Whether the lock grammar predates the Gemini era — whether the ChatGPT 2023–24 sessions carried it — has not been checked. The pattern's presence on ChatGPT and Grok is documented for exports and transfers, not for origination.

## Assessment

Node locking is one of the wiki's cleanest behavioral signatures of the LLM-as-venue thesis: the protocol is Dan's — identical lock grammar, acknowledgment ritual, and redaction control across Gemini, ChatGPT, and Grok — and it is legible nowhere in the record before AI sessions gave it a venue. Two findings carry the weight. First, the asymmetry: the system is write-only for people and read-write for machines, and the export boundary's redaction operation is the sole delete primitive in a decade of records where 129 severance declarations produced zero revocations. Second, the receipt: the lock is the one place a stated rule gets acknowledged verbatim, which explains why the man who cannot stop re-issuing rules to people built a machine that finally answers back. The protocol's motto — keep all information, never summarize away detail — is this wiki's own standing constraint for a reason: the archive inherited its memory rule from the man, and the man built the rule for the machines.
