# Non-Fiction Only

Dan consumes non-fiction almost exclusively. Goodreads runs ~97.8% nonfiction across 120 rated books; the YouTube record is news, commentary, history, and video essays; the film canon is auteur-observational rather than genre fiction. This is not a preference stated once and forgotten. It is an identity-level trait with a behavioral paper trail across three media — and it has exactly two documented exceptions, both of which obey a deeper rule than the one they appear to break.

The rule is adult-life-specific. On 2007-07-21, eighteen-year-old Dan's Facebook status read "reading Harry Potter nonstop"; on 2011-07-16 he tweeted "Harry Potter 7.2 - goodbye, childhood." Teen Dan read the defining fiction of his cohort straight through. The non-fiction rule is not a lifelong constitution but a developed position — the forensic stance hardening into a consumption law sometime in the 2010s, contemporaneous with the Ti-dominant profile's full expression.

## The books: subject-level monomania

The 120-book shelf is not broad taste. The wiki's corrected analysis reads it as **subject-level monomania through many witnesses**: 40 of 120 books tagged trump/jan-6 across 30 authors, 20 tagged roman-republic/ancient-history/caesar across 14 authors, the two sets non-overlapping. Eighty-five of 98 authors appear exactly once. He does not read widely. He reads two events — the Trump presidency and the fall of the Roman Republic — through as many distinct witnesses as he can find, one account per witness being all that exhaustive coverage of a single event requires.

**Trump-era politics/journalism** (read dates 2022–2025, heavy 2024): Michael Wolff (5 books, 3 five-stars — the 2025 refresh completed all four Trump-era volumes plus *All or Nothing*, a complete-series read of one author), Bob Woodward (5 books, zero 5-stars — read thoroughly, not loved), Jonathan Karl (3 books, 2 five-stars). Tags: trump 40, politics 79, journalism 31, jan-6 18. The pattern is depositional: the same event re-examined through every available witness, including witnesses he does not rate.

The Wolff–Woodward contrast is the shelf in miniature. Wolff gets the five stars — the novelistic, access-driven, scene-reconstructed Trump books, all five volumes including the May 2025 capstone. Woodward gets read cover to cover five times and starred at nothing: the institutional, notes-and-tapes, both-sides record, consumed as duty rather than pleasure. What Dan rewards is not agreement — Woodward is the more careful reporter by any conventional measure — but narrative density and insider texture. The complete-series read is set-closure behavior (the same Architecture A the eclecticism page measures): once Wolff becomes a witness, *all* of Wolff must be taken, the way the Kubrick completism demands the whole filmography. Eighteen books carry the jan-6 tag specifically — the event as the shelf's gravitational center, the hinge the whole Trump collection orbits.

**Ancient history / Roman Republic** (detonation across a few months of 2024, nearly all rated 5): the standard modern Republic syllabus read back to back — Dan Carlin's *Death Throes of the Republic* (Hardcore History, 5★, Jan 2024), Tom Holland's *Rubicon* (5★, Apr 2024), Michael Parenti's *Assassination of Julius Caesar* (Apr 2024), Adrian Goldsworthy's *Caesar: Life of a Colossus* (5★, Jul 2024). Goldsworthy accounts for four titles total (*Caesar*, *Augustus: First Emperor of Rome*, *Philip and Alexander*, *In the Name of Rome*). The Parenti placement matters: a Marxist people's-history of Caesar read alongside Goldsworthy's establishment biography is comparative method, not strongman admiration — the same event through hostile witnesses.

The Republic interest carries a **hard stop at Augustus**, and the boundary is precise rather than decorative. He read Goldsworthy's *Augustus* (4★, Jun 2024) — the transition figure, the fall's endpoint — and stopped there. Nothing on the shelf continues into the Empire proper. The subject is the fall, not the thing that replaced it. Twenty books carry the ancient-history/roman-republic tags; the tags he attached — `great-man-theory`, `caesar`, `top-shelf` — show he read it as leadership-and-collapse material.

The timing is the tell. The Rome detonation lands in the first half of 2024 — the same window as the Annie decade's terminal phase and the year before the 2025 collapse. A man watching his own republic fall reads about a republic falling. The wiki does not claim he said this; the claim is structural. The Parenti placement sharpens it: a Marxist people's-history of Caesar read alongside Goldsworthy's establishment biography is comparative method, not strongman admiration. He is not reading for a hero. He is reading for the mechanism of collapse — how republics end, told by witnesses who disagree with each other. The hard stop at Augustus is the thesis statement: the interesting part is the breaking, not the empire that follows.

**War/history shelf:** Carlin (5★), Annie Jacobsen's *Nuclear War* (5★), Shirer's *Rise and Fall of the Third Reich* (5★, Jan 2023). Eighteen tagged war, 69 history. *American Prometheus* (5★, Jul 2023) sits on the great-man shelf beside Plutarch — though the Plutarch ratings carry a data-quality caveat (see Limits below).

**The want-to-read list** (149 titles, never mined before this wiki's favorites work) runs the same obsessions — Rome, Trump-era politics, intelligence/conspiracy history — plus a distinct NYC-history thread (*The Power Broker*, *Broadway: A History of New York City in Thirteen Miles*, *Lost New York*). Its highest-rated title is *The Sinister Truth: Mk-Ultra* (4.75). The wishlist is the shelf's shadow: the same evidence room, unbuilt.

**How he reads** is part of the pattern. The modality is audio: *"I'm convinced that Scribd is far superior to Audible if you listen to a lot of audiobooks"*; *"Politics has been so boring that I've plowed through a cumulative 92 hours of history audiobooks over the last 2 months using only my monthly audible credit."* Ninety-two hours in two months is not casual listening — it is the depositional habit at full throughput, history as something to be *processed* in bulk. He pre-ordered James Comey's *A Higher Loyalty* and chased it through Audible: the witness-acquisition instinct operating on release day. And the verse direction runs one way only: on 2015-12-03 he wrote an unprompted love poem in the small hours (*"I'm all alone I'm in my room I don't know what to do / I feel my heart beat in my chest / It only beats for you"*), but the corpus holds no poetry *reading* — no poets, no collections, no verse consumed. Poetry is output, never intake. The same asymmetry as the fiction rule: he will produce aesthetic signal, but he does not take it in as information.

## The two exceptions

The first draft of this page said one. That was wrong, and the correction is load-bearing: the two exceptions obey a common rule that refines the concept.

**Gore Vidal's *Narratives of Empire*** is historical fiction — novels — and an earlier fixation. Dan lists Vidal among the heroic figures he identifies with, and Vidal operates in the corpus as a cultural-literacy litmus test: the Cato bootloader document's people-sorting filter asks "Have they heard of DJ Shadow or read Gore Vidal?" — taste as a test of kinship, the reference frame as the gate. A 2025-05-05 hypothetical-groomsmen joke seated "the ghost of gore vidal" alongside "a bag of cocaine, the first 3 fall out boy albums" — the humor depends on Vidal being a known constant, not a random name. On 2025-05-29 he texted about a visit: "OMG he's got the gore vidal historical fiction series," noting the books sat "all the way on the left of the book shelf" — registering someone else's set by shelf position, the way someone tracks their own library.

The identification runs deeper than the books. The Vidal page files him as the type specimen of the corpus's one identification figure: right about the empire for fifty years, changed nothing — the polemicist as Cassandra, the forensic auditor whose findings never moved the system. That is Dan's own threat model in period dress ("competence correctly deployed, outcome still catastrophic"), and it explains why the *essayist* Vidal matters as much as the novelist: the *Narratives of Empire* are polemics wearing novels' clothes, the same way the Rome shelf is collapse-mechanics wearing history's clothes. The Vidal page's parallel is exact: Vidal is the *earlier* Rome-and-empire fixation, the 2024 Republic binge re-running the same imperial-decline preoccupation through history rather than fiction. The exception was the obsession before the obsession had its proper medium.

**Jacob Bacharach's *The Doorposts of Your House and on Your Gates*** (Liveright, 2017) is the stronger exception and the more revealing one. It is a contemporary literary novel — not historical fiction, not history-adjacent — and Dan rated it 5 stars (shelved 2022-03-24, tagged "fiction, 2022"). The discovery chain is fully dated: 2021-02-06, Dan texts his mother *"I was listening to a podcast and Jake Bacharach was the guest lol"* — the podcast was Chapo Trap House, episode 94, served by autoplay from the back catalogue. Bacharach is from Uniontown; he was in a TV commercial for Dan's father's store as a teenager; his novel features Uniontown prominently, including a Virginia Avenue passage. Dan wrote to him; Bacharach replied (Feb 2021), naming *"my Doorposts of Your House novel"* as the Uniontown book. Dan then called it by the wrong title — *The Bend of the World* — for five years, a clean specimen of testimony decay in a high-salience memory that the wiki's own correction pass caught in August 2026.

Unlike Vidal (books spotted on someone else's shelf), this is fiction Dan owns on his own shelf, read, five-starred — and evangelized. On 2022-01-02 he recommended Bacharach's Uniontown novel to Joe Oshnack unprompted, framing the author as proof "not everyone from our hometown turned into a reactionary fascist-adjacent class traitor." That is the same recommend-and-recruit behavior he applies to the eleven-film canon: the exception gets the canon's treatment, which means the exception is load-bearing, not marginal.

The two exceptions share a structure, and it is the concept's real shape: **fiction is admitted when it functions as documentary evidence about a tracked subject.** Vidal's novels are evidence about empire and decline — the same subject as the Rome shelf. Bacharach's novel is evidence about Uniontown — Dan's own geography, the most tracked subject in the corpus. Neither exception is fiction-as-fiction. Both are the evidence room admitting a witness who happened to write in novel form. The rule survives its exceptions because the exceptions were never about fiction at all.

The asymmetry is worth stating plainly. Vidal is the *distant* exception: someone else's shelf, a historical series, an identification figure from the heroic list — fiction engaged at arm's length, as cultural literacy. Bacharach is the *intimate* exception: his own shelf, his own five stars, his own correspondence, his own street in the text, his own recommendation to a friend. The Bacharach file even records the relationship's asymmetry in the primary sources — Dan's framing to Tom is *"someone I know like really well,"* Bacharach's reply is *"I remember you and your family,"* a courteous reintroduction to a person half-recalled. Dan pursued the novelist; the novelist obliged politely. The exception that cost him the most effort is the one about his own town. The evidence room admits fiction in proportion to how much of Dan's world the fiction contains.

The canon is closed and auteur-shaped: he completes directors, not genres. The wiki's own caveat stands — four titles (*Pulp Fiction*, *Kill Bill*, *The Graduate*, *Parasite*) sit on the list on a reading rather than on hard evidence — but the shape holds either way. Set-closure, not sampling: the same Architecture A the books page measures, run on film.

The fiction rule is policed from the other side, too. On 2013-02-25, at the height of the monoculture: *"if i gave any less of a fuck about 'game of thrones', i would literally not exist."* Not indifference — performed contempt, the disdain as identity work. The prestige-TV event of the decade gets the same treatment the novels get: conspicuous non-consumption, stated publicly. What he does not watch is as curated as what he does.

The diet is not fandom. It is intake — and intake gets audited. The inward criticism is the tell that the commentary spine is an evidence source rather than a comfort source: you do not fact-check your church unless you came for the facts. The Vidal litmus test works the same way on people; the Hasan criticism works the same way on media. Everything in the diet is subject to the forensic stance, including the diet itself.

## Why: the forensic stance

The mechanism is the Ti-forensic stance documented at [[wiki/mind/concepts/forensic-method]]: reality is a high-fidelity system and the job is to find where it breaks. Fiction is low-information input for that engine. An invented system carries no ground truth to audit against — there is nothing to check, no witness to cross-examine, no docket to pull. The non-fiction rule is not snobbery about novels. It is the same reason the history shelf runs 69 deep and the want-to-read list tops out at Mk-Ultra: he is stocking an evidence room, not a library.

The evidence-room metaphor is exact, not decorative. Consider what the shelf *does*: forty witnesses on one event, fourteen on another, the hostile witness shelved beside the friendly one (Parenti next to Goldsworthy), the five-starred novelist's Uniontown set beside the depositions. This is not reading for pleasure or edification. It is case-building — the same case-building the wiki documents in his J6 work (the "tourist-wandering" behavioral signature as evidentiary anchor), his 765-message August forensics, his stylometric self-surveillance. The reading diet is one instance of a general faculty: everything gets filed, cross-examined, and kept. Fiction cannot be filed because there is no case it belongs to — unless, as the two exceptions show, the novel *is* the case file (empire, hometown).

The lyric qualifier runs parallel: he hears sung lyrics as timbre, not language — narrative carried in an aesthetic medium does not land as information for him ([[wiki/interests/music/concepts/lyrics-as-timbre]]). Prose fiction may fail the same way, though that is inference from the musical finding, not a separate attestation. The dance-phases synthesis holds the same parallel from the other side: the fiction-avoidance and the dance-only phases may run on one perceptual mechanism — timbre-first, narrative-last. Note the convergence with the poetry asymmetry: he *writes* verse (the 2015 burst) but does not *read* it, just as he *produces* musical narrative (as GRIPNOTIC) but does not *receive* lyrical narrative. The aesthetic channel is output-only. Information flows in through the documentary channel or not at all.

The explicit-over-inferred architecture converges here too ([[wiki/mind/concepts/autism]]): stated rules hold, inferred ones do not register. Non-fiction is explicit claim-staking — a witness asserting *this happened*, checkable, falsifiable — while fiction asks the reader to infer meaning from invented signal, the exact operation his neurology discounts. The reading diet and the cognitive profile describe each other: the shelf is what explicit-over-inferred processing *reads like* when it shops for books.

Three of this week's writing passes now point at one mechanism from three directions. The dance-phases synthesis files the lyric qualifier as explicit-over-inferred processing made audible — sung words registering as timbre because lyrics are inferential and ambiguous. The autism pass names the same architecture as the profile's load-bearing trait. This page files the reading diet as its third witness: prose fiction failing where documentary prose succeeds, for the same reason lyrics fail where beats succeed. Timbre-first, narrative-last, stated-over-inferred — one perceptual system, three media, no counterexamples on the record. That convergence is the strongest thing this page can claim, and it is held as a model, not a finding: the behavior is attested three times over, the unity of the mechanism is the wiki's inference.

## The shape of the negative

What the rule excludes is as documented as what it includes. A full-corpus sweep for the fiction Dan *doesn't* consume returned comprehensive nulls: no comics, no graphic novels, no manga; no poetry reading (the one poem in the corpus is his own); no Criterion, no film-theory canon; no Dostoevsky, Hemingway, Fitzgerald, Steinbeck, Murakami, Vonnegut, Pynchon, Cormac McCarthy, Stephen King, Tolkien, or Infinite Jest — the entire prestige-fiction syllabus at zero hits; no short stories; no bookstore culture, no library habit, no Kindle life beyond one unreadable bit.ly. Orwell's *1984* circulates as slogan ("1984 = 2012") with no evidence of reading. Hunter S. Thompson appears only on the heroic-figures list, which the corpus itself flags as unattested.

This is not the profile of someone who tried fiction and bounced off it. It is the profile of someone for whom the category barely exists as a consumption option — the way a person with no sweet tooth doesn't *resist* dessert but never thinks of it. The nulls matter because they rule out the obvious alternative explanations: he is not avoiding fiction out of snobbery (he evangelizes pop-punk and Fall Out Boy), out of anti-intellectualism (the shelf is 120 deep), or out of inattention (he tracks his own consumption forensically). The avoidance is structural, downstream of the perceptual mechanism — the same reason the nulls cluster around *narrative-as-information* in every medium: no novels, no lyrical meaning, no prestige TV, no film theory. The negative space has the same shape as the positive claim.

## Limits, corrections, and what the record cannot show

- **The "one exception" error:** the first draft of this page named Vidal as the single documented exception. The Bacharach file — 5-starred novel, author correspondence, dated discovery — was already in the wiki. The correction is dated 2026-09-12 and the page's structure changed to carry it: the concept is now *fiction admitted as documentary evidence*, not *fiction avoided with one curiosity*.
- **The Goldsworthy error:** the first draft called Goldsworthy "the only ancient-history author at 5 stars." Tom Holland's *Rubicon* is 5★ and ancient-history. Corrected 2026-09-12.
- **The Plutarch discrepancy:** the books page's author aggregation lists Plutarch among multi-5-star authors, but its detailed snapshot rates *Lives of the Noble Romans* 3 stars, while the roman-republic page rates both Plutarch titles 5. The sources disagree; this page does not adjudicate and makes no Plutarch rating claim.
- **The 97.8% figure** rests on the context-core [DOC] line and the Goodreads/FAVS exports it summarizes. The 2025 export (63 marked "read") and the 120-book masterlist are different snapshots of the same shelf at different times, not a recount.
- **Absence of evidence:** the "only two exceptions" framing is only as strong as the record's completeness. Fiction engagement that left no Goodreads trace, no text, no correspondence would not appear here. The behavior is established; the completeness is not sworn. A full-corpus sweep (2026-09-12) found no comics, no poetry reading, no Criterion, no canonical fiction authors (Dostoevsky through Stephen King all zero), no short stories, no bookstore culture, and no fiction-writing of his own — the nulls are comprehensive, but they are nulls in *this* corpus.
- **Orwell is slogan-level:** "1984 = 2012" appears as surveillance-era sloganeering with no evidence he has read the novel. Do not cite him as a fiction reader on that basis.
- **No book-writing intent:** the corpus lines about writing a book ("This is definitely one for my book!", "I should write a book") are Suz's, not Dan's. There is no evidence Dan intends to write a book of any kind. Do not attribute.
