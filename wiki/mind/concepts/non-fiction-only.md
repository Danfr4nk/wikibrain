# Non-Fiction Only

Dan consumes non-fiction almost exclusively. Goodreads runs ~97.8% nonfiction across 120 rated books [TESTIMONY — the context-core profile's dated (DOC) line, summarizing the Goodreads/FAVS exports]; the YouTube record is news, commentary, history, and video essays; the film canon is auteur-observational rather than genre fiction. This is not a preference stated once and forgotten. It is an identity-level trait with a behavioral paper trail across three media — and it has exactly two documented exceptions, both of which obey a deeper rule than the one they appear to break.

The rule is adult-life-specific. On 2007-07-21, eighteen-year-old Dan's Facebook status read "reading Harry Potter nonstop"; on 2011-07-16 he tweeted "Harry Potter 7.2 - goodbye, childhood" [PRIMARY — platform-timestamped]. Teen Dan read the defining fiction of his cohort straight through. The non-fiction rule is not a lifelong constitution but a developed position — the forensic stance hardening into a consumption law sometime in the 2010s, contemporaneous with the Ti-dominant profile's full expression.

The pattern holds at every scale the record can show. He does not read widely; he reads events — forty witnesses on one fall, twenty on another, one account per witness being all that exhaustive coverage requires. And the avoidance is as curated as the intake: what he doesn't watch is policed with the same energy as what he does.

## The books: subject-level monomania

The 120-book shelf is not broad taste. The corrected analysis reads it as **subject-level monomania through many witnesses**: 40 of 120 books tagged trump/jan-6 across 30 authors, 20 tagged roman-republic/ancient-history/caesar across 14 authors, the two sets non-overlapping; 85 of 98 authors appear exactly once [DERIVED — FAVS MASTERLIST CSV via the wiki's favorites analysis]. He does not read widely. He reads two events — the Trump presidency and the fall of the Roman Republic — through as many distinct witnesses as he can find, one account per witness being all that exhaustive coverage of a single event requires.

**Trump-era politics/journalism** (read dates 2022–2025, heavy 2024): Michael Wolff (5 books, 3 five-stars — the 2025 refresh completed all four Trump-era volumes plus *All or Nothing*), Bob Woodward (5 books, zero 5-stars — read thoroughly, not loved), Jonathan Karl (3 books, 2 five-stars). The Wolff–Woodward contrast is the shelf in miniature: Wolff gets the stars (novelistic, access-driven, scene-reconstructed); Woodward gets read cover to cover five times and starred at nothing (institutional, notes-and-tapes). What Dan rewards is not agreement — Woodward is the more careful reporter by any conventional measure — but narrative density and insider texture. The complete-series read is set-closure behavior: once Wolff becomes a witness, *all* of Wolff must be taken. Eighteen books carry the jan-6 tag specifically — the hinge the whole Trump collection orbits.

**Ancient history / Roman Republic** (detonation across a few months of 2024, nearly all rated 5): the standard modern Republic syllabus read back to back — Carlin's *Death Throes of the Republic* (5★, Jan 2024), Holland's *Rubicon* (5★, Apr 2024), Parenti's *Assassination of Julius Caesar* (Apr 2024), Goldsworthy's *Caesar: Life of a Colossus* (5★, Jul 2024); Goldsworthy accounts for four titles total. The Parenti placement matters: a Marxist people's-history of Caesar read alongside Goldsworthy's establishment biography is comparative method, not strongman admiration — the same event through hostile witnesses. (The Parenti star count itself is disputed between the wiki's own pages; see Conflicts.)

The great-man-theory tag runs under the whole ancient-history set and across it: Caesar, Alexander, and Oppenheimer — *American Prometheus* read Jul 2023 and 5-starred — are all singular-figure-versus-the-system stories, the profile's self-myth read back through the sources [DERIVED — shelves via roman-republic.md and the INTP page]. The subject is leadership-and-collapse, not heroics.

The Republic interest carries a **hard stop at Augustus**, and the boundary is precise. He read Goldsworthy's *Augustus* (4★, Jun 2024) — the transition figure, the fall's endpoint — and stopped. Nothing on the shelf continues into the Empire proper. The subject is the fall, not the thing that replaced it.

**How he reads** is part of the pattern. The modality is audio: *"I'm convinced that Scribd is far superior to Audible if you listen to a lot of audiobooks"*; *"Politics has been so boring that I've plowed through a cumulative 92 hours of history audiobooks over the last 2 months"* [PRIMARY — his messages]. Ninety-two hours in two months is the depositional habit at full throughput — and the read dates say the same thing: 53 of 79 dated reads land in 2024, against 16 in 2023 and 5 in 2025 [DERIVED — FAVS MASTERLIST via books.md]. The rating distribution tells its own story: 29 five-stars, 42 fours, 32 threes, 7 twos, 9 zeros — and exactly one 1-star in the corpus, Joe Trohman's Fall Out Boy memoir *None of This Rocks* (read Feb 2023) [DERIVED]. He pre-ordered Comey's *A Higher Loyalty* and chased it through Audible: the witness-acquisition instinct operating on release day. And the verse direction runs one way only: on 2015-12-03 he wrote an unprompted love poem in the small hours, but the corpus holds no poetry *reading* — no poets, no collections, no verse consumed. Poetry is output, never intake.

**The want-to-read list** (149 titles) runs the same obsessions — Rome, Trump-era politics, intelligence/conspiracy history — plus a distinct NYC-history thread (*The Power Broker*, *Broadway: A History of New York City in Thirteen Miles*, *Lost New York*). Its highest-rated title is *The Sinister Truth: Mk-Ultra* (4.75). The wishlist is the shelf's shadow: the same evidence room, unbuilt — and its top slot goes to an unsolved file.

## The two exceptions

**Gore Vidal's *Narratives of Empire*** — historical fiction, and an earlier fixation. Vidal operates as a cultural-literacy litmus test (the Cato bootloader's people-sorting filter: "Have they heard of DJ Shadow or read Gore Vidal?"); a 2025-05-05 groomsmen joke seated "the ghost of gore vidal" next to "a bag of cocaine" and "the first 3 fall out boy albums" — Vidal as a known constant, not a random name; a 2025-05-29 text registered someone else's Vidal set by shelf position ("all the way on the left of the book shelf") [PRIMARY]. The identification runs deeper than the books: Vidal sits in the corpus's heroic-figures list alongside Bernie Sanders, Hunter S. Thompson, Henry Rollins, John Brown, Fred Hampton, and Eugene Debs [DERIVED — gore-vidal.md] — the corpus's one identification figure: right about the empire for fifty years, changed nothing — the polemicist as Cassandra, which is Dan's own threat model in period dress ("competence correctly deployed, outcome still catastrophic"). The *Narratives* are polemics wearing novels' clothes, the way the Rome shelf is collapse-mechanics wearing history's clothes. Notably, the favorites/books record carries no Vidal entry at all: the distant exception lives on someone else's shelf, which is exactly the asymmetry the rule predicts [DERIVED — gore-vidal.md].

**Jacob Bacharach's *The Doorposts of Your House and on Your Gates*** (2017) — the stronger exception. A contemporary literary novel, not history-adjacent; Dan rated it 5 stars (shelved 2022-03-24). The discovery chain is dated to the hour [PRIMARY — jacob-bacharach.md]: 2021-02-06 13:39, the text to Suz — *"I was listening to a podcast and Jake Bacharach was the guest lol"* (Chapo Trap House episode 94, served by autoplay); 2021-02-11 22:01, Bacharach's reply forwarded to Suz in full, signed "-from Jacob Bacharach," with the episode link and *"You should listen to a few minutes if that"*; 2021-02-12 06:44, the same link to Tom with the discovery narrated. Bacharach is from Uniontown; his novel features Uniontown prominently, including a Virginia Avenue passage. Bacharach's reply confirms, unprompted in its first sentence, the teenage TV-commercial job for the family auto-parts store — the piece of the chain Dan ranks most improbable — and names *The Doorposts of Your House* as the Uniontown novel, which is what retires the five-year wrong-title drift [PRIMARY]. Dan then called it by the wrong title — *The Bend of the World* — for five years, a clean specimen of testimony decay in a high-salience memory, caught by the wiki's own correction pass in August 2026. On 2022-01-02 he recommended it to Joe Oshnack unprompted, framing the author as proof "not everyone from our hometown turned into a reactionary fascist-adjacent class traitor" — the exception gets the canon's treatment. The attention is measurable: 26 Bacharach searches across 2021–2023; two Bacharach videos watched two minutes apart at 1 AM on 2022-11-10 (a Hill clip, then the Chapo episode) — a search signature, not autoplay; and an interactive HTML artifact Dan built out of the whole chain [DERIVED/TESTIMONY — jacob-bacharach.md]. The Uniontown Glitch is the forensic method's flagship demonstration: a low-prior anomaly flagged, four confident model explanations, all four killed against ground truth only Dan holds [DERIVED — forensic-method edge].

The two exceptions share a structure, and it is the concept's real shape: **fiction is admitted when it functions as documentary evidence about a tracked subject.** Vidal's novels are evidence about empire and decline — the same subject as the Rome shelf. Bacharach's novel is evidence about Uniontown — the most tracked subject in the corpus. Neither exception is fiction-as-fiction. The rule survives its exceptions because the exceptions were never about fiction at all. And the asymmetry is exact: Vidal is the *distant* exception (someone else's shelf, an identification figure, fiction at arm's length); Bacharach is the *intimate* one (his own shelf, his own five stars, his own street in the text). The evidence room admits fiction in proportion to how much of Dan's world the fiction contains.

## Why: the forensic stance

The mechanism is the Ti-forensic stance ([[wiki/mind/concepts/forensic-method]]): reality is a high-fidelity system and the job is to find where it breaks. Fiction is low-information input for that engine — an invented system carries no ground truth to audit against. The evidence-room metaphor is exact: forty witnesses on one event, the hostile witness shelved beside the friendly one (Parenti next to Goldsworthy), the five-starred novelist's Uniontown set beside the depositions. This is case-building — the same case-building as the J6 work, the stylometric self-surveillance, the corpus forensics. Fiction cannot be filed because there is no case it belongs to — unless, as the two exceptions show, the novel *is* the case file.

The lyric qualifier runs parallel, and it is byte-exact, not a vibe: sung words register as timbre, not semantics — *"I hear the lyric as timbre"* — and exactly three songs in his life have ever parsed as a conceptual message; even with a lyric sheet in hand, interpreting them gets harder, not easier, so the bottleneck is processing, not access; Spotify's speechiness metric reads to him as voice-as-instrument density, never wordiness [PRIMARY — the two first-person captures]. Narrative carried in an aesthetic medium does not land as information ([[wiki/mind/concepts/lyric-qualifier]]). The explicit-over-inferred architecture converges too ([[wiki/mind/concepts/explicit-verbal-commitment]]): non-fiction is explicit claim-staking — a witness asserting *this happened*, checkable, falsifiable — while fiction asks the reader to infer meaning from invented signal, the exact operation his neurology discounts. Three of the wiki's passes now point at one mechanism from three directions: timbre-first, narrative-last, stated-over-inferred. The behavior is attested three times over; the unity of the mechanism is the wiki's model, held as such.

## The shape of the negative

A full-corpus sweep for the fiction Dan *doesn't* consume returned comprehensive nulls [DERIVED — 2026-09-12 sweep]: no comics, no graphic novels, no manga; no poetry reading (the one poem is his own); no Criterion, no film-theory canon; no Dostoevsky, Hemingway, Fitzgerald, Steinbeck, Murakami, Vonnegut, Pynchon, Cormac McCarthy, Stephen King, Tolkien, or Infinite Jest; no short stories; no bookstore culture, no library habit. Orwell's *1984* circulates as slogan ("1984 = 2012") with no evidence of reading. This is not someone who tried fiction and bounced off it. It is someone for whom the category barely exists as a consumption option. The nulls rule out the easy alternatives: not snobbery (he evangelizes pop-punk), not anti-intellectualism (the shelf is 120 deep), not inattention (he tracks his consumption forensically). The avoidance is structural — downstream of the perceptual mechanism, the way a person with no sweet tooth doesn't *resist* dessert.

## An open hypothesis: the Rome binge as unacknowledged self-forensics [SPECULATION]

The totality invites a reading the old page never attempted. The Rome detonation lands in the **first half of 2024** — the Annie decade's terminal phase, the year before the 2025 collapse (NYC exit, her move to her parents, the February–April 2025 rupture sequence). A man watching his own republic fall reads about a republic falling. The hard stop at Augustus is the thesis statement: the interesting part is the breaking, not the empire that follows. The Parenti-next-to-Goldsworthy comparative method is the same faculty he turns on his own life — hostile witnesses, cross-examined.

Evidence for: the timing (H1 2024 against the terminal phase); the subject (collapse mechanics, not heroics — the tags read `great-man-theory`, `caesar`, `top-shelf`, i.e. leadership-and-collapse material); the hard stop (the fall, not the aftermath); the precedent that his intake tracks his life (the 2022–23 Hasan corrections track his political self-auditing).
Evidence against: he never said this — the claim is structural, not attested; the Trump shelf (the larger obsession) has no such personal mapping and is better explained by pure depositional instinct; correlation of timing is not mechanism. The speculation is labeled and stays labeled: the binge *could* be self-forensics, and the shape fits, but the record does not say so.

A smaller, firmer finding: the want-to-read list's top slot — *The Sinister Truth: Mk-Ultra* (4.75) — shows the depositional instinct pointed at an unsolved file. The evidence room keeps a cold-case shelf.

## Assessment

The record supports the concept at major weight: a decades-documented, three-media intake pattern with dated primary exemplars, a full correction history, and a mechanism attested independently on three wiki passes. The operative judgment this page carries is behavioral, not interior: non-fiction-only is the intake side of the forensic method — sets close around events through unique witnesses, and the fiction category never clears the explicit-over-inferred gate. The Rome-as-self-forensics reading stays hypothesis-grade: structural fit without attestation, labeled as such.
