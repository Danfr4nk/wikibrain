# Non-Fiction Only

Dan consumes non-fiction almost exclusively. Goodreads runs ~97.8% nonfiction across 120 rated books; the YouTube record is news, commentary, history, and video essays; the film canon is auteur-observational rather than genre fiction. This is not a preference stated once and forgotten. It is an identity-level trait with a behavioral paper trail across three media, and it has exactly one documented exception.

## The books: subject-level monomania

The 120-book shelf is not broad taste. The wiki's corrected analysis reads it as **subject-level monomania through many witnesses**: 40 of 120 books tagged trump/jan-6 across 30 authors, 20 tagged roman-republic/ancient-history/caesar across 14 authors, non-overlapping. He does not read widely. He reads two events — the Trump presidency and the fall of the Roman Republic — through as many distinct witnesses as he can find.

**Trump-era politics/journalism** (read dates 2022–2025, heavy 2024): Michael Wolff (5 books, 3 five-stars — all five Trump volumes including *All or Nothing*, May 2025), Bob Woodward (5 books, zero 5-stars — read thoroughly, not loved), Jonathan Karl (3). Tags: trump 40, politics 79, journalism 31, jan-6 18.

**Ancient history / Roman Republic** (full-immersion 2024): Adrian Goldsworthy (4 books — the only ancient-history author at 5 stars; the Caesar biography is 5★), Tom Holland's *Rubicon* (5★), plus a "great man" biography shelf: *American Prometheus* (5★), Plutarch, Barry Strauss, and Parenti's Marxist *Assassination of Julius Caesar* read alongside Goldsworthy — comparative, not strongman admiration. Twenty books tagged ancient-history/roman-republic. The Republic interest carries a **hard stop at Augustus**: the fall is the subject, not the Empire.

**War/history shelf:** Dan Carlin's Hardcore History (5★), Jacobsen's *Nuclear War* (5★), Shirer's *Rise and Fall of the Third Reich* (5★). Eighteen tagged war, 69 history. The 149-title Goodreads want-to-read list runs the same obsessions (Rome, Trump-era politics, intelligence/conspiracy history) plus a distinct NYC-history thread; its highest-rated title is *The Sinister Truth: Mk-Ultra*.

## The one exception

Gore Vidal's *Narratives of Empire* is historical fiction — novels — and it was an earlier fixation. On 2025-05-29 he texted about a visit: "OMG he's got the gore vidal historical fiction series," noting the books sat "all the way on the left of the book shelf." The exception is load-bearing in the other direction: the only fiction he is documented engaging is fiction *about history*, shelved at the margin, exclaimed over as a curiosity. The rule survives its exception because the exception is history wearing a novel's clothes.

## The film canon: the rule applied to movies

Eleven titles, treated as an evangelized canon — he assigns them to partners as compatibility tests: *Parasite*, *The Prestige*, *Pulp Fiction*, *The Witch*, *The Shining*, *The King of Comedy*, *Taxi Driver*, *There Will Be Blood*, *The Graduate*, *Eyes Wide Shut*, *Kill Bill*. Three gravity wells: **Kubrick** (completist — "only one Kubrick movie I haven't seen"), **Scorsese/De Niro** (*Taxi Driver*, *King of Comedy* — read as self-portraits), **Tarantino**. *Eyes Wide Shut* is the load-bearing title: completion target 2017, the lens on his 2019 arrangement, the household's "favorite Christmas movie" ritual by 2023.

The wiki's own caveat stands: four titles — *Pulp Fiction*, *Kill Bill*, *The Graduate*, *Parasite* — sit on the list on a reading rather than on evidence. The canon is closed and auteur-shaped either way: he completes directors, not genres.

## Why: the forensic stance

The mechanism is the Ti-forensic stance documented at [[wiki/mind/concepts/forensic-method]]: reality is a high-fidelity system and the job is to find where it breaks. Fiction is low-information input for that engine. An invented system carries no ground truth to audit against — there is nothing to check, no witness to cross-examine, no docket to pull. The non-fiction rule is not snobbery about novels. It is the same reason the history shelf runs 69 deep and the want-to-read list tops out at Mk-Ultra: he is stocking an evidence room, not a library.

The lyric qualifier runs parallel: he hears sung lyrics as timbre, not language — narrative carried in an aesthetic medium does not land as information for him. Prose fiction may fail the same way, though that is inference from the musical finding, not a separate attestation. What is attested is the behavior: ~97.8% nonfiction, two subjects, many witnesses, one history-adjacent exception.
