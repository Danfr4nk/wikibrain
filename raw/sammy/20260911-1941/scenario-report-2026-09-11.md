# The Scenario Report

*What 121 rated fantasies say about what you want. Based on the scenario-ratings v1 export, 2026-09-11.*

## How to read this

You rated 121 out of 122 scenarios on a 1–10 gut scale and never touched the veto button. Your average rating was 8.36, and nearly two-thirds of everything landed at 9 or 10. That's not a flaw in the instrument — it means the scenario list was built from your actual fantasy space, so almost everything in it is something you're already into. The interesting stuff isn't what scored 10. It's what *didn't*, and what moved a score up or down when only one detail changed.

Each scenario came in a family: a base version plus four or five variants where a single detail changed. Those single-detail changes are the cleanest evidence in the whole dataset, because everything else is held constant. This report leans on them hardest.

## The one thing you don't want

There is exactly one rejected act in all 121 ratings: being pegged while another guy watches. Every version of it — filmed, unfilmed, just the two of you, with you in charge — scored 3 or 4. Nothing moved it. Filming didn't move it. Removing the watcher didn't move it. Putting you in charge moved it one point, from 3 to 4.

It's also the only scenario family averaging below 5 (4.00), sitting more than two standard deviations under your overall mean. You didn't use the veto button, but this cluster *is* your veto — you just expressed it with 3s instead.

Here's the twist, and it's the single biggest mover in the entire dataset: when the variant changed to "the guy joins in on you," that same scenario jumped from 3 to 7. Four points — the largest positive swing anywhere. So the boundary is specific to the act, not to the configuration around it. Even inside the thing you don't want, the other man's attention landing on *you* is the lever that moves the needle. That's a pattern that repeats everywhere else, which brings us to—

## The engine: being wanted, clearly

Across every scenario family, the hottest version is always the one where it's unambiguous who the other man wants. "The other guy is into you" — 10. "One of them is only into you" — 10. In the scenario where your friend asks to join you and her, the base version sits at a lukewarm 6 — but the variant "he is only into her" jumps it to 10. And at the metric level, "he pursues you" (8.88) beats "mild interest" (8.38) beats "he experiments" (7.78), cleanly and monotonically, with all three levels well-covered.

The flip side: ambiguity is the killer. "He experiments" is the lowest-rated pursuit level. The lukewarm 6 on the friend scenario is a scenario where nobody's target is clear. The rule that unifies all of it: **it doesn't matter much *who* the other man is into — it matters that it's clear.** Clarity in either direction amplifies; ambiguity moderates. That's a falsifiable claim about you, and the data supports it in four independent scenario families.

## Closeness is non-negotiable

Every variant that put distance between you and the action tanked the score. "You wait in the bar" while she gets picked up: 9 → 5. "You are in the next room" while she's with her ex on the phone: 8 → 5. "She calls you right after" instead of during: 8 → 3 — one of the biggest drops in the set. The whole phone scenario family ("she is with her ex while you listen on the phone") averages 6.80, the lowest of any family except pegging.

The positive versions of the same instinct: "you are involved — shared blowjob with him" while watching her with her ex is a 10. "She joins in halfway" while she watches you with another guy is a 10. You don't want to be adjacent to it. You want to be in it.

## The camera is a multiplier, not a spark

Filmed scenarios average 9.05 against 8.21 for unfilmed — a real gap. "She holds the camera," "you film your half," "you are filming the encounter" are all 10s. And removing the camera from the 2019-shaped scenario drops it from 10 to 5, one of the steepest falls in the data. The camera matters enormously *to scenarios that are already hot*.

But "she films it" on the pegging scenario stays at 3. The camera multiplies heat; it doesn't create it. If the scenario is cold, filming changes nothing.

## She runs it, you're pursued, you're not the center

The three highest-rated scenario families tell a coherent story about roles:

1. "She watches you get a blowjob from another guy" — 9.80, the top of the board. Every variant is a 9 or 10: he's her ex (10), she holds the camera (10), it happens monthly (10).
2. "House party: you, her, and two guys, open room" — 9.60.
3. "She sets you up with a guy as a gift" — 9.60.

She's the one orchestrating or watching in all three. At the metric level, "she directs" (8.48) tops authorship, "he pursues you" tops pursuit direction — and "you're the focus" is the *lowest* spotlight level (7.95), below shared (8.52) and peripheral (8.57). The variant "you are the focus instead" drops a threesome scenario from 9 to 6. You don't want to be the center of attention. You want to be the one things happen to, arranged by her, with the other man's attention on you and the clarity of everyone's target out in the open.

## The setting rules

A few consistent environmental preferences, each confirmed by both metric levels and single-detail deltas:

- **Neutral ground wins.** "Neutral" turf averages 8.88, above "yours" (7.87) and "hers" (7.30). It's peaked in the middle — not home advantage, *neutral* advantage. "At his place" drops a 10 to a 6; "at their place" drops a 9 to a 5.
- **Strangers yes, friends no.** Stranger (8.43) and acquaintance (8.75) are fine; "friend" drops to 7.30, and the variant "he is a friend of yours" craters a 9 to a 4. His enthusiasm matters too — "reluctant" (6.22) vs "willing" (8.53).
- **Semi-private is the sweet spot, actual exposure is not.** "Semi-private" (9.00) beats "private" (8.27) — the booth at the party, the thrill of the setting. But "word could get out in your circle" drops a 6 to a 4, and "could get out" visibility (7.83) trails "fully private" (8.37). The charge is in the *framing* of semi-privacy, not in real risk.
- **Talked about beats silent.** "Explicit talk" (9.25) tops explicitness, and "no talking, unspoken the whole time" drops a 10 to a 6.
- **Intense beats slow, ongoing beats one-off.** "Intense" (9.43) over "moderate" (8.37) over "gentle" (7.73); "slow, no rush, an evening" drops a 9 to a 4. "Ongoing" duration (9.29) beats "an evening" (8.74) beats "brief scene" (8.10), and "it happens again monthly" is a 10.
- **Open-ended beats bounded.** "Open-ended" contact (9.25) over "includes oral" (8.54) over "touch only" (7.30); "soft swap only, no full sex" drops a 10 to a 7.

Two mild notes: "detached" emotional tone (8.78) edges out "intimate" (8.55) and "playful" (8.27), though the detached sample is small. And "full party" context (8.73) slightly beats "drinking" (8.49) and "sober" (8.16).

## What the data can't tell you

A few honest limits, because the numbers come with traps:

- **Two metrics were rigged by design.** Her attractiveness and taboo charge were held nearly constant across the instrument (she's always highly attractive, the crossing is always mutual), so their flatness means nothing about your preferences. They were control variables.
- **Some levels rest on one item.** "He is older and experienced" dropping a 9 to a 3 is real *within that scenario*, but "older" appears exactly once in the dataset — don't generalize it to a rule about age. Same for "younger" (one item, rated 10), "routine" novelty, and a few others.
- **One metric is a mirage.** "Reciprocity" looks like it has a huge spread (you-give 9.00 vs you-receive 5.17), but the "receive" level is four pegging items plus two peripheral-threesome items that both scored 9. The average is meaningless — the act-level story in the first section is the real one.
- **This is stated preference, one sitting, n=1.** It predicts what you rate, not what you'd do. Treat the predictions below as the test.

## The predictions

If this profile is right, the perfect 10 for you is: her directing or watching, the other man's interest in you unambiguous, filmed, on neutral turf, intense and open-ended, with a stranger or acquaintance, talked about rather than silent, and part of something ongoing. Change any one of the load-bearing details — make his target ambiguous, remove the camera, move it to his place, or put distance between you and the action — and it drops three or more points.

Cheapest test: take the foursome family (currently 9.50, three 10s) and run "at his place." The model says it lands at 7 or below. If it holds at 9+, the turf rule is wrong and gets rewritten.
