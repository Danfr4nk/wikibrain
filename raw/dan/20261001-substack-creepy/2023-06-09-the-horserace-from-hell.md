Less than an hour ago, the frontrunner for the Republican nomination announced (via social media post) that he will be indicted by the federal government in a Miami courthouse early next week. Of course, this is hardly the extent of his legal issues..he already faces felony charges in NY state and is likely to face indictment in TWO MORE cases in DC and Georgia. Let me reiterate that Donald Trump leads the pack of declared candidates by +30 percentage points - a decisive margin of advantage.

The 2024 election is going to absolutely, unequivocally batshit insane. It will be unlike anything in American history - even if Trump is unable to capture the Republican nomination. 

Over the last week, as rumors and speculation about these new charges emerged, a new batch of Republican candidates have officially filed their FEC paperwork and launched their campaigns. Trump’s worsening legal woes have given rise to a new theory of viability to GOP strategists advising long-shot candidates: Trump is increasingly vulnerable and there may yet be hope for anyone savvy enough to enter the fight early. The ‘one on one’ primary might actually become a horserace. 

Single digit pollers (who had been watching & waiting on the sidelines) like Mike Pence and Chris Christie are now in full overdrive mode as they rush to develop campaign infrastructure and fundraising necessary to build the ground game resources in places like Iowa and New Hampshire where an early win might reshape the race. 

So - the real question about this race has become clear - will the broad swaths of GOP voters rally behind Trump or prefer a candidate who isn’t facing time in federal prison? Similarly, what approach do the Republican candidates take to combat Trump?

We got a preview of how this may play out last night during Mike Pence’s CNN town hall. Asked by Dana Bash about Trump’s legal jeopardy and whether or not his “nobody is above the law” stance was hypocritically voided when applied to the former President, Pence demurred. He avoided any critical accountability for Trump and refused to answer whether he would consider a pardon for him. Basically - “no comment”.

While this may be a winning general election strategy to avoid ostracizing MAGA voters, it’s going to be interesting to see how it works out during the primary (particularly during the debates.) Donald Trump is going to seize on any opportunity he is given to condemn Republicans who fail to vocally and enthusiastically proclaim his innocence. The disingenuous nature of Pence’s triangulating non-answer will become painfully obvious and will open the floodgates for public scorn from Trump loyalists. 

If we’ve learned one thing about Trump, it’s that he plays politics as a zero sum game. One must either challenge him outright or entirely get behind his narrative of a deep-state conspiracy to ruin him. Hedging or line-riding isn’t viable.

It’s hard to overstate just how unprecedented this territory is going to be. Tonight’s developments are one in a series of circumstances that will complicate the 2024 election in ways we would have once thought unimaginable.
