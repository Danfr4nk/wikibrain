Election 2024 has OFFICIALLY begun. 

And while it’s still too early to look at polls with any reasonable amount of confidence in their individual results…it’s worth the effort to start following now as the trends begin to emerge. 

Getting up to speed: 

  * RealClearPolitics has tracked the results of 88 general election polls from reputable polling agencies beginning in August 2022. Based on the entirety of these results, Trump current leads Biden by a margin of +1.4

RCP has tracked the results of 97 GOP primary polls beginning in November 2021. Based on the entirety of these results Donald Trump overwhelmingly leads the race with 53.9% support, a +32.6 point lead over the 2nd place finisher Ron DeSantis with 21.3%

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!snFb!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F43db01aa-73f3-4305-95f3-7be8560a878a_640x256.jpeg)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!snFb!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F43db01aa-73f3-4305-95f3-7be8560a878a_640x256.jpeg)

  * FiveThirtyEight aggregate results show a similar spread between GOP candidates sampling around 40 results from pollsters rated C+ or higher. Trump leads by +33.3 points with 54.0% support over DeSantis at 20.7%

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!PjzT!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F88f4a1e6-af2e-4e09-9461-2538fdc26b5c_319x640.jpeg)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!PjzT!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F88f4a1e6-af2e-4e09-9461-2538fdc26b5c_319x640.jpeg)




Betting markets also corroborate this substantial gap between Trump and DeSantis. PredictIt markets currently list Trump ‘yes’ shares at 58¢ and DeSantis ‘yes’ shares at 32¢ - a +26 point spread.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!YzV8!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fc92469ca-b508-4923-bb32-02ecec3ea266_599x640.jpeg)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!YzV8!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fc92469ca-b508-4923-bb32-02ecec3ea266_599x640.jpeg)

  * PredictIt markets for the General Election list ‘yes’ shares for Biden at 45¢ / Trump at 32¢ / DeSantis at 22¢. _Note: due to the way these markets function, it is difficult to gauge general election results at this stage. After a nominee is chosen, capital from shares of inactive candidates will be reallocated to the nominee, reflecting the state of the race._

Now let’s dig in to some of this week’s newly released results - starting with Quinnipiac’s 5/16-5/22 weekly survey. 

> _In a hypothetical general election matchup for president, the race is neck and neck, with President Joe Biden receiving 48 percent and Trump receiving 46 percent among all registered voters. These results are unchanged since late March._
> 
> _When Biden is matched up against DeSantis, the race is also very close, with DeSantis receiving 47 percent and Biden receiving 46 percent among all registered voters. These results are essentially unchanged since late March._

There’s no clear picture to be painted from these polls just yet, other than to say that somehow Donald Trump doesn’t seem to have shed as much support as many people had thought. 

With a new challenger in Ron DeSantis and the murmurs of special counsel Jack Smith’s investigation into the Mar-A-Lago document scandal…we’ll see if Republican voters can hold on through historic amounts of incoming.
