# Trump Was Always Going To Win

*It’s the down-ballot destruction that is so shocking and terrifying.*

Published: 2024-11-12T03:38:49.648Z
Canonical URL: https://creepy.substack.com/p/trump-redux
Author: dan frank
Publication: Committee to Re-elect the President

---

Over two years ago, I concluded that a Trump victory in 2024 had become the most likely outcome of the coming election. At the time, media focus had spent months fixated on the (yet unannounced) DeSantis candidacy and its favorable position to overcome Donald Trump in the primary races. From Fox to MSNBC, DeSantis was cast as a rising force in GOP, most pundits expecting him to win the nomination by simply running a halfway competent campaign. Rupert Murdoch had pushed Fox News to champion the Florida governor, directing anchors to praise DeSantis and ignore (without antagonizing) Trump. The former President was already facing four separate criminal cases and had not yet fully shaken the fallout from January 6, which forced him to carefully navigate public opinion on the issue. The prevailing assumption was that Trump had become too toxic, while DeSantis offered Trump’s culture-focused politics in a more competent, reliable, and donor-friendly package.

But watching all of this play out, I became convinced of just how little the press truly understands Trump as a politician—or the movement he commands. It quickly became [obvious](https://creepy.substack.com/p/the-desantis-delusion) that nothing short of a miracle would prevent Trump from winning the nomination.

The GOP primary, in essence, had been a fait accompli. Trump, the party’s dominant force and the ultimate powerbroker, had backed the GOP into a corner. Even if moderate Republicans were exhausted by him, or his legal troubles turned out to be politically ruinous, he retained the fierce loyalty of at least 35% of the party’s base. And he would undoubtedly sabotage the race of any Republican who crossed him. Rejecting Trump meant surrendering a third of the GOP’s most dedicated voters in a country where elections are often won by razor-thin margins in a few swing states. The donor class and GOP leaders like Mitch McConnell might want to move on from Trump, but to do so would be to forfeit the election. Barring a voluntary exit, Trump held absolute control of the party’s destiny—and he has a long history of taking petty vengeance on anyone who defies him.

While the primary was simple math, the general election presented more complex variables.

To really understand the environment, we must rewind to the 2020 Democratic primaries for context. Here’s a recap:

\- **20 Democrats** entered the race with Biden as the perceived front-runner.

\- **Bernie Sanders** gained momentum and eventually led the polls.

\- **Biden stumbled** in early contests; Sanders took the lead, with Pete Buttigieg close behind.

\- Democratic leadership panicked as Super Tuesday neared. **Obama** coordinated a strategic dropout of moderates who then endorsed Biden, while James Clyburn broke tradition to endorse him in South Carolina, giving Biden a critical boost. Liz Warren split the progressive vote.

\- **COVID** hit, and the stakes for 2020 shifted from critical to existential. Biden, now the consensus moderate, became seen as the “safe” choice, while Sanders’ agenda was framed as utopian and unelectable.

\- **Biden** consolidated support through this triangulated strategy and ran a pandemic-era “no-show” campaign, barely defeating a president who had bungled the pandemic response and stoked racial animus in a culturally charged moment. Expanded mail-in voting helped him win by a slim margin.

But none of this provided me enough reason to believe Trump would easily win in 2024. The odds started to tilt in Trump’s favor as Biden’s vulnerabilities became more glaring. Republicans took the House in the midterms, stalling Biden’s legislative agenda. Inflation and immigration became major liabilities. Biden’s age compounded these issues; what was once chalked up as “Biden gaffes” now looked more like serious cognitive decline. A picture began to emerge of a president literally incapable of campaigning.

From there, the election narrative became surreal: a showdown between two of the least appealing and capable candidates possible.

Trump’s criminal cases, rather than hobbling him, paradoxically strengthened his base. Each indictment took on the appearance of “lawfare,” with Trump’s supporters viewing the cases as politically motivated persecution. Ironically, the weakest case—the Stormy Daniels hush-money trial—was the only one to reach trial before the election, solidifying MAGA’s belief in a DOJ targeting Trump for purely political reasons. For the MAGA base, Trump’s victimhood was now part of the campaign’s appeal, despite the mountain of evidence behind each case.

With every development, my confidence grew that a second Trump term was imminent.

The most shocking aspect? The down-ballot success of the GOP. Trump, a candidate known for his lack of impulse control and minimal grasp of history or governance, now presides over a Republican-controlled Congress filled with loyalists. The few Republicans willing to oppose him are gone. Paul Ryan has exited; McConnell is no longer speaker. The GOP is now Trump’s party, entirely under his control, with a judiciary he helped shape. He is out for blood and isn’t interested in appeasing the establishment wing of his party. 

Most alarming, Trump now holds a popular vote victory and the mandate that comes with it. He can claim to be enacting the will of the American voter, and in a sense, he would be right.

Meanwhile, the Democratic Party lies shattered, devoid of credibility or a foundation for “resistance.” The hybrid Harris campaign is widely regarded as an embarrassment, and infighting has taken hold. Moderates argue that she was too “woke,” alienating Hispanic and blue-collar voters. Progressives counter that her lukewarm stances on issues like Palestine and immigration turned the Democrats into “Republican-lite,” alienating the base and disincentivizing young voters.

The Democrats are a party in shambles, while Trump has a clear path to implement his agenda.

This may become an outlet for tracking what happens next. It will be interesting, if nothing else, to watch what unfolds when we hand the reins to someone who is, by any reasonable standard, unqualified—and let him off the leash.

Brace yourself for… something.
