In the modern landscape of American politics, predicting political outcomes is an exercise almost entirely reserved for upstart grifters hoping to get lucky on a shot-in-the-dark guess to jumpstart their pundit career. Indeed, as politics descend further down the rabbit hole into complete absurdity, it becomes less reasonable to criticize or hold accountable someone for being wrong about what will happen next.

On the complete other end of the spectrum, an elite club of political clairvoyants have emerged - people like Michael Moore, Helmut Norpoth, Bob Woodward and Michael Wolff who saw a growing populist fervor as a serious electoral threat to the arrogant and disconnected campaign being run by Hillary Clinton and went on-record with their warning. They were met with derisive laughter from CNN hosts and in-studio audiences…until they weren’t, and those same clips re-emerged as a symbol of the exact coastal-liberal dismissive smugness that fueled Trump voters in the first place. Most famous of these moments was Maggie Haberman’s reaction to Keith Ellison’s message “we better be ready for Donald Trump to become the nominee” which became the symbol of the newly victorious MAGA movement

 _For the sake of accuracy, I’m excluding extreme partisans like Ann Coulter and Steve Bannon who had a vested partisan interest in making their questionably sincere pro-Trump predictions._

There was one 2016 predictor who stood out even among those who took Trump seriously and said unequivocally in a Washington Post column that Trump would defeat Clinton…and he could prove it. American University professor Alan Lichtman is famous for one thing - he created a system for forecasting the outcome of presidential elections. His system is called “The 13 Keys to the White House” and has been used to correctly predict every election since 1984. And yes, that includes correctly predicting that Al Gore would win the 2000 election…which, of course, he did. 

Lichtman’s perfect record (10/10) has made him _the_ premiere soothsayer in political media, but his 2016 forecast may have built more creditibility and notoriety than all of the others combined. Having already established a reputation from three decades of successful predictions, the response to his WP column was a combination of shock and confusion - it’s difficult to overstate just how far fetched this outcome felt at that time. Don’t forget - it would be another month before the Access Hollywood tape would emerge and further solidify Clinton’s victory as a certainty.

So what are the “13 Keys”?

And what do they tell us about 2024?

First, it’s important to understand that the ‘keys’ in Lichtman’s system don’t examine individual candidates, but the incumbent political party. The keys themselves are value indicators which are pass/fail. If the party currently holding the White House fails 5 or less of the keys, they will win election and retain executive power. Traditional indicators like fundraising totals and polling/approval results are completely absent from this determination. 

Let’s look at the keys:

>   1. Midterm gains: After the midterm elections, the incumbent party holds more seats in the [U.S. House of Representatives](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives) than after the previous [midterm elections](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_midterm_election).
> 
>   2. No primary contest: There is no serious contest for the incumbent party nomination.
> 
>   3. Incumbent seeking re-election: The incumbent party candidate is the sitting president.
> 
>   4. No third party: There is no significant third party or independent campaign.
> 
>   5. Strong short-term economy: The economy is not in recession during the election campaign.
> 
>   6. Strong long-term economy: Real per capita economic growth during the term equals or exceeds mean growth during the previous two terms.
> 
>   7. Major policy change: The incumbent administration effects major changes in national policy.
> 
>   8. No social unrest: There is no sustained social unrest during the term.
> 
>   9. No scandal: The incumbent administration is untainted by major scandal.
> 
>   10. No foreign/military failure: The incumbent administration suffers no major failure in foreign or military affairs.
> 
>   11. Major foreign/military success: The incumbent administration achieves a major success in foreign or military affairs.
> 
>   12. Charismatic incumbent: The incumbent party candidate is charismatic or a national hero.
> 
>   13. Uncharismatic challenger: The challenging party candidate is not charismatic or a national hero.
> 
> 


So, a year from now we can apply these statements to Joe Biden’s administration and if 6 or more answers are ‘false’, he will be predicted to lose. 

On its face, it feels like a generic and obtuse way to view an election. The temptation to use more superficial considerations like personality and ‘electability’ or subjective qualities like cultural momentum as our gauge of how these contests will resolve is understandable…particularly in the age of Trump. But it’s hard to dismiss the successful record of Lichtman’s ‘keys’, which predicted Trump’s victory against a sea of objection and doubt.

Several outlets have already talked to Alan Lichtman about how the “13 keys” will apply in 2024 and while he has stipulated that it’s too early for the official prediction to be made - the keys show that the current climate will favor Biden. 

Democrats shouldn’t take too much comfort from this, a lot can happen in a year and the ‘13 keys’ are unbiased and clinical in their application. They are a testament to how distracted and oblivious the American electorate is about what drives our electoral contests.
