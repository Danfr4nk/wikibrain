## PART FOUR: THE INTERPERSONAL DYNAMIC — DIAGNOSIS

### 4.1 The Exchange Economy

This relationship operates on an exchange economy that neither party has ever made explicit and both parties have occasionally weaponized. The economy includes: cocaine and marijuana (bidirectional, with John historically the more reliable supplier); cash (John borrows from her, Suzanne accepts loans from him, roughly balanced if not precisely tracked); food (John cooks for her, she brings him things); logistics (he helps with her car, house, practical problems; she runs errands for him when he lacks transportation or a driver's license); emotional labor (Suzanne provides it consistently; John provides it in crises); sex (present and acknowledged in the fight, historically embedded in the relationship).

The December fight is an explosion of the suppressed economy. Both parties believe they have been giving more than the other. John invokes the material ledger. Suzanne invokes the sexual one. Both revelations are accurate, and both are deployed as weapons rather than as honest assessments. The fight doesn't resolve the economy — it just makes it briefly visible.

The ongoing pattern after reconciliation is that the economy continues, and both parties resume pretending it isn't there. The drug transactions continue (multiple references to ordering, picking up, splitting through 2025 into 2026). The cash flow continues. The sex presumably continues — there is no evidence of a stated ending to that dimension, and several messages in the 2025-2026 period carry the specific tone of two people who remain physically familiar (the "ass to mouth" joke, the crude sexual banter of March 17-18 2025, the "come up here and hold this meat" message of January 22 2026).

### 4.2 The Core Wound

Suzanne told John what the core wound is: her father's rejection. She didn't explain it extensively — just one sentence, during reconciliation, while explaining why his blocking behavior destroyed her. *"It's what my Dad did and I doubt I'll ever get over it."*

John's behavior pattern — present in crisis, absent in maintenance, explosive in perceived disrespect, punitive in departure — maps precisely onto what someone with an anxious attachment wound from paternal rejection would be most vulnerable to. He is intermittently available and intermittently withdrawing in exactly the pattern that keeps someone in an anxious cycle. Not deliberately. He is not doing this strategically. He is just being himself. But the effect on Suzanne is to activate and perpetuate the wound she described.

Her self-portrait identifies her "receiving gap" — her difficulty accepting care, her tendency to give without allowing return, her quiet need to be needed. John is the optimal vessel for this pattern too: he accepts without asking, he needs things she can provide, and his crises give her the experience of mattering without the discomfort of being the recipient. When John is in the driveway at midnight, she is needed. When John needs $80 for gas, she can provide it. When John is in the hospital after a rib injury, she can be useful. The giving is effortless and the receiving of his gratitude doesn't ask her to be vulnerable in the way that receiving sustained attention and care would.

They are, in this specific sense, terrible for each other in ways that are also the source of their attachment.

### 4.3 What the Relationship Has Provided

For Suzanne: physical intimacy, practical competence in her daily life, the emotional experience of being needed by someone who isn't a client or professional contact, a person who knows her actual life rather than her professional presentation, someone she can be herself with (the bawdy humor, the late nights, the shared substance use), and a form of love that does not require her to perform.

For John: the most reliable source of warmth and non-judgmental presence in his life. Someone who will pick him up from the hospital, find his card, run errands, wait for him, let him in at midnight, stay connected through a fight and an 8-month silence, and still ask how he's doing. Someone who corrects his English without malice and calls him on his bullshit and doesn't leave. A person who, as he texted himself, he "needed her here babbling on all day" — the experience of being accompanied through a day without having to perform or manage.

### 4.4 The Resolution

The resolution of this relationship is not available in the data. As of March 26, 2026, John is texting Suzanne about a paint job he's on, she is presumably responding, and the dynamic continues. The question is not whether the relationship will end but whether it will ever be honestly named.

The honest naming would require both of them to acknowledge what the December 2023 fight made visible: that this is a long-running intimate relationship between two people who care about each other significantly, who share a life at a level of proximity that most "friends" don't, who have hurt each other badly, who have come back from it, and who have never decided what that means or what either of them actually wants from it.

John will not initiate that conversation. The anxiety about "I need to talk to you" type messages is documented and genuine. His response mode for emotional complexity is to physically show up or physically disappear — not to sit in the uncomfortable middle space of a direct conversation about the relationship.

Suzanne could initiate it. She has the vocabulary (her self-portrait makes this clear). She has the courage — she told him about her father, unprompted, during a difficult moment. She has tried to reach the truth of things with him before and been deflected.

The gap between what they have and what it's acknowledged to be is the central unresolved fact of this relationship. John is capable of more self-awareness than his communication style suggests. He is not capable, currently, of the sustained sobriety and emotional availability that a stable version of this relationship would require. The DUI, the D&A appointments, the March 2026 message about not being paid while acknowledging he gave her money he needed — all of it points to a man still in the thick of the struggle that defines this period of his life.

He is not a villain. He is a person who is loved more carefully than he loves, who gives more materially than he gives emotionally, and who has not yet done the work that would make him a safe person to be in a named relationship with. Whether he ever does is not a question the data can answer.

What the data can answer is this: Suzanne has been the more invested party, the more consistent party, and the more honest party throughout this history. She told him what mattered to her. She reached back out after he exploded and blocked her. She has been doing the maintenance that keeps this alive. If and when she stops, it will go quiet — and John, in the silence, will probably not know why.
