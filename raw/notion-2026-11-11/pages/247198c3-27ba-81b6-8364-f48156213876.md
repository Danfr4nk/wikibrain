### Step 1: <b>Identify Competitors</b>

Understanding the landscape starts with identifying direct and indirect competitors. This prompt helps discover who your competitors are, whether they are well-known or newer, and whether they directly target your audience.

<b>Prompt:</b>

<i>I am the founder of \[Insert Company Name\]. Who are my competitors in the \[industry\] space?</i>

<b>Example Response:</b>

- <b>Direct Competitors:</b>
  - List direct competitors offering similar services/products to yours.
- <b>Indirect Competitors:</b>
  - Identify businesses providing alternatives or substitute solutions.

### Step 4: <b>Competitive Positioning</b>

To find ways to stand out, use competitive research to identify features and tactics that attract your competitors’ audience. You can then apply these tactics to enhance your own brand’s appeal.

<b>Prompt:</b>

<i>What should I do on my site to attract competitors’ audiences? Analyze competitors’ sites for the most effective tactics.</i>

<b>Example Response:</b>

- <b>Highlight Core Features:</b> Emphasize unique features like customization, scalability, and integrations.
- <b>Content Variety:</b> Showcase diverse use cases to appeal to a broader creator base.
- <b>Creator Tools:</b> Offer tools that simplify the content creation and marketing process for creators.
- <b>Monetization:</b> Clearly highlight monetization options and success stories.
- <b>Community Engagement:</b> Focus on building a strong, interactive community for users.

---

### Step 5: <b>Identify Competitor Weaknesses</b>

This step digs into competitors’ weaknesses by examining customer reviews, complaints, and feedback. Identifying gaps allows you to position your product as the solution to these pain points.

<b>Prompt:</b>

<i>Research reviews of \[Competitor Name\] and identify common complaints. What are the gaps Substack could address?</i>

<b>Example Response:</b>

- <b>Pricing Structure:</b> Users feel pricing is high, especially with growing audiences.
- <b>Platform Complexity:</b> Some users complain about the platform’s growing complexity.
- <b>Deliverability Issues:</b> Customers mention challenges with email delivery rates.
- <b>Free Plan Limitations:</b> Recent changes have limited features on the free plan.
- <b>Customer Support:</b> Users report frustration with customer service responses.

---

### Step 6: <b>Final Strategic Takeaways</b>

To conclude, you can synthesize your research into actionable insights that help refine your business strategy and improve your competitive positioning. These takeaways should guide decisions for product development, marketing, and customer engagement.

<b>Prompt:</b>

<i>Using the insights from the competitor analysis, summarize what changes you should make to your product/service or marketing strategy to gain a competitive edge.</i>

<b>Example Response:</b>

- <b>Product Enhancements:</b> Introduce simpler, more intuitive tools for beginners.
- <b>Pricing Adjustments:</b> Offer a more competitive pricing structure with better value at each tier.
- <b>Feature Improvements:</b> Improve customer support, enhance email deliverability, and provide more generous free-tier offerings.
- <b>Marketing Focus:</b> Emphasize ease of use and integration capabilities.

---

### Conclusion: <b>Monitoring and Adapting</b>

Competitor analysis is an ongoing process. Continuously monitor your competitors, track changes in their offerings, and adapt your strategy accordingly. Use these steps to stay ahead of the curve, offering unique solutions that solve your audience’s pain points.

<b>Remember:</b> The more specific you can be in defining your competitors, target audience, and unique offerings, the more actionable your analysis will be.

---

This framework will guide you through a comprehensive competitor analysis that’s not just about tracking competitors, but about strategically positioning your own brand for success. Use these steps as an ongoing resource to refine your business approach.
